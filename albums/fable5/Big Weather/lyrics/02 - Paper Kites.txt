Why this piece

The album's lightest weather: A-major jangle-pop built on wall-to-wall
picked add9 eighth notes, with a glide lead that slides between hook notes
like a kite on a string. The signature move is the pre-chorus: the choir
swells from a closed "oo" to an open "ah" as the pad rises - the sound of
wind picking up. The hums say "paper kites / catch the wind / over
rooftops / fly on".

Listening guide

0:00 - Intro: the jangle engine starts alone, then piano joins.
0:16 - Verse: melodic bass walking under the eighth-note shimmer.
0:48 - Pre-chorus: the choir swell, oo to ah, snare sixteenths launching.
1:05 - Chorus: the glide lead sings the hook, portamento engaged.
2:26 - Middle-8: strings gate in for the first time, everything softens.
2:42 - Final chorus: a drive guitar lifts the jangle, the choir opens wide.
3:23 - Outro: the kites let go - the engine winds down to a last chime.

MIDI techniques

CC5/CC65 portamento gives the lead its glide; CC68 slurs tie its phrases.
CC70 vowel curves drive the oo-to-ah morphs (proved audible in the render,
not just present in the file). CC64 pedals the piano, CC11 shapes the
string swells, CC94 throws echo accents. The second guitar chimes on GM25
and lifts to GM29 only for the last chorus. All sustained beds centred;
the render loses only 0.08 dB summed to mono.
