Why this piece

Drum feature number one: a D-mixolydian rooftop chase at 132 BPM where the
kit is the protagonist. Two composed solos use the drum kit's real stereo
stage - hats left, crash-1 and splash far left, high tom a third left,
ride right, china and crash-2 far right - so the phrases genuinely move
across the room. Brass stabs and a wah rhythm guitar keep the chase alive
between them. The hums say "run the rooftops / wire to wire".

Listening guide

0:00 - The chase riff: overdriven bVII stomp, brass answering.
0:43 - Chorus: the lead hook over D-D-G-G, riff underneath.
1:12 - SOLO I (16 bars): hat-call/ride-answer, then china-and-crash
       antiphony over tom circuits, two 32nd-note burst bars, and a
       crash-trading summit - a sparse bass pedal is the only companion.
2:40 - Breakdown: the band thins to a heartbeat.
2:54 - SOLO II (8 bars, drums alone): the antiphony figure returns twice,
       a two-bar 32nd shimmer, and a rising floor-to-high "rooftop rush".
3:09 - Double final chorus; 4:07 the riff jumps one last parapet.

MIDI techniques

The solos are oracle-checked for width and density: 18-22 hits per bar,
nine distinct pan groups, mandatory 32nd-burst bars, and left/right
alternations - and the render must prove the spread (side/mid energy in
the solo windows). Velocity opens the kit's timbre (harder = brighter);
fills track the toms' overshoot-and-settle pitch glide. CC74+CC71 drive
the wah guitar; brass growls are aftertouch; the bass glides with CC5/65.
