Why this piece

The album's power ballad and its clearest demonstration of "the orchestra
arrives as the song builds": from a rubato solo piano to a thirteen-channel
tutti in one unbroken staircase, every entry gated and machine-checked.
The final chorus lifts the whole song from C major to D. The choir hums
"glass anthem / hold the light / through the storm / we shine on".

Listening guide

0:00 - Rubato piano alone: the tempo genuinely breathes (86-94 BPM)
       before locking to 100 at the first verse.
0:26 - Verse 1: una corda piano and a single choir wisp.
1:05 - Pre-chorus: the drums' first heartbeat; 1:14 chorus 1, bass and
       clean guitar now in.
2:12 - Chorus 2: drive guitar and second choir layer.
2:41 - Middle-8: the pullback - strings enter under the hush, CC11 swells.
2:58 - A timpani roll on the dominant opens the door...
3:01 - ...and the final chorus lands in D major: brass, third choir layer,
       organ, the album's largest single lift (16 dB over verse 1).
3:59 - Outro: the intro motif returns, lifted with the new key, and the
       tempo eases away.

MIDI techniques

Ten real tempo events (rubato intro, middle-8 pullback, outro easing).
CC67 una corda in the verses, CC64 pedalling throughout, CC11 string
swells, CC70 choir morphs, channel-aftertouch brass growls, Leslie ramps
after the EP-to-organ program change, drive lift 29 to 30 at the key
change. The layer staircase is pinned by an oracle counting active
channels per section: 1, 2, 4, 6, 7, 7, 9, 13.
