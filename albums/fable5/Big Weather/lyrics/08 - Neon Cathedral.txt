Why this piece

The album's breather, and its most architectural track: E minor at 96 BPM,
opening inside a real cathedral - the synth's pipe-organ voice with its
long decorrelated stone reverb - whose theme the band then carries out
into the neon. Echo-guitar phrases are answered by their own delays; the
long swell in the back half is the track's single dynamic peak. The hums
say "neon cathedral / every window burning / hold up the sky".

Listening guide

0:00 - PRELUDE: the cathedral organ alone; the THEME, stated plainly.
0:30 - The band enters quietly: brushes, una corda piano, echo guitar,
       bass; the organ becomes a drawbar instrument and stays.
1:10 - Chorus: the hook arches on the echo guitar.
2:10 - Chorus 2: a dark choir bed arrives.
2:40 - Bridge in G major: a portamento square lead, the first real light.
3:05 - The SWELL: strings gate in, sticks replace brushes, everything
       rises for twenty bars...
3:25 - ...to the peak: the hook returns on a driven guitar, aftertouch
       leaning into the organ and strings.
3:55 - Reprise: the prelude theme again - now on the band, quietly,
       the cathedral remembered rather than revisited.

MIDI techniques

The cathedral program (GM19) is confined to the prelude by oracle - after
the first verse the organ channel is re-programmed and never returns to
it, keeping the mono-hostile stone tail contained (measured: 0.09 dB mono
loss). The echo probe is a true A/B: the same staccato tag played dry
(CC94 authored to zero) and thrown (CC94 spike), 3.9 dB apart in the
render. Aftertouch swells organ and strings; CC5/65 glides the bridge
lead; theme and hook recalls are recomputed from the score, never copied.
