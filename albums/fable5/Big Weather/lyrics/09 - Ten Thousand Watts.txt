Why this piece

The fastest, hardest song on the record, and drum feature number two -
built to contrast the first: where "Run the Rooftops" soloed in long
phrases over a pedal, this one trades strict fours with the band, then
takes eight bars completely alone. A-minor power chords, orchestra hits,
late-gated timpani - and a produced drop (drone and cymbal swell, never
silence) before the re-entry slam. The hums say "ten thousand watts /
every light on".

Listening guide

0:00 - The stomp riff: bVII/bVI power chords at 138.
0:55 - Chorus: the hook surges an octave in its third bar.
2:19 - TRADING FOURS: three rounds - four bars of band, four of drums
       alone; the drum fours escalate from kick-snare engine to cymbal
       chokes to a preview of the cyclone.
3:00 - BREAKDOWN SOLO, unaccompanied: the 32nd-note around-the-kit
       CYCLONE, chokes, the engine at maximum - phrase-built, not noise.
3:14 - The DROP: a fretless-smooth bass drone slides A3 to A2 under a
       cymbal swell, the room holds its breath (13 dB down)...
3:21 - ...SLAM: the final chorus lands 13.6 dB up in a single beat,
       timpani now underneath everything.
3:56 - Outro: the riff, the watts cut one by one.

MIDI techniques

The trading fours are an oracle (band bars and drum bars strictly
alternate; drum fours band-silent); the solo passes the album's full
drum-solo gauntlet (density, burst bars, six-plus keys over five-plus pan
groups, wide-voice quota, left/right alternations) and its stereo spread
is re-proved in the render. The drop's drone uses CC5/65 portamento and
CC11 ducking so the gap oracle never trips. Orchestra hits are stabs
only; timpani enters at the slam (late-gated).
