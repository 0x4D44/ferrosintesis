Why this piece

The title track and closer: D major at 120, the album's fullest sound and
its quietest ending. The piano states the title hook alone; twelve gated
entries later the entire thirteen-channel band and orchestra are singing
it together; and then the storm rolls out - a real ritardando, from 120
down to 84 - until only the piano is left, playing exactly the phrase it
began with. The choir hums "big weather / coming in / hold together /
here it comes".

Listening guide

0:00 - Solo piano: the title hook, twice (una corda the first time).
0:16 - Verse 1: bass, clean guitar and drums arrive.
1:04 - Chorus 1: the lead sings the piano's hook, note-for-note.
2:16 - Chorus 2: choir and brass gate in.
2:40 - Middle-8: the eye of the storm - strings enter, four bars of
       dominant build.
3:04 - THE DOUBLE FINAL CHORUS: thirty-two bars of full tutti - three
       choir layers, brass, strings, timpani on every downbeat, organ,
       both guitars driven - the album's total sound.
4:08 - Outro: the tutti decays; at the hush a woodblock taps "BW" in
       Morse; the tempo lets go (120 to 84); the solo piano closes the
       circle at 4:24.

MIDI techniques

The bookend is an oracle: the outro piano statement must equal the intro
statement, recomputed by the same code. The tutti is counted, not
claimed: every final-chorus bar carries at least eleven channels, most
all thirteen. Six tempo events shape the ritardando. CC70 morphs the
choirs, CC2 and aftertouch push the brass, CC11 swells the strings, the
Leslie spins on the organ, and the Morse garnish is en.morse(), velocity
38, dead centre.
