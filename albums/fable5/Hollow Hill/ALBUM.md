# HOLLOW HILL

**An instrumental epic in two parts — 26:29 — after the school of Mike Oldfield.**

One work, two sides, in the tradition of the two-part LP epic: a 13/8 ostinato
that wakes the piece and returns transfigured, a singing folk theme that is minor
on the way out and major on the way home, interlocking guitars, an ambient ocean
on each side, a chant, a storm, a roll-call of instruments crowned by tubular
bells, a false ending, and a reel to see everyone out the door.

## 01 — Hollow Hill, Part One · 13:56

*Mood: patient, gathering, finally triumphant. Role: the ascent.*

| from | movement | character |
|------|----------|-----------|
| 0:00 | **I. Dawn** | the Hill ostinato — 13 quavers, 3+3+3+2+2 — alone on piano; glockenspiel, a singing bass and picked nylon guitar steal in (E dorian) |
| 1:24 | **II. First Light** | a walking bass line and fingerpicked guitar set the figure in motion; the flute calls a fragment of the theme |
| 2:32 | **III. The Hill Theme** | the tune in full, three statements — guitar, flute, then fiddle and guitar in octaves — floating over the odd metre |
| 3:40 | **IV. Interlock** | two acoustic guitars in 16th-note hocket, hand percussion arriving in waves |
| 5:31 | **V. Deep Water** | the ambient pool: warm pads, a singing fretless bass, crystal arpeggios, echoing clean guitar, a music-box echo of the bell motif (C lydian) |
| 8:01 | **VI. Stormrise** | distorted riff climbing E → F# → G → A over driving strummed acoustics, timpani, tremolo strings; a hard cut and one ringing bell |
| 9:57 | **VII. The Summoning** | over a four-chord ground the instruments are called in one by one — grand piano, glockenspiel, reed and pipe organ, two acoustic guitars, mandolin, Spanish guitar, overdriven guitar, strings and choir… **plus: tubular bells** — and the hill turns to E major |

## 02 — Hollow Hill, Part Two · 12:34

*Mood: pastoral, then uncanny, then jubilant. Role: the way home.*

| from | movement | character |
|------|----------|-----------|
| 0:00 | **I. Green Morning** | picked guitars and harp in D major; the Hill theme now smiling, on flute and fiddle |
| 2:17 | **II. The Dance Under the Hill** | a 6/8 jig — fiddle, whistle, bodhrán-ish toms — lifting from D to E for the last lap |
| 4:12 | **III. Stone Voices** | a low phrygian chant in organum over drones and a slow drum; the bell tolls through the last verses |
| 5:52 | **IV. The Night Ocean** | the second ambient pool (A major); celesta plays the bell motif over fretless bass and ringing clean-guitar arpeggios — and far away a woodblock taps a name in Morse code |
| 8:32 | **V. The Return** | the 13/8 ostinato reborn in E major, layer on layer, the theme in counterpoint, bells pealing — the summit, and a great ringing chord… |
| 10:49 | *(a breath)* | …three seconds of silence. It's over. It is not over. |
| 10:52 | **VII. The Hollow Hill Reel** | a fast folk finale, accelerating 112 → 138 bpm, banjo and handclaps aboard; three stab chords, one bell, and a cheeky woodblock wink |

## The recurring threads

- **The Hill ostinato** — 13 quavers grouped 3+3+3+2+2: a falling inner voice
  (8–7–6…–2) threaded through a pedal on the fifth. E dorian at dawn, E ionian
  at the return.
- **The Hill theme** — a 32-beat folk tune that arches to the ninth and comes
  home; minor in Part One, major in Part Two.
- **The bell motif** — a six-note peal for tubular bells, echoed by music box
  and celesta in the two ocean movements.
