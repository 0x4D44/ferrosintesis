**The nine bells.** Eight tubular-bell tolls frame the sections (the
theme's rising-sixth cell, inverted into a falling toll); the ninth — a
single A, the theme's withheld resolution — is the piece's last note.
That is where the title comes from.

**The material.** One 13-note theme carries everything: stated as a cello
lament, whispered by the music box in the void, and pealed fortissimo at
the climax. Its opening rising minor sixth (the anguish leap) becomes the
bells; its collapsing-staircase countersubject is verifier-clean over both
the home ground and the hardened climax ground, so it can run straight
through the peak. The theme ends hanging on the second degree, wanting the
tonic — and only the ninth bell grants it.

The brief uses Gabriel-Knight-vocabulary gestures (Robert Holmes'
dark-cathedral idiom) and asks for a new composition rather than a
reconstruction of a named piece.
