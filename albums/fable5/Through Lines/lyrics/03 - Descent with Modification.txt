Why this piece

This is evolution made audible, not merely illustrated. A seeded genetic algorithm breeds four-bar motifs with mutation and crossover; the same fitness rule that selects the lineages is rerun by the verifier. Population growth, extinction and renewed diversity are therefore the recorded history of an actual reproducible evolutionary run.

Listening guide

0:00 — Primordial Waters: warm pad drone and one repeated marimba note establish generation zero.
1:04 — First Replicators: kalimba, vibraphone, celesta, harp, nylon guitar and xylophone arrive as copies accumulate mutation errors.
2:37 — Cambrian Explosion: eight mallet, pluck and pizzicato lineages flower over percussion.
3:45 — Selection: weaker lineages starve while the surviving variants continue.
4:44 — Extinction: bank-B reverse cymbal becomes an orchestra hit, joined by timpani and percussion; the population crashes.
5:13 — Re-radiation: survivors occupy new niches as vibraphone becomes flute, celesta becomes oboe, harp becomes fiddle and guitar becomes bass.
6:29 — Emergence: four choir parts harmonize the fittest descendant with flute, fiddle, drone and timpani.
7:30 — A half cadence leaves the evolutionary story unfinished.

MIDI techniques

Program changes at re-radiation make instrumental adaptation literal. CC11 shapes the persistent drone and the four-part choral emergence; CC70 opens the choir vowels, while CC1 adds vibrato to the evolved flute and fiddle. CC0 bank 1 selects the alternate reverse-cymbal voice before its later orchestra-hit change. Static CC7 spans 90-110 and CC91 30-60. Transient lineages are spread at CC10 40, 76, 88 and 112; drones, evolved winds/strings, choir and low voices remain centred. The beat-zero bank select occurs while its channel is silent, but it is a persistent setup for the later cataclysm, not audible automation.
