Why this piece

The album's only straight-ahead swing imagines climbing three flights to a small night-jazz room above a bar. The head's rising motif makes the stairs audible, while the form honours the social logic of a working quartet: introduce the tune, give everyone space, trade compact ideas, shout together, then say the tag three times.

Listening guide

0:00 — Stairwell: genuinely solo piano breathes through a sculpted rubato introduction.
0:28 — Head In: muted trumpet states the three-flight tune over piano, walking upright bass, jazz guitar and brush kit at 92 bpm.
1:10 — Piano Solo: the rhythm section maintains a 2:1 swing.
1:52 — Guitar Solo: jazz guitar takes the next half-chorus.
2:13 — Trading Fours: piano, guitar and drums alternate strict four-bar turns; markers name every hand-off.
3:15 — Shout Chorus: the quartet lifts briefly to 95 bpm and plays together.
3:36 — Head Out: the trumpet head returns transposed with the same melodic design.
4:16 — Tag x3: three closing tags tighten the cadence; trumpet expression falls after the final accent.
