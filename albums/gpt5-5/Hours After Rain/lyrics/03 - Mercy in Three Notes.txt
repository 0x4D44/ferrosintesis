Why this piece
A deliberately small three-note cell carries a long wounded lament. The point is not melodic abundance but the changing weight of the same appeal as harmony, register, and silence repeatedly reconsider it.

Listening guide
0:00 — Opening: slow piano frames the cell in F minor at 58 bpm; ensemble and low strings enter at 0:17, cello at 0:33, and celesta at 0:50.
1:22 — The pulse lifts gently to 62 bpm.
2:09 — Development: violin enters at 2:25 and viola at 2:45, lengthening the piano/string/celesta lament.
3:11 — At 66 bpm the wound opens into the track's broadest span.
4:09 — Turn: piano, upper strings, ensemble, basses, and celesta begin giving the cell back to silence.
5:00 — Tempo falls to 54 bpm.
5:44 — Coda: piano, cello, ensemble, and low strings make the final appeal without resolving it.

MIDI techniques
GM programs are acoustic piano, cello, viola, violin, string ensemble, contrabass, and celesta. Static CC7 volume, CC10 pan, and CC91 reverb provide the chamber space. Per-bar CC11 curves breathe with the track's intensity on every lane; piano CC64 alternates sustained chords with shorter releases. The lament is written phrasing and tempo, not pitch effects. There is no percussion, CC1 vibrato lane, CC74 filter, chorus/echo automation, pitch bend, aftertouch, portamento, or program change after 0:00.
