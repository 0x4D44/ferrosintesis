Why this piece
An interior remembers its occupants through resonance rather than narrative. Warm piano harmonics and answering cello phrases make absence feel inhabited; the returning minor harmony asks whether memory belongs to people or places.

Listening guide
0:00 — Opening: piano enters a warm G-minor room at 68 bpm; ensemble and low strings follow at 0:14, then cello at 0:28.
1:24 — The memory stirs at 72 bpm.
1:41 — Development: celesta enters at 1:42, violin at 1:51, and viola at 2:49; answering phrases appear in different registers.
3:11 — Tempo reaches 76 bpm.
3:17 — Turn: piano, viola, ensemble, basses, and celesta retain the room while cello and violin recede.
4:14 — Tempo falls to 64 bpm.
4:29 — Coda: piano, cello, ensemble, and low strings return as the room's oldest memory.

MIDI techniques
GM programs are piano, solo and ensemble strings, contrabass, and celesta. Static CC7 volume, CC10 pan, and CC91 reverb create the haunted interior. Every lane carries per-bar CC11 expression waves tied to the score's intensity; piano CC64 sustain lets harmonies overlap and release. The answering effect is composed counterpoint, not delay. There is no percussion, mod-wheel automation, wah/filter, chorus/echo send, pitch bend, aftertouch, portamento, or mid-piece program change.
