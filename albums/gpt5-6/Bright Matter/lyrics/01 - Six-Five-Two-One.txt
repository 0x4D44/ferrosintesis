Why this piece
The album opens by turning its 6–5–2–1 harmonic premise into an ecstatic build-and-drop track. A circling steel-pan hook and steadily growing ensemble make the abstract number pattern feel physical before the finale lifts it from C to D major.

Listening guide
BRIGHT MATTER — INSTRUMENTAL LISTENING GUIDE

0:00  I. COUNT THE SPARKS
A steel-pan transient begins its first full circle. Electric piano and the vi–V–ii–I bass premise appear one layer at a time.

0:14  II. FIRST CLIMB
Hats subdivide, bass wakes up, brass trades short calls, and melodic-tom fills grow from eight to sixteen to twenty-eight notes.

0:43  III. DROP ONE
The number hook lands over four-on-the-floor drums, sidechain-shaped pads, antiphonal brass, and orchestra-hit punctuation.

1:34  IV. NEGATIVE SPACE
Tempo halves. The kick and bass pulse vanish; harp, high strings, choir, and a distant version of the hook hang in the air.

2:03  V. SECOND CLIMB
The same ascent returns with earlier kick entry, choir vowel motion, distorted-guitar bends, brighter piano pulses, and denser fills.

2:47  VI. DROP TWO
The orbit rises an octave. Sixteenth hats, tambourine, guitar, harp, choir, synth drum, and full brass make this drop measurably larger.

3:45  VII. EVERY NUMBER AT ONCE
All established layers stack while the hook gains a diatonic upper harmony.

4:07  VIII. TWO STEPS BRIGHTER
The whole arrangement lifts from C to D major without losing the 6–5–2–1 ground.

4:28  IX. AFTERIMAGE
The arena disappears, leaving high strings, harp, one slowed hook, and a final point of light.

MIDI techniques
CC10 automation carries the steel-pan figure around the full stereo circle while sustained beds remain centred for mono safety. Authored expression curves create sidechain-like pumps; progressively denser melodic-tom fills, exact 6–5–2–1 bass roots, a drumless half-tempo bridge, and the two-semitone finale lift are all encoded as score events and checked by track oracles.
