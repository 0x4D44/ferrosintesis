# Every Voice Forward — listening notes

Four small melodic ideas recur across the album:

- **Daybreak** rises and answers itself: curiosity becoming confidence.
- **Engines** is more rhythmic and grounded: forward motion under load.
- **Open Sky** reaches across a wider interval, designed for breath, bends and long lines.
- **World** is pentatonic-leaning and communal, able to live in folk strings, tuned percussion or electronics.

The first four tracks introduce one idea each while surveying a quarter of the GM map. The fifth assembles all four.

## 1. Daybreak Relay — 3:55

**Programs 0–31.** The hook is passed hand-to-hand rather than merely replayed over patch changes.

- **0:00 — Wake.** GM/GS/XG state, then a small breath before the first note.
- **0:02 — Eight Keys, One Sunrise.** Acoustic and electric pianos divide melody, pulse, bass and sparkle. Sustain, sostenuto and soft-pedal curves make the section physically played rather than quantised furniture.
- **1:00 — Light Learns to Bounce.** Celesta through dulcimer turn the same material into reflected points of light. Vibraphone motor, tuned bells and moving stereo placement keep the colour in motion.
- **1:58 — The Air Starts Singing.** Drawbar organs accelerate their Leslie through CC1; church organ, reed organ, accordion, harmonica and tango accordion gradually become one breathing machine.
- **2:56 — Strings Catch Fire.** Nylon and steel acoustics begin cleanly; jazz, clean, muted, overdriven and distorted electrics take over. Legato retunes ringing strings, CC74/71 create wah and resonance, and wider RPN bends push the final solo through an XG amp-simulator insertion.
- **3:25 — Relay Acceleration.** Earlier colours flash past in compressed form.
- **3:39 — Every String Upward.** The guitar family stacks without abandoning the hook.
- **3:51 — Sun on the Last Chord.** A held major sonority lets body resonance and effects decay speak.

## 2. Brighter Engines — 4:21

**Programs 32–63.** A low, mechanical beginning gradually discovers breath and wings.

- **0:02 — Eight Engines Under One Road.** The eight bass programs form a contrapuntal machine: acoustic/electric/fretless/slap/synth basses trade roots, answers and syncopations.
- **1:03 — The Road Becomes a Bow.** Violin, viola, cello, contrabass, tremolo and pizzicato strings, harp and timpani convert the groove into an orchestral climb. RPN fine tuning offsets selected desks by a few cents; mod wheel and expression produce a sectional swell. Individual violin notes receive polyphonic pressure.
- **2:04 — A Choir Finds the Motor.** String ensembles and synth strings support three vocal programs. CC70 moves their formants through Ferrosintesis’s four anchors—closed hum to open vowel—while pressure grows inside held notes.
- **3:05 — Brass Against the Blue.** Trumpet through synth brass inherit the engine rhythm. Breath and expression open the bore; channel pressure adds growl and the fortissimo model’s brass edge. Wide bends are used sparingly as lip gestures rather than keyboard wheels.
- **4:04 — Every Engine Becomes a Wing.** One voice from each family returns in an eight-bar coda.
- **4:17 — Horizon Release.** The machine stops pushing and the harmony continues on its own.

## 3. Open-Sky Signal — 3:50

**Programs 64–95.** The album’s most airborne track moves from acoustic breath to synthetic atmosphere.

- **0:02 — The Reeds Draw a Skyline.** Sax quartet and woodwind quartet interlock; clarinet carries the Open Sky theme. Breath, mod wheel, legato and pressure create player-shaped lines.
- **0:58 — Air Above the Buildings.** Piccolo, flute, recorder, pan flute, bottle, shakuhachi, whistle and ocarina take the melody upward. Sampled attacks remain articulate while bend/legato turns longer notes into continuous breath.
- **1:55 — Seven Steps Through the Cloud.** Eight synth leads enter a 7/8 hocket. Every part has authored portamento time, switch and source, a ±12-semitone bend range, and its own filter/effect profile.
- **2:19 — The Sky Answers in Four.** The metre resolves to 4/4; saw and square leads state a broad answer while bends stretch beyond the grid.
- **2:51 — Eight Colours of Horizon.** New-age, warm, polysynth, choir, bowed, metallic, halo and sweep pads each hold a separate register. CC74 opens from dark to daylight while CC71 raises and releases resonance; pan, expression, chorus and delay move independently.
- **3:19 — Filters to Daylight.** The horizon narrows, then opens one final time.
- **3:41 — Open-Sky Signal.** All eight pads land together without masking the last melodic statement.

## 4. The World in the Chorus — 4:29

**Programs 96–127, plus every GM percussion key.** Persistent bass, strings, pad, lead, steel drums, sitar and the banked mandolin keep this from becoming a patch audition.

- **0:02 — Weather with a Pulse.** Rain, soundtrack, crystal, atmosphere, brightness, goblins, echoes and sci-fi voices become long-form colour over a stable groove. The LSB-96 mandolin supplies a dry offbeat human pulse.
- **1:04 — The World Joins In.** Sitar, banjo, shamisen, koto, kalimba, bagpipe, fiddle and shanai share the World theme. The sampled bagpipe later returns through its modeled MSB-1 alternate.
- **2:06 — Everything That Can Ring.** Tinkle bell, agogo, steel drums, woodblock, taiko, melodic toms and synth drum form a pitched percussion ensemble rather than a sequence of isolated hits.
- **3:08 — The World Becomes the Chorus.** Guitar fret noise, breath noise, seashore, bird, telephone, helicopter, applause and gunshot are treated as scene changes around continuing harmony.
- **4:10 — Forty-Seven Drums, One Celebration.** A GS SysEx message converts a second channel into a rhythm part. Keys 35–81 all sound exactly as part of an eight-bar parade; uncommon percussion appears as fills while kick and snare preserve the groove.
- **4:25 — One World, Open Hands.** The second rhythm part is explicitly released and the anchors close on a quiet major chord.

## 5. Every Voice Forward — 4:49

Fifteen selected voices: grand piano, vibraphone, cathedral organ, mandolin, fretless bass, harp, strings, choir, French horn, tenor sax, flute, saw lead, warm pad, sitar and steel drums.

- **0:02 — A Small Spark Chooses Forward.** The Daybreak theme begins almost privately.
- **0:29 — The Road Learns to Lift.** Engines enters below it; the first theme remains rather than yielding.
- **0:57 — The Air Opens Above Us.** Sax, flute and lead introduce Open Sky across the upper register.
- **1:24 — The World Steps Into the Chorus.** Sitar and steel drums add the fourth idea.
- **1:51 — Seven Steps, No Ceiling.** Fragments of all four tracks hocket through 7/8. The point is anticipation, not maximal loudness.
- **2:27 — First Full-Voice Drop.** Four themes begin to coexist over organ, rhythm and transient orbit. Controllers widen gradually so the drop still has somewhere to go.
- **3:09 — Room Enough for Hope.** Drums disappear. Sostenuto piano, soft pedal, harp, choir and the MSB-2 cathedral organ create negative space; opposing RPN fine tuning keeps the held texture alive.
- **3:36 — The Second Ascent.** The pad temporarily becomes XG Hollow Release, leaving a long wake behind the returning rhythm.
- **4:03 — Every Voice Forward.** A two-semitone lift reaches B major. Daybreak, Engines, Open Sky and World now run simultaneously for a verified extended span; answer voices and a bending lead grow around them without replacing them.
- **4:43 — Open Hands, Open Sky.** Tempo relaxes. All fifteen voices take different registers of the final chord, then controllers and sound are explicitly released.

## Structural intent

“Use every instrument” can easily produce a catalogue demo that is technically exhaustive and musically disposable. This suite uses **rotation instead of accumulation**: eight related programs share a chapter, while recurring themes, anchors and harmonic motion preserve identity. Coverage is strict; orchestration is selective.
