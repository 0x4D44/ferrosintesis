# The Architecture of Air

Stone does not sing. Air does.

The first C is deliberately given the whole building. Its lowest rank sits near
the edge of hearing, where pitch becomes pressure. From there, the piece learns
height: principals begin to walk, lines cross in the vault, and a tremulant
makes one unmoving chord breathe.

At the centre, ten pipes ask for wind from one chest. Their shared pressure
settles under the load; one survivor then recovers in the sudden space. The high
mixtures answer with light rather than weight. Bells appear only after the organ
has proved that brilliance is already inside its own ranks.

The ending is not a fade. Twelve notes release together. The instrument stops,
but the architecture keeps the harmony for a little while longer.
