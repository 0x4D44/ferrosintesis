Why this piece

This is VIGIL's romantic centre: grief made direct rather than implied. The falling D-C-B-flat memory figure is coloured by G harmonic minor, where F-sharp pulls painfully toward G. Each return adds weight until cello and high violin speak the same unsent thought at the largest point, then the music can only fall away.

Listening guide

0:00 — Contrabass and strings begin the eight-bar lament, including the exposed D/F-sharp dominant pull.
0:29 — Cello states the low D-C-B-flat theme.
0:58 — Piano, viola, and a lower second string bed join the next pass.
1:27 — Violin answers above the cello as the harmony grows warmer and more urgent.
1:56 — Broken piano motion increases and the full ensemble gathers.
2:25 — The extended climax begins: high violin and cello share the theme over the densest string writing.
3:24 — Violin withdraws; two long ground cycles carry the broken descent.
4:22 — The coda returns to quieter pad, bass, and cello.
4:51 — G minor dissolves beneath a high A, an unresolved ninth standing for the unanswered letter.

MIDI techniques

This type-1 MIDI file runs at a constant 66 BPM with 480 ticks per beat. Seven GM channels use piano and the six bowed/string colours of the album palette. The harmonic-minor F-sharp is written directly into the D-major dominant chord and bass line; it is not a pitch-bend effect. Voice-led tied pads, sustained bass, and CC11 build the long arc, while the piano moves from sparse pedalled chords to eighth-note patterns near the summit. Seeded humanisation separates attacks across the ensemble. Aside from program changes, CC11, and piano CC64, no controllers, send effects, percussion, or tempo changes are authored.
