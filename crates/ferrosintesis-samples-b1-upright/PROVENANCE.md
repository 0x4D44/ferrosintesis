# Provenance — ferrosintesis-samples-b1-upright

Upright-piano attack/body bank for a **GM 0 Acoustic Grand alternate** (CC0 bank 5):
three recorded timbre layers (soft / normal / hard), mono 16-bit 44.1 kHz WAVs,
one per struck note per layer. Generated by
`tools/ferrosintesis-samples/prepare.py` (`_bake_b1upright`) from the committed
Opus archive under `samples/b1-upright/`. The WAVs under `samples/` are **source,
not build output** — committed and embedded via `include_bytes!`.

**This is a real upright, not a grand** — Arthur's own Yamaha B1, a warmer, boxier
acoustic with genuine per-note inharmonicity and Railsback stretch.

## Source (first-party)

Arthur recorded his own **Yamaha B1 acoustic upright** on a **Tascam DR-05**
(24-bit / 48 kHz / stereo) on **2026-07-23**. Each pass plays the keyboard
ascending in thirds, every note held to full ring-out, sustain pedal up. No
third-party licence: the recording is Arthur's, released under the repo's own
MIT OR Apache-2.0 terms.

The reproducible source of record is the committed Opus archive (the raw 24-bit
takes are hundreds of MiB and are not committed):

| file | pass(es) | note |
|---|---|---|
| `samples/b1-upright/DR0000_0195_normal_soft.opus` | **normal** then **soft** | clean; peak −11.0 dBFS |
| `samples/b1-upright/DR0000_0200_hard.opus` | **hard** (re-recorded) | clean; peak −4.8 dBFS |

The hard take was re-recorded at reduced input gain after a first attempt clipped.
It sits **−9.4 dB ± 0.6 dB** below the normal/soft take, but Arthur played it at
the **same dynamic** (measured force difference −0.1 dB) — a true fortissimo layer,
not a re-labelled mezzo. The level offset is a recorder gain change, normalised out
by the per-file peak-normalisation in the bake; the brighter *spectrum* is what the
hard layer contributes.

## Layers & coverage

- **soft** ← 0195 soft pass (A0–B3 shared chain, then C4 E4 G4 B4 D5 F5 A5 C6 …B6).
- **normal** ← 0195 normal pass (A0 C1 E1 … B7 C8 chain).
- **hard** ← 0200 (same chain as normal).

Not every ladder rung survived clean pitch detection: A0 (weak upright fundamental)
and the very top of the soft pass (D7 F7 A7 C8, near-inaudible at that dynamic) are
absent, and the engine's nearest-zone selection covers those keys from the adjacent
recorded zone. The hard layer is complete A0–C8.

## Processing (deterministic)

1. Decode each committed opus to a temp WAV (`ropusdec`), named for the b1-slice
   TAKES table.
2. Slice per note with `tools/b1-slice/slice.py` (channel L only; onset by attack
   steepness; pitch by an inharmonic partial fit). The hard take's top B7 is
   force-assigned by ladder position — the top-octave detector cannot pitch it.
3. Per assigned slice: **20 Hz 4th-order Butterworth high-pass** (kills the DR-05's
   2–5 Hz infrasonic rumble + DC) on the 48 kHz signal; band-limited resample to
   44.1 kHz; `trim_to_onset` keeps the onset + **1.5 s** of body (0.6 s squared
   fade-out), peak-normalised to 0.9; 16-bit mono.

`root` (in `crates/ferrosintesis/src/sampler.rs`) is each slice's **measured first
partial f1**, so the note plays at exact equal temperament while its recorded
inharmonicity rides along untouched. The one exception is B7 on the hard layer,
whose top-octave pitch is unmeasurable — its root falls back to the ET frequency.

## Regenerating

```
python tools/ferrosintesis-samples/prepare.py --only=b1upright
```

Pure stdlib Python plus `ropusdec` on PATH (to decode the opus archive).
