# VIGIL
### a neo-classical song-cycle in twelve parts · ~53 minutes

*Music for the hours between. In the world of Max Richter, Philip Glass ("The Hours")
and Howard Shore ("The Departed"): strings and piano, slow time, a repeating ground
that remembers.*

Twelve pieces, one emotional journey — from a held breath of grief, through fragile
hope, restless motion, descent, tragedy, anxiety, stillness, catharsis, bittersweet
momentum, the deepest elegy, healing, and finally a light that turns at last to major.

The MIDI files are in **`midi/`**, numbered as an album. Play them through an
orchestral-strings library for the intended sound; the General-MIDI defaults are a
serviceable preview.

brief asked for — rapid (3, 6, 9), slow (1, 4, 5, 8, 10, 12), energetic (3, 6),
introspective (1, 2, 4, 7, 11), tragic (5, 8, 10) — shaped into one through-composed
journey.

## What makes twelve pieces one work — the album's DNA

- **The memory motif** — a falling three-note figure (scale-degrees 5–4–3 in the
  minor, e.g. A–G–F in D minor). It surfaces, transposed and re-coloured, across the
  cycle; tracks 1, 10 and 12 share it most directly, so the finale *remembers* the
  opener and finally resolves it.
- **The lament bass** — stepwise *descending* bass lines (the passacaglia / falling
  tetrachord) underpin the slow tracks: the oldest sign of sorrow in Western music.
- **Suspensions that resolve downward** — the "sigh." The feeling lives here, in
  voice-leading, not in chromaticism; the writing stays modal.
- **D is home.** The cycle opens in D minor and the finale turns, for the first and
  only time, to **D major** — the long-withheld cadence, catharsis as a Picardy third.
- **One palette.** Acoustic chamber forces throughout — Piano, Violin, Viola, Cello,
  Contrabass, and two string-ensemble beds — so the colour is consistent end to end.
- **One long breath per track.** Each piece is a single dynamic arc: enter from
  near-silence, swell to one climax, recede. The strings *breathe* via a continuous
  expression (CC11) envelope; nothing sits at a dead level.

## How it was made

Everything is generated by a small composition engine, **`engine.py`** — voice-leading,
a humanised event model, the dynamic arc, and idiomatic building blocks (`pad`, `bass`,
`arpeggiate`, `ostinato`, `melody`, `piano_chords`, expression swells, fades) plus a
MIDI writer and a structural analyser. Each track is one short, declarative score under
**`tracks/`** (see `tracks/01_of_the_light_that_stays.py` for a fully-commented example).

The eleven new tracks were composed and then **independently reviewed** track-by-track
for musical quality, voice-leading, fidelity to the brief, and distinctiveness. Review
findings were applied — most notably eliminating three accidental sustained
minor-second clashes where an under-pad doubled an inversion's bass note instead of the
chord root, restoring a soaring violin line that had dropped out of a climax, and
lifting a buried high-violin "sigh" into its true register. Every track is fully
diatonic to its mode (with only the declared, intentional chromatic devices: the
harmonic-minor leading tone in *Letters Unsent*, single grief-stabs in *Elegy*, and the
major/minor mixture of the finale), idiomatic in range, and ends musically rather than
cutting off.

*Honest caveat:* this machine has no MIDI synth or SoundFont, so verification was
**structural and analytical** (harmony, voice-leading, ranges, dynamic shape, length,
held-dissonance scans) rather than by ear. Audition through a real strings library and
fine adjustments may still be wanted — tell me what you hear.

## Regenerate / render

```bash
python build.py            # rebuilds all 12 midi/*.mid and reports the album length
python tracks/05_letters_unsent.py   # rebuild a single track
```

Bounce a track to audio with a SoundFont:

```bash
fluidsynth -ni <orchestral>.sf2 "midi/05 - Letters Unsent.mid" -F "05.wav" -r 44100
```

See **`ALBUM.md`** for the full creative design and per-track specifications.
