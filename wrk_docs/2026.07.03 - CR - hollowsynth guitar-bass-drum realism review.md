# CR — hollowsynth guitar / bass / drum realism review

_Date: 2026-07-03 · Repo: midi-music · Target: fable5/hollowsynth_

## Method

Six domain-expert reviewers (acoustic guitar, electric/distorted guitar, bass, drum membranes, drum metal/cymbals, Karplus-Strong & shared DSP) read the full source (`voices.rs`, `drums.rs`, `dsp.rs`, `engine.rs`) and proposed concrete, code-level realism improvements. Every finding was then adversarially verified through two independent lenses — a physical-acoustics lens (is the mechanism real and audible?) and a DSP-implementation lens (is the current-behaviour claim accurate and the fix stable/implementable?). **All 41 findings survived** (88 agents, 0 errors).

## Headline

hollowsynth is a strong *modelling* synth, but its realism ceiling for these three families is capped by five systemic gaps, in priority order:

1. **No guitar speaker-cabinet** — the single most defining colour of electric guitar is absent (one gentle lowpass stands in for a 4×12). *(GTR-1, I5)*
2. **Velocity moves loudness, not timbre** — across guitar, bass and drums a soft and a hard hit differ only in level, never in spectral centroid/attack. Real instruments change *tone* with force. *(KS-2, BASS-2, GTR-dyn, DRM-2)*
3. **No attack transients** — guitars/bass have no pick/finger click; drums have no sampled/modelled snap. The ear identifies an instrument by its onset, yet these families are 100% synthesised with a soft filtered-noise burst. *(GTR-2, BASS-1, DRM-3)*
4. **Cymbals & toms are under-modelled** — 6 partials + white noise cannot make a real cymbal's dense chaotic shimmer; toms are a single sine; no hi-hat choke group; no bloom/swell on crashes. *(CYM-1..7, DRM-1)*
5. **No kit/ensemble glue** — no drum room/overhead ambience, no sympathetic open-string halo on guitars/bass (the resonance bus is gated to piano only). *(DRM-7, KS-6, GTR-3)*

## Detail — GUITAR (16)

### GTR-1 — Amp has no speaker cabinet — a gentle lowpass replaces the single most defining coloration of electric guitar
*guitar · impact 5/5 · effort med*

**Now:** After the tanh nonlinearity the only post-EQ is one 2nd-order lowpass at 3400 Hz, Q 0.8. The pre stage is a single 90 Hz highpass. There is no model of a guitar speaker/cabinet.

**Gap:** A real distorted guitar tone is ~80% the loudspeaker + closed-back 4x12 cabinet: a resonant low bump around 90-120 Hz (cone/box resonance), a broad presence peak near 2-4 kHz, and a very steep rolloff (a hard cliff, ~24-48 dB/oct) above ~4.5-5 kHz that kills the fizz tanh generates. A flat Q=0.8 lowpass at 3400 Hz has none of these resonances and only a 12 dB/oct slope, so the tone reads as 'buzzy fuzz through a hi-fi' rather than a guitar amp. This is the biggest single realism gap for distorted guitar.

**Fix:** Replace the single `tone` lowpass with a minimal cabinet emulation applied post-tanh (still at 2x): (a) a low resonance `Biquad::peak(100.0, 1.2, +4.0)`, (b) a presence peak `Biquad::peak(2600.0, 1.5, +5.0)`, (c) a steep rolloff — cascade two `Biquad::lowpass(f, q)` sections to make ~24 dB/oct, e.g. lowpass(4800, 1.2) into lowpass(5200, 0.7). Optionally add a small notch near 400-600 Hz (`peak(500,1.0,-3)`) for the cab 'scoop'. This is a 3-5 biquad IIR cabinet, cheap and dependency-free. Because distortion is post-sum per channel, one cab per Drive insert is correct.

**Risk:** Overdoing the presence peak can make it harsh/ice-picky; the steep rolloff plus existing bus glue could dull leads. Tune peak gains conservatively (3-5 dB) and audition power chords vs single notes.

### KS-2 — Velocity does not move the spectral centroid - no dynamic brightness
*guitar · impact 4/5 · effort low*

**Now:** pick_lp is fixed at the preset value, the loop damping cutoff `bright` gets only a +/-8% random jitter, and velocity feeds in solely as amplitude (v/peak on the excitation plus the global vel_amp). Nothing about velocity changes the excitation lowpass or the loop cutoff.

**Gap:** On a real guitar/bass, playing harder dramatically raises the spectral centroid: the attack gets much brighter, more and higher harmonics appear, and the pick transient sharpens; soft playing is round and dull. Here a pp and an ff note differ only in level, so ff notes sound too dull and pp notes too bright. Velocity-dependent timbre is the single most audible dynamics cue and it is entirely missing for these families.

**Fix:** Compute vnorm = vel as f32 / 127.0 in Pluck::new and use it to open the excitation lowpass, e.g. pick_lp_eff = p.pick_lp * (0.45 + 1.3*vnorm), and the loop cutoff, e.g. bright_eff = p.bright * (0.6 + 0.7*vnorm), before constructing the OnePole filters and KsLoops. Optionally deepen the pick comb and move `pos` slightly toward the bridge at high velocity for extra bite.

**Risk:** At maximum velocity the scaled cutoffs can exceed sr*0.49 (already clamped in OnePole/Biquad, so safe) and a brighter loop lengthens high-harmonic ring; keep t60/loop_gain unchanged so decay stays controlled.

### GTR-1#2 — Dynamics change only loudness, never timbre — no velocity-dependent brightness
*guitar · impact 4/5 · effort low*

**Now:** In Pluck::new, `bright = p.bright * (1.0 + 0.08*rng.white())` and the excitation lowpass `OnePole::lowpass(p.pick_lp, sr)` are both velocity-independent. Velocity enters only as `v = vel_amp(vel)` scaling the excitation amplitude (line 521). Because the KS loop and its damping filter are linear, scaling excitation amplitude changes level but leaves the spectrum identical.

**Gap:** On a real guitar dynamics are primarily a timbral control, not a level control: a hard pluck displaces the string far and releases the pick sharply, exciting the full harmonic series and a bright edgy attack; a soft pluck is dark, round and fundamental-heavy. Here fff and ppp on the same key are spectrally identical, just louder/quieter — the single most audible 'synthetic' tell for plucked strings.

**Fix:** Derive `let vn = vel as f32/127.0;` and scale both filters: `let pick_lp = p.pick_lp * (0.45 + 0.85*vn);` (clamp to e.g. 400 Hz min) feeding the excitation OnePole, and `bright = p.bright * (0.65 + 0.5*vn) * (1.0+0.08*rng.white())` feeding the loop damper. Optionally raise the pick-comb depth with velocity (`0.75 + 0.2*vn` in place of the fixed 0.9 at line 517) so hard picks get a sharper notch. Keep amplitude scaling as-is.

**Risk:** Very soft notes can go muddy if pick_lp drops too far — clamp the lower bound (~600 Hz nylon, ~900 Hz steel). Re-check the pluck_pitch_a4 test tolerance since attack spectrum shifts slightly.

### GTR-2 — No pick/finger attack transient — the onset is a soft filtered-noise burst, not a pluck click
*guitar · impact 4/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Excitation is one full period of one-pole-lowpassed white noise with a pick-position comb, loaded into the delay line as the initial string shape. Output is purely the KS loop taps (line 590) plus body EQ. There is no separate broadband transient; the onset energy is the same lowpassed noise that becomes the sustained tone, smeared across a whole wavelength (~12 ms at E2).

**Gap:** A recorded pluck opens with a short (~1-3 ms) broadband transient that is louder and brighter than the steady tone: the pick/nail releasing and snapping past the string, the string slapping the first fret, and a low body knock. This click is what reads to the ear as 'a pick hit a string.' Filling one full period with band-limited noise produces a soft, breathy, almost harp-like onset instead.

**Fix:** Add a short click generator to the struct: a ~1.5 ms exponentially-decaying noise burst, highpassed (~1.5 kHz steel, ~1 kHz nylon), amplitude ∝ velocity and per-family 'pick hardness' (steel/clean brighter and louder than nylon), summed into `y` for the first ~64-128 samples of render before the body EQ. Optionally add a parallel ~120 Hz lowpassed body-knock thump of a few ms. Store `click_env`/`click_filt` and decay them per sample like the organ chiff.

**Risk:** Overdone it becomes a synthy 'tick' divorced from the note; keep it subtle, scale hard with velocity, and make nylon markedly quieter/darker than steel. Feed the click through the body EQ too so it isn't detached from the instrument.

### GTR-4 — No pre-distortion mid voicing — the amp lacks the mid scoop/boost that defines rhythm vs lead tone
*guitar · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** The only pre-gain shaping is a 90 Hz highpass. The signal hits the tanh essentially full-range, and all tone shaping happens after clipping (the lowpass).

**Gap:** On a real amp the tone stack sits BEFORE the power/clip stage, so the EQ shape changes what gets distorted, not just what comes out. The midrange contour is the identity of a guitar sound: a scooped-mid pre-EQ gives the classic modern metal 'chug', while a mid-boost gives singing lead sustain. Shaping only after the clipper (as here) cannot reproduce this — the harmonic generation is always from a flat input, so every note distorts the same way regardless of the intended voicing.

**Fix:** Add one or two pre-tanh peaking biquads in the `pre` chain, keyed off program: for prog 30 (lead/distortion) a mid boost `Biquad::peak(800.0, 0.8, +4.0)`; for a scooped rhythm voice `Biquad::peak(600.0, 0.9, -5.0)` plus a slight `peak(2000,1.0,+3)`. Because these run before `*gain` and tanh, they steer which frequencies clip. Combine with the pre-HP already present. Keep the post-cab EQ (GTR-1) separate.

**Risk:** Pre-gain boosts increase the effective drive into tanh (can over-saturate/clip harder than intended) so gain may need re-trimming; a deep scoop thins single-note leads.

### GTR-2#2 — tanh is symmetric — produces only odd harmonics, missing tube even-harmonic warmth and the asymmetric-clip character
*guitar · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** The waveshaper is a pure symmetric tanh. Both half-cycles clip identically, so the output spectrum contains only odd harmonics (3rd, 5th, ...).

**Gap:** Real overdriven tube (and even most solid-state) guitar stages clip asymmetrically: the two halves of the wave hit different thresholds, generating strong 2nd/even harmonics that the ear reads as 'warm/creamy' rather than the hollow, clarinet-like odd-only sound of a symmetric clipper. Symmetric tanh is the classic giveaway of a naive distortion. Chords in particular lose the even-order glue.

**Fix:** Add a DC bias before the shaper so the curve is asymmetric, then block DC after: `let s = pre.process(x)*gain + BIAS;` with BIAS ~0.3-0.7, shape with tanh, then remove the DC offset with a highpass or by subtracting tanh(BIAS). Alternatively use an asymmetric shaper, e.g. positive half `tanh(k*s)` and negative half a softer `tanh(k*s)/(1+|s|)`. Vary BIAS slightly between the prev/current 2x samples is unnecessary; a fixed bias plus the existing 90 Hz pre-HP (which already blocks the injected DC) is enough. Consider a slightly larger BIAS for prog 30 (higher gain) than 29.

**Risk:** Bias adds DC that, if not removed, offsets the signal and wastes headroom / thumps on note onsets; the existing pre-HP is upstream of the shaper so add a post-shaper DC block (or subtract tanh(bias)). Too much asymmetry sounds gated/sputtery.

### GTR-5 — Palm-mute (prog 28) never reaches a distortion stage and its chug body is a clean sine — no grind, no percussive attack
*guitar · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** MUTED (prog 28) plays the Pluck with a short t60 and a `sub` fundamental that is a pure `Sine` ramped in over 220 samples. The engine only instantiates a `Drive` insert for programs 29 and 30, so prog 28 is never distorted. The chug 'weight' is therefore a clean sine plus a fast-decaying muted KS pluck.

**Gap:** The iconic palm-mute 'chug' (metal/hard rock) is a DISTORTED palm mute — the grind is the point, and the pick attack through the distortion becomes a percussive 'chuff/click'. A clean sine sub gives a soft round thud with no harmonic grit and no attack transient; it sounds like a muted bass note, not a chug. Even for clean funk muting (the other reading of prog 28), the pure sine sub is unnaturally pure — a real muted string has a buzzy, inharmonic bark, not a fundamental sinusoid.

**Fix:** (1) Give prog 28 an optional distortion path: either add 28 to the `matches!(prog, 29|30)` set with a lower-gain `Drive` (gain ~4, tighter pre-HP at ~120 Hz), or add a per-voice soft-clip on the MUTED sub/pluck sum. (2) Make the chug body less pure: drive the `sub` sine through a mild tanh or add its 2nd/3rd harmonic (a couple of extra low-gain Sines at 2f, 3f), and shorten the sub attack ramp (220 samples ~5 ms is soft for a chug — drop to ~1-2 ms) so the transient punches. (3) Add a short broadband 'pick chuff' click to the excitation for the muted preset (a few ms of highpassed noise) to model palm-damped pick attack.

**Risk:** Distorting prog 28 changes the character of clean funk-muting parts that also use GM 28; consider gating the drive on velocity or making it gentle. A too-sharp sub attack can click/thump on the master bus.

### GTR-6 — Clean electric has no speaker-cabinet coloration — a bare 6 dB/oct lowpass instead of a cab
*guitar · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** CLEAN = KS + pickup comb (0.12) + a single `OnePole::lowpass(5500, sr)` (out_lp) and empty body. The one-pole is a gentle first-order (6 dB/oct) rolloff with no presence peak and no low-mid shaping.

**Gap:** A recorded clean electric is a guitar speaker in a cab: effectively a bandpass ~80 Hz to ~5 kHz with a presence peak around 2-3 kHz, a low-mid body bump ~200 Hz, and a steep (>12 dB/oct) rolloff above ~5 kHz. A lone 6 dB/oct one-pole leaves too much fizz above 5 kHz and no midrange voicing, so the model sounds thin and DI-like rather than like a miked amp.

**Fix:** Give CLEAN a minimal cab: use the (currently empty) body Vec for a low-mid peak ~200 Hz (+2 dB) and a presence peak ~2.5 kHz (+3 dB), and replace the output one-pole with a steeper `Biquad::lowpass(~4500, 0.9, sr)` (add a biquad out-filter option, or reuse the pattern). A highpass ~90 Hz trims subsonic mud.

**Risk:** Over-shaping the presence peak makes it honky/nasal; keep bumps modest (2-3 dB) and verify the top rolloff isn't so steep it dulls the pick attack added in GTR-2.

### GTR-3 — Guitars get no sympathetic open-string resonance (the resonance bus is gated to piano only)
*guitar · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** The Sympathetic comb bank (engine.rs lines 152-190) is fed only when `is_piano = ci != 9 && strip.program <= 7` (line 472). Guitar programs 24-31 never feed it, and the per-voice body EQ in Pluck colours each note in isolation — nothing couples one string's energy into the others.

**Gap:** On an acoustic guitar the undamped open strings ring sympathetically with every note fretted elsewhere, producing the characteristic bloom, halo and beating on open-position chords and any note sharing a partial with an open string. Its absence makes held/arpeggiated acoustic parts sound dry and one-note-at-a-time.

**Fix:** Add a second resonance bus (or generalise Sympathetic) with 6 comb resonators tuned to standard tuning E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4 (82.41, 110.0, 146.83, 196.0, 246.94, 329.63 Hz), each a delay of `sr/f` with an in-loop lowpass ~3200-3500 Hz and feedback ~0.8. Feed it from guitar channels (gate `program 24..=27`, i.e. acoustic/clean) at a low send (~0.03) and return quietly, mirroring the piano wiring at lines 313/492-497 and 176-189.

**Risk:** Can muddy dense strummed mixes and introduce tuning-dependent beating; keep the send low, damping fairly fast, and restrict to acoustic/clean (24-27) rather than driven electric. Bass-heavy combs may need a highpass like the piano bus already has.

### GTR-6#2 — Program 31 (and 28) get the DRIVE/MUTED voice but no amp insert — 'guitar harmonics'/overdrive plays clean
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** voices::make routes prog 29,30,31 to the DRIVE preset (t60 8 s, meant to be amp'd), but the Drive insert is only created for prog 29 and 30. Program 31 therefore renders the long-sustain DRIVE string with zero distortion. fx_profile also treats 26..=31 as electric with delay send, implying amping.

**Gap:** The DRIVE preset is explicitly voiced for an amp (very long 8 s t60 that only makes sense as feedback-sustained, no body resonators, bright pickup). Played clean, that voice is a thin, unnaturally-sustaining plucked string — it neither sounds like GM 'guitar harmonics' (which should be pinch/artificial-harmonic-flavored) nor like a driven guitar. It is an audible inconsistency between the voice design and the insert routing.

**Fix:** Extend the insert match to cover the driven presets: `if matches!(prog, 29 | 30 | 31)` (and 28 per GTR-5), choosing gain per program (31 could reuse the 29 overdrive settings). If prog 31 is intended as 'harmonics', additionally bias its excitation toward a high partial (start the KS at a node / emphasize a 2nd-4th harmonic in the pick comb) so it reads as a harmonic rather than a fundamental pluck.

**Risk:** Adding distortion to a t60=8s voice with the bus compressor can produce very long sustained loud tails; may need to trim the DRIVE t60 or amp gain to keep levels controlled.

### GTR-7 — Note-off fades amplitude only — the tail stays bright instead of darkening as a damped string does
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** On note_off the voice multiplies its whole output by `rel_mul = t60_mul(p.rel_t60, sr)` each sample (line 614), a pure amplitude decay. The loop damping cutoff (`bright`) is untouched during release, so the spectral content of the fade is identical to the sustain.

**Gap:** When a guitarist damps a string (lifting the fretting finger or palm-muting on release), flesh contact damps the high partials far faster than the fundamental, so the note darkens rapidly as it dies. A spectrum-preserving amplitude fade keeps the tail unnaturally bright and 'electronic' on staccato passages.

**Fix:** On release, in addition to the amplitude multiply, glide the KsLoop damper cutoff downward (e.g. lerp `bright` toward ~700-900 Hz over the release, retuning `damp` via `OnePole::set_cutoff` and recompensating the delay), so the tail loses highs as it fades. A small amount is enough.

**Risk:** Changing loop bright mid-ring also nudges the compensated delay/tuning; retune the delay when you move the cutoff or accept a tiny, brief pitch wobble on release (usually inaudible during a fast fade).

### GTR-4#2 — Body resonator is only three peaks — too sparse for a real guitar body's mode cluster
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** NYLON body = 3 peaks (98, 210, 420 Hz); STEEL = 3 peaks (105, 215, 2800 Hz). These become peak biquads applied in series per voice. CLEAN and DRIVE have `body: &[]`.

**Gap:** A real guitar top/back/air system has roughly a dozen strong resonances between ~100 Hz and ~3 kHz — the main air mode (~100), main top (~200), cross-dipole (~400), a mid cluster (~500-1000) and a presence region (~2-3 kHz). This fixed formant set is what stays put as you play up the neck and gives the instrument its identity. Three isolated peaks read as a boxy honk and leave the 500-2500 Hz 'wood' presence missing, so notes lack the complex resonant body of a miked guitar.

**Fix:** Expand the tables to ~6-8 modes with moderate Qs and small gains, e.g. NYLON add roughly (270, 3.0, -2.0) as a dip, (540, 4.0, 2.5), (820, 3.0, 2.0), (2600, 2.0, 1.5); STEEL add (330, 3.0, 2.0), (550, 4.0, 2.0), (1050, 3.0, 1.5) alongside its 2800 sparkle. Keep the summed gain modest so the low peaks don't boom.

**Risk:** High-Q peaks can ring or add boom, especially the sub-200 Hz air modes stacking with the sub sine; keep Q in the 1.5-4 range and audition the summed magnitude for a flat-ish broadband tilt.

### GTR-3#2 — Oversampling is only 2x and uses a box-average decimator — high-gain tanh still aliases audibly
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort med*

**Now:** '2x oversampling' is a linear-interpolation upsample (mid = average of prev and current) and a 2-tap box-average downsample (0.5*(y0+y1)). With gain up to 7.0 (prog 30) this is the entire anti-alias strategy aside from the gentle tone lowpass.

**Gap:** tanh at gain 7 generates harmonics far above the base Nyquist. At only 2x, harmonics above 22 kHz fold back into the audible band as inharmonic 'crackle/fizz' that moves with pitch — the tell-tale digital-distortion artifact. Worse, the up/down filters are extremely weak: linear interpolation is a poor anti-imaging filter, and a 2-tap box has its first null only at fs (2x-Nyquist), so it barely attenuates the aliasing it is supposed to remove. Real high-gain modelers run 4x-8x with steep half-band filters.

**Fix:** Go to 4x for the high-gain path. Insert 3 interpolated points between samples using a short FIR (e.g. a 4-6 tap windowed-sinc / halfband) instead of a single linear midpoint, run pre-HP, tanh, and a steep decimation lowpass at each 4x sample, then decimate with a proper FIR halfband rather than a plain average. If keeping it lightweight, at minimum add a steeper decimation filter (cascade two biquad lowpasses near 0.45*fs in the oversampled domain) before the 2:1 drop, and raise to 4x for program 30. The tone/cab EQ from GTR-1 can double as part of the decimation filtering if placed pre-downsample.

**Risk:** 4x quadruples the nonlinear-path cost per guitar channel; on dense mixes that is real CPU. FIR interpolation adds latency/ringing if taps are long. Keep filters short and only bump to 4x for the gain-7 program.

### GTR-5#2 — Pure Karplus-Strong has no string stiffness — low steel strings lack inharmonicity and wound-string character
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort med*

**Now:** KsLoop is a fractional delay plus a one-pole lowpass damper (line 465), which yields perfectly harmonic partials. Register only enters through the smooth `t60` and `bright` power laws (lines 504-509); the same NYLON/STEEL preset drives everything from E2 to the high E with no distinction between wound bass strings and plain treble strings.

**Gap:** Wound bass strings (E/A/D) are physically stiff, giving measurable inharmonicity (progressively sharp upper partials) and a zingier, slower-settling low character that is audibly different from the plain treble strings (G/B/E). A dispersion-free KS makes low steel notes sound too pure and almost nylon-like, erasing the wound-string signature that anchors the low end of an acoustic part.

**Fix:** Insert a first- or second-order Thiran/Zolzer dispersion allpass into the KsLoop feedback path, with a stiffness coefficient that rises for low keys (and steel more than nylon), so upper partials stretch sharp. Recompensate the loop delay for the allpass's phase delay in `delay_for`/`retune` so the fundamental stays in tune. Optionally split the preset's bright/damp by register (wound vs plain crossover around key ~44/G3).

**Risk:** The allpass detunes the string; without recomputing the compensated delay length the pitch drifts sharp (would break pluck_pitch_a4). Excess stiffness turns pluck into a clang — keep the coefficient small and pitch-verified.

### KS-6 — Sympathetic resonance is piano-only - guitars/bass have no open-string halo
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort high*

**Now:** The Sympathetic comb bank exists but is fed only by piano channels (is_piano = program <= 7); guitar and bass channels contribute nothing to it, and there is no resonator bank tuned to guitar open strings.

**Gap:** On a real guitar the undamped open strings (E A D G B E) ring sympathetically with played notes and especially chords - a shimmering halo that is a strong 'acoustic guitar in a room' cue. Because guitars are 100% synthesized isolated voices with no shared resonator, that halo is completely absent and chordal acoustic parts sound dry and disconnected.

**Fix:** Add a second lightly-damped comb/KS resonator bank tuned to the six guitar open-string pitches (or generalize Sympathetic with per-family tuning) and feed it a small send from guitar/nylon/steel/clean channels (programs 24-31), returned quietly and highpassed so it only rings during sustained notes.

**Risk:** Adds bus cost and can muddy dense mixes or build up low end; needs a conservative send level, highpass, and enough damping that it decays with the played note rather than droning.

### GTR-7#2 — High-gain sustain is a linear KS decay — no feedback bloom, so long notes fade instead of singing/blooming
*guitar · impact 2/5 · effort high*

**Now:** prog 30's sustain is faked purely by a long KS loop t60 (8 s at 220 Hz). The note decays exponentially from the pick; the distortion stage is memoryless and has no path back into the string, so there is no regeneration.

**Gap:** On a real high-gain rig, a held note does the opposite of decaying: as the pick energy fades, compression + amp/speaker acoustic feedback take over and the note holds level or BLOOMS (swells, and often flips up to a harmonic/octave — controlled feedback). The current model gives a slowly dying pluck, which sounds like a clean guitar turned up, not a screaming sustained lead. The characteristic 'note that won't die / blooms into feedback' is absent.

**Fix:** Model bloom without a full feedback loop: give the DRIVE/lead voice a sustain envelope that, after the initial pick decay, ramps a low-level regeneration back INTO the KS loop_gain (nudge loop_gain toward/over unity as amplitude drops, clamped for stability) or cross-fade in a second KS loop tuned to the octave/12th to emulate a feedback harmonic after ~0.5-1 s. Simpler: add a slow amplitude 'swell' term (an envelope that rises 1-3 dB over 0.5-1.5 s once the note is held and below a threshold) so held leads bloom rather than fade. Gate this to prog 30 only.

**Risk:** Regeneration into a KS loop is a stability hazard (runaway/self-oscillation) and must be clamped; a bloom envelope on every note can make short/rhythmic passages pump or swell unnaturally, so it should be conditioned on note length/level.


## Detail — BASS (8)

### BASS-2 — Bass timbre is velocity-invariant — a soft finger and a hard dig sound identically dark
*bass · impact 4/5 · effort low*

**Now:** vel only feeds `vel_amp(vel)` which scales the excitation amplitude (and sub gain). `bright` (loop damping cutoff) and `pick_lp` (excitation lowpass) are taken straight from the preset with only a ±8% round-robin jitter — no velocity term. Contrast piano() at line 149 which does `bright = 0.55 + 0.40*(vel/127)`.

**Gap:** On a real bass, pluck force changes spectrum, not just level: digging in drives the string into the frets/pickup harder, adding high harmonics and a sharper, brighter attack; a gentle fingered note is round and dark. A synth that only changes amplitude with velocity sounds 'sampled at one dynamic' — the defining dynamic expressivity of bass lines (ghost notes vs accents) is lost.

**Fix:** In Pluck::new compute `let vn = vel as f32/127.0;` and open both filters with velocity: `let pick_lp = p.pick_lp * (0.55 + 0.9*vn);` and `let bright = p.bright * (0.7 + 0.6*vn) * (1.0 + 0.08*rng.white());`. Optionally scale the excitation comb depth or add a small extra HF term at high vn. This is a few lines and applies to all plucks, but gate the coefficients per-family if steel/nylon regress.

**Risk:** Changing bright/pick_lp globally affects guitar presets too; verify the pluck_pitch/bend tests still pass (they measure fundamental only, so should be safe) and re-audition steel/nylon so accents don't turn harsh.

### BASS-1 — Slap bass (GM 36/37) has no slap-thump or pop transient — sounds identical to a soft fingered note
*bass · impact 4/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Programs 36 (Slap Bass 1) and 37 (Slap Bass 2) use the exact same BASS preset as fingered bass: pick_lp=850, bright=1100, out_lp=1900, no attack transient. The Karplus-Strong excitation is just a one-period filtered-noise burst scaled by velocity.

**Gap:** Slap technique produces two signature transients the ear identifies instantly: the thumb 'slap' (a broadband percussive knock as the string hits the fretboard, energy up past 3-5 kHz) and the finger 'pop' (a sharp metallic snap when the string is yanked and slaps back). Neither exists here — with pick_lp=850 everything above ~1 kHz is gone, so slap bass renders as a dull round fingered note with zero attack bite.

**Fix:** Add a SLAP preset (route 36/37 to it) with pick_lp≈4500, bright≈3500, out_lp≈4000, pos≈0.12. In Pluck::new, when a `slap` flag is set, prepend a short (≈2-3 ms) highpassed noise burst to the excitation vector: generate `n_click = (0.0025*sr)` samples of white noise through a Biquad::highpass(1500, 0.7) scaled by ~0.6*v and mix it ahead of the KS loop injection (feed via the existing `hammer`/inject path or a dedicated one-shot buffer), so a bright transient snaps at onset before the string tone settles. For the pop character, bias pick position very close to the bridge (pos 0.10-0.12) to emphasize high harmonics.

**Risk:** The added highpassed transient can read as a click or alias if too short/loud; keep the burst ≥2 ms and lowpass-limit it below Nyquist. Over-bright pos values can thin out the fundamental — compensate with the body/sub weight.

### BASS-7 — No fret/finger attack noise on fingered or fretless bass — onsets are too clean
*bass · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Excitation is one period of pick_lp-lowpassed noise combed at the pick position, scaled to velocity. For BASS pick_lp=850 and FRETLESS pick_lp=550, so the onset is a soft dark burst with no broadband component. No separate finger/fret noise layer.

**Gap:** Fingerstyle and especially fretless bass have audible non-tonal attack noise: the fingertip releasing the wound string (a short 'zip'/buzz with HF content), and on fretless the finger sliding on the fingerboard. Because the excitation is lowpassed at 550-850 Hz, all of that HF attack detail is filtered out, leaving a synthetic, too-clean onset that undercuts the 'human hands on strings' impression fretless is prized for.

**Fix:** Add a short broadband attack layer independent of pick_lp: generate ≈3-5 ms of white noise through a Biquad::bandpass(≈2000 Hz, Q≈0.8) at low gain (≈0.08*v, and for fretless scale it *up* at low velocity since soft fingerstyle exposes finger noise), and mix it into the first samples of output (or inject via the hammer path). Keep it out of the KS feedback so it stays a transient, not a sustained hiss.

**Risk:** Bandpassed noise can sound hissy or clicky if the gain/duration is off; tune per family. Adding it to every pluck could make dense guitar strums noisy — gate to bass/fretless presets.

### KS-3 — No string stiffness/inharmonicity - the pure-delay loop is perfectly harmonic (and even skews flat)
*bass · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** The loop is a pure fractional delay plus a one-pole damper; partials fall exactly on n*f. The damper-phase compensation in delay_for and the linear-interp tap actually push the upper partials slightly flat rather than sharp.

**Gap:** Real steel and especially wound bass strings are inharmonic: partial n sits near n*f*sqrt(1+B*n^2), stretched progressively sharp. That upper-partial stretch is a primary identifier of a real electric bass/guitar 'clang' and metallic shimmer. Its total absence - compounded by the flat skew - makes sustained low notes read as a synth/organ tone instead of a wound string.

**Fix:** Insert a cascade of first-order allpass sections (a dispersion filter, per Jaffe-Smith / Rauhala) into the loop signal path in KsLoop::tick, y = allpass(a, damp(s)); choose the allpass coefficient a and section count from a target B (more sections / larger |a| for lower strings and bass). Subtract the allpass group delay at f inside delay_for so the fundamental stays in tune. Even a single section adds an audible stretch on bass.

**Risk:** An allpass inside a gliding-delay loop can transient when the note bends/retunes; the group-delay compensation adds cost and must track `bright`; an over-large coefficient detunes the fundamental.

### BASS-3 — Fingered (33) and picked (34) bass share one dark preset — no pick attack, no articulation split
*bass · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Acoustic bass (32), fingered electric (33) and picked electric (34) all instantiate the same BASS preset with pick_lp=850, a very dark excitation with no distinct attack shape.

**Gap:** Picked electric bass has a hard plectrum transient — a bright click and faster rise — versus the soft, rounded onset of fingerstyle; acoustic upright is woodier with a big body resonance and shorter sustain. pick_lp=850 kills the pick click entirely, so 34 loses the one cue that says 'picked.' These are timbrally distinct instruments the listener separates immediately.

**Fix:** Add a PICK preset for program 34: pick_lp≈2600, bright≈2200, pos≈0.15, plus a short brightened attack (small highpassed excitation transient as in BASS-1 but gentler, ~0.4*v). Keep 33 on the current round BASS. Optionally give 32 (upright) a shorter t60 (≈2.4), a second body peak (e.g. add (110.0,1.0,3.0) alongside 65 Hz), and a touch more attack noise for the woody thump.

**Risk:** More presets to maintain; a brighter picked preset can clash with the per-channel Drive insert if a track layers them. Keep pick attack modest to avoid a synthetic 'tick'.

### BASS-4 — Synth bass (GM 38/39) is modeled as an acoustic plucked string with body EQ — wrong timbre class
*bass · impact 3/5 · effort high*

**Now:** Synth Bass 1 (38) and Synth Bass 2 (39) run through the same KS string model with an acoustic body resonance at 65 Hz and a pickup comb — i.e. they are rendered as a plucked acoustic-ish bass.

**Gap:** Synth bass is an electronic sound: a sawtooth or square (or two detuned) through a resonant lowpass with a fast amplitude+filter envelope (the classic 'wow' filter sweep), punchy and buzzy, with zero acoustic body resonance. A Karplus-Strong pluck cannot produce the continuous buzzy sustain, resonant filter movement, or the square/saw harmonic stack — it decays like a string and carries a body EQ that makes no physical sense for a synth.

**Fix:** Route 38/39 to a small dedicated synth-bass voice built from existing blocks: one or two BlepSaw oscillators (39 slightly detuned/square-ish) summed, through a Biquad::lowpass with resonance (Q≈3-6) whose cutoff is driven by an Adsr (e.g. base 300 Hz + envelope*1500 Hz) plus a sustained amplitude Adsr. Reuse the SawStack/Adsr machinery rather than Pluck. No body EQ, no pickup comb.

**Risk:** New voice type = more surface area and level-matching against the KS basses; a resonant filter can self-oscillate or clip if Q/cutoff not clamped. Needs its own gain calibration so it sits with the fingered bass in a mixed track.

### BASS-5 — Envelope-locked sub is a pure sine at the fundamental — synthetic, one-note 'boom' in the tail
*bass · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** sub = (Sine::new(f, sr, 0.0), p.sub*v, decay). Each sample adds `osc.next()*gain*sub_env`, a mathematically pure sine at exactly f, fixed initial phase 0, with its own exponential decay (t60*0.8). BASS sub=0.18, FRETLESS 0.15.

**Gap:** A real bass string's low end is harmonically rich (strong 2nd/3rd partials, the body reinforcing a band, not a single frequency). A pure sine reinforcing only the fundamental reads as a synthetic sub-oscillator: as the string's harmonics decay faster than this clean sine, the tail collapses to an obviously electronic sine 'boom,' and every note sounds equally boomy regardless of pitch/room. The fixed phase-0 sine can also slow-beat against the KS fundamental (and the vert loop at f*1.0013), giving an unnatural throb.

**Fix:** Make the sub non-pure and string-coupled: (a) run the sub through a gentle waveshaper before summing — `let s = osc.next(); y += (s + 0.25*s*s.abs()).tanh() * gain * sub_env;` to add a 2nd-harmonic tint; or (b) add a quiet second sine at 2f (≈0.4 of sub gain); and (c) instead of an independent decay, drive sub_env from the string's own running amplitude (`self.env`) so it tracks the actual string decay rather than diverging. Also randomize the sub's start phase from rng to avoid a fixed beat pattern.

**Risk:** Waveshaping/2f content adds harmonics that could muddy dense low passages; keep the extra harmonic quiet. Coupling sub_env to self.env changes the decay feel — re-check that muted/chug presets (which rely on sub for the thud) still land.

### BASS-6 — No release thump when a bass note is stopped — the note fades cleanly instead of thudding
*bass · impact 2/5 · effort med*

**Now:** note_off only sets `released=true`; render then multiplies by `rel_mul` (rel_t60 0.12 s for BASS). The string simply decays exponentially to silence with no new energy injected.

**Gap:** When a bassist lifts/damps a finger to stop a note, the string is physically stopped against the fretboard, producing a short low 'thump' or fret-contact thud — a percussive transient, not a clean fade. This release articulation is a strong part of the rhythmic feel of walking and funk bass lines. Its absence makes stops sound artificially smooth.

**Fix:** In note_off, inject a brief low-frequency damped noise burst representing the finger/fret contact: build a small buffer (≈4-6 ms of white noise through a OnePole::lowpass(≈500 Hz)) scaled by a modest gain (≈0.15) and feed it into the loops via the existing `hammer`/`hammer_pos` injection path, then set `released`. Only do this for the bass/fretless presets (gate via a preset flag) so guitars keep clean releases.

**Risk:** A release transient during fast passages could sound like an extra note or click if too loud; keep it short, low-passed, and quiet. Must not fire on natural note-end (min_life expiry), only on explicit note_off.


## Detail — DRUMS (14)

### CYM-2 — Crashes have no bloom/swell — energy peaks at t=0 instead of building
*drums · impact 4/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Every Tone and NoiseBand starts at full amplitude at t=0 and decays monotonically (tone.amp *= tone.decay; band.amp *= band.decay). A crash (49/57) therefore has its loudest, brightest instant at the moment of impact and only fades afterward.

**Gap:** A real crash is a nonlinear system: the stick deposits energy into low plate modes, which then pumps energy UP into the hundreds of high shimmer modes over ~30-150 ms. The wash audibly BLOOMS a fraction of a second AFTER the strike (the shimmer swells, then fades). Monotonic decay-from-full is the single most recognizable 'synthetic cymbal' tell — it sounds like a filtered noise gate, not a crash.

**Fix:** Give the noise band and the upper (i>=3) tones a short attack stage. Add fields `env: f32` (init 0.0) and `atk: f32` to NoiseBand; in render, before the decay multiply, ramp `env += atk*(1.0-env)` until env~1 then switch to decaying, and use `band.filt.process(white)*band.amp*env`. Set attack ~40-90 ms for crash (base>=800 with long t60), ~0 (instant) for hats. Equivalently precompute a rising-then-falling gain: peak the noise envelope at ~50 ms rather than at 0. Apply a milder version to tones[3..6] so the high inharmonic shimmer arrives late.

**Risk:** If attack is too long the hit loses its transient 'crack'; keep the fundamental/stick attack instantaneous and only delay the shimmer/wash. Must gate attack to long-decay cymbals so hats/ride pings stay tight.

### CYM-4 — No hi-hat choke group — closing the foot doesn't cut a ringing open hat
*drums · impact 4/5 · effort high*

**Now:** Every drum note spawns an independent Drum voice. An open hat (46, life 0.95 s) rings out fully regardless of any subsequent closed (42) or pedal (44) hat event. Cymbal voices also have no note-off effect (Drum::note_off is empty, drums.rs:137).

**Gap:** On a real kit the hi-hat is ONE instrument: closing the pedal (44) or hitting it closed (42) instantly damps a ringing open hat (the 'tss-chk' figure). Letting open hats ring through the following closed hats is a glaring drum-programming artifact — the groove smears into a continuous wash instead of tight open/close articulation.

**Fix:** Introduce an exclusive-group concept. Give Drum an optional `choke_group: Option<u8>` (hats = group 1) and a `choke()` method that force-shortens all envelope decays (set every tone/noise `decay` to a ~5 ms t60 equivalent and shrink `life`). In engine.rs NoteOn for ch==9, before spawning, if the new key is in {42,44,46} iterate `active` and call `choke()` on any live voice whose key is also in that hat set. This is the standard GM hat-group behavior.

**Risk:** Requires plumbing a downcast/trait method through the boxed Voice and touching the engine's spawn path; a too-fast choke can click. Ramp the choke over ~5-10 ms rather than hard-zeroing to avoid a discontinuity.

### CYM-6 — Ride bell is two pure sines — no inharmonic clang or stick attack
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Ride bell is `d()` with two clean tones (1700 Hz, 2600 Hz) plus a tiny highpass noise band, t60 0.6/0.5. The 1700:2600 ratio is nearly harmonic (~1:1.53) and the partials are pure sines with no attack differentiation.

**Gap:** A ride bell is a loud, penetrating, CLANGY metallic tone: a hard stick attack over a dense stack of inharmonic partials with strong upper-mode content that rings and beats. Two pure sines with a near-harmonic ratio sound like a mellow tuned bell / FM bell, not the cutting 'ding' of a ride bell.

**Fix:** Route 53 through the inharmonic path: use several METAL_RATIOS-style partials on a ~1500-1800 Hz base (more partials than a plain sine pair, with the upper ones lively), give it a short bright click band (~3 ms, >7 kHz) for the stick attack, and keep a moderate t60 so it rings but stays articulate. Add small per-strike freq jitter for the beating.

**Risk:** Too many high inharmonic partials at high amp can sound harsh/glassy; weight upper partials down and rely on the click for attack rather than raw partial level.

### CYM-7 — China (52) falls through to the generic 1 kHz tick; splash (55) is a long crash
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** GM key 52 (Chinese cymbal) is not matched and hits the `_ =>` generic voice: a single 1000 Hz bandpass tick, life 0.15 s. GM 55 (Splash) is lumped with 49 (Crash), inheriting t60_first=2.0 and life 3.6 s.

### CYM-3 — Closed hat lacks the distinct broadband stick 'tick' transient
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Closed/pedal hat is cymbal(base=3300, one highpass noise band t60=0.035, life 0.14). The noise decays exponentially from full — an instantaneous onset, but with the same spectral content throughout.

**Gap:** A closed hi-hat's identity is a very short, ultra-bright stick-on-metal 'tick/chick': a 1-3 ms broadband click that is spectrally brighter and shorter than the metallic body decay that follows. With a single decay band the attack and the body are the same color, so the hat sounds like a burst of shaped noise rather than a stick striking metal.

**Fix:** Add a second, very short, very bright click band to the hat voicing: e.g. `(amp~0.9, t60~0.002, Biquad::highpass(9000.0, 0.7, sr))` alongside the existing wash band. ### CYM-1 — Noise wash is uncolored highpassed white noise — no metallic resonance or shimmer/beat
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** The wash is one `Biquad::highpass(hp, 0.7, sr)` over white noise — a spectrally flat (above cutoff), smooth hiss. Six discrete sines sit on top but the sustained texture is essentially pink-ish hiss.

**Gap:** Real cymbal sheen is white noise shaped by hundreds of closely spaced high-Q modes: the spectrum is bumpy/colored and, crucially, adjacent modes beat, producing the characteristic shimmering amplitude modulation ('shhhh' with a metallic sparkle). A single Q=0.7 highpass has neither coloration nor beating, so it reads as tape hiss / rushing air rather than metal.

**Fix:** Replace the single band with a small resonator bank fed by the SAME white sample (render already shares one `white` across bands, drums.rs:124). Keep the highpass wash, then add 3-4 `Biquad::bandpass` resonators at incommensurate highs (e.g. 4.2k, 6.1k, 8.3k, 10.9k) with Q~6-12 and modest amps. Summed high-Q bandpasses on correlated noise give colored peaks whose ringdowns beat against each other — instant metallic shimmer. `bands` is already a Vec, so this is just pushing more `(amp, t60, filt)` tuples.

**Risk:** High-Q bandpasses can ring/whistle if Q is too high or amps unbalanced; keep total noise amp constant (scale each resonator down) to avoid boosting level, and jitter resonator freqs per strike (reuse the existing jf) so repeated hits don't sound identical.

### DRM-1 — Toms are a single sine partial — missing drumhead modal overtones
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Each tom is one decaying sine with a downward glide plus a single bandpass noise stick-click, e.g. key 41 = [(100.0, 1.0, 0.32, 10.0)] + bandpass(900). One pure partial per drum.

**Gap:** A real tom head is a circular membrane whose transverse modes ring at inharmonic ratios (~1.0, 1.59, 2.14, 2.30, 2.65 of the fundamental). With only the fundamental present the tom reads as a pitched sine 'boop', not a resonant drum — the ear identifies a drum largely by the cluster of fast-decaying overtones sitting above the fundamental in the first ~150 ms.

**Fix:** Add 2 extra partials per tom at membrane ratios with progressively lower amp, faster T60, and slightly steeper glide, e.g. key 41 -> [(100,1.0,0.32,10),(159,0.45,0.20,13),(214,0.22,0.13,13)]. **Risk:** Too-loud upper modes make toms sound metallic/steel-drum-like; the added partials raise per-voice peak slightly, so amps must stay modest to avoid re-clipping the drum bus.

### CYM-5 — Ride reads as a small crash — no articulate stick 'ping' distinct from the wash
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Ride is cymbal(base=1150, 6 ratios, tone t60 0.7->0.35, noise amp 0.35, t60 1.0). All six tones share one decay slope starting from t=0, same as a crash, just shorter and with less noise.

**Gap:** A ride cymbal's defining feature is the 'ping': a clear, short, stick-defined bright attack (a pitched tick) sitting on top of a comparatively quiet, slowly-accumulating wash. The ear tracks the repeated pings as an articulate pulse. With every partial decaying on one envelope and no separate fast transient, the ride loses its stick definition and blurs into crash-like wash.

**Fix:** Split the ride into two time-scales: (a) a short bright PING transient — a high partial (or a 3-5 ms >6 kHz click band, amp scaled by velnorm) with t60 ~0.05-0.08 s for stick articulation; (b) a low-level sustaining wash — keep the lower tones and a quiet long-t60 noise band but drop their attack level so they build across successive hits rather than dominating each strike. Widen the tone t60 spread so the fundamental rings longer than the shimmer.

**Risk:** Over-emphasizing the ping makes it sound like a woodblock/cowbell; balance ping vs wash by velocity and keep the inharmonic partials audible under the ping.

### DRM-5 — Snare wires are one instant-onset exponential band — no delayed engage, no two-stage buzz tail
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** Wire rattle = highpass(~2800) noise, amp 0.75, single T60 0.19, starting at full amplitude on the same sample as the head. Only the clap (key 39) uses the with_bursts delayed-onset path; the snare does not.

**Gap:** ~20 physical snare wires do not sound at t=0: the head must rebound and slap them, so the buzz engages ~1-3 ms after the stick hit, and its decay is two-stage — a bright initial sizzle then a longer, darker rattle tail as individual wires ring down at different rates. A single instantaneous exponential HP band gives a 'shhht' that starts too early and dies too uniformly, the classic giveaway of a synthetic snare.

**Fix:** Build the snare via the Drum::new(...).with_bursts path instead of d(): start the wire band near-silent and burst it at ~0.0015 s so it engages after the head; then add a second, lower, darker HP band (e.g. highpass(1800), amp 0.3, T60 0.35) for the rattle tail so the buzz decays in two stages.

### DRM-2 — No velocity-dependent timbre — hard and soft hits differ only in level
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** vel_amp(vel)=(vel/127)^1.6 scales overall gain, and velnorm nudges the kick body amp and the snare/cymbal HP cutoff. Partial frequencies, glide amount, decay times and click level are velocity-independent.

**Gap:** On a real membrane a harder strike deflects the head further, momentarily raising tension: the pitch starts higher and bends further (the characteristic 'pitch-up' of a hard tom/kick hit), the attack transient/click gains disproportionate high-frequency energy, and the head sustains longer. Scaling only amplitude makes every dynamic a photocopy of the same timbre, which the ear reads as sampled-and-turned-down, not played harder.

**Fix:** Thread velnorm into the voice constructor: multiply each membrane tone's start freq by (1.0 + 0.03*velnorm), multiply glide_oct_per_s by (0.6 + 0.8*velnorm) so hard hits bend more, multiply noise/click band amp by velnorm^2 (not linear) so the click blooms on hard hits, and lengthen T60 by (0.85 + 0.3*velnorm). Apply in Drum::new or as a per-key scale before calling d().

**Risk:** Over-scaling detunes/dulls soft hits (they go flat and lifeless) or makes hard hits pitch-wobble; coefficients must stay small. Touches many table entries unless centralized in the constructor.

### DRM-7 — No cohesive drum room/overhead ambience — kit pieces only hit the long global hall
*drums · impact 3/5 · effort high*

**Now:** Channel 9 drums default to reverb_send 0.3 into the single global Reverb (RT ~0.86, a long hall). There is no short early-reflection room or overhead-bleed path that treats the kit as one source in one space.

**Gap:** Real kit recordings glue because every piece shares the same close-mic + overhead + short room signature: 10-40 ms early reflections and a fast room decay bloom the transients and place kick/snare/toms in one box. Feeding dry parametric hits straight into a long hall (or dry) leaves them sounding isolated and 'sample-player' dry, with no shared acoustic that fuses the backbeat.

**Fix:** Add a short drum-room send for ch 9: a small early-reflection network (a few DelayLine taps at 8-35 ms with a fast <0.3 s decay and mild HF damping), summed to L/R like the existing Chorus/PingPong buses, fed only from the ch 9 bus. Keep it pre-hall so the kit gets its own tight ambience before any long tail.

**Risk:** A new bus adds comb-filtering/phase risk and can muddy or box-up the kit if tap times/decay are wrong; needs level-matching against the master glue compressor to avoid pumping.

### DRM-6 — Per-strike variation jitters only global pitch/decay, not modal balance (strike position)
*drums · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Each strike gets jf=1±0.03 (all tones scaled together) and jd=1±0.10 (all decays scaled together), plus random start phase and fresh noise. The relative amplitudes of the partials are identical on every hit.

**Gap:** Real repeated hits vary in strike position (center vs off-center vs rim-ward), which shifts energy between the fundamental and the overtones hit-to-hit — center favors the fundamental, edge favors upper modes. Because hollowsynth keeps the modal balance fixed and only dithers overall pitch/decay, fast repeated toms/snares still 'machine-gun' spectrally even though pitch wanders slightly.

**Fix:** In the tones map, give each partial an independent amplitude dither: amp: a * (1.0 + 0.22 * rng.white()) (drawn per partial, per strike). Optionally couple it — bias upper modes up when the fundamental dithers down — to emulate off-center strikes. Bound to keep sum roughly constant.

**Risk:** Unbounded per-partial dither can occasionally produce an anemic (fundamental-starved) or harsh (overtone-heavy) outlier hit; keep the coefficient modest and clamp.

### DRM-4 — Snare shell/head under-modeled — only two partials, thin pitched ring
*drums · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Snare tonal part = [(186.0, 0.8, 0.10, 4.0), (330.0, 0.4, 0.07, 0.0)] — a fundamental and one higher partial, plus two noise bands.

**Gap:** A tuned snare batter head rings with a family of inharmonic modes (roughly 1.0, 1.5, 1.7, 2.1x the fundamental) that together produce the pitched 'crack/ring' under the wire noise. With just two partials the snare's tuned body sounds hollow and the pitched attack lacks the woody 'thock' that a real snare pings between wire bursts.

**Fix:** Add one or two more head modes, e.g. [(186,0.8,0.10,4),(280,0.35,0.08,2),(330,0.30,0.07,0),(430,0.18,0.05,0)], keeping upper modes short and quiet. Give the fundamental a small upward-then-settling glide feel by keeping its 4 oct/s down-glide but starting a hair higher (ties into DRM-2).

**Risk:** Extra partials can push the snare toward a pitched 'timbale' character if amps are too high or ratios too harmonic; keep them inharmonic and low.

### DRM-3 — Kick beater click is a single 5 ms HP-2.5 kHz burst — lacks the sharp beater knock
*drums · impact 2/5 · effort low*

**Now:** Kick = two membrane tones (165 Hz knock @T60 0.16, 86->~45 Hz body) plus one noise band one(0.5, 0.005, highpass(2500)). A single 5 ms highpassed noise transient supplies the entire attack.

**Gap:** A real kick attack is two distinct events: a very short (1-2 ms) broadband beater-on-head 'click/tick' with energy to 4-6 kHz, then the pillow-damped body/sub. One 2.5 kHz-cornered 5 ms burst is too dark and too slow to read as a modern beater knock — the top-end 'point' that makes a kick cut through a mix is absent, and there is no fast tick separate from the body.

**Fix:** Add a second, shorter/brighter noise band for the tick: bands = [(0.5,0.005,highpass(2500)), (0.35,0.0015,highpass(4500,0.7,sr))], and scale the tick amp by velnorm^2 (see DRM-2) so hard kicks click harder. Optionally add a tiny 120-150 Hz-region click partial with T60 ~0.01 for the 'knuckle' between click and body.

**Risk:** An over-loud HF tick makes the kick sound clicky/typewriter-ish and can fatigue on repeated eighths; keep the second band amp low and very short.


## Detail — SHARED (3)

### KS-1 — Excitation is filtered white noise, not a released string-displacement initial condition
*shared · impact 4/5 · effort med*

**Now:** The pluck excitation is one period of white noise, lowpassed by pick_lp and passed through a pick-position comb (raw[i] - 0.9*raw[i+comb]), then scaled to the velocity peak and pushed into both KS loops.

**Gap:** A real plucked string starts from a static triangular displacement with its apex at the pick point, released at t=0. That is a deterministic initial condition with a smooth ~1/n^2 harmonic envelope and hard nulls at the pick position. Filtered noise instead gives a stochastic, breathy/'spitty' onset, random note-to-note fundamental energy and phase, and no consistent pick transient - the hallmark 'synthetic Karplus-Strong' attack that never sounds like a real pick releasing a string.

**Fix:** Build exc as a normalized triangular displacement: linear ramp 0->1 over samples [0,comb] then 1->0 over [comb,exc_len] (comb = pick position already computed). Remove DC by subtracting the mean, then add only a small filtered-noise scrape term (~0.12*noise through the existing pick_lp) for finger/pick grit. Keep the v/peak velocity scaling. This yields the correct pluck spectrum, pick-position notches, and a repeatable transient.

**Risk:** A triangle carries strong DC/fundamental energy; must DC-block (subtract mean) or the loop acquires an offset. Excitation energy changes, so the preset amp values likely need re-leveling to keep loudness matched.

### KS-4 — The two polarizations are uncoupled - summed independent loops give no beating or secondary-decay life
*shared · impact 3/5 · effort low*

**Now:** horiz and vert are two fully independent KS loops summed at fixed weights (0.74/0.26) with a fixed 1.0013 (~2.2 cent) detune and vert at 0.42x t60. There is no energy exchange between them.

**Gap:** In a real string the two polarizations exchange energy through the bridge, producing amplitude beating and a distinctly non-exponential envelope - the famous 'secondary rise' and slow flutter as energy sloshes between planes. A pure sum of two independent exponentials only yields a static fast-then-slow blend with a fixed beat; it lacks the living amplitude modulation the ear reads as a real vibrating string.

**Fix:** Cross-couple the loops: each sample, feed a small fraction (~0.002-0.005) of horiz's output into vert's input and vice versa (both are already ticked in render, so this is a couple of extra adds). Optionally make the detune register-dependent instead of a fixed 1.0013. Cheap change, large gain in perceived aliveness.

**Risk:** Feedback coupling adds loop gain and can drift toward instability; keep the coefficient small and account for it within the existing loop_gain budget.

### KS-5 — Linear-interpolation delay tap is a fractional lowpass - dulls treble and mistunes high notes
*shared · impact 3/5 · effort med*

**Now:** The fractional delay read is plain linear interpolation between two samples: a + (b-a)*frac.

**Gap:** Linear interpolation attenuates content near Nyquist by an amount that depends on the fractional part, and its effective delay drifts with frac (a pitch error). For short (high-pitched) strings the loop's own harmonics sit high in the spectrum, so every lap through the loop shaves the top octave and slightly flattens the pitch - banjo and high steel-string notes lose sparkle and tune imperfectly, and the loss compounds because the tap is inside the feedback path.

**Fix:** Replace the fractional read with a first-order Thiran allpass fractional delay (flat magnitude, phase-accurate) for the sub-sample part, or with 3rd-order Lagrange (cubic) interpolation over four taps. The allpass folds naturally into the KS loop and preserves high-harmonic energy.

**Risk:** An allpass fractional delay produces transients when `delay` glides (bends/vibrato/legato), so the coefficient update must be handled smoothly; cubic interpolation needs four taps and a slightly wider ring-buffer guard.
