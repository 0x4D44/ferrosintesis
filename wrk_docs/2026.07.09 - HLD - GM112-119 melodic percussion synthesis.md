# HLD - GM112-119 melodic percussion synthesis

Status: SIGNED-OFF

## Goal

Replace the GM112-119 steel-pluck fallback with high-quality default-on modeled
synthesis for the eight General MIDI melodic-percussion programs:

| zero-based program | GM name |
|---:|---|
| 112 | Tinkle Bell |
| 113 | Agogo |
| 114 | Steel Drums |
| 115 | Woodblock |
| 116 | Taiko Drum |
| 117 | Melodic Tom |
| 118 | Synth Drum |
| 119 | Reverse Cymbal |

Acceptance summary: every program in 112-119 routes away from `STEEL`, has a
program-specific audible signature, responds to written pitch where that is
musically meaningful, and has focused numeric oracles rather than ear-only
claims.

## Scope

In scope:

- `crates/ferrosintesis/src/voices.rs`
  - New modal tables/helpers for programs 112-118.
  - One small `ReverseCymbal` one-shot voice for program 119.
  - `voices::make` routing for 112-119.
  - Focused audio/routing tests.
- `crates/ferrosintesis/src/testutil.rs`
  - Extend `gm_routing_pins_voice_kinds`.
- `crates/ferrosintesis/README.md`
  - Document GM112-119 in the instrument table.
- Version-bearing files
  - One patch bump because this changes shipped synthesis behavior.

Out of scope:

- Rerendering committed listening `.opus` files in this change.
- Adding samples or external dependencies.
- Making channel-10 drum internals public just to reuse code.
- Adding a new feature flag or opt-in seam.
- Solving unrelated GM gaps or alt-bank behavior.

## Approach

Keep the implementation in `voices.rs`. The new instruments are melodic-channel
voices, not channel-10 drum hits, so they must take written pitch, pitch bend,
note-off lifecycle, and normal channel processing through the existing engine.
Use `Modal` for struck pitched objects and membranes because it already provides:

- deterministic per-note modal partials;
- strike noise;
- pitch-bend retuning;
- note-off release;
- the timpani strike-glide hook needed by drum-like programs.

Add no new shared abstraction until implementation proves it removes real
duplication. The likely shape is a few small helpers:

- direct `bell(...)` calls or per-program constants for Tinkle Bell and Agogo.
- `steel_drum(...)` for steelpan partials and tuned detuned pairs.
- `woodblock(...)` through `wood_bar(...)` or a short modal table.
- one membrane helper for Taiko, Melodic Tom, and Synth Drum only if the
  implementation shows real stable duplication.
- `ReverseCymbal` for the only non-decay envelope.

### Program Designs

#### 112 - Tinkle Bell

Bright small metal bell with a clear high sparkle and long but quiet decay.

Implementation:

- Modal table around ratios `1.0, 2.35, 3.88, 5.42, 7.1`.
- Short high-passed/band-passed strike noise.
- Slight per-partial jitter through existing `bell()`.
- Longer decay than glockenspiel but lower gain, so it is a glitter layer rather
  than a mix-dominating bell.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- Strong high-frequency body: level-normalized high-band energy, not centroid
  alone, exceeds Agogo and Woodblock at matched pitch.
- Rings longer than Agogo/Woodblock but below a tubular-bell tail.

#### 113 - Agogo

Hollow struck metal bell, harder and shorter than tinkle bell, with two strong
clanky modes.

Implementation:

- Modal table with prominent ratios around `1.0, 1.7, 2.85, 4.1`.
- Band-passed stick transient around 3-4 kHz.
- Medium-short decay.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- At matched pitch, Agogo has stronger `~1.7f` band energy than Tinkle Bell and
  Steel Drums.
- Decay is short: late RMS `[0.7, 1.1]` below a small fraction of early body.

#### 114 - Steel Drums

Pitched steelpan, not a steel-string pluck. It should sound like tuned metal
with a visible pitch center, octave/twelfth support, and a warm pan-like clang.

Implementation:

- Custom `steel_drum()` helper using `Modal::new` directly rather than `bell()`,
  so it can create tuned partial pairs.
- Partial set: fundamental pair, octave pair, twelfth/fifth color, weaker upper
  modes. Slight detuning per seed for pan shimmer.
- Key-scaled decay: low notes ring longer, high notes tighten.
- Soft strike noise around 1.5-3 kHz.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- `peak_locate` in a mid body window lands within 3% of written pitch.
- Octave/twelfth support exists: energy near `2f` and `3f` exceeds a floor
  relative to `f`.
- It is not a pluck: decay envelope lacks the fast-then-slow guitar string shape
  and `kind()` is not `STEEL`.

#### 115 - Woodblock

Dry wooden block/cavity knock. It should be short, woody, and pitch-aware.

Implementation:

- Use a very short `wood_bar()`-style modal table, not channel-10 side-stick.
- Ratios around `1.0, 2.65, 4.35` with short T60.
- Strong band-passed contact noise, short release.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- Shortest modal decay in 112-118.
- Low spectral sustain: late `[0.25, 0.50]` RMS is near silent, while early
  `[0.0, 0.08]` is audible.
- Pitch follows written key enough for melodic use: `peak_locate` near `f` after
  the contact transient, not in the first noisy samples.

#### 116 - Taiko Drum

Large pitched drum. It should have a low body, strike thump, and downward pitch
settle, not a string pluck or tiny GM tom.

Implementation:

- New membrane modal helper in `voices.rs`, borrowing the existing drum/timpani
  idea rather than the private `drums.rs` type.
- Ratios around circular membrane modes `1.0, 1.59, 2.14`.
- Low-pass thump/skin noise.
- Strike glide: start 2-3 semitones sharp, settle to written pitch over roughly
  70-120 ms.
- Longer decay than Melodic Tom, shorter than timpani.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- Early pitch > late pitch and late pitch near written `f`.
- Low band energy below 220 Hz is materially stronger than Melodic Tom at the
  same written pitch.
- Decay longer than Melodic Tom.

#### 117 - Melodic Tom

Pitched tom set on a melodic channel. It should be tighter than taiko and more
acoustic than Synth Drum.

Implementation:

- Same membrane helper as Taiko with smaller shell parameters.
- Ratios `1.0, 1.59, 2.14`, shorter T60, brighter click.
- Strike glide starts about 1-2 semitones sharp and settles faster than Taiko.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- Pitch settles near written `f`.
- Decay shorter than Taiko.
- Brighter attack than Taiko by centroid or high-band ratio in `[0, 80 ms]`.

#### 118 - Synth Drum

Electronic pitched drum. It should have a recognizable pitch sweep and synthetic
attack, not an acoustic tom clone.

Implementation:

- Modal helper with dominant sine/fundamental plus a synthetic click/noise band.
- Stronger pitch sweep: start roughly +7 semitones, settle to pitch in
  70-120 ms.
- Shorter, cleaner tail than acoustic toms.
- Optional weak upper mode or second oscillator for analog color.

Oracles:

- Routes to `modal`.
- Early pitch is much higher than late pitch; late pitch lands near written `f`.
- Body is cleaner/less noisy than Melodic Tom after the attack window.
- Decay shorter than Taiko and distinct from Melodic Tom.

#### 119 - Reverse Cymbal

Noise/metal crescendo that swells toward a peak, then dies. Written pitch is
not musically meaningful here; velocity controls level/brightness.

Implementation:

- New `ReverseCymbal` voice in `voices.rs`.
- One-shot fixed-length crescendo, roughly 0.9-1.2 s.
- High-passed noise plus a few inharmonic metal partials.
- Envelope rises with a smooth curve to a peak, then short post-peak decay.
- `note_off` before the swell peak does not kill the musical event; it may arm
  post-peak release. `note_off` after the peak can accelerate final death.
- Test-only `kind() == "reverse_cym"`.

Oracles:

- Routes to `reverse_cym`.
- RMS envelope rises: late pre-peak windows exceed early windows by a large
  ratio.
- Signal decays after the peak and voice eventually dies.
- Same program rendered at very different keys is bit-identical for the same
  seed, proving that Reverse Cymbal is an intentional pitch-agnostic GM
  exception. This is not the main semantic oracle; the swell envelope is.

## Test Oracle Plan

The focused oracle should use fixed windows first, then calibrate numeric
thresholds from the first real implementation render. Calibrated thresholds must
then be frozen in tests with comments explaining the measured feature they pin.
Pitch checks use `peak_locate` in body windows after the contact transient; zero
crossings are out of scope for these inharmonic voices.

Whenever an identity oracle compares programs, render every compared program at
the same named key for that assertion. For example, Tinkle Bell's high-band check
renders Agogo and Woodblock at key 84 too; Agogo's `1.7f` check renders Tinkle
Bell and Steel Drums at key 76 too. Otherwise the test measures pitch/register
instead of instrument identity.

| program | key / render | pitch or glide oracle | identity oracle |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 112 Tinkle Bell | key 84, 1.6 s | body `[0.06, 0.40]`, `peak_locate` within 5% of `f` over `[0.90f, 1.10f]` | `hp_rms(4 kHz+) / rms` in body is materially above Agogo and Woodblock; late `[0.8, 1.2]` remains audible |
| 113 Agogo | key 76, 1.0 s | body `[0.04, 0.20]`, `peak_locate` within 8% of `f` over `[0.85f, 1.15f]` | band energy near `1.7f` in body exceeds Tinkle Bell and Steel Drums; late `[0.65, 0.95]` is low |
| 114 Steel Drums | key 72, 1.2 s | body `[0.08, 0.35]`, `peak_locate` within 3% of `f` over `[0.95f, 1.05f]` | `2f` and `3f` support exceed calibrated floors relative to `f`; decay is modal metal, not steel-pluck |
| 115 Woodblock | key 72, 0.7 s | body `[0.025, 0.140]`, `peak_locate` within 10% of `f` over `[0.85f, 1.15f]`, or a calibrated band-energy-at-`f` check if the contact transient makes peak argmax unstable | shortest 112-118 decay: late `[0.25, 0.50]` is near silent while attack/body is audible |
| 116 Taiko Drum | key 48, 1.2 s | early `[0.035, 0.090]` above late; late `[0.18, 0.36]` within 4% of `f` over `[0.95f, 1.06f]` | at shared key 48, low-band energy below 220 Hz exceeds Melodic Tom; decay longer than Melodic Tom |
| 117 Melodic Tom | key 55, 0.9 s | early `[0.025, 0.075]` above late; late `[0.12, 0.28]` within 4% of `f` over `[0.95f, 1.06f]` | at shared key 55, brighter attack than Taiko by high-band ratio, but shorter decay |
| 118 Synth Drum | key 60, 0.8 s | early `[0.015, 0.055]` at least 25% above late; late `[0.14, 0.28]` within 4% of `f` over `[0.95f, 1.06f]` | at shared key 60, stronger early/late pitch sweep than Melodic Tom and cleaner synthetic body after attack |
| 119 Reverse Cymbal | keys 48 and 84, 1.6 s | pitch intentionally ignored; same seed renders must be bit-identical across keys | RMS envelope rises: `[0.75, 0.95]` exceeds `[0.10, 0.25]`, peak occurs in the fixed contract window around 0.9-1.2 s, `[1.20, 1.50]` falls, output stays below the normal voice safety ceiling, and voice returns false eventually |

Short-note lifecycle oracle:

- For 112-118, render a held note and a note released after 50-100 ms with the
  same seed. The released note must retain the natural struck tail rather than
  hard-choking; compare an instrument-appropriate tail window against the held
  reference, with Woodblock allowed to be naturally short.
- For 119, a note released after 50-100 ms must still reach the pre-peak swell
  window. A note released after the peak may decay faster than the held render.
- For 116-118, repeated `set_pitch` calls while the strike glide is active must
  not restart or double-apply the glide. Reuse the existing timpani anti-glide
  pattern for these new glide voices.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. `voices::make` routes 112-118 to modal voices and 119 to `reverse_cym`; no
   program in 112-119 reaches the `STEEL` fallback.
2. `gm_routing_pins_voice_kinds` includes all eight programs.
3. A focused oracle, e.g. `gm112_119_melodic_percussion_are_modeled`, proves:
   - all eight programs are audible and finite;
   - all seven pitched programs follow written pitch or an intentional pitch
     settle;
   - each program has at least one distinguishing numeric signature, so cloned
     modal presets are caught;
   - reverse cymbal swells rather than decays from sample zero.
4. Short 50-100 ms notes retain the intended struck tail/swell for 112-119; the
   melodic-channel `note_off` path must not hard-choke percussive one-shots.
5. Existing GM120-127 SFX fallback behavior remains unchanged.
6. README instrument table documents 112-119.
7. Version files are bumped once.
8. Existing-album blast radius is explicit:
   - scan every MIDI referenced by `render_opus.py::ALBUMS` and committed demo
     MIDI for program changes 112-119;
   - if none are present, render at least one prior album with baseline and
     changed binaries and `cmp` the WAVs for byte identity;
   - if any are present, treat that as intentional default-on synth improvement
     and require an explicit listening/rerender decision before integration.
9. Validation before commit:
   - `$null | deltic timeout 240 cargo test gm112_119_melodic_percussion_are_modeled --manifest-path crates/ferrosintesis/Cargo.toml`
   - `$null | deltic timeout 240 cargo test gm_routing_pins_voice_kinds --manifest-path crates/ferrosintesis/Cargo.toml`
   - `$null | deltic timeout 240 cargo test gm_sfx_120_127_are_toneless_noise_fallbacks --manifest-path crates/ferrosintesis/Cargo.toml`

## Decision Log

- Default-on, not opt-in: Arthur explicitly wants MIDI files to improve as the
  synth improves.
- Keep work in `voices.rs`: these are melodic-channel GM programs, and exposing
  `drums.rs` internals would widen ownership for little gain.
- Use `Modal` for 112-118: it already gives pitch bend, note-off, deterministic
  partials, and strike glide.
- Add `ReverseCymbal`: modal decay cannot express a reverse swell cleanly.
- No samples: best current fit is modeled synthesis; samples would add asset and
  licensing surface.

## Design Review Notes

Surviving findings from the two-reviewer adversarial pass:

- Program-specific oracles are required. `Modal::kind()` is deliberately coarse,
  so routing tests alone cannot distinguish seven modal programs.
- Pitch/glide oracles need fixed keys, windows, search bounds, and tolerances.
  The HLD now defines an oracle matrix and avoids noisy contact windows.
- Centroid-only brightness checks are brittle. The HLD now prefers normalized
  high-band or planned-partial band checks, with thresholds calibrated from real
  renders.
- A default-on `voices.rs` change needs explicit existing-album validation, not
  only unit tests.
- Melodic-channel `note_off` can choke percussive voices. The HLD now requires
  short-note lifecycle tests and qualifies Reverse Cymbal note-off behavior.

Refuted findings:

- Reverse Cymbal being pitch-agnostic is intentional GM behavior, not accidental
  SFX fallback reuse. Its main semantic oracle remains the reverse swell.
- The abstraction-creep risk was already covered by the "no shared abstraction
  until implementation proves duplication" decision.

## Residual Risks

- Numeric oracles can prove shape and separation, not actual taste. Thresholds
  must be calibrated from real renders during implementation.
- Reverse Cymbal has no lookahead to align peak with NoteOff; this design uses a
  fixed swell length. That is musically useful but not a sequencer-style
  reverse-sample stretch.
- Formal implementation-time validation found 0 GM112-119 program changes across
  58 MIDI files in the worktree. Baseline origin/main and current-branch renders
  of `albums/opus4-8/midi/07 - Snow.mid` were byte-identical
  (`SHA256 CF6517BEBFB423A4894D5D8F6EE23EA0F6E66825BD8230E8CD607CE9E4C95F46`).
