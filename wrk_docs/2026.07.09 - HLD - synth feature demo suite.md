# HLD - Synth Feature Demo Suite

**Status:** SIGNED-OFF

## Intent

Build a compact `demos/synth_feature_showcase/` package with five fast, dramatic,
epic instrumental MIDI tracks that deliberately exercise ferrosintesis's current
and planned instrument/control surface.

## Scope

In scope:

- Add a reproducible standard-library Python demo generator under
  `demos/synth_feature_showcase/`.
- Commit generated `.mid` files and `album_manifest.json`.
- Add structural MIDI oracles proving the suite authors the intended programs,
  controller gestures, stereo placement, and feature coverage while the relevant
  program is active and sounding.
- Target current ferrosintesis features and accepted future feature seams. Future
  targets get labelled musical placeholders with explicit contracts; they are
  not presented as current-audible showcase moments until the synth implements
  them.
- Keep the demos original, catchy, high-energy, and musically arranged rather
  than a dry one-note-per-program audit.

Out of scope:

- Changing ferrosintesis voices, controller handling, Rust tests, or version files.
- Committing rendered `.wav` intermediates.
- Committing `.opus` listening copies unless Arthur separately asks for an audio
  release pass.
- Cloud CI, release workflows, migration/back-compat work, or feature flags.
- Depending on the untracked `demos/` directory currently visible in the main
  clone. This worktree starts from `origin/main`, where no tracked `demos/`
  package exists.

## Success Criteria

- `python build.py --verify` in `demos/synth_feature_showcase/` rebuilds the
  suite to temporary bytes, byte-compares the generated `.mid` files and
  `album_manifest.json` against the worktree artifacts, and prints a green
  oracle table. Verification must not silently overwrite stale committed output.
- The package emits five valid MIDI files, each roughly 2:15-3:45, with a total
  suite length around 12-17 minutes.
- Every track has a clear hook, an escalation arc, and at least one dense climax,
  not just isolated instrument examples.
- The suite authors the complete current controller surface:
  - pitch bend and RPN 0 bend range;
  - RPN 1 fine tune;
  - CC1 mod wheel / Leslie;
  - CC5 plus CC65 portamento;
  - CC7 volume, CC10 pan, CC11 expression/breath;
  - CC64 sustain, CC66 sostenuto, CC67 una corda, CC68 legato;
  - CC70 vowel morph, CC71 resonance, CC74 brightness/wah;
  - CC91 reverb, CC93 chorus, CC94 echo;
  - channel aftertouch;
  - non-zero channel-10 Program Change for the v2 drum kit seam.
- The suite authors all important current/future program targets, split by
  priority so the music does not collapse into a token roll-call:
  - Tier A, current-audible showcase: each family gets a musically prominent
    representative passage; individual GM variants can appear as shorter musical
    color changes inside that passage.
  - current keys and chromatic percussion: GM 0 and GM 8-14, including current
    vibraphone GM 11; GM 15 is not a headline target because it is not a current
    distinct ferrosintesis feature;
  - current organs: GM 16-19;
  - guitars/basses/ethnic plucks: GM 24-39 and GM 104-108, including palm mute
    GM 28, drive GM 29/30, fretless GM 35, kalimba GM 108;
  - bowed/harp/timpani: GM 40-47 plus fiddle GM 110, including current harp GM 46
    and current timpani GM 47;
  - strings/choir/orchestra hit: GM 48-55;
  - brass and reeds: GM 56-71;
  - winds and synth leads: GM 72-87;
  - pads/synth FX/crystals: GM 88-103 plus GM 97/99/101/103 sustain behavior;
  - current unusual instruments and fallbacks: GM 104-108, fiddle GM 110, and
    safe SFX fallbacks GM 120-127.
  - Tier B, future placeholders: accepted future seams that may not sound
    correct today still get labelled phrases and standard expressive controls:
    GM 4-7, GM 20-23, GM 109, and GM 111.
  - Tier C, current voices with accepted future refinements: GM 11, GM 46, and
    GM 47 are showcased as current-audible instruments today. Their future
    improvements need ordinary exposed notes rather than special authored
    controls.
  - Out of target: GM 112-119. They currently fall through to generic pluck
    routing and have no accepted requirement in `reqs/`; add them later if they
    become synth features.
- Stereo design is intentional: sustained tonal beds stay mostly center to avoid
  Haas mono-collapse; width comes from doubled transient/rhythmic parts, echo
  throws, drum-kit imaging, chorus sends, and controlled pan motion.
- A local render pass with ferrosintesis produces non-silent WAVs for all five
  tracks, and `python analyze.py` passes. `ropusenc` remains out of scope.

## Package Shape

Use the older, smaller `tracks/` shape rather than the full `fable5/` movement
shape:

```text
demos/synth_feature_showcase/
  build.py
  engine.py
  verify.py
  analyze.py
  README.md
  tracks/
    01_ignition_court.py
    02_cathedral_mechanica.py
    03_skyline_brass_reactor.py
    04_choir_of_circuitry.py
    05_atlas_of_unbuilt_machines.py
  midi/
    01 - Ignition Court.mid
    02 - Cathedral Mechanica.mid
    03 - Skyline Brass Reactor.mid
    04 - Choir of Circuitry.mid
    05 - Atlas of Unbuilt Machines.mid
  album_manifest.json
```

`engine.py` should be a focused local copy of the proven fable5 composition
toolkit:

- deterministic `Score`, tempo, markers, program changes, CCs, pitch bend,
  channel aftertouch, and overlap resolution;
- helpers for `cc_curve`, `bend_range`, `fine_tune`, `portamento_on/off`,
  `vowel_curve`, `wah`, `leslie`, `sustain`, `sostenuto`, `soft_pedal`,
  `autopan`, and `echo_throw`;
- direct standard MIDI writing and parsing; no third-party dependencies.

`verify.py` should inspect the generated `Score` and the written MIDI, not rely
on human listening. It should assert:

- track count, duration windows, valid parse counts, and deterministic rebuild;
- no same-channel same-pitch overlaps after write;
- required program coverage by track and across the suite;
- required controller coverage by track and across the suite;
- per-track oracle scope: each track can go green against its own assigned
  feature matrix before the full suite exists; suite-wide coverage is asserted
  only by the complete build;
- feature-interaction coverage: each headline controller must occur while an
  intended program family is active and notes are sounding. Examples: CC70 only
  counts on choir/choir-pad material, CC68 only counts on monophonic slur spans,
  CC11 breath counts on brass spans, CC67 counts on piano spans, and a non-zero
  channel-10 Program Change only counts if it precedes v2-relevant hits;
- value-window coverage: the controller must travel far enough to be meaningful
  (for example CC74 low/high, CC71 low/high, CC1 slow/fast or vibrato depth,
  aftertouch pressure, widened RPN bend range, and non-centre pitch bend);
- all pitch-bend/RPN/fine-tune/portamento/filter/pedal/aftertouch demo channels
  recentre or switch controls off before program reuse and at track end;
- stereo discipline for sustained beds with numeric pan/duration bounds, plus
  enough authored pan/echo/chorus events for spatial demonstration;
- each track has a velocity/RMS-intent arc in MIDI terms: intro < build <
  climax, with a drop or breath before the final hit.

`analyze.py` is a required local audio-side gate after the MIDI has been rendered
with ferrosintesis. It should use only the standard library and should inspect PCM
samples, not just WAV headers:

- non-silence, peak headroom, no gross DC offset, and no all-zero channel;
- broad dynamic arc windows for each track: intro/build/climax/drop must follow
  the intended contour in RMS, mirroring the MIDI arc oracle;
- mono fold-down safety: the mono downmix must not lose more than about 3 dB
  overall, and sustained-bed spotlight windows should stay within about 2 dB;
- stereo sanity: the mix should not be a mono point source, but wide sections
  must be transient/doubled rather than long off-centre sustained beds;
- headline control deltas where cheap: render or window matched `--solo`
  control-on/control-off material for flagship controls and assert a measurable
  audio difference, not just non-silence. Priority deltas: CC1 vibrato/Leslie,
  CC70 vowel, CC74/CC71 wah-resonance, CC11 brass breath, channel aftertouch,
  and CC67 piano soft pedal. This still cannot prove taste or hook quality; it
  catches missing, ignored, or buried feature material.

internal design, but it can make sure the right musical material is present.

## Track Plan

### 1. Ignition Court

Purpose: guitar/bass/drum/lead feature piece.

Musical job: a fast 7/8 + 4/4 hybrid chase with a palm-muted hook, distorted
power-chord responses, fretless bass slides, and synth-lead tradeoffs.

Feature targets:

- GM 24-31 guitars, GM 32-39 basses, GM 28 palm mute, GM 29/30 drive insert,
  GM 35 fretless mwah, GM 80-87 synth leads.
- Channel 10 v2 kit via non-zero Program Change.
- CC68 hammer-on/pull-off phrases, pitch bend, RPN 0 widened bends, CC1 vibrato,
  CC74 wah plus CC71 resonance, CC94 echo throws, CC10 doubled rhythm-guitar
  width, CC93 chorus on leads.

Stereo plan:

- Bass/kick/snare centered.
- Two rhythm guitars hard left/right with different registers.
- Lead stays near center but throws echo alternately left/right through CC94 and
  controlled pan moves.

### 2. Cathedral Mechanica

Purpose: modal, organ, piano pedal, bells, and future percussion improvements.

Musical job: a 6/8 machine-toccata that opens with soft-pedal piano ostinato,
adds church/rock organ Leslie, then erupts into bells, timpani, and crystalline
sequencers.

Feature targets:

- GM 0 and GM 8-14, especially piano GM 0, vibraphone GM 11, wood bars GM
  12/13, and tubular bells GM 14.
- GM 4-7 future electric-piano/harpsichord/clavinet placeholders.
- GM 16-19 current organs plus GM 20-23 future reed
  organ/accordion/harmonica placeholders.
- GM 47 timpani, GM 96-103 crystal/synth effects.
- Modal/organ pitch controls from MM-REQ-KILN-00008: pitch bend, RPN bend
  range, RPN fine tune, CC5/CC65 portamento, aftertouch.
- CC64 sustain washes, CC66 sostenuto capture, CC67 una corda, CC1 Leslie,
  CC11 expression swells, CC91 reverb depth.

Stereo plan:

- Piano and organ centered; bells answer in symmetric left/right registers.
- Crystalline sequencers use short notes and pan motion; sustained organ stays
  center to keep mono collapse under control.

### 3. Skyline Brass Reactor

Purpose: v0.9 brass/reed/wind plus bowed-string orchestral middle showcase.

Musical job: a 12/8 cinematic sprint with low brass ostinato, horn/trumpet hook,
bowed-string engine figures, reed counterlines, whistle/flute descants, and
sectional call-and-response.

Feature targets:

- Representative brass/reed/wind voices carry the hook and countermelodies; all
  brass GM 56-63, reeds GM 64-71, and winds GM 72-79 appear as musical color
  changes inside the arrangement.
- Bowed strings GM 40-45 plus fiddle GM 110, with at least one exposed slurred
  solo and one ensemble answer.
- CC11 breath/timbre ramps on brass, channel-aftertouch growl, CC1 vibrato,
  CC68 legato slurs, pitch bend under vibrato, CC91 hall depth.

Stereo plan:

- Low brass centered/slightly wide but not auto-panned.
- Trumpets/trombones/horns staged as an orchestra arc.
- Bowed-string sustained lines remain centered; short spiccato/answer figures
  may be doubled left/right.
- Reeds and winds take moving counterlines across the front, using short notes
  and echo/reverb rather than off-center sustained pads.

### 4. Choir of Circuitry

Purpose: SawStack strings/choir/pads, orchestra hit, synth FX, and control-heavy
sound design.

Musical job: a fast 5/4 electro-choral anthem with a string ostinato, vowel
choir hook, orchestra-hit punctuation, pad swells, and synth lead ribbons.

Feature targets:

- GM 48-51 strings, GM 52-54 choir, GM 55 orchestra hit.
- Pads GM 88-95 plus sustained synth FX GM 97/99/101/103.
- Secondary lead ribbons may appear here, but primary GM 80-87 coverage belongs
  to track 1 so the verifier has one clear home for the family.
- CC70 vowel morph, CC1 strings/choir vibrato, CC68 slurs, CC93 chorus,
  CC94 echo, CC10 pan motion, CC74/71 filter sweeps.

Stereo plan:

- Sustained choir/string beds stay center.
- Width comes from doubled ostinati, pad/lead echoes, orchestra-hit punctuation,
  and transient synth pulses.

### 5. Atlas of Unbuilt Machines

Purpose: future-facing GM gap suite and unusual instrument roll-call.

Musical job: a compact through-composed finale that moves from plucked world
instruments into free reeds, bagpipe/shanai leads, harp/timpani drama, and SFX
noise impacts, all over a memorable ostinato.

Feature targets:

- Current implemented gap targets: kalimba GM 108, sitar GM 104, banjo GM 105,
  shamisen GM 106, koto GM 107, fiddle GM 110, SFX GM 120-127.
- Accepted future placeholders: electric pianos/harpsichord/clavinet GM 4-7,
  free reeds GM 20-23, bagpipe GM 109, and shanai GM 111.
- Current voices with accepted future refinements: vibraphone GM 11, harp GM 46,
  and timpani GM 47 get exposed ordinary-note material so their future changes
  are audible without needing special MIDI controls.
- Authored controls likely to matter later: CC1 vibrato/tremolo, CC11
  breath/bellows, aftertouch, CC68 legato, pitch bend, RPN range, CC74/71
  brightness/resonance.

Stereo plan:

- Treat unusual instruments as short spotlight phrases across the stage.
- Keep bagpipe drone and harp bass centered; SFX noise impacts can be wide
  because they are transient.

## Implementation Approach

1. Create the demo package scaffolding and a compact shared `engine.py`.
2. Write `verify.py` first with the tiered feature matrix, feature-interaction
   checks, drift checks, reset hygiene, mono-safe pan rules, and arc checks.
3. Compose one track at a time against that track's matrix, running `python
   build.py --verify --track N` or an equivalent per-track oracle mode after
   each track is added. The complete suite adds the suite-wide coverage gate.
4. Keep Tier A features musically prominent. Tier B future placeholders are
   labelled and short enough that today's fallback render does not mislead the
   listener.
5. Add `README.md` and `album_manifest.json` generation from `build.py`.
6. Build ferrosintesis if needed and render the five MIDI files to scratch WAVs
   for audio validation. Do not commit WAVs.
7. Run final focused validation:
   - `python build.py --verify` from `demos/synth_feature_showcase/`;
   - render the five MIDI files with ferrosintesis to scratch WAVs;
   - `python analyze.py` over those WAVs;
   - `cargo build --release -p ferrosintesis-cli` only
     if the render binary is missing or stale enough to block the smoke render.

## Risks

- Musical density can fight feature clarity. Mitigation: each track has a primary
  feature family; the verifier requires Tier A feature prominence and
  program/controller overlap, not simultaneous use of every feature.
- "All features" can become a checklist instead of music. Mitigation: the fifth
  track absorbs the roll-call pressure; the first four remain idiomatic pieces;
  Tier B future placeholders are explicit and short.
- Stereo ambition can create mono-collapse on sustained tonal parts. Mitigation:
  sustained beds stay centered; width comes from transients, doubles, chorus,
  and echo; `verify.py` and `analyze.py` both include mono-safety checks.
- Future seams may choose different CC semantics later. Mitigation: author
  per-feature contracts using standard expressive controls already likely to be
  meaningful: CC1, CC11, aftertouch, CC68, pitch bend/RPN, CC74/71. If a future
  implementation chooses different semantics, update the demo contract then.
- Verification may pass while rendered audibility is weak. Mitigation: add a WAV
  analysis gate and solo-render headline stems during implementation. Machine
  checks still cannot prove taste; Arthur's listening pass remains the final
  judgment on "catchy" and "epic".

## Decision Log

- Five tracks, not four: the future-facing GM gaps are broad enough that a fifth
  finale keeps the first four musically focused.
- Album-like package, not loose MIDI files: reproducibility and oracles matter
  more than a quick export.
- Older `tracks/` shape, not full fable5 movement architecture: this is a demo
  suite, not a narrative album; smaller structure is enough and easier to verify.
- No synth changes in this task: the demos should target the synth's surface, not
  move it.
- MIDI-only commit by default: `.opus` listening copies are useful but a separate
  release/render decision.
- Tiered feature matrix: current-audible features must carry the showcase; future
  seams are placeholders with contracts, not promises that today's render already
  sounds right.
- GM 112-119 left out deliberately: they are not current ferrosintesis features and
  have no accepted future requirement.
- Per-track gates first, suite-wide gate last: otherwise no intermediate track
  can be green until all five exist.

## Review Notes

Adversarial review survivors folded into the design:

- Program/controller coverage must be family-aware and must overlap sounding
  notes, or the MIDI can author ignored controls.
- Generated MIDI and manifest artifacts need drift detection; verification must
  not overwrite stale files and call that green.
- Audio validation must be required, not optional, because MIDI presence is not
  audibility in the render.
- Mono safety needs numeric checks, not prose about centering.
- Sticky controls must reset before channel/program reuse, not only at track end.
- GM 40-45 bowed strings need an explicit track home.
- The program matrix needs priority tiers so the suite stays musical.
- Future feature claims need per-feature contracts and must not imply today's
  fallback render is already correct.

Refuted or rejected findings:

- The local `engine.py` scope is already bounded by a concrete helper/API list.
- Bagpipe GM 109 and shanai GM 111 are already labelled accepted future seams,
  not current-audible showcase material.

  them synth features.

External Claude review found no blocking issues. Folded:
