# HLD — Cathedral organ synthesis

**Status:** SIGNED-OFF (2026.07.10, Arthur) — implementation authorised.
**Date:** 2026.07.10
**Worktree:** `D:/worktrees/midi-music/20260710-DEV-CDX-cathedral-organ-synthesis`
**Crate:** `ferrosintesis` 0.11.0 (planned 0.12.0)

## 1. Intent

Replace default-bank GM 19 (Church Organ) with a convincing English
cathedral/symphonic full organ: real 32-foot pedal weight, distinct pipe-rank
colour, brilliant mixtures, subtle wind interaction, and a long cathedral
acoustic that survives capable hi-fi playback. Move the current GM 19 additive
pipe-bank voice, unchanged, to the existing non-zero-CC0 secondary bank.

This follows the repository rule that the best current instrument is the
default. It is an intentional timbre change, not an opt-in controller feature.

## 2. Scope

### In scope

- A new, default GM 19 `CathedralOrgan` voice with an English
  cathedral/symphonic full-organ registration.
- A 32-foot low register, a principal chorus, chorus reeds, and a broken
  mixture that stays bright without folding into aliasing.
- Pipe-level harmonic and attack behaviour, small deterministic tuning/level
  variation, and a shared per-channel wind-pressure response.
- A dedicated cathedral reverb bus for the new default GM 19. It bypasses the
  global hall's 150 Hz send high-pass so the pedal can energise the room.
- Real organ controller semantics: CC1 becomes a subtle fixed-rate tremulant
  on the new organ. The legacy secondary organ retains its existing Leslie
  ramp exactly.
- Explicitly move *The Ninth Bell*'s organ lane to CC0=1 because that score
  names and verifies a Leslie arc. Its artistic intent is the legacy organ,
  not the new cathedral default.
- Rewrite the GM 19 slice of *Cathedral Mechanica* to expose the default
  cathedral organ's pedal, plenum, mixture, tremulant, and decay, plus a short
  CC0=1 legacy-organ comparison.
- Numeric DSP oracles, the repository render-diff inventory, affected
  listening refreshes, documentation, and one version bump.

### Out of scope

- A virtual console, arbitrary stop-by-stop MIDI control, pistons, multiple
  manuals, temperament selection, or an organ-builder UI.
- Exact emulation of a named historical organ or cathedral.
- Revoicing GM 16–18 (drawbar/percussive/rock organs) or GM 20–23 (free reeds).
- VST/plugin or real-time-host work.
- Shipping a multisample or impulse-response library. No new Rust dependency,
  feature flag, environment variable, or network access at render/test time.
- Fixing general channel-effect behaviour during held-note program changes.

## 3. Current state and why it cannot reach the target

The current GM 19 implementation in `voices.rs` is six sine oscillators at
ratios 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 under one ADSR. It has no 16- or 32-foot rank, and
each ratio is treated as a stop even though a real rank's individual pipe has
its own harmonic spectrum and attack. Velocity changes its steady level, and
CC1 is routed through the engine's Leslie model.

The shared hall is an eight-comb Freeverb-style tank. Every melodic reverb send
is high-passed at 150 Hz before that tank, so low pedal energy remains mostly
dry instead of coupling into the building. The source is otherwise mono and
the organ receives the generic organ chorus profile.

The committed listening set contains seven MIDIs that select default-bank
GM 19 and none that currently authors CC0. *The Ninth Bell* explicitly authors
and documents Leslie motion. After moving that lane to the secondary bank, the
expected synth-audio blast radius is six MIDIs:

- both *Hollow Hill* parts;
- *The Long Turning*;
- *Atlas of Becoming* track 14, “Every Door Opens at Once”;
- *RIVERWAKE*;
- *Synth Feature Showcase* track 2, “Cathedral Mechanica”.

Of those, only the two *Hollow Hill* tracks and *Cathedral Mechanica* currently
have committed Opus listening copies. The other three albums are MIDI-only.

## 4. Proposed design

### 4.1 Default/secondary routing

Keep the existing CC0 bank seam:

- `voices::make(19, ...)` creates the new `CathedralOrgan`.
- `altbank::make(19, ...)` creates the current GM 19 voice, byte-for-byte.
- GM 16–18 and 20–23 retain their current factories and fingerprints.

The current GM 19 constructor becomes a narrow `pub(crate)` legacy factory;
the old `Organ` struct stays in place because the other organ/free-reed
programs still use it. The legacy route keeps the pinned current hash
`11750116236685652893` at 44.1 kHz, key 69, velocity 104, seed `0x5eed`.

Bank-sensitive controller and effect decisions use the spawn-time
`(Active.program, Active.alt)` pair for held voices. Program 19 is a
Leslie-family voice only when `alt=true`; default-bank program 19 is a
cathedral-tremulant voice.

The current engine's single post-sum channel buffer is not sufficient when a
held default GM19 overlaps a newly spawned secondary GM19, or vice versa. Each
active note therefore contributes to three separately accumulated source
classes before effects: ordinary/global, legacy-GM19/global, and
default-GM19/cathedral. Their dry signals may be summed after common channel
gain/pan, but their room and default chorus/echo sends remain separate.
Spawn-time program/bank also filters CC1 and CC121 application. The legacy
source keeps its present 0.20 default chorus; the cathedral source defaults to
zero chorus/echo. Authored CC93/CC94 override those defaults for both sources;
two small authored flags distinguish user sends from program defaults.

### 4.2 Pipe-rank model

`CathedralOrgan` remains additive, but models physical ranks rather than
drawbars. Additive parameters generate two band-limited 1024-sample cyclic
tables per `RankPipe`: a speech spectrum and a steady spectrum. The runtime
pipe performs linear wavetable interpolation and one timed crossfade between
them. This retains per-rank harmonic/attack character without evaluating every
partial for every sample. The rank adds a short, band-shaped wind/chiff
component; note-off closes the dry pipe in roughly 80–120 ms and leaves the
building to provide the long tail.

Four compact rank spectra cover the useful pipe families:

- **Open wood / stopped flute:** strong fundamental, restrained upper
  partials, slow low-pipe speech.
- **Open diapason / principal:** a controlled harmonic slope with a bright,
  quicker upper-partial onset.
- **Chorus reed:** dense harmonic series, slower low-reed establishment, and
  a small deterministic noisy edge.
- **Mixture principal:** lean, bright pipes with automatic octave breaks.

The compact spectral contract below makes the voicing implementable. Values
are dB relative to H1 at the C2/C4/C6 key anchors; intermediate keys interpolate
linearly in dB. H4 and all other integer harmonics interpolate in log-frequency
between the stated H1/H2/H3/H6 anchors, with a hard maximum of 24 source
harmonics per generated table. “Speech tilt” is applied to the speech table
relative to steady state.

| Family | H2 at C2/C4/C6 | H3 at C2/C4/C6 | H6 at C2/C4/C6 | Speech tilt | Speech→steady C2/C6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| open wood | −28/−25/−22 | −9/−8/−7 | −40/−36/−32 | H3+ delayed −6 dB | 180/90 ms |
| principal | −6/−5/−4 | −11/−9/−8 | −24/−18/−15 | H3+ +3 dB | 90/35 ms |
| chorus reed | −2/−3/−4 | −4/−5/−6 | −10/−11/−12 | H2+ +2 dB, 15 ms overshoot | 130/60 ms |
| mixture principal | −7/−6/−5 | −12/−10/−9 | −24/−20/−18 | H2+ +2 dB | 55/20 ms |

Per-pipe level, attack, and tuning variation is stable for the lifetime of the
instrument: hash `(fixed instrument seed, rank id, MIDI key)`, not the engine's
changing `voices_spawned` seed. Tuning scatter is bounded to ±1.5 cents. The
event seed controls only starting phase and transient turbulence. A repeated
key therefore operates the same virtual pipe even after unrelated notes.

At every pitch retune, the voice composes performance pitch, static tuning,
wind, and tremulant before enforcing both safety bounds: a rank fundamental
below 12 Hz is omitted, and no partial may exceed `0.45 * sample_rate`. A rank
table is regenerated only when that composed pitch changes the safe harmonic
set; phase remains continuous. This covers maximum RPN bend as well as spawn
time and makes the generated source alias-free and infrasonically bounded by
construction.

This deliberately follows the proven shape used by Aeolus rather than adding
samples: harmonic level and attack behaviour vary by pitch and partial, with
small pipe variation. The clean CC0 FreePats organ bank is itself rendered
from Aeolus, not recorded from real pipes, so embedding it would add about
13 MiB without adding a real acoustic onset. It may be used as a development
listening/spectral reference, but no reference audio ships and tests do not
download it.

### 4.3 Fixed full-organ registration

GM 19 gets one opinionated, immediately useful registration rather than a
stop API. Gains below are pre-normalisation linear weights:

| Division/rank | Ratio | Family | Gain | Key range |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| Manual diapason 16' | 0.5 | principal | 0.28 | all |
| Manual principal 8' | 1.0 | principal | 0.72 | all |
| Manual octave 4' | 2.0 | principal | 0.34 | all |
| Manual twelfth 2 2/3' | 3.0 | principal | 0.18 | all |
| Manual fifteenth 2' | 4.0 | principal | 0.22 | all |
| Manual mixture IV | table below | mixture principal | 0.20 total | all |
| Manual trumpet 8' | 1.0 | chorus reed | 0.18 | all |
| Pedal open wood 32' | 0.25 | open wood | 0.32 | full ≤46, fades to zero at 50 |
| Pedal diapason 16' | 0.5 | principal | 0.26 | same |
| Pedal principal 8' | 1.0 | principal | 0.18 | same |
| Pedal trombone 16' | 0.5 | chorus reed | 0.16 | same |

The four mixture-pipe ratios break on C boundaries and retain the same total
0.20 gain after each break:

| MIDI keys | Mixture ratios |
|---|---|
| 0–47 | 6, 8, 12, 16 |
| 48–59 | 4, 6, 8, 12 |
| 60–71 | 3, 4, 6, 8 |
| 72–83 | 2, 3, 4, 6 |
| 84–127 | 1.5, 2, 3, 4 |

Every break crossfades the old/new ratio sets over one semitone. Rank weights
normalise by active registration energy, not oscillator count, so neither the
pedal split nor a mixture break creates a level cliff. A note runs at most 14
runtime rank-wavetable oscillators (10 manual plus four pedal), independent of
the conceptual harmonic count used to generate their tables.

The automatic low-key pedal layer is the smallest interface that makes normal
GM files produce real sub-bass without requiring authored stop controls. It is
documented as part of the preset, not presented as a general organ-console
model. The steady output is normalised by active rank weight, not by oscillator
count, so the pedal layer adds weight without a level cliff.

### 4.4 Expression, tremulant, and wind

- Note velocity does not act like hammer force. It may alter only the tiny
  onset/chiff component; steady-state RMS from velocity 32 to 120 stays within
  1.5 dB. CC7 and CC11 remain the musical level controls.
- Default GM 19 starts with tremulant off. Authored CC1 smoothly raises a
  subtle 5.5 Hz wind tremulant (amplitude plus a few cents of pitch), without
  changing speed or simulating a rotating speaker. The phase is channel-global,
  so staggered notes breathe together rather than becoming a chorus.
- Secondary-bank GM 19 preserves the current CC1 Leslie slow/fast ramp and
  inertia exactly.
- The engine derives a per-channel cathedral-organ load from the active
  default-GM19 notes. One smoothed pressure and one signed tremulant sample are
  sent per block through a defaulted `Voice::set_organ_pressure` hook. The
  voice composes frequency once as `base * static_pipe_tune * performance_pitch
  * wind_factor(rank) * trem_factor(rank)`. Pedal ranks use 0.25× pitch
  sensitivity, mixture ranks 0.35×, principals 1.0×, and reeds 1.2×; amplitude
  sensitivity follows the same order. Full chords produce a bounded 1–6 cent
  pitch settling and 0.3–1.8 dB level settling, then recover within two
  seconds. The hook is a no-op for every other voice.

### 4.5 Cathedral acoustic

Add a dedicated `CathedralReverb` beside the existing `Reverb`; do not alter
the existing hall. It uses:

- 40 ms pre-delay and early taps at 48, 57, 71, 91, and 117 ms from input,
  with gains 0.40, 0.31, 0.25, 0.18, and 0.13 alternating left/right;
- an eight-line FDN with 44.1 kHz lengths
  `[2087, 2609, 3169, 3691, 4241, 4751, 5261, 5813]`, scaled and rounded at
  other sample rates;
- the normalized 8×8 Walsh-Hadamard matrix (`H8 / sqrt(8)`) as the fixed
  feedback matrix; normalized input vector `[+,+,−,+,−,−,+,−]`, left output
  `[+,−,+,+,+,−,−,−]`, and right output `[+,+,−,−,+,+,−,−]`;
- delay-proportional feedback plus one low/high decay filter per line, tuned
  to the octave-band RT60 contract in §6;
- a second-order 10 Hz high-pass at `CathedralOrgan` output, before the dry/send
  split, plus the same DC blocker on the cathedral return. This protects both
  the dry hi-fi path and the FDN while retaining the 16.35 Hz 32' fundamental.

The active render loop classifies every note before channel aggregation as
described in §4.1. Dry audio still follows CC7/CC11, CC10, and the normal mix.
The cathedral-class buffer feeds the cathedral bus after channel expression
but before pan, with CC91 as its send level. It bypasses `rev_hp`; ordinary and
secondary-organ classes continue through the existing global hall. CC93 and
CC94 remain explicitly authorable, but default cathedral-organ chorus/echo
sends are zero.

`--wet 0` and CC91=0 remain truly dry. The new acoustic is therefore the
default path for normal GM settings, but it does not override an explicit dry
render request.

### 4.6 Demo and authored Leslie preservation

*The Ninth Bell* receives CC0=1 on its organ channel before program 19. Its
copied `Score.cc` writer assigns CC0 bank select priority 0 (program change is
priority 1; ordinary CC is priority 2), and a serialized-MIDI oracle asserts
CC0 precedes program 19 and the first organ note. The same rule is applied to
the showcase engine. The builder, committed MIDI, manifest/oracles, and
comments are updated to say “secondary organ / Leslie”. Baseline and new
binaries must render that updated MIDI bit-identically, proving both its
artistic intent and the legacy route.

The program-19 window in *Cathedral Mechanica* becomes a compact listening
test: exposed low C pedal, mid-register principal chords, high mixture fanfare,
full-organ cadence, then isolated decay. It returns briefly with CC0=1 for the
legacy Leslie comparison and resets CC0 to zero afterwards. Its MIDI and audio
checks are regenerated rather than hand-edited.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

### Routing and containment

1. Default GM19 routes to `CathedralOrgan`; `CC0 != 0` GM19 routes to the
   present legacy organ and preserves its pinned render hash. GM16–18 and
   GM20–23 retain their current voice fingerprints and controller behaviour.
2. Default GM19 never enters the Leslie driver or generic organ chorus by
   default. Secondary GM19 preserves the existing Leslie inertia test.
3. Overlap tests in both directions (hold default → CC0=1 → legacy, and hold
   legacy → CC0=0 → default) keep each voice on its spawn-time CC1, CC91,
   CC93, chorus-default, and room paths through CC121 resets.

### Pipe tone and sub-bass

4. At MIDI 36 (C2 key, steady dry window), Goertzel peaks are present within
   0.8% of 16.35, 32.70, and 65.41 Hz. The 32' 16.35 Hz component is no lower
   than −18 dB relative to the 8' 65.41 Hz component, and 15–40 Hz band RMS is
   at least −14 dB relative to 40–120 Hz.
5. A matched-pitch comparison against secondary GM19 shows at least 6 dB more
   15–40 Hz energy at MIDI 36. At MIDI 84, default GM19 has at least 4% of
   steady RMS above 4 kHz. All created partial frequencies remain below
   `0.45 * sample_rate` after static tune, maximum supported RPN bend, wind,
   and tremulant are composed; no rank fundamental is created below 12 Hz.
   Across MIDI keys 0–35, dry energy below 10 Hz stays at least 24 dB below
   12–40 Hz. Every tested sample is finite with DC below −70 dBFS.
6. The pedal/manual crossfade over keys 46–50 has no adjacent-note RMS jump
   greater than 3 dB. At every mixture break, old/new spectra overlap for one
   semitone, total RMS changes by no more than 2 dB, and centroid changes by no
   more than 25%. High keys retain at least two audible mixture ranks without
   frequency folding.
7. Velocity 32 versus 120 changes steady dry RMS by no more than 1.5 dB.
   Low-pipe partials establish later than upper principal partials, and the
   onset has measurable shaped chiff without a full-scale single-sample click.
   Repeating one key before and after unrelated notes preserves its steady
   spectrum/tuning while changing only phase/transient turbulence.

### Wind and controllers

8. A ten-note held chord settles by 1–6 cents and 0.3–1.8 dB per note relative
   to a single note, remains stable, and recovers within two seconds after the
   load is removed. Staggered notes share tremulant phase. Simultaneous bend,
   fine tune, wind, and tremulant equal the single composed-frequency formula;
   sustain and all-notes-off still work.
9. Default GM19 CC1 produces a stable 5–6 Hz subtle tremulant and no Leslie
   speed sweep. Secondary GM19 CC1 still sweeps through the existing slow/fast
   Leslie behaviour. `CC121` resets either modulation path without clearing
   bank select.

### Cathedral acoustic

10. The dedicated impulse response has no wet output before 35 ms. Octave-band
    RT60 lies within 5.0–6.5 s at 31.5 Hz, 5.5–7.0 s at 63 Hz, 5.5–7.2 s at
    125/500 Hz, 4.5–6.5 s at 2 kHz, and 2.5–4.5 s at 8 kHz. The cathedral
    31 Hz/500 Hz transfer ratio is at least 18 dB higher than the existing
    high-passed hall route; together with the source-class routing test, this
    proves the cathedral path bypasses `rev_hp`.
11. In the 0.8–2.0 s late tail, spectral flatness is at least 0.18 and the
    largest 1/6-octave modal peak is less than 12 dB over the median. Stereo
    correlation stays between 0.05 and 0.85, side RMS is at least 8% of mid,
    and mono collapse loses less than 3 dB. A defined eight-note low C chord at
    CC7/11/91=127 keeps the pre-glue cathedral return below 0 dBFS and finite.
    Adding one MIDI36 32' pedal note under a plenum raises pre-normalisation
    peak by no more than 3 dB and lowers post-normalised 250 Hz–8 kHz RMS by no
    more than 1.5 dB. Silence stays silent; `--wet 0` / CC91=0 are exact
    bypasses.

### Repository outcome

12. The first post-sign-off implementation slice is a release-mode kernel
    spike for the generated wavetable pipe and 14-rank voice. The finished
    voice runs at most 14 runtime rank oscillators per note and uses at most 24

14. The regenerated *Ninth Bell* MIDI places CC0=1 before program 19 and the

    Arthur's hi-fi, the exposed demo must deliver tactile low-pedal weight

## 7. Decision log

- **D1 — New GM19 is the default; current GM19 moves to CC0 secondary.** Arthur
  explicitly chose the best-instrument-as-default doctrine.
- **D2 — English cathedral/symphonic full organ.** This is the proposed tonal
  tradition unless Arthur corrects it at sign-off; it best matches “deep
  rumbling and amazing highs”.
- **D3 — Additive-generated rank wavetables, not shipped samples or per-sample
  partial banks.** A high-quality organ can be additive when harmonic/attack
  parameters vary by partial and key. Generating compact speech/steady tables
  keeps that control while bounding runtime at 14 rank oscillators. The clean
  CC0 FreePats bank is itself Aeolus synthesis; embedding it would spend about
  13 MiB without adding a real-pipe recording.
- **D4 — Fixed registration with automatic low-key pedal layer.** It makes
  ordinary GM files impressive without inventing a stop protocol. A full
  console is intentionally deferred.
- **D5 — Dedicated, fully specified Hadamard FDN isolated from the global
  hall.** Extending the current short Freeverb tank to six seconds risks a
  metallic tail and changing every instrument. Fixed delay/matrix/vector
  choices plus decay/density oracles contain both sound and blast radius.
- **D6 — Preserve intentional Leslie composition, not accidental default.**
  *The Ninth Bell* moves to CC0=1; all other ordinary GM19 users receive the
  improved default.
- **D7 — No new module or dependency.** The new voice sits beside the existing
  organ code and the new room beside the existing reverb. The design adds one
  defaulted, organ-specific `Voice` hook (`set_organ_pressure`) and no general
  framework.
- **D8 — Stable virtual pipes; event seed only for turbulence.** Rank/key
  voicing derives from a fixed instrument seed so unrelated note history
  cannot retune a physical pipe.
- **D9 — CC0 sorts before Program Change.** The two affected copied score
  engines give bank select priority 0 and verify serialized order; ordinary CC
  ordering remains unchanged.

## 8. Evidence used

- Fons Adriaensen's Aeolus project describes a non-sampled additive organ with
  per-stop voicing, stereo/Ambisonics output, and a large-church reverb:
  <https://kokkinizita.linuxaudio.org/linuxaudio/aeolus/>.
- The Aeolus design presentation identifies harmonic level, attack time/type,
  pitch-dependent interpolation, and small pipe variation as the key additive
  parameters: <https://kokkinizita.linuxaudio.org/papers/aeolus-pres.pdf>.
- FreePats' CC0 organ bank is documented as a 13 MiB recording of the Aeolus
  emulator, not a real pipe organ: <https://freepats.zenvoid.org/Organ/pipe-organ.html>.
- Jot and Chaigne's AES paper establishes unitary feedback matrices and
  frequency-dependent decay control for artificial reverberation:
  <https://secure.aes.org/forum/pubs/conventions/?elib=5663>.

## 9. Review record

### 9.1 Independent adversarial review and cross-refutation

Two reviewers ran independently, then each received only the other's findings
and attempted to refute them. All refutation checks ran; there are no
unverified findings.

Surviving findings folded into this revision:

1. **CRITICAL — pre-sum route containment.** Both reviewers confirmed the
   single channel buffer cannot preserve overlapping default/legacy GM19 room
   and controller paths. Section 4.1 now requires three spawn-time source
   classes and two-direction overlap tests. The one allowed targeted re-review
   returned **RESOLVED**: classification happens before aggregation and the
   acceptance tests cover both bank-switch directions through CC121.
2. **HIGH — runtime budget.** The original 72-rotator ceiling conflicted with
   the 3× target. The voice now uses additive-generated rank wavetables, at
   most 14 runtime oscillators, and an early release-mode spike.
3. **HIGH — voicing was underspecified.** Sections 4.2–4.3 now pin spectral
   anchors, speech transitions, rank gains, mixture ratios, and breakpoints.
4. **HIGH — virtual pipe identity.** Variation now derives from fixed
   rank/key identity rather than the changing note-spawn seed.
5. **HIGH — infrasonic and tail safety.** The cathedral path now has a 10 Hz
   safety filter, octave-band RT60 bounds, and a dense-low-chord headroom test.
6. **HIGH — FDN density/colour.** Section 4.5 now pins matrix, delays, vectors,
   early taps, sample-rate scaling, spectral-flatness/modal, and stereo tests.
7. **MEDIUM — modulation composition.** Performance pitch, wind, and
   channel-coherent tremulant now compose once with rank sensitivities.
8. **MEDIUM — MIDI event order.** Bank select gets priority 0 plus a serialized
   order oracle, because same-tick ordinary CC currently sorts after Program.
9. **MEDIUM — low-frequency route proof.** A differential 31/500 Hz transfer
   test replaces “measurable 31 Hz tail”, which could pass through a finite
   150 Hz high-pass.
10. **MEDIUM — runtime alias guard.** The partial ceiling now applies after all
    pitch factors, including maximum RPN bend.

Refuted finding:

- A second monolithic legacy-engine golden fixture was judged redundant. The
  existing standalone hash, focused two-direction controller/effect tests, and
  full old/new-binary *Ninth Bell* PCM identity together cover the promised
  legacy path more strongly.

### 9.2 External review

A single fresh Claude Opus pass ran read-only in plan mode with no subagents.
It returned one **HIGH** finding: the design's 10 Hz protection originally
covered only cathedral input/return, leaving the dry 32' path unbounded below
and able to consume peak-normalisation headroom (the existing master also adds
1.5 dB around 95 Hz). The finding was confirmed against `BusGlue` and the CLI
normaliser, then folded into §4.2/§4.5 and AC5/AC11: every rank now has a 12 Hz
fundamental floor, the 10 Hz filter precedes the dry/send split, and a defined
post-normalisation loudness test prevents the pedal from hollowing out the
rest of the mix. No external finding was rejected and no confirmation round
was run.
