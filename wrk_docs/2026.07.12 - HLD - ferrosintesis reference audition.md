## 1. Goal

Build `demos/ferrosintesis_reference/` — an audition suite in which **every distinct
ferrosintesis voice, drum voice, controller and alt-bank voicing can be heard individually
and identified**, backed by oracles that prove each one actually sounds.

A **reference tool, not a record.** `demos/synth_feature_showcase/` remains the showpiece.

### Why the showcase cannot do this

It is a *family-parallel sweep set to music*: in `tracks/03_skyline_brass_reactor.py:20-52`
brass runs beats 0–224, reeds enter at 112, winds at 160, bowed at 232, and a kit plays
throughout. Of its 130 (track, program) uses, **69 never sound alone even once**. Each family
gets a *different* phrase, so no two instruments are comparable. Its own signed-off HLD rules
this shape out explicitly — *"rather than a dry one-note-per-program audit"*. It got what it
asked for. A musical demo misses instruments *because* it is musical: nobody writes a tune
that needs an agogo.

## 2. Scope

**In:** every distinct melodic voice (~109 slots — see §3.2); every alt-bank voicing that is a
genuinely different voice (~25); every implemented channel-10 drum voice + the Brush kit + the
hi-hat choke group; a named list of audible expressive features (§3.7); structural oracles;
and **audio oracles proving every voice is audible**. One `render_opus.py::ALBUMS` line;
committed `.mid` + `listening/*.opus`.

**Out:** musical merit (dry, flat, repetitive **by design**); any change to
`crates/ferrosintesis/` — **no version bump**; fixing the 8 source-vs-README disagreements the
audit found (→ `scratchpad.md`); back-compat, migration, feature flags, env vars.

## 3. Approach

### 3.1 Structure — 6 tracks, ~16 min

| # | Track | Content |
|---|-------|---------|
| 1–4 | Melodic audition | GM 0–127 by distinct voice, **each alt-bank twin inlined immediately after its default** |
| 5 | Kit roll-call | every drum key, Brush kit, hi-hat choke, the static pan map |
| 6 | Controllers & effects | a named list of *audible* features (§3.7) |

Tracks 1–4 walk **ascending program order** — a reference is looked *up*.

### 3.2 One slot per distinct voice; aliases are indexed, not re-rendered

Several GM programs share one voice factory **and** (once dry — §3.5) one effect profile, so
their slots would be indistinguishable renders of each other. Auditioning them is not
"completeness", it is duplicate audio.

**Aliases (audition the first, index the rest):** `0-3` (all → `acoustic_piano()`, the program
byte never reaches the model, `voices.rs:6978`); `16|17`; `36|37`; `50|51`; `96/98/100/102`
(one `bell(CRYSTAL)` arm); and — **because the reference is dry** — `88-94` + `97/99/103` (one
generic `pad()`), and `95` + `101`. Dry-ing the reference *merges* the last two sets, since
they differ only in their default chorus/echo sends.

≈ **109 melodic slots**, not 128. A skipped program still appears in the marker index, the
`lyrics/` sidecar and the README as `GM 002 Electric Grand — alias of GM 000 (0:12)`. That is
strictly **more** informative than an unlabelled duplicate, and costs zero seconds of audio.
Programs that share a voice but ship different default sends are listed in the README; T6
demonstrates what those sends do.

### 3.3 The reference phrase

One shape, transposed into each program's register, with duration by **gesture class**:

- `SUSTAIN` — 4-note rising figure, then a **held note** ≥ 2 beats.
- `STRUCK` — same figure, then a **chord**, allowed to ring until the slot's CC120.
- `ONESHOT` (GM 55, 119, 120–127) — a **single hit**; the audible length is the voice's own
  envelope, not the note length.

**Every note is authored `jt=0, jv=0`.** `Score.note()` applies seeded timing *and* velocity
jitter by default (`engine.py:191-200`) — which would silently destroy the suite's entire
premise that an A/B compares *voices*, not *writing*. `check_flat` pins this.

### 3.4 Registers — 16 family rows + ~10 overrides

A per-program register keeps each voice in its natural range (a tuba at C5 tells you nothing)
and keeps the LA sample layer engaged. **One register per GM family (16 rows) plus documented
overrides** — not 128 hand-derived rows mirroring Rust internals, which would rot.

**Honest limits, stated rather than papered over:**
- The voice's own declared `range:` field exists on **only 18 presets** (winds 72–79, reeds
  64–71/109/111). Everything else is a judgement call from body/bore constants.
- The LA layer's cutoff is measured against the **nearest sample-zone root**
  (`sampler.rs:812-815`), *not* the voice's register. So the register table does **not**
  mechanically prove the sample layer stays engaged. For the ~15 LA-layered programs a
  `check_la_band` oracle asserts `0.5 ≤ key_freq(k)/nearest_zone_root ≤ 2.05` directly.

### 3.5 Dry — and it is **not** just CC91

**A program change re-derives a NON-ZERO chorus and echo send** from `fx_profile`
(`engine.rs:400-424`, applied `:1349-1357`) — pads → 0.45 chorus, crystal → (0.30, 0.35),
electric guitars → (0.10, 0.30), leads → (0.15, 0.25) … **~76 of 128 programs**. The echo bus
runs 0.34 feedback, so its tail lands squarely in the next slot's gap.

→ **Author `CC91 = 0`, `CC93 = 0`, `CC94 = 0` AFTER every program change.** (Before it, they
would be wiped.) `check_dry` asserts all three, per slot.

### 3.6 Ring, then choke — and the choke is measured, not assumed

**Nothing else stops a ringing voice.** `CC121` only note-offs *held* notes
(`engine.rs:1468-1471`); `CC123` is a release, not a stop; only **`CC120` All Sound Off**
calls `voice.choke()` and drops the voice (`engine.rs:1386-1398`). Plucked decay clamps at
**14 s** (`voices.rs:2100`); a GM 88 pad measures 0.029 RMS **3 s after note-off**.

**But do not choke the instrument's decay — the decay *is* the instrument.** The alt-bank gong
blooms over 0.3–0.7 s and rings 6–15 s; piano sympathetic resonance is part of the piano, not
contamination. Rev 2 choked every slot immediately, which optimised for the oracle at the
artifact's expense — exactly backwards.

→ **Each slot rings for its natural decay, bounded by a per-gesture `tail` budget (cap 3 s),
and *then* CC120 fires.** You hear the decay; the gap stays clean and bounded (~0.8 s).

**CC120 does NOT flush the bus tanks** (reverb/chorus/echo keep processing under zero input —
`reverb.rs:41`). It does not need to: **because the sends are zeroed, the tanks are never
fed.** Measured on the real binary (`demos/ferrosintesis_reference/probe_cc120.py`),
RMS in dBFS after the choke:

| case | pre-choke | +0.05–0.30 s | +0.30–1.0 s | +1.0–2.0 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| piano, dry (**sympathetic comb**, 0.85 feedback, not CC-gated) | −30.5 | **−96.3** | −96.3 | −96.4 |
| koto, dry (**t60 = 7.0 s**) | −64.5 | **−96.3** | −96.3 | −96.4 |
| lead, **not** dry (echo bus) | −17.6 | −39.3 | −47.6 | **−67.1** |

CC120 + zero sends reaches the **dither floor within 50 ms** — sympathetic comb and 7-second
koto included. The third row is the counterfactual: without `CC93=0`/`CC94=0` the echo rings
**~2 s past the choke**, straight into the next slot. The dry discipline (§3.5) is doing
load-bearing work, and this is the evidence.

**Slot shape:**
```
marker(t, "GM 040 - Violin")
slot_reset(ch, t-0.25)          # see below - NOT engine.reset_controls
program(ch, prog, t-0.20)
cc(91,0); cc(93,0); cc(94,0)    # t-0.15 .. -0.13, AFTER the program change
<gesture notes, jt=0, jv=0>
<ring: natural decay, bounded by the gesture's tail budget, cap 3 s>
cc(120, 0, t+len-gap)           # All Sound Off - choke whatever is left
<gap ~0.8 s>
```

**`slot_reset` is a fork of `engine.reset_controls`, and must be.** The shipped one sends
`CC71 = 0`, which **instantiates the wah filter unconditionally** (`engine.rs:1286-1293`) —
after which `CC74 = 127` merely parks that filter at 12 kHz instead of bypassing it. For a
true raw-voice audition the channel must stay *virgin*: `slot_reset` emits bend 0,
CC64/65/66/67/68/70 = 0, aftertouch 0 and the RPN null — and **omits CC71 and CC74 entirely**.

This also dissolves an outright contradiction in Rev 1, which kept `check_resets` (mandating a
reset that sends CC74 = 127) *and* a trap forbidding CC74 from parking on 127. For T1–T5 the
bypass is **correct** — no filter is exactly what auditioning a raw voice wants. The CC74 rule
is scoped to T6, where 127 is a genuine trap.

### 3.9 Oracles

**Dropped from the showcase's set** (they demand dynamics and stereo a flat, dry reference must
not have): `check_arc`, `check_stereo`, and `analyze.py`'s unconditional arc + mono-loss
checks. `verify.py`/`analyze.py` are per-album copies, so this costs nothing shared.

**`verify.py` (structural):**
| Oracle | Asserts |
|---|---|
| `check_coverage` | **(a)** T1–T4: every distinct voice exactly once, ascending; **(b)** every alt-bank voicing once, with CC0=1 in force; **(c)** T5: every implemented drum key at least once; **(d)** T6: every named feature present. Four assertions, not one sentence. |
| `check_gap` | Every slot ends with a **CC120** before the next slot's first note-on. |
| `check_flat` | Every note lands on the exact authored tick with the exact authored velocity (`jt=0, jv=0`) — the A/B premise. |
| `check_dry` | **CC91=0 AND CC93=0 AND CC94=0**, authored **after** the program change, per slot. |
| `check_written_register` | Every note inside its declared register. Justified as the **sampler-repitch guard** (`sampler.rs` keys off the written key), *not* as a claim about sounding pitch. |
| `check_la_band` | For LA-layered programs: `0.5 ≤ key_freq(k)/nearest_zone_root ≤ 2.05`. |
| `check_structure` | (kept, retuned) duration window, MIDI round-trip. |

**`analyze.py` (audio) — the payoff. Every threshold is a RATIO, never an absolute floor.**

The CLI **peak-normalises the whole render**: `normalize_to_i16` computes `scale = target /
peak` from the loudest sample in the file (`engine.rs:2045`). So an absolute per-slot RMS floor
is **globally coupled** — make GM 30 2 dB louder in some future synth change and every quiet
slot's absolute level shifts, failing oracles that have nothing to do with the change. Rev 2's
"absolute floors with ≥ 6 dB margin" was therefore wrong. Ratios are scale-invariant: they
survive normalisation **and** the cross-machine render nondeterminism the repo has already
recorded (`scratchpad.md`). One new check kind, `rms_rel`.

| Oracle | Asserts |
|---|---|
| **`audible`** | Each slot's **peak**, measured over `[note_on, note_on + audible_len]` (a per-gesture window from data — *not* the whole slot, which dilutes a 0.12 s one-shot by 17 dB), is **≥ −40 dB relative to the render's global peak**. **Proves every voice makes a sound.** Nothing today proves GM 119 renders anything. |
| **`unmasked`** | The 0.25 s window **before each slot's onset** is **≥ 24 dB below that slot's own peak**. Replaces Rev 2's absolute `silent`. This is the property that actually matters — *the previous instrument does not mask the next attack* — it is a ratio (so normalisation cancels), and it **fails when a tail bleeds through**. |

Scoped to T1–T4. T5's drum room (no MIDI off switch) and T6's deliberate overlaps are exempt,
by name.

**Drum coverage is asserted structurally, not acoustically.** An "every drum key made a sound"
oracle would be **vacuous**: unmapped channel-10 keys deliberately emit a generic tick
(`drums.rs:2023`), so *every* key "sounds". `check_coverage(c)` instead asserts the MIDI
contains exactly the **intended** key list, mirrored from the `drums.rs` match arms.

**Deleted: `check_isolation` and the `distinct` oracle.** The first was *vacuous* — a
channel-level MIDI predicate that cannot fail once T1–T4 sit on one channel (which they now
do). The second had no defensible threshold and would have forced an unverifiable
hand-maintained uniqueness map into Python that asserts facts about `voices.rs` internals it
cannot check. `audible` proves every voice sounds; **the human ear is what this tool exists to
serve** — detecting a family collapse is its job, not a fragile spectral oracle's.

### 3.10 What it buys beyond listening

1. **An audio oracle that every voice sounds** — not currently provable; it needs isolation,
   which only this artifact has.
2. **A listening baseline for the render-diff inventory** `CLAUDE.md` already mandates for any
   `voices.rs`/`engine.rs`/`drums.rs`/`sampler.rs` change. `cmp` tells you *that* bytes moved;
   this tells you *what it sounds like*, per instrument, in seconds. The box has no ears —
   Arthur is the ears, and this makes them efficient.

## 5. Acceptance criteria

1. `python build.py --verify` → **all oracles green, exit 0**, from a clean tree.
2. `check_coverage` (a)–(d) pass: every distinct voice, every alt-bank voicing, every drum key,
   every named T6 feature.
3. `check_dry`, `check_gap`, `check_flat` pass on T1–T4.
4. **`python analyze.py` green: every slot `audible`, every slot `unmasked`.**
   *This is the criterion that proves the suite does its job.*
5. **The `.mid` rebuild is byte-identical** (fixed seeds). The **render is not reproducible** —
   neither across machines (`scratchpad.md`) nor under peak-normalisation (`engine.rs:2045`).
   So every audio threshold is a **ratio**, and **no hashes are committed**.
6. `python render_opus.py --album "…"` produces the committed `listening/**.opus`.
7. `cargo` untouched; no crate version bump.

*Not an acceptance criterion:* a human listening spot-check. It cannot gate a build ("this box
has no ears"). It is a **post-merge sign-off note** for Arthur; `analyze.py` is the gate.

## 9. The cheap alternative — Arthur's call, not mine

Codex's strongest strategic point deserves a straight answer rather than a defence.

**Its proposal:** don't build this. Render `--solo` stems of the *existing* showcase, ship a
timestamped cue sheet, done in an afternoon.

**Why I think it does not work** (but you should know the option exists):
1. **`--solo` is a *channel* mask, and the showcase multiplexes eight programs onto one
   channel.** `--solo 0` on track 1 gives you all eight guitars in sequence over a bass sweep —
   not one instrument. The isolation you want is not available at channel granularity.
2. It cannot reach what isn't there: **12 programs** (incl. 8 individually modelled voices and
   GM 119, a whole voice family), **43 of 48 drum voices**, and **15/16ths of the alt bank**.
3. No fixed reference phrase ⇒ **no A/B**. Comparing GM 26 and GM 27 would compare *writing*,
   not voices.
4. It yields **no oracle**. Nothing would prove every program sounds.

Codex concedes point 2 itself ("not a literal 'all instruments' answer"). But it is a real
option, it is much cheaper, and if "90% of the listening value for 10% of the work" is the
trade you want, say so and I will build that instead.
