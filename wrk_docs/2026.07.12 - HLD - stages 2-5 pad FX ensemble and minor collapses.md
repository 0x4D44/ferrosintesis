# HLD — Stages 2–5: Pad, FX, Ensemble & the minor collapses

**Status:** proposed, awaiting sign-off. Supersedes §7.2–7.5 of
`wrk_docs/2026.07.11 - HLD - woodwind and synthwide LA synthesis comprehensive design.md`,
which sketched these four stages in one paragraph each. Everything else in that HLD stands.

**Task mode:** design-only. No shipped code changes here; no version bump.

---

## 0. Why this document exists

Stage 1 (pipe family, GM 72–79) shipped and worked. It worked for one reason, and it is worth
naming precisely, because it is the thing the remaining stages were missing:

> **§7.1.2 gave the family a physical identity axis.** A stopped pipe zeroes its even harmonics.
> A Helmholtz resonator collapses to a single partial. Those two sentences are what turned eight
> programs into eight *instruments* rather than eight flutes with the tone control moved.

§7.2–7.5 name a model and a sample tier for each remaining family. They never say what makes the
eight members *different*. That is the entire job, and without it the predictable outcome is eight
pads distinguished by cutoff frequency — which would pass a spectral test and fail a listener.

This document supplies the missing identity axes, and one load-bearing fact the original HLD did
not have.

## 1. The load-bearing discovery: usage is wildly skewed, and distinctness is pairwise

A program-change census over the **99 committed album MIDIs** (plus 9 demo MIDIs, counted
separately because demos ship no `listening/*.opus` and so cost nothing to re-render):

| GM | program | album files | GM | program | album files |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| **0** | acoustic grand | **71** | **48** | string ensemble 1 | **76** |
| 1 | bright acoustic | 14 | **49** | string ensemble 2 | **23** |
| 2 | electric grand | **0** | 50 | synth strings 1 | **0** |
| 3 | honky-tonk | 3 | 51 | synth strings 2 | **0** |
| **16** | drawbar organ | **12** | 88 | new age pad | **0** |
| 17 | percussive organ | **0** | **89** | warm pad | **19** |
| **29** | overdriven gtr | **17** | 90 / 91 | polysynth / choir | 1 / 2 |
| **30** | distortion gtr | **19** | 92 / 93 / 94 | bowed / metallic / halo | **0** |
| 36 / 37 | slap bass 1 / 2 | **0** | 95 | sweep pad | 8 |
| 86 | fifths lead | **0** | **98** | crystal | **7** |
| 87 | bass+lead | 2 | 96, 97, 99–103 | the other FX | **0** |

Two facts combine into the central design rule.

**Fact A — the distribution is brutally skewed.** A handful of programs (0, 48, 89, 49, 30, 29,
16, 98) carry almost all committed music. Eighteen of the programs we must fix appear in **no
album at all**: 2, 17, 36, 37, 50, 51, 86, 88, 92, 93, 94, 96, 97, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103.

**Fact B — distinctness is a *pairwise* property.** To make a colliding pair distinct you only
have to move **one** of them.

> ### The rule: freeze the anchor, differentiate the rare.
> In every collapsed cluster, the most-used member is frozen bit-identical and the rare members
> are given the identities they were always supposed to have.

This is not a compromise. It is the correct answer twice over: it is *free*, and it is *right* —
GM 50 "Synth Strings 1" being a byte-identical clone of the acoustic GM 48 is precisely the bug,
and giving it a genuinely synthetic character fixes it without touching the acoustic strings that
76 albums are built on.

### What that buys

| stage | move | freeze (bit-identical) | **album re-renders** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2 — Pad** 88–95 | 88, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94 | **89** (19), 95 (8) | **~3** |
| **3 — FX** 96–103 | 96, 97, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103 | **98** (7) | **0** |
| **4 — Ensemble** 48–51 | 50, 51 | **48** (76), **49** (23) | **0** |
| **5a — Organ / Bass / Lead 86** | 17, 36, 37, 86 | **16** (12) | **0** |
| **5b — Lead 87** | 87 | — | 2 |
| **5c — Piano** *(deferred, §7)* | 1, 2, 3 | **0** (71) | **~17** |
| **5d — Guitar** *(deferred, §7)* | 29 *or* 30 | the other | **~17** |

**Stages 2, 3, 4, 5a and 5b clear every remaining collapse in the synth except piano and guitar,
for about five album re-renders.** The original HLD's implied plan — redesign each family whole —
would have moved GM 0 and GM 48 and re-rendered essentially the entire catalogue.

### The honest caveat, stated once and loudly

**This is cheap because it is the *distinctness* goal, not the *fidelity* goal.**

Arthur's ask was "bring everything up to a decent level — ideally as close to SoA as we can get."
Freezing the anchors delivers eight distinct pads and four distinct ensembles. It also means
**GM 0 (piano, 71 albums) and GM 48 (strings, 76 albums) — the instruments actually heard most —
do not improve at all.** Raising *those* is a separate and genuinely expensive programme: full
catalogue re-render plus a human listening pass on every album.

Do not let the cheap win masquerade as finishing the job. §7 lists what remains.

## 2. Correction: the Stage-0 anti-clone matrix is sounder than feared

Before designing on top of the matrix, I tested whether it can actually *see* the identities these
stages trade in. Pads, FX and ensembles are separated largely by **motion** — attack swell, filter
sweep, repetition — while every one of the matrix's five features is steady-state spectrum, read
after deliberately discarding the first 50 ms (`testutil.rs`, `feat_one`).

The worry was that the matrix would not merely *miss* a bad Stage 2 but would **drive** one:
if temporal identity is invisible, the cheapest way to turn the numbers green is to add spectral
difference — pushing the design onto the wrong axis.

**Tested, and the worry does not hold.** Prediction: GM 48 vs 49 differ today by attack time alone
(`slow = program == 49`), so a longer probe window should collapse them toward zero if the pass is
a window artifact.

| pair | SECS = 0.7 (shipped) | SECS = 3.0 |
|---|---|---|
| 48 vs 49 — *attack-time difference only* | 0.1622 | **0.1565** |
| 88 vs 95 — base pad vs sweep pad | 0.1756 | **0.1617** |

Stable under a 4× window change, and ~3.5× the margin. **A slow attack and a filter sweep both
perturb the window-averaged spectral statistics robustly.** The matrix detects motion indirectly
but reliably. It does not need rebuilding, and stages 2–5 can be gated on it as written.

### Two real holes remain (Stage 0.5 — small, optional, do it first)

1. **Release-blind.** The matrix's `render()` never sends a note-off. Decay and release tails are
   *never measured*. Organ 17's percussive character and the metallic pad's ring live partly there.
2. **Direction-blind** *(reasoned, not measured)*. A mean centroid cannot distinguish a rising
   sweep from a falling one. Two mirror-image pads could score as clones. Stage 2 deliberately
   builds both directions (§3, `fenv`), so this hole is one we are about to walk into.

**Proposed fix — cheap and provably safe.** Send a note-off and extend the render; add two temporal
features (attack time to 90 % of peak; signed centroid slope across the body).

**Why it cannot break anything:** `feat_dist` is a **max** over features. Adding features can only
*increase* pairwise distances. Byte-identical renders score 0 on every feature, old and new — so
genuine collapses stay at exactly 0.0000 and the allowlist stays valid, while good families gain
headroom. The change is monotone-safe by construction, not by luck. Verify by running the suite.

## 3. Stage 2 — Synth Pad 88–95

**Collapse today:** 88–94 are all **0.0000** to each other (21 pairs). Seven programs, one sound.
95 is the only distinct member.

**Freeze:** 89 (19 albums) and 95 (8 albums) — reproduce today's `pad()` bit-for-bit.
**Move:** 88, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94 (0, 1, 2, 0, 0, 0 album files).

### 3.1 The one DSP addition: a filter envelope (`fenv`)

`SawStack`'s only cutoff modulator is a **free-running, random-start-phase LFO**. That is one motion
shape: a periodic wobble. Every pad identity that matters is a **one-shot, note-synchronous,
monotone** cutoff trajectory — a bright front decaying into a warm bed, or a swell opening into the
sound. No combination of ADSR, detune, wave, LFO rate or Q produces it. **The amplitude envelope's
decay makes a note quieter, not darker; that is a different sound.**

A one-shot multiplier on cutoff, relaxing exponentially from `fenv.0` to `1.0` over t60 `fenv.1`,
composed multiplicatively with the existing sweep:

```rust
if self.fenv_k > 0.0 {
    self.fenv = 1.0 + (self.fenv - 1.0) * self.fenv_k;   // works UP and DOWN
}
```

`fenv.0 > 1` is a bright onset falling into a warm bed (88 new age, 90 polysynth, 93 metallic).
`fenv.0 < 1` is a slow swell opening (92 bowed). **One mechanism, both directions, four identities**
— the same "identity lives in the DATA" move that made `WindPreset` work.

**Byte-identity is preserved by construction:** `fenv_k == 0.0` leaves `fenv` at exactly `1.0`;
`cut * 1.0` is exact in f32; the guard means voices with no sweep never enter the branch. It draws
nothing from the RNG, so layer draw order is untouched.

> ⚠ **Build the coefficient at `sr / CTRL as f32`, not `sr`.** `control_tick` runs once per `CTRL`
> samples. This is exactly MM-BUG-KILN-00003 — the flute's labelled 5 Hz vibrato running at
> 0.31 Hz. It is the second time this trap has appeared in this codebase; see the deferred
> `control_lfo()` helper in `scratchpad.md`.

### 3.2 The eight identities

| GM | identity axis | axis type | realisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 88 new age | A **struck** pad — a bright resonant front decaying into a soft dark bed. The only pad with an onset *event*. | **TEMPORAL** | `fenv (5.0, 0.45)` over a dark 900 Hz base; attack 0.25 s; no sweep — the fenv *is* the motion. **Unreachable without it.** |
| **89 warm** 🔒 | The reference pad. Its identity is **negative space** — no signature gesture, which is exactly why 19 albums reach for it. | — | **Frozen verbatim.** |
| 90 polysynth | The pad that **plays** — fast enough for chord rhythm, wide audible detune beating. A rhythm instrument, not a bed. | TEMPORAL | attack 0.08 s (vs 89's 0.6), release 0.5 s (chords must clear), 7 layers, detune 0.020, light `fenv (2.2, 0.30)`. |
| 91 choir | **Formants** — fixed vowel resonances no other pad has. A sung "aah", not a filtered saw. | **SPECTRAL** | `StackFilter::Formant` **by default**. Today 91 only *pre-arms* a vowel and stays on the plain LP path unless the channel authors CC70 — an unauthored channel gets no vowel at all. That is the hole. Formant values lifted from `ChoirV2`'s program-52 "aah". |
| 92 bowed | **No onset at all** — the filter opens *with* the amplitude over ~1.5 s, like a bow taking the string. | **TEMPORAL** | attack 1.6 s, release 2.2 s, and the *inverse* fenv: `(0.30, 1.4)`. `sweep: None` — a wobble would fight the swell. |
| 93 metallic | **Resonance, not harmonics** — the loudest peak is not a harmonic of f0. | SPECTRAL | narrow `Pulse(0.22)`, self-resonant `sweep_q 6.5`, `fenv (4.0, 0.18)` clang, fast shallow sweep. **See the caveat below — this is a compromise.** |
| 94 halo | **Air** — a soft tonal core almost buried in a *swept band of noise*. The only pad where noise is a first-class layer. | SPECTRAL/textural | `breath 0.40` — the family is at 0.0 today and the airiest lead tops out at 0.05, so this is a deliberate order-of-magnitude step, and it *is* the identity. Noise is injected pre-filter, so the sweep shapes it: the shimmer is free. |
| **95 sweep** 🔒 | **The sweep is the instrument** — 1.8 octaves of slow resonant travel. | — | **Frozen verbatim.** |

**Honest caveat on 93, not buried.** `SawStack` layers are saws at ≈f0, so every partial is
harmonic. "Metallic" in the GM sense means *inharmonic* partials, and **no `PadSpec` can conjure
them.** What ships is a high-Q self-resonant *clang* — real, distinct, useful, but not a bell. The
correct fix is a layered `Modal` + `SawStack` voice: a **new voice, not a preset**, and its own req.
This table must not pretend to have solved 93.

### 3.3 `PadSpec` and oracles

Struct shape imitates `WindPreset` (all-`pub`, const-constructible, `#[cfg(test)] name`), with one
deviation I recommend: make the filter an **enum**, not an `Option`. A `formant: Option<…>` that is
`Some` on exactly one of eight rows and silently makes three *other* fields inert is a trap for the
next reader. `enum PadFilt { Lp { cutoff, q, sweep_q }, Formant { … } }` makes the mutual exclusion
structural and lets the compiler carry it.

Oracles (all values gut-feel until the first measured run — **the durable content is the axes and
the oracles, not the digits**):

- **PD-O1 — the frozen pair is bit-identical.** Golden-hash `pad(89)` and `pad(95)`. **Land the
  table freeze-first and get this green *before* adding the fenv.** 27 albums ride on it.
- **PD-O2 — pairwise distinctness.** Per program: onset centroid, sustain centroid, time-to-90 %-RMS.
  Every pair must differ on at least one. This *is* the "eight pads with the tone control moved"
  failure, made machine-checkable.
- **PD-O3 — TEMPORAL: the fenv moves the *spectrum*, not the *level*.** For 88/90/93 (falling):
  `centroid(onset) > 1.6 × centroid(sustain)` **while** `rms(onset) < 1.2 × rms(sustain)` — proving
  the darkening is spectral, not the note merely getting quieter. For 92 (rising): the inverse.
  For the inert four: no gesture. **This is the oracle a preset-only design cannot pass**, and the
  one that justifies the new capability.
- **PD-O4 — TEMPORAL: sweep travel**, over a 30 s note (the sweep's start phase is a random draw, so
  the window is long rather than the threshold loose).
- **PD-O5 — 91 has formants and nobody else does.**
- **PD-O6 — level parity and no clipping at vel 127.** A Q-6.5 lowpass gives ≈ +16 dB at resonance;
  93's trim is a guess and this is the gate that measures it. **If it cannot be tamed, drop Q to
  ~3.5 and accept a less metallic 93 rather than shipping a clipper.**

**Expected render-diff:** exactly `gpt5-6/Bright Matter` 03/04/05 and the synth-feature-showcase
demo. **Any other album differing is a bug.**

## 4. Stage 3 — Synth FX 96–103

**Collapse today — three sounds, not the two the original HLD claimed:** a bell cluster
{96, 98, 100, 102}, a pad cluster {97, 99, 103}, and goblins 101 on its own (0.4330 from the bells,
0.1756 from the pads).

**Freeze:** 98 (7 albums). **Move:** everything else — **all seven appear in no album.**
**Album blast radius: zero.**

> *An earlier draft of this section claimed GM 96 was used in ~11 album tracks and treated Stage 3
> as the expensive stage. That was wrong — a byte-scan of all 99 album MIDIs finds GM 96 in none of
> them. The stage is free.*

### 4.1 The anchor, and an honest flag

GM's FX family has **no physics** and is the **least standardised family in the spec** — it gives
only eight names. We anchor on the Roland SC-55/GS reading (*Ice Rain, Soundtrack, Crystal,
Atmosphere, Brightness, Goblins, Echo Drops, Star Theme*): it is what a composer writing "program
98" actually heard, and that box is our house reference (`mdsc55` is a sibling repo).

**Flag:** those descriptions are *recalled, not measured.* If we want the anchor to be a fact rather
than an assumption, auditing against `mdsc55` is a short job worth doing before values are frozen.
The *identities* below would survive being wrong; only the numbers would move, and they are all
table entries.

### 4.2 The eight identities

**These eight are separated by time and randomness, not timbre.** Four could share one oscillator
core and stay unmistakable, because what makes "echoes" *echoes* is that it repeats, and what makes
"rain" *rain* is that it is a sparse stochastic event train. Ship eight sets of steady-state filter
knobs and we will have eight pads — which is the hole the current code already fell into.

| GM | identity axis | axis type |
|---|---|---|
| 96 rain | A pitched glass core with **sparse aperiodic droplets** falling around it. | **STOCHASTIC** |
| 97 soundtrack | A **slow cinematic swell** — dark at the strike, opening over seconds. | **TEMPORAL** (filter opens) |
| **98 crystal** 🔒 | A bright inharmonic glass bell that **beats** against itself. | SPECTRAL — **frozen** |
| 99 atmosphere | A percussive pluck **decaying into** a soft dark wash; filter **closes**. | **TEMPORAL** |
| 100 brightness | The **high end arrives late** — air blooms on top over ~2 s. | **TEMPORAL–spectral** |
| 101 goblins | **Unstable** — lurching random-walk pitch (±35 cents) re-thrown every ~0.4 s. Never settles. | **STOCHASTIC** |
| 102 echoes | Literally **repetition** — a droplet then a decaying train at a fixed ~0.22 s. | **TEMPORAL** |
| 103 sci-fi | A **falling resonant zap** — pitch scoops down, filter sweeps down. | **TEMPORAL** |

Every neighbouring pair is separated on a *different* axis: 97 opens / 99 closes / 100 blooms;
96 is aperiodic / 102 is periodic; 98 is static / 101 never is.

### 4.3 What genuinely cannot be reached with a preset

**Rain.** Every noise source we have is a *continuous* bandpassed stream. Filtered noise is smooth
by construction — the filter's memory sums many inputs, so it sits at kurtosis ≈ 3 regardless of
level, centre or Q. **No knob turns "shhh" into "plink … plink".** Minimal addition: `dsp::GrainGate`
(~15 lines), a PhISEM-style sparse grain train; the silence *between* grains is what makes rain rain.
It knowingly duplicates a private `Grain` in `drums.rs` — unifying them would perturb the drums' RNG
draw order and byte-change every drum-bearing album for zero listener benefit. Twelve lines of
documented duplication is the cheaper trade. *(One improvement over the drums version: express rate
as grains/second, not a raw per-sample probability, which is silently sample-rate dependent.)*

**Echoes must not use the engine's echo bus.** It is global, tempo-derived, and **absent entirely
when `opt.delay_s == 0`**. An identity that evaporates depending on render options is not an
identity. 102's repeats live **inside the voice**.

**Brightness must not use `SawStack`'s sweep.** A random-start-phase sinusoidal LFO cannot produce a
deterministic monotone rise, which is exactly what "the air blooms in over 2 s" means.

### 4.4 Structure

Two cores are genuinely needed (a decaying inharmonic bell is not a detuned saw stack, and faking
either with the other is worse than the collapse we are fixing) — but the temporal machinery, which
*is* the family's identity, must live in **one** place. So: **one `Fx` voice owning all the motion,
wrapping an existing core.**

```
core → + onset Burst → + noise·GrainGate → LP (one-shot sweep) → internal echo → out
```

This buys something important: a preset with no filter, noise, echo, wobble, scoop or onset makes
the wrapper a **pure pass-through**, taken by an `if self.inert` fast path outside the sample loop.
**GM 98 is exactly that preset** — so crystal renders bit-identically to today while still living in
the family table. That is the R1 "move the flagship least" rule, applied structurally.

### 4.5 Oracles

- **FX-O1 — echoes repeat.** `env_autocorr_peak` ≥ 0.45 at 4.55 Hz ± 10 %; 98 must read < 0.20.
  ⚠ **Detrend corner must be 1.0 Hz, not the 15 Hz default** — a 4.55 Hz AM sits *below* a 15 Hz
  highpass and would be erased. (`lessons_learnt.md` records this exact trap for the bow tremolo.)
- **FX-O2 — rain is granular *and* aperiodic.** Kurtosis ≥ 1.5× a continuous bed (differential —
  bandpassed noise is already leptokurtic), **plus an anti-cheat**: autocorrelation peak < 0.25, which
  forbids implementing rain as a *periodic* gate.
- **FX-O3 — brightness blooms late.** HF band peaks ≥ 0.8 s after the LF band; a bell reads ≤ 0.2 s.
- **FX-O4 — goblins are unstable.** Pitch std-dev ≥ 15 cents across windows (vs ≤ 5 for a stable pad),
  and the amplitude motion is a *wander*, not a tremolo (high variance, low periodicity).
- **FX-O5 — crystal is preserved**: `make(98)` is bit-identical to today. The strongest guard here.
- **FX-O6 — the echo tail may finish**: `alive()` must be `inner.alive() || echo_tail > 0`, or 102 is
  truncated with a click.

## 5. Stage 4 — Ensemble 48–51

**Collapse today:** {48, 50, 51} are byte-identical; 49 is that sound with a slow attack.
So the family is 2 sounds wearing 4 hats.

**Freeze:** 48 (76 albums) and 49 (23 albums). **Move:** 50 and 51 — **both used in no album.**
**Album blast radius: zero.**

The GM names tell us what they should be, and it is exactly what makes this free:

| GM | identity axis | axis type |
|---|---|---|
| **48** 🔒 | The reference orchestral section: many players, normal bow attack, independent intonation drift. | — |
| **49** 🔒 | The same section **bowed slowly** — a swell, not a stroke. Already correct in the model (attack 0.45 vs 0.07). | TEMPORAL |
| 50 synth strings 1 | **Not a section at all** — a *string machine* (Solina/divide-down). Its ensemble comes from **one shared BBD chorus LFO**, not many independent players. It has no bow. | **STRUCTURAL** — correlated modulation replaces independent modulation |
| 51 synth strings 2 | The lush/slow variant of the same machine — wider, slower, darker. | TEMPORAL + spectral |

`drift: 0.0` and `vib: (0,0,0)` for 50/51 (a divide-down organ's oscillators do *not* drift
independently — that is the whole point), plus a single chorus LFO with per-layer phase offsets.
And `la: None`: tier D, synth by identity, **no sample layer ever** — which deletes the
`48..=49` / `50 | 51` special-case in `make()`.

**Correction to the Stage-0 allowlist.** `(50, 51)` is currently marked `Why::Legit` — "acceptably
identical". That is a mislabel of mine: Synth Strings 1 and 2 are distinct GM instruments, both are
free to move, and separating them costs nothing. It becomes `Collapse(4)` and Stage 4 must clear it.

### Oracles

- **EN-O1 — 50/51 are synth by identity**: bit-identical with samples on and off.
- **EN-O2 — their ensemble is *correlated***: one LFO ⇒ the amplitude beats at a *fixed* rate.
  `env_autocorr_peak` ≥ 0.40 at 0.75 ± 0.20 Hz for 50; ≤ 0.25 for 48, whose independent per-player
  drift is aperiodic. **Risk flagged:** a multi-phase LFO can partially self-cancel in the layer sum.
  If this reads weak, keep EN-O1 as the hard gate and demote EN-O2 to a differential — **do not tune
  the music to fit the test.**
- **EN-O3 — 48 and 49 are bit-identical to today.** 99 albums ride on this.

## 6. Stage 5a/5b — the free minors

**Freeze:** 16 (12 albums). **Move:** 17, 36, 37, 86 — **all used in no album** — plus 87 (2 albums).

| GM | identity axis | axis type | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **16** 🔒 | The reference Hammond registration. | — | **Frozen.** |
| 17 percussive organ | The **Percussion tab**: a pitched harmonic tap (3rd harmonic) fires at key-strike and **decays away** while the drawbar sustain holds — and the tab cancels the 1' drawbar. | **TEMPORAL + STRUCTURAL** | `Organ::with_percussion(ratio, amp, t60)`. |
| 36 slap bass 1 | The **thumb slap** — low, blunt, the wound string clanking the frets; the fundamental carries. | TEMPORAL + spectral | darker, more sub, more stop-thump. |
| 37 slap bass 2 | The **pop** — the finger pulls the string off the board near the bridge; bright HF snap, thin, dies fast. | TEMPORAL + SPECTRAL | bridge-ward pluck position, bright, short t60. |
| 86 fifths lead | A parallel **perfect fifth** on every note — a *second oscillator group*, not a timbre. | **STRUCTURAL** | `LeadSpec.interval: Option<(ratio, n, detune, amp)>`; ET fifth 1.498307. |
| 87 bass+lead | A **sub-oscillator an octave down**. | **STRUCTURAL** | `interval: Some((0.5, 1, 0.0, 0.9))`; layer dropped below 25 Hz. |

**86/87 belong in the voice layer, not the engine.** A real "fifths" patch is *one voice* whose
second oscillator is tuned +7 semitones — one filter, one envelope, one release. Implement it as a
layer and pitch-bend, legato and voice-stealing all work for free and for the right reasons.
Implement it in the engine (spawn a second voice a fifth up) and you double polyphony, double CC
routing, break the one-voice-per-note invariant that stealing depends on, and give the two pitches
independent envelopes — which is not what the patch is.

**On 87:** GM's literal "Bass + Lead" is a *keyboard split*. Rejecting that — the SC-55 reading (and
every soundfont) is a lead with a sub-octave. Say so in the code comment so the next reader does not
"fix" it.

Oracles: organ 17's 3rd harmonic decays ≥ 4× relative to its own fundamental while 16's stays flat
(normalising by the fundamental stops a global envelope faking it); lead 86 puts real energy at
`f0 × 2^(7/12)` — which sits *between* h1 and h2, so a saw's own spectrum cannot fake it — **and the
fifth tracks pitch bend**, which is what proves the interval lives in the voice; lead 87's sub-octave
guard actually fires below 25 Hz.

## 7. Deferred — the two that actually cost, and one bug

These are **not** in the stages above. Each needs its own go, its own render-diff and its own
listening pass.

**7a — Piano 1/2/3 (~17 albums).** GM 0–3 are all byte-identical. Freezing GM 0 (71 albums) is
obvious. But GM 1 (bright acoustic) is in 14 albums and there is **no free move** — separating
(0, 1) means moving 1. GM 2 is used **nowhere** (free) and GM 3 honky-tonk is 3 albums (cheap; and
its identity is pure **detune**, the cheapest distinctive change available anywhere in this document).

**7b — Guitar 29/30 (~17 albums).** Overdrive vs distortion, 17 and 19 albums. **No free move
exists**; separating them re-renders ~17 tracks whichever we pick. The real axis is *clipper
symmetry* — a tube overdrives **asymmetrically** (even harmonics, "warm"), a pedal clips
near-symmetrically (odd harmonics). Generalising `grit: bool` to `grit: (drive, bias)` reduces
exactly to today's shaper at `(2.0, 0.0)`, so `MUTED` and `SITAR` stay bit-identical. **Aliasing is
the honest risk** — hard-clipping a Karplus-Strong loop *will* alias and a post-clipper cabinet
filter cannot fix it (aliases fold *down* into the passband). If the alias oracle fails, the fix is
to route through the existing ADAA machinery, **not to back the drive off until the test goes green.**

**7c — a suspected bug in GM 49's sample layer (~23 albums to fix).** `LaVoice::wrap` plays the
sampled attack transient at **full gain from t = 0**, and GM 48 and 49 share one `LA_STRINGS`
setting. So "slow strings" opens with a *fast sampled bite* before the slow model swells in
underneath: **the one thing that makes 49 not 48 is destroyed exactly where the ear decides.** The
model is right; the sample layer contradicts it. Needs a per-program LA gain and a fade-in on the
sample layer.

**This is a defect, not an enhancement — it should be raised in `bugs/` regardless of whether we
fix it now.** Verify it before writing the entry; it is inferred from the code, not yet heard.

## 8. Definition of done

Each stage's completion is forced by the Stage-0 matrix, exactly as Stage 1's was: **the stage
deletes its own `Collapse(n)` entries from `distinctness::ALLOW`**, and
`allowlisted_collapses_are_really_clones` fails the moment a stage lands without them. The test
cannot be skipped and the exemption cannot be quietly kept.

Plus, per repo policy, a **render-diff inventory** for every stage: the expected diff set is written
*above* for each stage, and **any album outside it that differs is a bug, not a surprise.** That is
the whole point of freezing the anchors — it makes the expected diff small enough to be a real check
rather than a rubber stamp.

## 9. Risks

1. **The freezes are load-bearing and fragile.** Every "bit-identical" claim above rests on an
   exactness argument (`x * 1.0 == x`, `y + 0.0 == y`, `tanh(0)/k == 0`, an untouched RNG draw
   order). Each is sound, and each is *a claim the render-diff inventory will check*. If an album
   outside the expected set diffs, the argument is wrong — **investigate, do not re-baseline.**
2. **Every number in this document is engineering gut-feel** unless marked as frozen or cited. The
   durable content is the **identity axes** and the **oracles**; the digits will move on the first
   measured run. Do not treat the tables as measurements.
3. **93 metallic is a compromise** (§3.2) and **rain scales with polyphony** (each voice carries its
   own droplets, so a six-note chord is six times as rainy — the *ratio* to the core is preserved so
   it cannot swamp the mix, but it is a real artifact). Both are accepted and flagged, not hidden.
4. **The FX anchor is recalled, not measured** (§4.1).
5. **We have not verified the engine's voice budget.** Stage 3's echo tails and long releases hold
   voices alive longer than today's FX voices. Confirm before implementing.
