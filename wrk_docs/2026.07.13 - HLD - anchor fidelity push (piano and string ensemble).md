## A. Executive summary

**Recommendation for both anchors: better additive/modal DSP + parameterisation of the existing engine + a narrowly-scoped sample refresh — NOT a physical (waveguide/FDTD) model, and NOT gigabyte multisampling.** The bones of the state of the art are already in the codebase. Both anchors are **under-parameterised, not architecturally absent**, and their fidelity gaps sit almost entirely in regions the current models already expose: the piano's post-attack tail and its pedal behaviour, and the strings' post-attack sustain.

**What "SoA" concretely means here:**

- **Piano (GM 0–3) = "modal-plus."** Keep the closed-form modal bank; make its inharmonicity pitch-dependent and make the *already-built* sympathetic bus respond to the sustain pedal. The technical fact: after the hammer leaves the string the system is linear and freely decaying, and a sum of decaying inharmonic partials is its closed-form solution — which is exactly what `acoustic_piano` computes (`voices.rs:438-475`). A time-domain physical string would converge to the very tail the additive bank already produces, at far higher cost, inheriting the tuning/stability tax this repo has already been bitten by (a waveguide string that detuned progressively flat, `lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.11, lines 201-208). The one genuinely non-linear phase — hammer-felt contact — is already carried by a real recorded sample for the first 0.18–0.85 s (`LA_PIANO = (0.42,(0.18,0.85))`, `voices.rs:5453`).
- **Strings (GM 48–51) = "better ensemble."** Keep the subtractive `SawStack` (`voices.rs:3893-3917`); couple dynamics to timbre and add a friction bed and body colour. A section's identity is an emergent statistical texture of many uncorrelated players, synthesisable from a bare note-on, on axes orthogonal to the continuous bow control a GM file cannot send. A per-player bowed **waveguide** section is the wrong cost/benefit: the repo already has a working `BowedString` (`voices.rs:5182`, used for GM 42/43 cello/bass), but twelve phase-locked physically-perfect violins sound like one loud violin, not a section.

**The leverage asymmetry between the two anchors must drive effort allocation:**
- **Piano is sample-carried** to ~0.85 s of every note (`README.md`; `LA_PIANO` fade end 0.85 s, `voices.rs:5453`). Sampling is the **highest-ceiling** lever for piano — but it is licence-bound (see §D) and eats the byte budget fast. The cheap, licence-free piano wins are the two model/engine parameterisations above.
- **Strings are model-carried** past the sample crossfade (`LA_STRINGS` fade end 0.40 s, `voices.rs:5465`). Sampling is one of the **lowest-value** levers for strings (the best CC0 section source is already in use); all the headroom is model-side.

**One correctness item falls out regardless of the larger push, but its severity is narrower than "confirmed defect."** GM 48 and 49 share one `LA_STRINGS` constant (`voices.rs:7146` for the 48/49 dispatch), so slow-strings GM 49 gets the **same sampled onset envelope** as fast-strings GM 48: the sample plays at full gain for the first 0.10 s then crossfades out by 0.40 s (`sampler.rs:844-857`; `ag = 1.0` while `t < fade_start` because `smooth()` clamps its negative argument to 0, `sampler.rs:776-778`). That contradicts GM 49's distinct **slow model swell** (0.45 s attack, `voices.rs:3905`). The *mechanism* is confirmed in code; whether the mismatch is **audible** as an early "bite" over the swell is **unverified by ear** — and the crossfade was in fact deliberately tuned so a section "comes into focus, it does not bite" (comment `voices.rs:5462-5465`, that tuning targeting the GM-48 case). Route it to `bugs/` for verify-by-ear, as `2026.07.12 - HLD…` §7c already does; if confirmed, it becomes the smallest, lowest-risk first stage (§F).

**The gating fact, stated once.** Measured over the 99 committed album MIDIs (mido re-count during the blast-radius audit, matching the `2026.07.12 - HLD…` §1 census): **GM 48 sounds in 76 of 99**, **GM 0 in ~70** (program-change present in 71), **GM 49 in 23**, and **GM 50/51 in 0**. The union of albums carrying *either* anchor is **≈84 of 99**. Every change here is default-on under the repo's synth-change policy, so each obliges a **catalogue-wide re-render + human listening pass in the same task**. This is the expensive programme; start it deliberately, staged, smallest audibly-better increment first, with a human A/B panel *before* each full sweep (§F).

## C. The concrete design — mechanism, files, NEW DSP vs parameterisation

Design rule throughout, per the repo's incremental doctrine: **improve what exists; do not fork DSP speculatively.** The design is tiered by certainty and cost.

- **Tier 1 (definite, cheapest, highest certainty):** S1, P2 — pure parameterisations of existing DSP, both testable model-side, each fixing a confirmed fixed/dead behaviour.
- **Tier 2 (strong, risk-flagged, panel-gated, each revertible):** P1, S2.
- **Tier 3 (deferred / measure-first / gated — the "on-ramp" register, NOT built now):** S3, S4, S6, P3, P4, the soundboard IR, the release-sample layer, the piano sampling track, and the two variants held back below.

**The strings design deliberately does NOT touch** the `SawStack` architecture, the chorus/reverb buses, the LA sample layer for 48/49, or the existing drift decorrelation. **No per-player waveguide section** (§C.3).

### C.3 Rejected forks and deferred on-ramps (kept in the design on purpose — the "on-ramp to nowhere" rule)

- **Full time-domain physical piano** (felt-hammer contact + FDTD/waveguide): the post-strike tail is linear and the modal bank is its closed form; a simulator buys nothing on the tail and inherits the tuning/stability tax already demonstrated here (`lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.11, lines 201-208). The non-linear attack it would model is already a real sample.
- **Per-player bowed-waveguide string section** (12× `BowedString`, `voices.rs:5182`): a section's realism is statistical decorrelation, not per-string accuracy; the `SawStack`'s decorrelated layers already deliver the statistics at a fraction of the cost.
- **Held-back variants** that a reviewer may try to talk back in — keep them out: the **soundboard IR** (PN-D3, R1), the **pitch-tracked sympathetic comb** (P1's expensive form), the **CC11→cutoff** strings variant, and the **release-sample layer** (a whole release-triggered voice path for a staccato-only gain — out of scope).

## D. Sampling plan — collapsed to a decision gate

The two anchors deserve **opposite verdicts**, and the sampling plan is deliberately reduced to a gate: **do not design the multisample spreadsheet before confirming a licence-clean source exists.**

### D.1 Piano — high-ceiling but licence-bound

Because the sample carries 0–0.85 s of every note, a better piano sample improves the *actual tone* of GM 0 across ~70 albums. Ranked value: grand-vs-upright body (PN-D6) > more velocity layers (PN-D5) > register coverage (PN-D7) > finer zone spacing.

**The sourcing ceiling, stated up front (all `[verify]`):**
- **Salamander Grand** (Yamaha C5) — the obvious gold-standard free grand — is **CC-BY 3.0, not CC0** *(sampler read "fairly certain"; **verify licence**)*, and CC-BY is **blocked** for embedded PCM by the repo's rule (parent HLD §6.4). The single best free grand is unusable here.
- **VCSL** (CC0) is the trusted-provenance path but its piano identity and velocity depth are **unverified** — it may be another upright/tack piano.
- **FreePats** piano sets are **mixed-licence** — each must be licence-checked individually.
- **Iowa MIS** is "free to use" ≠ CC0 — treat as **blocked** until a real PD dedication is confirmed.

**The decision gate (replaces all layer/zone/byte arithmetic):** *IF a licence-clean grand-or-upright of clearly-better identity and velocity depth is found, THEN scope a sample refresh as its own sourcing-gated stage and size the byte cost against a source in hand. ELSE make no sampling promise.* Do not promise a grand the CC0 pool may not contain. This is why the cheap, licence-free model wins (S1, P2, then P1) ship first and the sample swap is a **parallel, gated** track.

### D.2 Strings — low-value

The dominant GM 48 use (sustained beds) is 100% model after 0.40 s; no CC0 onset sample improves it unless strings are re-architected to be sample-carried like piano, which the 0.63 s one-shots (no loop points) can't support and which would trade away the model's bendable, driftable sustain. We already use the best CC0 section source (VSCO 2 CE, per `README.md`). Marginal CC0 headroom for *sections* is small. Narrow real win: more dynamic layers (2 today, `sampler.rs:708-714`) for sharper fast entries.

### D.3 Byte budget

Current bank: **202 WAV, 16.68 MiB** (`CLAUDE.md`); approved growth to ~45–55 MiB (parent HLD §0/§6.4, cited/verified). Piano WAVs are longer (1.8 s vs ~0.63 s), so the piano bank is already the largest single consumer *(**~8.21 MiB** is the sampler read's `du` measurement — **not independently re-verified** `[gut-feel]`)*. **Directional conclusion only:** deepening piano is byte-expensive, deepening strings is nearly free — the budget points where the audible value points (piano, not strings). No file-count arithmetic until a source is in hand (§D.1 gate).

### D.4 What a sample categorically cannot fix (both anchors)

Sustain shape and decay tail; **pedal interaction** (runtime state, not a recording); **sympathetic resonance** (an engine bus, `engine.rs:549`); release behaviour (`note_off` fast-decays the transient, `sampler.rs:863-867`). These are model/engine problems — a recording of an onset is silent about them.

## E. Oracles

Render at matched pitch / worst-case register (`lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.08, lines 24-32). ## F. Blast radius and staging

**Say it plainly: this re-renders essentially the whole catalogue.** Measured (mido over the 99 committed MIDIs, matching `2026.07.12 - HLD…` §1): **GM 48 in 76**, **GM 0 in ~70** (program-change 71), **GM 49 in 23**, **GM 50/51 in 0**; the union carrying either anchor is **≈84 of 99**. GM 0–3 all route through `acoustic_piano`; GM 48–51 all route through `strings()`. Every change is default-on, so each stage owes: the **render-diff inventory** (baseline binary in a throwaway `git worktree add <path> HEAD`, render every `render_opus.py::ALBUMS` MIDI with both binaries, `cmp` — a *report* whose expected-diff set is written per stage; any album outside it is a bug — **investigate, do not re-baseline**), a **pre-sweep A/B panel** (see below), an **opus refresh + human listening pass IN THE SAME TASK**, exactly **one version bump**, and an explicit integration go.

**Two non-negotiable process points folded from the critiques:**

1. **Refresh opus and listen per integrated stage — do not defer.** The repo's Synth-change policy (`CLAUDE.md`: "re-render and re-commit the affected `listening/*.opus` … **in the same task**"), parent HLD §0.1, and sibling §7 ("its own listening pass") all forbid integrating a stage with opus lagging the synth. A stage integrates only after its own opus refresh and listen. **If Arthur wants to conserve his (scarce) listening time, the lever is to reduce the NUMBER of stages by bundling low-risk disjoint-family changes into one build — NOT to integrate multiple stages with a deferred listen.** Machine re-render time is cheap; a shipped-but-unlistened change is the failure the policy exists to prevent.

2. **A human A/B panel comes BEFORE each full sweep, not after.** Oracles confirm the *feature* works (the centroid rises, the bloom appears); they cannot hear that a change makes a *finished mix* worse. GM 48 pads sit under **unchanged** `Bowed`/`BowedString` foreground voices (GM 40/41 `Bowed`, 42/43 `BowedString`, `voices.rs:7104`/`:7123`/`:7139`), so S1's spectral shift re-balances the bed against an unchanged foreground — a concrete regression vector oracles can't see (`lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.06 lines 82-86, 2026.07.09 lines 120-131). So each timbre stage: build both binaries, render a **4–6 album representative panel** (a bright pop-strings album, a soft chamber bed, a GM-48-under-Bowed album, a heavily-pedalled piano album) old-vs-new, **Arthur A/Bs first**; only on approval does the full sweep + commit proceed. This is a transitional validation gate, **not** a per-album freeze — the policy explicitly rejects freezing old albums in worse renderings.

**Determinism precondition — resolved.** The render-diff inventory is a **same-machine, two-binary WAV `cmp`**; the ferrosintesis WAV render is deterministic within one environment. The cross-machine nondeterminism flagged in commit `1dbdeda` is in the **opus encoder downstream of the WAV** (per JRN 2026.07.12), so it does not threaten the WAV diff — run it on one box. Operational rule: refresh the whole catalogue from *one* machine; judge staleness by synth-version/history, not by byte-diffing another machine's opus. *(One latent, non-active caveat: the linear-interp resample `data[j]*(1-frac)+data[j+1]*frac`, `sampler.rs:851`, is a candidate for FMA-contraction divergence across compilers/CPUs — irrelevant to a same-machine diff, worth a one-line note, not a blocker.)*

## G. Risks, definition of done, and the gut-feel register

### Design risks
- **R1 — Soundboard/pedal muddying.** The soundboard IR (PN-D3) is held out of the cheap pass on purpose: a shared-bus filter hitting *every* piano note (maximum blast radius), with space already supplied by the sympathetic bus + global reverb. **P1 itself may under-deliver:** boosting a fixed 12-comb send (`engine.rs:549-553`) is not the same cue as undamped strings ringing across the keyboard. Validate P1 by ear on a pedalled panel; be willing to conclude the cheap version is enough *or* that honest bloom is out of scope — **do not silently escalate to the deferred pitch-tracked-comb version.**
- **R2 — Shared-struct collision (S4, and any future per-layer field).** `SawStack`/`Layer` are shared by strings, pads **and leads** (`voices.rs:3922-3924`). The freeze condition for pads/leads byte-identity is **"consume zero extra RNG draws for non-opted-in families"** — the per-layer RNG draws happen *inside* the constructor loop (`voices.rs:3722`/`:3730`/`:3731`), so a "zero-spread" scatter computed as `base·(1+spread·rng.white())` still consumes a draw and shifts every later draw. Gate any new draw behind a per-family opt-in flag. The contamination canary (ST/PN-O5) catches this **only if it covers a pad/lead program** (`lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.08 lines 33-39). This is why S4 is cut, not merely deferred.
- **R3 — S3 re-rolls the strings voice.** Adding layers changes RNG draw order, so old→new strings renders differ *everywhere*, not subtly; the Stage-diff would be large. Attribution must come from `--solo` string stems — which are **independently peak-normalised**, so un-normalise via the CLI's reported `peak` before comparing levels (`lessons_learnt.md` 2026.07.09 lines 120-131). This is the reason S3 is gated behind a measured A/B, not committed.
- **R4 — The freeze holds only where we keep it.** S1 deliberately *moves* GM 48. Every "byte-identical" claim we *do* make (pedal-up piano for never-pedalled channels, pads/leads under any shared-struct touch, the contamination canary) rests on exactness arguments (`x·1.0 == x`, an untouched RNG order) that the render-diff inventory checks. If an album outside the expected set diffs, **investigate — do not re-baseline.**

### Definition of done (per stage)
1. Oracles green, **calibrated against the OLD code first** and measured on the correct window (§E measurement notes).
2. Render-diff inventory run **on one machine**; the diff set equals the written expected set (else investigate).
3. **Pre-sweep A/B panel approved by Arthur** (timbre stages).
4. `listening/*.opus` refreshed and re-committed **in the same task**; human listening pass passed.
5. Exactly **one** version bump on `crates/ferrosintesis/Cargo.toml`.
6. Explicit integration go (never push unsolicited).

## Critiques considered and rejected (or refined)

- **"S5 is not a proven defect, just an unheard inference; the crossfade was designed so it does not bite" (simplicity).** *Refined, not rejected.* The crossfade `[0.10,0.40]` was tuned against a bite **for the GM-48 section-swell case** (`voices.rs:5462-5465`). The GM-49 item is different: 49 inherits 48's *fast* onset envelope despite its own *slow* 0.45 s model attack (`voices.rs:3905`), because 48/49 share one `LA_STRINGS` (`:7146`). That **mechanism is confirmed in code**; only its **audibility** is unverified. So I neither call it "settled correctness, not taste" (simplicity is right to object) nor dismiss it — it goes to `bugs/` for verify-by-ear, and becomes the smallest first stage only if confirmed.
- **"Delete S3 and S2 entirely; the whole win is three parameter edits" (simplicity).** *S3: accepted (demoted to a measure-first gated candidate, not committed) — the "5 saws are thin" premise is unmeasured and the fix is maximal-blast. S2: kept but demoted to Tier 2 with its payoff explicitly bounded* — it is a genuine parameterisation of an existing HIGH-ranked gap (`breath=0`, path exists at `:3815`), and the simplicity critique itself assumes S2 is present ("thin after S1+S2 are already added"), so a clean deletion isn't what it actually argues.
- **"Batch the anchor stages into one big-bang and listen once at the end" (draft's own §F, echoed as a fallback).** *Rejected.* It violates the same-task opus-refresh policy (`CLAUDE.md`, parent §0.1, sibling §7). The correct efficiency lever is fewer *stages* (bundling disjoint-family Tier-1 changes), not deferred *listens* — §F point 1.
- **"ST-D1 touches every dynamic gesture on 76 albums" (draft framing).** *Rejected as overstated.* The rendered attack (0–0.40 s) already carries velocity brightness via the sample bank split (`sampler.rs:708-714`); the dead region is the post-0.40 s sustain. S1's value is real but scoped to sustained gestures — and ST-O1 must be measured there, not over the whole note.
- **"strings() only serves GM 48/49" (implied by the draft's stage scoping).** *Rejected.* It also serves GM 50/51 (`voices.rs:7161`). This is moot in practice (GM 50/51 are in 0 committed MIDIs and sibling Stage 4 splits them off first), but the expected-diff set must account for them if ordering slips (§F).
