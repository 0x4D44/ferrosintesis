**measurement** I ran on this box (no ears here). Where I could not settle a claim, I say
so and name the measurement that would. I re-derived the numbers in the brief rather than

## 1. Scope

**Goal (one line):** make every ferrosintesis GM voice sound like the instrument it names —
fix the ~24 audition complaints at their real layer, and repair the oracle regime so this
class of defect (a voice that is wrong yet green) cannot recur.

Arthur has **unfrozen all anchors** (decision 2): there is no bit-identity constraint on any
voice. The committed `.opus`/`.wav` are git-ignored build output and the committed `.mid`
album sources are unaffected by a synth change, so we design the *right* voice and let every
album re-render. The one residual constraint is loudness: an album's mix was balanced by ear
against today's sound, so a change that moves a voice's **loudness** a lot is riskier than
one that moves its **timbre**. Each work item below names where that bites.

### Non-goals
- **No new GM program coverage.** SFX 120–127 stay toneless-noise fallbacks; we are not
  modelling helicopters. The orchestral middle is already filled (README).
- **No new third-party dependencies, no new asset crates.** Zero-third-party-code stands.
  We reuse the 202-file attack bank as-is; we do not commission new samples (there is still
  no CC0 brass-section or steel-string source — §2.7, §2.9).
- **No controller semantics change.** An *unauthored* CC stays inert (decision 3). New
  pressure controls (CC2/CC11/aftertouch on reeds/winds) engage only once a channel authors
  them.
- **No feature flags, minimise env vars, no back-compat/rollback machinery** (dev sandbox).
  Where "stay-byte-identical" scaffolding exists purely to preserve a now-unfrozen anchor, we
  **delete** it rather than extend it.
- **No cloud CI.** Gates stay local.

## 2. Approach — the voice fixes

Worked hardest-first. §2.1–2.4 are the confirmed bugs; §2.5–2.13 are voice defects; §2.14 is
the audition rig; §3 is the oracle regime (the multiplier).

### 2.1 B1 — electric pianos emit no pitch (headline)

**Confirmed.** `Modal::new` documents its table as `(freq Hz, amp, T60 s)` (`voices.rs:237`)
and builds `Sine::new(f, sr, …)` from column 0 verbatim (`voices.rs:248`). Every *other*
Modal multiplies a ratio table by the key frequency before calling — `bell()` at
`voices.rs:580` does `(f * r, …)`, `timpani_partials` at `:700` does `(f * r, …)`. But
`electric_piano_1` (`voices.rs:482`) and `electric_piano_2` (`voices.rs:510`) pass the **bare
ratios** (1.000, 1.003, 2.000, 2.820, …) as Hz. Their modes therefore oscillate at 1–9 Hz —
infrasound, key-independent. The only audible output is the 10 ms noise burst plus the
onset. It hid for its whole life because `f` is still used in the noise bandpass
(`voices.rs:497`), so there is no unused-variable warning. I checked all 11 `Modal::new` call
sites: **only these two** pass ratios; every other site pre-multiplies by `key_freq`.

**Fix (layer: the two constructors).** Multiply each partial's column-0 ratio by
`f = key_freq(key)` before constructing, exactly as `bell()`/`timpani` do. One `.map()` per
constructor:
```
// electric_piano_1 / _2: partials are RATIOS, like every other Modal table
let partials = RAW_TABLE.map(|(r, a, t)| (f * r, a, t));
```
Keep the tables as ratios (they read as a Rhodes/Wurlitzer inharmonic tine series and are
correct *as ratios*). The `scale`/velocity terms already applied to amp/T60 are unaffected.

**Loudness risk: high-ish.** These two voices currently emit almost nothing audible, so any
album that used GM 4/5 has been mixing near-silence. After the fix they will speak at full
level for the first time. This is a *large* loudness move by construction — flag every album
using GM 4/5 for a mix re-check at the render-diff stage. (Grep `render_opus.py::ALBUMS`
sources for program 4/5 before declaring the blast radius.)

### 2.2 B2 — the audition muted the choir (rig bug, not synth)

**Confirmed.** `demos/ferrosintesis_reference/tracks/audition.py:57` includes `70` in the
slot-reset controller loop, sending **CC70 = 0** on every slot. `engine.rs:1319-1324` latches
`s.vowel_authored = true` for *any* CC70 value including 0, so every choir slot spawns on the
closed "mm" vowel — Arthur never heard an "aah." This is correct MIDI (an authored CC70=0
legitimately means "closed vowel"); the bug is that the rig authored it. The same function's
docstring already omits CC71/CC74 for exactly this reason (they instantiate the wah filter) —
CC70 slipped through the same trap.

**Fix (layer: the rig).** Drop `70` from the reset loop in `slot_reset`
(`audition.py:57`), joining 71/74 in the "omit — it authors a controller" set. One-line.
Do **not** change the engine's latch: `vowel_authored` on CC70=0 is correct.

### 2.3 B3 — BowedString vibrato runs 16× slow (MM-BUG-KILN-00004, Open/High)

**Confirmed, and it is the third instance of one recurring idiom bug.** `BowedString::new`
builds its vibrato LFO at the **full** sample rate — `voices.rs:5621`
`vib: Sine::new(vib_rate, sr, u(&mut rng))` — but `render` advances it only inside the
`if self.t.is_multiple_of(CTRL)` gate (`voices.rs:5665-5671`). So the phase increment is
computed for one audio sample but stepped once per 16 → **≈ 4.6/16 ≈ 0.29 Hz**, below the
rate the ear reads as vibrato. GM 42 cello and GM 43 contrabass are the default bank for this
voice (`voices.rs:8056`), appearing in 46 and 42 committed album MIDIs. The repo already has
the exact guard against this — `control_lfo()` (`voices.rs:90`) builds LFOs at `sr / CTRL` —
and used it to fix the same bug in `Wind` (MM-BUG-KILN-00003) and `Bowed`.

**Fix (layer: the constructor + a systemic guard).**
1. Build the vibrato through `control_lfo(vib_rate_base, jitter, &mut rng, sr)`
   (`voices.rs:90`) instead of a raw `Sine::new(_, sr, _)`. `BowedString` hardcodes a single
   ~4.6 Hz base (`voices.rs:5593`) with no per-program term, while the demoted `Bowed`
   presets name 4.8/4.2 Hz for 42/43 (`voices.rs:5271,5281`). **Decide one:** I recommend
   giving `BowedString` per-program rates (cello 4.8, contrabass 4.2) to match the presets
   the oracle already names, so the fixed oracle (§3) has a single source of truth.
2. **Systemic:** this is the fourth place the "LFO at `sr`, ticked at `CTRL`" mistake could
   live. Audit every `Sine::new(_, sr, _)` / `Drift::new` that is `.next()`-ed only inside an
   `is_multiple_of(CTRL)` gate. Grep target: `Sine::new(` where the same field is advanced
   under a CTRL gate. Prefer routing all control-rate LFOs through `control_lfo` so the
   reduced rate is impossible to forget. A `debug_assert` inside `control_lfo`'s callers is
   not worth it; a lint-by-construction (one constructor) is.

**Loudness risk: low** (pitch modulation, near-zero level change). But it is a **default-on
timbre change** to 42/43 → owes a render-diff inventory and an opus refresh of every
cello/contrabass album (already noted in the bug's cost section).

### 2.4 B4 — GM 43 contrabass tuning (with a correction)

**Confirmed, and re-measured — the brief's cents figure is register-dependent.** The
waveguide's loop-latency compensation `loop_comp` is 3.85 for GM 42 but **1.0** for GM 43
(`voices.rs:5573-5582`), with a comment admitting the residual flatness. The physics
(lesson 2026.07.11, `lessons_learnt.md`): the in-loop reflection filter + read/write add
~3.85 samples the bare `sr/f − loop_comp` never subtracts, so the string renders
progressively flat *with pitch*. My model (residual ≈ 3.85 − 1.0 = 2.85 samples), which
reproduces the cello's known "≈ 50 cents flat at A4" to within 0.03 samples, gives:

| note | flat |
|---|---|
| E1 41 Hz (true bass register) | **−4.6 cents** |
| C4 262 Hz (audition main root) | −29 cents |
| G4 392 Hz (audition landing note) | −43 cents |

So the diagnosis's "29–43 cents" is real **but measured at the rigged-high audition register**
(§2.14). At the contrabass's actual register it is ≈ 5 cents — audible to a tuner, marginal
to an ear, and genuinely "characterful in the bass." **This does not mean don't fix it** —
the correct fix is cheap and removes a real defect — but it does mean the *severity* is lower
than the diagnosis implies, and the loudness/pitch move at the real register is tiny.

**Fix (layer: the constructor).** Set GM 43's `loop_comp` from a single-note autocorrelation
sweep, the same method that produced the cello's 3.85 (lesson 2026.07.11 — never assume
`sr/f − 1`; peak-locate AND autocorrelation agreed for the cello, so trust a real measurement,
not a guess). It will land near 3.85. Delete the "stay byte-identical to Stage 1" justification
in the comment — the anchor is unfrozen. **Tripwire:** if the measured `loop_comp` differs
from the cello's by more than ~0.5 samples, the two voices have different in-loop filter phase
(different `refl_sustain`? they share 2600 Hz) — investigate before shipping.

### 2.5 Chromatic percussion modal families (B: music box; NOT celesta)

**GM 10 music box — wrong modal family.** `MUSICBOX = [1.0, 2.81, 5.40]` (`voices.rs:611`) is
the free-free-bar series (a glockenspiel's), differing from `GLOCK` only in decay. A music-box
comb tooth is a **clamped-free cantilever**, whose modal ratios are 1 : 6.267 : 17.55 : 34.39
(Euler–Bernoulli). Fix: retune `MUSICBOX` to the cantilever ratios (keep 3 modes:
`[1.0, 6.27, 17.55]` with amplitudes falling steeply — a music box is nearly a pure tone with
a metallic tick). This makes GM 10 audibly distinct from GM 9 and removes the distinctness-
matrix collapse risk.

**GM 8 celesta — correct as-is; DO NOT change.** `CELESTA = [1.0, 2.76, 5.40]`
(`voices.rs:610`) is the free-free-bar series, which is *right* for a celesta (a struck steel
bar over resonators, like a glockenspiel with felt hammers). The brief's grouping of celesta
with the music box is the one physics slip in the diagnosis. Correcting the music box alone
splits 9/10; celesta was never wrong.

**GM 13 xylophone vs GM 12 marimba.** `XYLOPHONE = [1, 3.0, 6.2]` (`voices.rs:628`) — the 3:1
quint is right for a xylophone. But `MARIMBA = [1, 3.0, 5.2]` (`voices.rs:623`) is *also* ~3:1,
where a marimba bar is deeply arched to **4:1** (the repo's own `VIBES = [1, 4, 9.8]`,
`voices.rs:620`, already encodes the arched-bar series). Fix: retune `MARIMBA` toward
`[1, 4.0, 9.8]` (marimba and vibraphone share the arched-bar tuning; they differ in
resonators/motor, not modal ratios — the motor tremolo `with_amp_trem` already separates
them). **Explicit trade the lead must accept:** the marimba is used in **13 committed tracks**
vs the xylophone's 2 — retuning the marimba to sharpen a 2-track program re-voices 13 tracks.
The physics is unambiguous (a marimba *is* 4:1), and anchors are unfrozen, so I recommend
doing it, but it is the single most-consequential retune in this document by album count.
Flag it at render-diff.

**GM 47 timpani — balance and time, not pitch.** The modal ratios
`[1.0, 1.504, 1.742, 2.0, 2.245]` (`voices.rs:670`) are textbook kettledrum preferential
modes — correct. Two failures: (a) the strike thump is injected at `1.1 * v` through a 300 Hz
lowpass (`voices.rs:715`) — at or above the fundamental's own amplitude (1.0). (Precision, per
review: the thump is a ~45 ms burst (`T60 = 0.045`), so it dominates the *attack/onset*, not the
1 s sustained pitch — but at the strike it buries the note's pitched information, which is what
"just low thumps" is.) (b) the partial T60s are 1.0/0.85/0.70/0.60/0.50 s where a real kettle
rings 3–8 s (`voices.rs:670-675`). Fix: drop the thump to ~`0.5 * v`, and lengthen the partial
T60s ~3× (e.g. `[3.0, 2.4, 1.9, 1.5, 1.1]`). `TIMPANI_RELEASE_T60 = 6.0` (the note-off release)
is already long — the bug is the *sounding* T60s are short. **Loudness risk: moderate** —
cutting the thump reduces sub-bass energy on timpani-heavy tracks; lengthening the ring is a
safer timbre move. Flag at render-diff.

### 2.6 The drive/sag inversion (GM 29/30 overdrive/distortion)

**Confirmed backwards by construction.** The string voice (`DRIVE`, `voices.rs:1455`) has
`grit: false` — all distortion is the engine `Drive` insert (`engine.rs:259-385`). Its sag
gain law is `target = (sag_target / env).max(1.0)` (`engine.rs:344`): at the pick (env
largest) `target → 1` (no boost); as the note decays (env small) `target` rises and `g_sag`
slews **up to ~4×** over ~150 ms (`slew_up`, `engine.rs:312`). So the effective drive
*quadruples 150 ms into the note* — Arthur's "the distortion only kicks in after a bit,"
described mechanistically. A real amp clips **hardest at the pick** (loudest signal into a
fixed nonlinearity), and power-supply sag *reduces* headroom under load, never boosts it.

**Design — get the two real phenomena right, then take the smallest version.**
- **Clipping** (most distortion at the loudest instant) is *already* delivered by the static
  two-stage tanh (`engine.rs:353-355`): at the pick the signal is largest, so
  `tanh(v·g1)` is most saturated. This is correct and needs no envelope.
- **Sag** (supply droop under sustained load) is a mild *compression* — gain falls slightly
  when the signal is loud, recovers toward unity as it falls — plus a gentle bloom. It is
  **≤ 1**, never a 4× boost.

**Fix (layer: the `Drive` insert) — ONE design, chosen (review-revised).** The original draft
offered three shapes (pure-delete / keep-sub-unity-compression / delete-all-machinery); both
reviews flagged that as internally contradictory and asked for a single call. **Decision: delete
the entire `g_sag` path.** Remove `g_sag`/`sag_target`/`g_max`/`slew_up`/the `idle`-snap machinery
(`engine.rs` `Drive` fields + the sag block around `:340-350`); the static two-stage cascade
(`engine.rs:353-355`) already clips hardest at the pick, which is the whole point. This is the
simplest correct version and a real hot-path simplification. Do **not** keep a "small correctly-
signed compression" as a hedge — a sub-unity pre-shaper gain at the pick would *reduce* the pick
distortion we are trying to preserve (external review), so it fights the objective. If a
physically-timed supply-sag stage is ever wanted, design it deliberately later, gated on a listen.

**Loudness risk: high — verify held-note decay, fix at the STRING layer.** The 4× late boost was
propping up the *sustain* of held distorted notes, so removing it makes held notes decay faster and
quieter → a real mix move on distorted-guitar albums. The correct layer for sustain is the KS e-bow
sustainer already present (`DRIVE.sustain = 0.35`, `DRIVE_LEAD.sustain = 0.6`). After deleting the
sag, level-match the static drive and measure held-note RMS on the distorted-guitar albums at
render-diff; if it collapses, **deepen `DRIVE.sustain`/`DRIVE_LEAD.sustain`** (or its engage
threshold), never restore the inverted sag.

**Oracle coupling (review):** the deletion breaks a characterisation test, `drive_sag_compression`
(`engine.rs`, ~`:2768`), whose legs assert the sag *holds the decaying tail up ≥6 dB* and read the
now-deleted `g_sag`/`env` fields — so it fails-to-compile / fails-red under this change. It is
listed in §3.2 for deletion and replacement by the O-ATTACK "no late bloom above the attack"
property, which pins the *correct* behaviour instead.

### 2.7 The sample/model onset-ownership contract (`sampler.rs:836-861`)

One `LaVoice` crossfade serves 17 programs. It fades the model *in* over `[0, fade_end]` via
`smooth(t/fade_end)` while fading the sample *out* over `[fade_start, fade_end]` via
`1 − smooth((t−fade_start)/fade_len)` (`sampler.rs:846-853`). Four failures trace to it:

1. **GM 61 wrong instrument.** `brass_bank` (`sampler.rs:633`) has explicit arms to GM 60,
   then `_ => trumpet_*()` — GM 61 (section) falls through to a **solo trumpet** attack
   (`sampler.rs:664-670`). Underneath, `BR_SECTION` (`voices.rs:6762`) is 3 statically-detuned
   saws (`players: 3, detune_cents: 16.0`) — a supersaw with every realism cue dialled *down*
   vs the trumpet (chiff 0.15 vs 0.35, brassiness 0.7 vs 1.0), and `LA_BRASS_SECTION_GAIN =
   0.6` (`voices.rs:5794`) turns the wrong sample down rather than fixing it.
2. **GM 24 gain cut to pass a blind oracle.** `LA_GUITAR = (0.25, (0.05, 0.20))`
   (`voices.rs:5787`) was cut from ~0.45 so the sample doesn't step 3.4× above the handover
   and trip `la_level_continuity` (`sampler.rs:1382`, cap 2.4×). But 0.25 makes the *pick*
   (the sample) quieter than the model's 200 ms sustain — the note is louder 200 ms in than at
   the attack, which for a plucked string is upside-down.
3. **GM 48/49 zone interleaving.** `strsec_p/f` (`sampler.rs:466-504`) interleave cello and
   violin zones in one bank; `nearest` (`sampler.rs:764`) picks by root, so a mid-register note
   can jump between a celli zone and a violin zone note-to-note — inconsistent timbre.
4. **GM 43 layer "dropping out" on the held note** is not a bug in the crossfade — the sample
   *is* only the attack; after `pos` runs past the data the model carries on (`sampler.rs:848`).
   It reads as "lifeless" only because the model underneath is the flat-vibrato BowedString
   (B3). Fixing B3 fixes this.

**Design — the contract: one owner per instant, level-matched at the seam, model onset
suppressed when wrapped.** The current crossfade lets the *model's own onset* run at reduced
gain underneath the sample's onset — a doubled attack — and never states who owns the first
300 ms. Make it explicit:

- **`[0, fade_start)` — sample owns the onset; the model runs but is SILENT.** The original
  draft said "start the model at its *sustain* level" — both reviews rejected that: a struck
  `Modal` has **no stationary sustain state** (setting `att_env = 1.0` at t=0 injects a
  discontinuity; "aging" it changes amplitude *and* phase). **Corrected contract: run the wrapped
  model from note-start as normal, but DISCARD its output until `fade_start`** — so its internal
  state (envelopes, filter memory, KS loop) ages naturally and there is no re-attack and no
  hand-authored start state. This keeps the onset logic *inside* `LaVoice` (the `wrapped`
  discard-window), so NO per-voice `suppress_onset()` edits to `Pluck`/`Modal`/`Bowed`/`Reed`/…
  are needed — which also dissolves the cross-wave collision the sequencing review flagged (§6).
- **`[fade_start, fade_end)` — a MEASURED crossfade** sample→model, level-matched at the seam.
  Do **not** assume equal-power: an attack sample and a pitched model at the same note can be
  *correlated*, so an equal-power law can boost or cancel at the midpoint. Use a linear or
  correlation-tested fade with per-key/velocity seam limits, chosen from a render probe. The point
  is the same: the sample is loud at t=0 and hands to the model on the model's natural *decay*, so
  the joint envelope peaks at the pick — which is what lets GM 24 keep a loud pick.
- **`[fade_end, ∞)` — model owns the sustain**, unchanged.

**Scope — which voices this touches, and the piano question (review).** `LaVoice` wraps SEVEN
distinct structs, not the "Pluck/Modal/Bowed/Reed" the draft named: `Modal` (acoustic piano 0-3),
`Pluck` (24), `Bowed` (40/110), `BowedString` (42/43), `SawStack` (48/49), `Brass` (56-61), `Reed`
(68-71), `Wind` (72/73). Because the corrected contract puts the discard-window in `LaVoice`
itself, it applies uniformly to all of them with no per-struct edits. **The piano (GM 0-3) is the
highest-album-count wrapped voice** — the discard-window changes its attack (the sampled piano
onset now owns the first ~fade_start ms with no modelled re-attack underneath). That is a
deliberate, universal consequence, and GM 0-3 joins the loudness-recheck list (§5) as the
largest-blast-radius entry. GM 61's wrong-sample removal (below) is **independent** of this
contract and need not wait for it.

Per-program consequences:
- **GM 61:** no CC0 brass-section sample exists (non-goal: no new assets). So **drop the
  sample layer for 61** (model-only) and improve the *section model* instead — replace the 3
  static-detuned saws with more players and real onset scatter (`BR_SECTION` already has
  `onset_scatter_s: 0.028`; raise `players` to ~5 and let the scatter + per-player drift do
  the width, as `BR_SYN1` does with `players: 5`). Remove `LA_BRASS_SECTION_GAIN` and the
  `program == 61` gain branch (`voices.rs:8103-8106`). The supersaw was a symptom of a wrong
  sample; fix the model and the sample crutch is unneeded.
- **GM 24:** restore the gain toward ~0.40–0.45 once the contract makes the model hand over on
  a *decay*; the rewritten `la_level_continuity` (§3) asserts "attack is the peak" so the
  0.25-to-pass hack is impossible.
- **GM 48/49:** split the interleaved bank so `nearest` chooses within one section per
  register (a low-register bank of celli zones, a high-register bank of violin zones, selected
  by key before `nearest`), or accept the interleave (it is the mildest of the four). Given
  "solve the problem at hand," I rank this **optional** — fix it only if the render-diff or the
  final listen flags 48/49.

**Loudness risk:** GM 24 goes *up* (louder pick); GM 61 loses the sampled transient (goes
*down* a touch) but the improved model compensates. Both are real mix moves — flag at
render-diff.

### 2.8 The reed & flue excitation source (the genuinely hard part)

This is GM 64–71 (`Reed`) and the eight `Wind` flue voices (72–79). The brief frames them as
"one narrowband filter doing two jobs." Two distinct sub-problems share one root, so I treat
the root first, then each family.

#### 2.8.1 First, verify the tanh really is inert (brief asked me to state this precisely)

**Inert on timbre to first order; NOT inert on level; edge caveat larger than first stated.**
The reed source is `ReedPulse` (`dsp.rs`, `ReedPulse`) — a band-limited difference-of-saws
rectangle of duty `w`, harmonic magnitude `2·|sin(πnw)|/(πn)` (documented at the struct). The
drive is `s = (s * drive).tanh() / drive` (the RD4 block in `Reed::render`) with
`drive = 1 + drive_vn·vn` **constant per note** (`Reed::from_preset`).

*Timbre:* a tanh is memoryless, so on the pulse's two plateaus the signal is flat at exactly
two levels (`(w−1)·osc_norm` and `w·osc_norm`); tanh maps each flat level to a fixed value →
the output is **still a two-level rectangle of the same duty**, whose harmonic ratios
`2|sin(πnw)|/(πn)` are unchanged. DC is removed by `dcb`. So the tanh cannot change sustained
brightness, and with `drive` frozen per note it cannot make brightness track blowing pressure.
This part of the original proof stands.

*Edge caveat, correctly sized:* the polyBLEP source is not perfectly two-level — it has ~1–2
sample transition regions at each of its two discontinuities. At the bottom of the range these
are a negligible fraction of the period, but at soprano-sax top (key 88, f0 ≈ 1318.5 Hz, period
≈ 33 samples at 44.1 kHz) the two edge regions are **~6–12 % of the waveform**, not "minute."
The correct statement is: **the tanh is degenerate on the plateaus (which set the sustained
harmonic ratios) and static within a note** — not that the edge effect is negligible. Any
replacement must therefore be level-matched against a **measured** spectrum/RMS of the current
voice, not a computed two-level idealisation.

*Level (the part the original section missed):* the tanh compresses the two levels
asymmetrically, so deleting it is a **source-level rise at forte**. For a two-level signal the
RMS ratio equals the peak-to-peak ratio, and closed-form:

```
post/pre = [tanh(d·a) + tanh(d·b)] / (d·(a+b)),   a = (1−w)·osc_norm,  b = w·osc_norm
```

with `osc_norm = 0.5/√(w(1−w))` (RD8). Numbers at vn = 1 (and vel ≈ 100 in parentheses):

| preset | w | drive d | post/pre | ΔdB on delete |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| saxes 64–67 | ≈0.30 | 1.9 | 0.699 | **+3.1 dB** (+2.7) |
| oboe/EH/bassoon | 0.14–0.16 | 1.35 | 0.616 | **+4.2 dB** (+3.9) |
| bagpipe 109 / shanai 111 | 0.18 / 0.12 | 1.45 / 1.42 | ≈0.657 | **+3.7 dB** |
| clarinet 71 | 0.50 | drive_vn = 0 (bypass) | 1.0 | 0 dB |

Note the double reeds move **more** than the saxes despite the smaller `drive_vn`: compression
is governed by `d·a`, and the narrow-duty spike level `a = 0.5·√((1−w)/w)` grows as the duty
narrows (oboe a ≈ 1.24 vs sax a ≈ 0.76), parking the double reeds' rare level deeper in
saturation. So "small drive_vn ⇒ small level effect" is false; **every ReedPreset except the
clarinet needs re-anchoring** (§2.8.3, and the loudness statement in §5). *(This corrects the
review finding, which had the double reeds moving less than the saxes; the closed form above is
the shown work.)*

#### 2.8.2 The invariant

A wind instrument's defining dynamic: **brightness (spectral centroid / high-harmonic content)
rises monotonically with blowing pressure**, from a rounded, dark *p* to a bright *ff*. State
the invariants precisely, including two the first draft got wrong:

- **What varies with pressure — the tilt.** The fraction of source energy in the upper
  harmonics rises with a `pressure` signal. Expressed in **harmonic count, not absolute Hz**:
  "at pressure p the source speaks ≈ N(p) harmonics" must mean the same thing at every key.
  (A fixed 1.2 kHz cutoff admits ~18 harmonics at bari-sax range bottom, key 36 ≈ 65 Hz, but
  **attenuates the fundamental** at soprano top, key 88 ≈ 1319 Hz. Absolute-Hz pressure
  curves cannot serve a family spanning that range.)
- **The honk is a FIXED-frequency formant whose gain grows — it does not sweep.** A sax's
  characteristic formant sits at a roughly fixed absolute frequency set by bore + vocal tract
  (F1 ≈ 1.1 kHz soprano … ≈ 0.5 kHz bari). What rises with blowing pressure is the harmonic
  **amplitude** in and above that formant region, not the formant's frequency. A resonant peak
  swept toward 8–11 kHz places emphasis where no sax formant exists and, under authored
  CC2/CC11, reads as a synth filter-sweep/wah. The `Reed` voice **already owns the correct
  machinery**: the fixed 3-slot formant bank (`ReedPreset.formants`, applied per-sample in
  `Reed::render` RD2), whose F1 slots already sit where the literature puts the sax formants
  (SOP_SAX 1100 Hz … BARI_SAX 480 Hz — verified against the preset table). The honk is that
  bank's **gains driven by pressure**.
- **`pressure` signal, honestly scoped:** `p ∈ [0,1] = clamp(vel_floor + env_term +
  authored(CC2, CC11, aftertouch))`, anchored so p(vel ≈ 100, sustain) ≈ 0.75 reproduces
  today's voicing. `vel_floor` (spawn) is the **dominant term on today's albums** — the reed
  amp envelope sustains at 0.90, so the envelope term contributes only an onset "speaking"
  transition (~a few % once sustained), and CC2/CC11/aftertouch are correctly inert until
  authored, which the committed MIDIs do not do. **Therefore: on today's catalogue this
  feature delivers note-to-note velocity brightening plus onset speak; within-note
  "breathing" is realised only for authored-controller (new/future) content.** We do NOT
  inflate the envelope term to fake within-note crescendo on every held note — that is
  phrasing the composer didn't write. The acceptance claim is scoped accordingly.
- **Odd/even structure is preserved BOTH ways across the whole p→ff sweep.** The duty encoding
  sets it (clarinet w = 0.5 → odd-only; sax w ≈ 0.30 → even-rich): a clarinet stays hollow
  when pushed **and a sax stays full when pushed**. The second half is now an explicit oracle
  leg (O-PRESSURE P3) because the deferred shaper design violated it exactly at ff (§2.8.4).
- **Aliasing budget is measured, not asserted.** Whatever generates brightening keeps
  off-lattice energy in the top octave (≈11–22 kHz) below the voice's ff breath-noise floor in
  that octave, minus margin — pinned as an **absolute** floor (lesson 2026.07.11), with the
  oversampling factor and any high-f0 derate **set from measurement** (§2.8.4, O-ALIAS).

#### 2.8.3 Primary design — pressure tilt + fixed-formant honk (reuses the existing bank)

Two stages, both linear, both driven by the same smoothed `pressure` (one-pole ~10–20 ms at
CTRL rate — zipper guard):

1. **Tilt — a LOW-Q pressure-swept lowpass in harmonic-count coordinates.** Replace the
   one-pole `lp` (RD3) with two cascaded one-poles or `Biquad::lowpass` at **Q ≤ 0.71 — no
   resonance** (12 dB/oct; today's 6 dB/oct is too shallow to make *p* convincingly dark).
   Cutoff:

   ```
   cutoff(p) = clamp( f0 · N(p), 500 Hz, 0.4·sr ),   N(p) = N_lo·(N_hi/N_lo)^p
   ```

   with per-preset `(N_lo, N_hi)` replacing the absolute-Hz `ReedPreset.lp` field — starting
   values ≈ saxes 3→24, double reeds 4→16, clarinet 3→14, pinned at the §2.8.4 probe. The
   existing 500 Hz floor and 0.4·sr clamp are kept (the floor keeps a bari low note speaking
   at pp; the clamp is the Nyquist guard at soprano top). "Reveal N harmonics at pressure p"
   now means the same thing at 65 Hz and at 1319 Hz.
   **Why low-Q is load-bearing, not taste:** a Q ≈ 1.5–3 peak dragged across partials spaced
   f0 apart comb-samples the ladder — band energy rises when the peak centres a partial and
   dips between them (worst at high f0, where peak bandwidth fc/Q < f0), so centroid and level
   move **non-monotonically** — an audible wah and an O-PRESSURE P1/P2 failure. Resonance
   lives on the formant bank, at fixed frequencies, where it cannot wah.
2. **Honk — drive the EXISTING formant bank's gains with pressure.** Keep every
   `ReedPreset.formants` centre and Q exactly as tabled; scale each slot's gain-dB:
   `g_n(p) = g_table_n · (α + β·p)`, anchored so p(vel ≈ 100) ≈ 0.75 reproduces the table
   (α + 0.75β = 1; starting slope pinned at the probe). Retune the three `Biquad::peak`s at
   CTRL rate (16 samples; three redesigns per tick is Brass-order cost). Gains only — **not
   Q, not centre**: real formant bandwidth does not narrow with blowing, and modulating poles
   near the unit circle chirps. This is the physically correct honk (fixed-frequency emphasis
   that intensifies with pressure), it is **fewer parts than the swept-peak design** (zero new
   filters), and it cannot read as a wah because nothing moves in frequency.
3. **Delete the `drive_vn` tanh and the `ReedPreset.drive_vn` field** — inert on timbre
   (§2.8.1) and superseded. Deletion sites: the RD4 block in `Reed::render`, the field on
   `ReedPreset`, its use in `Reed::from_preset`, AND a third consumer — the `reed_calibration`
   diagnostic test prints `p.drive_vn` (grep it; the field's comment says it exists partly "to
   keep the field live"), so audit-then-delete all three or it won't compile. Because deletion
   raises source level +3–4 dB at forte on every non-clarinet preset (§2.8.1 table),
   **re-anchor each `ReedPreset.amp` against a measured render of the current voice at vel ≈ 100
   mid-register** (measured, not computed — the formant bank and LP interact with the level
   change), and add GM 64–71 plus 109/111 to the loudness-recheck list.
4. **Add `Reed::set_breath(pressure, growl)`** overriding the trait default (mirror
   `Brass::set_breath`); the engine's authored-controller pass feeds it; inert until authored.
   `growl` is reserved for §2.8.4's pre-shaper noise fraction — also authored-only.
5. **Aliasing:** stages 1–2 are linear on an already-band-limited source — they generate
   nothing. The existing `reed_o12_alias_floor` keeps running as the regression pin; the new
   measured budget applies only when §2.8.4's shaper is in the chain.

**Mechanism → oracle map (feeds §3.1):** tilt monotonicity → O-PRESSURE P1/P2; register
consistency of the harmonic-count law → O-PRESSURE P6 (+ P1 at three keys); honk intensifies
at a FIXED centre → O-PRESSURE P5 (the anti-wah leg); tanh deletion level match → the
render-diff inventory + the loudness recheck (§5); controller inertness → the render-diff
(any un-authored channel diff is a bug).

#### 2.8.4 The sax rasp stage — a first-class A/B decided early, not a deferred pivot

Both reviews judge a collision shaper **probably load-bearing for the sax rasp** — the linear
sweep *reveals* the bore ladder but cannot *generate* the beating-reed texture of an over-blown
sax. So this is no longer "documented, deferred": §2.8.3 and this stage are an explicit A/B
**resolved in the first implementation slice by a cheap render probe**, with measurable gates.
Both mechanisms are ~a day to prototype combined.

**First, the corrected physics — the original limit claim was wrong.** Integrating the
zero-mean duty-w rectangle (leaky one-pole ≈ 18 Hz, below every fundamental in range) gives a
two-segment triangle ("tent"): falling for the fraction `w` of the period, rising for `1−w`.
Each segment is linear, so each segment's time-average is `(min+max)/2`, hence the tent's mean
is its mid-level **for any w**; and each linear segment crosses that mid-level at its temporal
midpoint, so the tent spends `w/2 + (1−w)/2 =` **exactly half the period above its mean,
regardless of duty**. Therefore `sign(tent)` — the g→∞ limit of `tanh(g·tent)` — is a **50 %
square: odd-only, zero even harmonics, for every w**. The original claim ("squares toward the
pulse, keeping even content") is false: even content *decreases* with drive and vanishes at
the limit, so a hard-blown sax would go hollow/clarinet-like — inverting the §2.8.2 invariant
exactly where we push hardest.

**The fix — bias the operating point (one constant, and it is the right physics).** Use
`y = tanh(g·(tent + b)) − DC` with a per-preset bias `b`. Physically, `b` is the static mouth
pressure offsetting the reed's operating point — a real reed's nonlinearity is not
odd-symmetric about the waveform mean. Two consequences:
- **Evens preserved at the limit by construction:** as g→∞, `y → sign(tent + b)` — a rectangle
  whose duty is the fraction of the period the tent spends above `−b`. Choose `b` so that
  fraction equals the preset duty `w`, and the hard-blown limit **is the duty-w bore rectangle
  itself** — the claim the original section made is now actually true.
- **Evens grow with drive at small g:** the cubic term of `tanh(g(x+b))` contains `3x²b` — a
  second-harmonic-series generator scaling with `g³·b`.

Cap `g_max` well below the near-sign regime anyway (the limit argument proves evens *cannot
collapse*, not that the limit sounds good), and keep the existing `dcb` 20 Hz highpass — the
biased tanh has signal-dependent DC (see new tripwire T-N2). Fallback if the bias misbehaves
under fast CC2 ramps: **cap-and-blend** — `y = (1−m)·tanh(g·tent)/g + m·pulse` with `m` rising
with pressure, the raw rectangle re-supplying the evens at ff. One mix, no bias, no new DC.

**What the shaper buys that the filter can't (and how a machine hears it):** route a small
per-preset fraction of the **breath noise into the shaper input** (turbulence passes through
the reed valve — that is where it physically enters). The saturating transfer intermodulates
noise × harmonics into sidebands ("skirts") around every partial that widen and rise with
pressure — the growl/rasp texture. A post-filter *additive* hiss cannot form skirts. This is
the measurable discriminator between the two designs (gate G3 below), replacing "does it sound
thin" with a number. Authored `growl` (set_breath arg 2) may scale this fraction; default inert.

**Corrected aliasing arithmetic (measured, not asserted):** at 2× internal oversampling
(fs′ = 88.2 kHz), a harmonic folds into the audible band when `n·f0 > 66 150 Hz`; at soprano
top f0 = 1318.5 Hz that is `n > 50.17 ⇒ n = 51` (not "~50"). At max drive the spectrum tends
to a 1/n-class rectangle ladder, so h51 sits ≈ **−32 to −34 dB re the fundamental** (not the
−40 dB the 1/n² triangle assumption gave) — right at the existing `reed_o12` floor. Therefore
**no a-priori "2× suffices, no ADAA" claim survives.** Instead: **A-MEAS-ALIAS** — build the
shaper stage runnable at 2×/4×/8× (reuse the Brass 2× oversampling machinery), measure at
worst case, and set the high-f0 drive derate from the measured in-band error (protocol in the
O-ALIAS spec below). Expected outcome from the arithmetic: 2× needs a derate above f0 ≈
0.8–1 kHz; 4× likely passes underated — but the measurement decides, and its outputs (factor,
derate curve, pinned dB constants) land in code with the measurement kept in-tree as an
`#[ignore]`d harness so it stays re-runnable.

**The A/B decision procedure (gates, then a confirmatory ear):**
- **G1** — O-PRESSURE P1–P6 all green in the candidate config.
- **G2** — measured brightness range: steady centroid(ff)/centroid(p) at mid key ≥ 2.0 for
  saxes, ≥ 1.6 for double reeds/clarinet (pinned *before* the probe; changed only with a
  logged reason).
- **G3 (saxes only)** — rasp skirts: off-lattice energy within ±(0.05–0.35)·f0 of harmonics
  2–6 at ff is ≥ 2× its *p* value and ≥ −40 dB re total.
- **Rule:** double reeds + clarinet ship **filter-only** (rasp_g = 0) if G1+G2 green —
  expected. Saxes: filter-only cannot pass G3 by construction, so GM 64–67 ship **with the
  shaper** provided it passes G1+G2+G3 **and** O-ALIAS post-derate; if it cannot, fall back to
  filter-only with a widened formant-gain range, log the miss in the decision log, and give
  Arthur the A/B pair to judge whether thin-sax is acceptable for this release.
- **Probe:** day one of the reed slice — one 4-bar alto swell (p→ff, CC2-authored) rendered
  both ways + the G-suite. Arthur's single listen is confirmatory; the gates are the decision.

#### 2.8.5 The flue voices (Wind, GM 72–79) — the air, and one topology fix

The "one narrowband filter doing two jobs" is literal: the single breath-bandpass output
`noise` in `Wind::render` feeds **both** the sustained breath bed **and** the onset chiff, so
the chiff cannot be the broadband edge-tone burst a flue attack actually is. Combined with the
air sitting 21–49 dB below the tone on 74/76/78 (brief's measurement; settle with `wd_o5`-style
band RMS on the model-only render), the flues read as a bare sine stack.

**Fix (layer: the Wind voice + presets):**
1. **Split the two jobs.** Give the onset chiff its own broadband source — a short highpassed
   noise burst (the `onset_post` precedent) independent of the f0-tracked breath bandpass.
2. **Raise the air to an audible fraction** for 74 recorder / 76 bottle / 78 whistle, per
   their own docstrings' intent, and make the breath level pressure-tracked (`vel_air` is
   currently spawn-fixed).
3. **Make the spectral tilt pressure-tracked** (`bright` and the per-partial `amps[i]` are
   spawn-fixed; retune at CTRL rate from the same `pressure`). **Same honesty scoping as
   §2.8.2:** with `vel_air`/`bright` currently spawn-only and no authored CC2/CC11 in the
   committed MIDIs, items 2–3 deliver **note-to-note** dynamics on today's albums; within-note
   breathing arrives with authored content. The acceptance criteria say so.
4. **GM 76 blown bottle — Helmholtz topology, with an honest re-estimate.** The direction
   stands: a bottle is one near-pure resonance excited by broadband breath, not a harmonic
   pipe; `BLOWN_BOTTLE` (harm ≈ 0, breath 0.80) fakes this on the additive core. But the
   original "~10-line branch" was wrong. A real resonator path needs: resonator **state** (a
   high-Q two-pole whose output level scales with Q × excitation bandwidth, so an explicit
   **gain normalisation** across keys); **excitation shaping** (breath-envelope drive);
   **note-off decay** (the resonator rings past release — `released()`/alive bookkeeping so
   the ring-out isn't truncated); **pitch tracking** (retune at CTRL with bend/scoop/vib, as
   the sine bank does); a **pressure leg** (harder blowing = louder, airier, slightly wider
   excitation); and **its own oracles** (O-PITCH on the resonance centre; a ring-time/T60
   check; level match across keys). Honest estimate: **~80–140 lines plus 2–3 oracles —
   half a day to a day**, not 10 lines. Still worth it: it is the one flue voice that is
   physically wrong at the root.

**Loudness risk:** unchanged from the first draft — raising the air on 74/76/78 is a modest
level move if `vel_air` is re-anchored so vel = 100 mid-register matches the current spawn
value; flag at render-diff. (The **reed**-family level move from the tanh deletion is the
larger one — see §2.8.1 and the loudness statement.)

### 2.11 GM 22 harmonica, and the free-reed routing

### 2.13 Pluck chain-order defect — the pick transient bypasses the pickup

**Confirmed.** In `Pluck::render` the pickup comb (`voices.rs:2382`) and coil RLC
(`voices.rs:2386`) run *before* the pick click `onset_pre` is summed (`voices.rs:2391`). So on
every pickup-equipped preset (CLEAN/JAZZ/DRIVE/MUTED/SLAP/PICK/BASS/… — any `pickup > 0`), the
pick attack — which physically *is* string motion the pickup senses — never passes through the
pickup comb + coil. It goes through the body EQ, cab and out-LP (`voices.rs:2395-2405`) but not
the electric-defining pickup.

**Fix — but FIRST establish what `onset_pre` models (review caveat, do not skip).** The code
comments `onset_pre` as "the pick click knocks the *body*." If it models body/contact noise, a
magnetic pickup would NOT sense it and the current post-pickup position is *correct* — reordering
on the strength of the variable name would be the bug. If it models string *displacement* at the
pick, it should enter the string state (or precede the pickup). **So the fix is conditional on that
determination, not automatic:** read the excitation `onset_pre` actually injects; only if it is
string-motion (not body-knock) inject it **before** the pickup comb (`voices.rs:2382`) for
pickup-equipped presets, guarded `if self.pickup.is_some()`. The finger/slap `onset_post`
(`voices.rs:2430`) is genuinely post-pickup body noise and stays. If `onset_pre` is body-knock, the
"electric pick bypasses the pickup" framing is wrong and this item is **dropped** — verify before
touching many electric presets at once. Low loudness risk; if applied, flag at render-diff.

## 3. The oracle regime (the multiplier)

The meta-finding is correct and is the real root cause: a voice emitting infrasound (B1)
passed every gate for its life because **there is no pitch oracle**, and the suite is full of
*characterisation pins* that freeze whatever the code did rather than encode physics. The repo's
own stated method is oracle-first ("write the oracle before the music"); the synth's oracles
drifted from it. This section is the part that stops the class from recurring.

Design principles (from `lessons_learnt.md`, load-bearing):
- **Audio oracles are RATIOS / relative tolerances, never absolute floors** — the CLI
  peak-normalises the whole render, and a frozen f32 hash is not valid across build profiles
  (lessons 2026.07.12, 2026.07.13). Freeze *aggregate* features (rms/centroid/band energy)
  with relative tolerance, not per-sample hashes.
- **Measure pitch with a Goertzel peak (`testutil::peak_locate`), not zero-crossings** — the
  crossing counter (`pitch_hz`) lies on bright voices (lesson 2026.07.07). And never
  `peak_locate` an FM carrier when β≳0.5 — use the known pitch (lesson 2026.07.12).
- **Fix pitch to measure timbre; render at matched pitch / worst case / the feature's live
  window** (lesson 2026.07.08).

### 3.1 New oracles (the ones that would have caught these)

**O-PITCH — a pitch oracle over every melodic program (the B1 catcher). Per-class, not one
estimator (review-revised).** Both reviews warned that one estimator + one tolerance cannot serve
every voice: many modal voices have a weak/absent/non-dominant fundamental, so "largest peak" can
lock onto an upper mode, while a fixed-key Goertzel bin is an *energy* check, not a pitch estimate;
and at 41 Hz, ±15 cents ≈ ±0.36 Hz needs a ~2.8 s window (before interpolation). So define **oracle
classes**, each rendered through `make(program, key, vel, sr, seed, samples)` for **both**
`samples=false` and `samples=true` (guarded on `embedded_samples_available()`), measured in a
**steady post-onset window** (skip scoop/attack):
- **Harmonic sustained/struck voices with a real fundamental** (pianos, plucks, bowed, reeds,
  winds, organs, leads without deep FM): estimated pitch via `peak_locate` (verify its
  interpolated resolution suffices at the lowest key; lengthen the window for bass registers),
  tolerance **±30 cents** mid-register, **±15 cents** where physics allows (Modal/Pluck).
  *B1's infrasound EPs miss by thousands of cents → instant fail; B4's contrabass fails at C4+ →
  forces the `loop_comp` fix.*
- **Inharmonic modal voices** (bells/mallets/tubular/timpani): assert energy at the **expected-key
  band** plus a permitted modal-ratio structure — NOT a single peak, which would lock onto a strong
  overtone.
- **FM / deep-vibrato voices** (some leads/pads): band-integrated or demodulated pitch evidence at
  the **known key**; never `peak_locate` when β≳0.5 (lesson 2026.07.12 — the first sideband can
  outrank the carrier).
- **Sampled attacks**: a **separate attack-window tuning check** — the post-onset model window
  cannot catch a mistuned or wrong-instrument SAMPLE (this is where GM 61's wrong sample and a
  mis-repitched zone would show, and O-PITCH's steady window alone would miss it).
This is the single most important addition and the guard the synth has never had.

**O-ATTACK — "a struck/plucked voice's attack is its loudest instant" (property, robustly
measured).** For Modal (bells/mallets/pianos/timpani) and non-sustaining Pluck presets, render
**through the engine** and assert the attack dominates — but use a **windowed-RMS / robust-envelope
percentile with a permitted ratio**, NOT a single global sample peak (review: a raw peak is
noise-sensitive and can be nudged by body resonance, beating, or effects; "first 80 ms" is not a
universal instrument law, so treat the window as per-class, not a hard constant). Excludes
sustaining plucks (`sustain > 0`: DRIVE/DRIVE_LEAD e-bow) — for those assert "no late-window RMS
exceeds the attack window" (the §2.6 sag-inversion catcher and the §2.7 GM-24 gain-cut catcher).
For the §2.6 Drive specifically, also test recovery **directly** with a known decaying input rather
than only via a rendered note, so the property is pinned at the unit, not just end-to-end.

**O-ENGINE-SAMPLES — render through the engine with `samples: true`.**
Today's distinctness matrix renders `make(…, false)` model-only and never through the engine
(`testutil.rs:1200`). Add a companion pass of O-PITCH and a level-continuity/attack check that
go through the **full engine path with samples on**, so sample-layer defects are in scope: GM
61 (wrong instrument → distinct-from-solo-trumpet check), GM 24 (attack-is-peak), GM 48/49
(within-section timbre continuity). Guard on `embedded_samples_available()` and skip the
sample assertions in a modeled-only build (as `prewarm` does, `sampler.rs:735`).

**O-PRESSURE — brightness rises with pressure; timbre class is preserved (the §2.8 invariant).**
Model-only (`make(prog, …, samples=false)`); steady post-onset window (skip scoop/attack;
pre-`vib_delay` where possible); harmonic measurement via Goertzel `mag_at` at known `n·f0`
(never zero-crossings); use the RD-O8a `breath = 0` seam wherever a clean lattice is needed.
Legs:
- **P1 — monotone, note-to-note (all 10 reed presets + all 8 flue presets), at three keys
  (range bottom/mid/top):** velocities {30, 55, 80, 105, 127}; steady-window spectral centroid
  non-decreasing at every step (tolerance −1 % for measurement noise) and cumulative rise ≥
  the family target pinned from gate G2.
- **P2 — within-note controller ramp:** one held mid-key note, CC2 ramped 20→110 over 2 s
  through the `set_breath` path; centroid over 4 consecutive windows non-decreasing, cumulative
  rise ≥ a pinned fraction of the P1 range. This leg is also the anti-wah smoothness check —
  a swept resonance comb-sampling the partials fails its per-window monotonicity.
- **P3 — the sax KEEPS its evens (the F3 catcher):** alto + tenor, at every P1 step and both
  P2 endpoints: even-harmonic energy fraction over harmonics 2–13 ≥ 0.35 absolute AND ≥ 0.6×
  its value at the lowest pressure step (no even-collapse with drive). Must fail against the
  un-fixed §2.8.4 shaper at high drive before it passes (regression-first).
- **P4 — the clarinet STAYS odd:** even fraction ≤ 0.10 at every step (extends
  `reed_o3_clarinet_hollowness` across the sweep).
- **P5 — the honk intensifies at a FIXED centre (the F1 catcher):** for every reed preset with
  live formant slots: energy fraction in the ±1/3-octave band around the preset's F1 rises
  with pressure, while the band's `peak_locate`d maximum stays within ±5 % of the tabled F1 at
  every step. A swept-peak implementation cannot pass the second clause.
- **P6 — speaks at p (the F2 register catcher):** at the lowest pressure step, at range
  bottom/mid/top keys, the fundamental is the strongest lattice harmonic. An absolute-Hz
  cutoff that attenuates the soprano-top fundamental fails here instantly.
Write P1–P6 **before** the mechanism (oracle-first).

**O-ALIAS (reeds/winds) — absolute floor, pinned from measurement, not asserted.** Two parts:
- **The shipping regression pin:** keep `reed_o12_alias_floor`'s separable-lattice method
  (f0 = sr/35.5 so half-integer bins are measurable; fold-back RMS < 0.02 and worst < 0.03 re
  the fundamental), now measured **with the shaper active at its shipped config**: soprano
  preset at top-of-range, minimum post-jitter duty, g_max, max pressure, breath = 0 (so noise
  cannot mask aliases), model-only. Add the §2.8.2 absolute budget: top-octave (≈11–22 kHz)
  off-lattice energy ≤ the voice's measured ff breath-bed level in that octave **minus 6 dB**,
  pinned as an absolute dB constant with a comment stating its provenance.
- **A-MEAS-ALIAS (dev-time measurement, kept in-tree as an `#[ignore]`d harness):** run the
  shaper stage at 2×, 4×, and 8× internal oversampling at the worst case above; 8× is the
  reference; the error metric is each candidate's off-lattice in-band energy excess over 8×.
  Ship the smallest factor whose excess ≤ 3 dB **after** applying a high-f0 drive derate
  (smoothstep g reduction above a corner f0, the Brass BR12 precedent), with the derate curve
  chosen so the shipped config passes the regression pin with ≥ 6 dB margin. Corrected
  arithmetic feeding the expectation: first audible-band fold at 2× for f0 = 1318.5 Hz is
  n = 51 (n > 66 150/1318.5 = 50.17), sitting ≈ −32 to −34 dB re the fundamental at max drive
  (1/n-class ladder, not the 1/n² triangle) — i.e. **at** the current floor, so a 2× derate is
  expected to be required and 4× likely passes underated; the measurement, not this paragraph,
  decides. The chosen factor, corner, and pinned constants go in the decision log.

### 3.3 The render-diff inventory is still mandatory — baseline the BRANCH-POINT, not HEAD

Every voices.rs/engine.rs/sampler.rs/drums.rs change here owes the render-diff inventory
(CLAUDE.md synth-change policy): build a baseline binary, render every album MIDI in
`render_opus.py::ALBUMS` with both binaries, and `cmp`.

**CRITICAL (external review): the baseline must be the branch-point commit, NOT `HEAD`.** Gate D
runs *after* every change has landed on the task branch, so a baseline built from `HEAD` at that
point is the CANDIDATE — the diff compares the candidate against itself and shows nothing. Record
the branch-point SHA (`git merge-base origin/main HEAD`, captured at task start) BEFORE any code
lands; build the baseline from that detached commit and the candidate from task-branch `HEAD`, and
record both SHAs beside the inventory. Never use a moving `origin/main` as the baseline.
It is a **report, not a gate**: expected diffs confirm the change reached the right albums;
*unexpected* diffs (a reed change altering a piano-only album, DC on silent channels) are bugs.
This overhaul touches nearly every family, so the diff will be large *by design* — the value is
catching the **unexpected** entries (a "canary" untouched album that changed). Note
`lessons_learnt.md` (2026.07.13): there is currently no reusable render-diff harness — the lead
should expect to script it.

## 6. Sequencing — serial GREEN slices, not parallel waves (review-rewritten)

Both reviews rejected the original parallel Wave A–D plan for two concrete reasons: (1) "O-PITCH
first" *deliberately goes red*, which contradicts "each an independently-landable green commit";
and (2) nearly every item edits the same 14k-line `voices.rs` and the shared test module, and — the
CRITICAL one — the §2.7 onset work and the §2.8/§2.13/§2.3 work were slated to edit **the same
render functions concurrently** (`Reed::render`, `Wind::render`, the `Pluck` onset block,
`BowedString::new`). That is the repo's own "shared voice structs are where parallel designs
collide" lesson. The corrected §2.7 contract (discard-window *inside* `LaVoice`, no per-voice
`suppress_onset` edits) removes most of that collision; the rest is handled by going serial.

**Step 0 — capture the baseline SHA** (`git merge-base origin/main HEAD`) and record it; the
render-diff (§3.3) depends on it. No code yet.

**Deliver as serial green slices — each slice writes its red regression test AND its fix in the
SAME commit** (never land a red oracle alone; that violates "green commits"):

1. **Rig + confirmed bugs.** 2.2 B2 + 2.14 audition rig (Python); 2.1 B1 electric pianos; 2.3 B3 +
   2.4 B4 BowedString (+ its rewritten vibrato regression). Write **O-PITCH** as part of this slice
   so B1/B4 are composed-to-pass, and land it green with the fixes (not before them).
2. **Reed/flue excitation (§2.8) + O-PRESSURE + O-ALIAS.** The largest item. Resolve the
   filter-vs-collision-shaper A/B *early* with the render probe (§2.8, revised), then land the
   `pressure`/`set_breath` seam on `Reed` and `Wind` together, the reed brightness+formant-honk,
   and the flue split-chiff/air/Helmholtz. This slice owns those two render functions exclusively.
3. **Sample contract (§2.7) + O-ATTACK + Drive (§2.6).** The `LaVoice` discard-window contract,
   then its consequences (GM-24 gain restore, `la_level_continuity` rewrite). GM-61's wrong-sample
   removal is independent and can land here or in slice 1. 2.6 Drive delete + `drive_sag_compression`
   deletion land with O-ATTACK. This slice touches `LaVoice` and `engine.rs`, not the reed/wind
   render functions slice 2 owns — so 2 and 3 don't collide.
4. **Lower-risk retunes.** 2.5 music box / marimba / timpani; 2.10 harpsichord→Pluck; 2.11
   harmonica; 2.12 basses; 2.13 pickup reorder. Each isolated; small commits.
5. **Gate & close.** Render-diff inventory (branch-point baseline), loudness review, distinctness /

Parallelism, if used at all, is **across slices that touch disjoint files** — never two agents in

`voices.rs` exceeds the wall-clock saving.

## 7. Decision log

- **Reed brightening (REVISED after review): low-Q harmonic-count tilt + pressure-driven fixed
  formant bank, with the collision shaper a first-slice A/B — NOT a swept resonant peak.** The
  original draft's swept resonant peak (to 8-11 kHz) mis-placed the sax formant (which is fixed
  ~1-1.5 kHz) and its absolute-Hz cutoff was register-inconsistent. Corrected: a LOW-Q lowpass at
  `f0·N(p)` for the brightness *tilt* (revealing K harmonics means the same at every pitch), and
  the *honk* is the existing `ReedPreset.formants` bank's GAINS driven by pressure — fixed
  frequency, no wah, zero new filters. The integrate+tanh rasp shaper is promoted from "deferred
  listen-gated extension" to a **first-slice A/B with measurable gates** (both reviews judge it
  probably load-bearing for the sax rasp), and its high-drive physics is corrected (a bias term
  preserves even harmonics; the un-biased shaper went odd-only/hollow at ff). **Rejected:**
  additive resynthesis (N oscillators) — ~150 harmonics at bari low is expensive, the BLEP avoids
  it. **Rejected:** a physical waveguide reed — fixed-event offline renderer. See revised §2.8.
- **Drive/sag (REVISED after review): delete the ENTIRE sag path — one design, no compression
  hedge.** Both reviews flagged the original three-shape wording as contradictory, and a sub-unity
  compression at the pick would *reduce* the pick distortion we want. So: delete `g_sag` and its
  machinery entirely; the static cascade clips hardest at the pick already; fix any held-note
  loudness loss at the string layer (deepen `DRIVE.sustain`), never by restoring the sag.
  **Rejected:** keeping a small correctly-signed compression — an unnecessary hedge that fights
  the objective; design a physically-timed sag stage deliberately later if a listen wants it.
- **CELESTA unchanged; only the music box retuned.** Celesta is a free-free steel bar; its
  ratios are correct. Changing it would be a defect introduced by over-trusting the diagnosis.
- **Marimba retuned to 4:1 despite 13-track blast radius.** A marimba *is* an arched 4:1 bar;
  anchors are unfrozen; the physics is unambiguous. Recorded as the highest-album-count retune
  so the lead accepts the trade knowingly. **Rejected:** leaving it at 3:1 to spare 13 tracks —
  that keeps 12/13 as near-twins, the exact "wrong yet green" failure this overhaul targets.
- **GM 61 goes model-only (drop the wrong sample), improve the section model.** No CC0 section
  sample exists and none will be commissioned (non-goal). A supersaw was the symptom of a
  wrong sample; more players + real scatter is the fix. **Rejected:** repitching the solo
  trumpet sample harder — it is the wrong instrument, not the wrong level.
- **Frozen f32-hash goldens converted to relative aggregate signatures / deleted.** Cross-
  profile bit-hashes are invalid (lesson) and their anchor reason is unfrozen. **Rejected:**
  re-pinning them per profile — that is the trap the lesson names.

## 8. Tripwires (my "stop and re-escalate" signals)

These are the conditions under which a design assumption breaks. If the lead hits one,
stop and reconsider rather than push through.

1. **The reed resonant sweep does not sound like pressure at the final listen** (it reveals the
   ladder but stays "thin," no honk/rasp). → the assumption that *revealing* the bore ladder ≈
   *generating* brightness is insufficient for the sax. Switch to the collision shaper (§2.8.4)
   with its high-f0 derate and the absolute O-ALIAS floor. This is the single most likely
   design pivot; I have flagged its full reasoning so the switch costs no re-derivation.
2. **B4's measured `loop_comp` for the contrabass differs from the cello's 3.85 by > ~0.5
   samples.** → the two waveguide voices have different in-loop phase despite sharing
   `refl_sustain = 2600` — my "residual ≈ 2.85 samples" model (which matched the cello to 0.03
   samples) does not transfer, and the flatness may be larger or smaller than the §2.4 table.
   Re-measure per voice; do not copy the cello's constant.
3. **Deleting the Drive sag collapses held-note loudness on the distorted-guitar albums** (the
   render-diff shows sustained RMS dropping hard). → the e-bow sustainer is not carrying the
   hold the sag boost was faking. Deepen `DRIVE.sustain`/`DRIVE_LEAD.sustain` (the correct
   layer) rather than restore the inverted sag; if that still fails, the sustainer's engage
   threshold needs work before the sag can be removed.

Two more worth watching (secondary):
4. **The render-diff shows an *untouched-family* canary album changed** → a shared struct
   (`Pluck`/`Modal`/`fx_profile`/a `LA_*` const) leaked across families (the 2026.07.08
   lesson). Bisect the offending commit; do not ship until the canary is explained.
5. **O-PITCH's ±30-cent tolerance flags voices with a legitimate onset scoop or deep vibrato**
   (reeds/winds/leads) → measure in a later steady window or use the known-key Goertzel bin;
   do not loosen the tolerance globally (that would re-admit B1-class defects).

New tripwires from the §2.8 review revision (the reed/flue redesign):
6. **T-N1 — CTRL-rate biquad redesign cost/zipper.** Three `Biquad::peak` redesigns + the tilt
   filter every 16 samples per reed voice; stepped coarsely it zippers, hot it costs. Smooth
   `pressure` with a one-pole and halve the redesign rate before reaching for anything cleverer.
7. **T-N2 — the shaper bias makes DC time-varying.** A CC2 ramp moves the biased tanh's DC,
   leaking through the 20 Hz `dcb` as a sub-audible thump. Check for sub-30 Hz energy during a
   CC2 sweep (> −60 dB re total ⇒ investigate); fall back to the cap-and-blend shaper if it fails.
8. **T-N3 — pressure now couples brightness AND level.** Formant-gain growth adds energy, so a
   crescendo brightens *and* loudens through two paths. If ff level overshoots after the amp
   re-anchor, trim the formant-gain slope β, never the anchor; and if the harmonic-count law
   leaves the bari dull at ff, raise N_hi or the formant-gain range — **never** revert to
   absolute Hz.
