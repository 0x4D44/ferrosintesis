## DECISION (Arthur, 2026-07-16) — the two-bank split *(resolves §2)*

The tam-tam is **not** a standard-GM instrument (GM14 = **Tubular Bells**; gongs live only in the Roland-GS / Yamaha-XG extended kits, which we render via the sibling `mdsc55` emulator, not here). So the tam-tam bank is a **100%-local extension** — no wider-world compatibility pressure, and we are free to arrange it cleanly:

| CC0 (bank select) | Voice | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **0** (default) | **Tubular Bells** (`bell(TUBULAR)`) | GM-correct default — **UNCHANGED**, byte-identical (G5 oracle). |
| **1** | **near-pitchless orchestral TAM-TAM** (NEW) | The "correct tam-tam" the round-3 complaint asked for. New voice (sampled one-shot; modeled dense-cloud is the `--no-samples` fallback). |
| **2** | **pitched GONG AGENG** | What our two *Through Lines* movements actually use. The current modeled `tam_tam` already *is* a pitched gong ageng → it moves here as the fallback; a real gong sample is the sampled upgrade. |

**Album edit (small, part of this unit):** the two movements currently latch `CC0=1`; they switch to **`CC0=2`** so they keep the pitched gong. Concretely `sc.cc(CH, 0, 1, …)` → `sc.cc(CH, 0, 2, …)` in `t08_ten_metres.py` and `t13_bronze_water.py`, then rebuild both `.mid`s. **No pitched-role re-voice is needed** — the gong is preserved, just at a new bank number.

**Rejected alternative:** adopting GS/XG wholesale — a multi-year, ~480-voice + control-architecture effort that duplicates `mdsc55` (which emulates the real SC-55/MU-80 from their ROMs) and changes what ferrosintesis is. We cherry-pick the one voice, we do not become an XG module.

## 0. Bottom line up front

The one-shot gong player is a straightforward new voice — it copies the **`SampledDrum`** shape (the sample *is* the whole voice), not the attack-only `LaVoice`. The engineering is low-risk and well-precedented.

**The real decision is not the code — it is the pitch of the gong**, and the research changed the picture:

> **GM14 is used as a PITCHED instrument in TWO committed movements**, not the one the plan assumed. *Ten Metres of Air* rings its **key-41 as a Bb-major final-chord tone**; *Bronze Water* strikes **C2 (key 36) as the tuned colotomy anchor of an entire gamelan cycle**. Both lean on the current voice's `fold_key` pitch.

So "make it a near-pitchless orchestral tam-tam" would silently break real harmonic content in both movements and force a two-movement re-voice. **I recommend a *pitched* one-shot gong** (a real *gong ageng* sample, repitched per key like the sampled toms): it is both *accurate* (a gong ageng genuinely has a low pitch centre and a chaotic bloom — exactly what the modeled voice fakes badly) **and** preserves the album use, so it needs **no album re-voice**. That is the §2 call I need you to confirm.

The other genuine gate is **assets**: there is **no gong source anywhere in the tooling today**, and the plan's cited "VSCO 1 … Gong" is a *different pack* from the pinned VSCO-2-CE — so this is a brand-new CC0 source that needs a **human download-and-inspect** for pitch/layers/quality before the crate is built (the same gate the plan mandates for every sample source).

## 1. Why we're here (the fork, restated)

GM14's alt bank (CC0≠0) is `voices::tam_tam` — a **modeled `Modal`** voice: 10 inharmonic modes register-locked to keys 36–47, a ~1.1 kHz noise splash, a mode-bloom (`voices.rs:2063-2123`; routed at `altbank.rs:1078`). Round-2 called it "not really a tam-tam"; Arthur's directive is to make GM14 *accurate*. A tam-tam/gong's identity is a dense, chaotic, slowly-blooming multi-second decay — the single hardest thing to synthesise and the classic case for a real one-shot sample. (The default bank, CC0=0, is **tubular bells** via `bell()` at `voices.rs:10513`; that stays exactly as-is — pinned byte-identical by the G5 oracle `alt_bank_gm14_tamtam_authored_only`, `engine.rs:7521`.)

`LaVoice` cannot carry this (it is an attack-only crossfade-*out* layer that discards the sample after a sub-second `fade_end` and *requires* a modeled sustain to hand to — `sampler.rs:759-916`). A gong has no modeled sustain to hand to. So we need the **`SampledDrum` shape** (own the whole voice), not the `LaVoice` shape.

## 2. THE DECISION — pitched gong vs near-pitchless tam-tam *(needs Arthur)*

The research found GM14 used **pitched** in committed source:

| Use | Where | How it uses GM14's pitch |
|---|---|---|
| *Ten Metres of Air* — **the Bb final chord** | `albums/fable5/Through Lines/movements/t08_ten_metres.py:1168` | `key 41` rung 8 beats as a **chord tone in a Bb-major stack** (choir/brass around it) |
| *Ten Metres of Air* — the **key-46 splash** | `t08:952` | percussive fff strike; oracle `_o_splash_anatomy` (`t08:452`) — **pitch-agnostic** |
| *Bronze Water* — **the colotomy anchor** | `albums/fable5/Through Lines/movements/t13_bronze_water.py:114-385` | `GONG_PITCH = 36` (C2) struck on every 16-beat boundary as the **tuned harmonic backbone** of a gamelan cycle, with cent-detune tables |

**Option A — a PITCHED one-shot gong (recommended).** Ship a real *gong ageng* sample and **repitch it per key** (`repitch = key_freq(fold_key(key,lo,hi)) / root`), exactly the sampled-tom pattern (`sampler.rs:1214-1219`). A gong ageng *is* a pitched instrument, so this is *more* accurate for these albums than a Western orchestral tam-tam, and both movements keep working with **no re-voice**. The splash still works (a pitched strike is still a strike).
- *Pro:* accurate, zero album breakage, smallest total change.
- *Con:* it is a *gong* (pitched, hums) not a *tam-tam* (near-pitchless crash). If the round-3 complaint was specifically "I want a Western orchestral tam-tam," this is not that.

**Option B — a NEAR-PITCHLESS tam-tam.** Ship an orchestral tam-tam one-shot with no clear pitch (a dense inharmonic wash). This is the "most correct *tam-tam*" reading of the complaint, but it **breaks the pitched content in both movements** → a follow-on that re-voices *Ten Metres of Air*'s key-41 Bb tone **and** *Bronze Water*'s entire C2 colotomy anchor onto genuinely pitched instruments (bells/tubular bells). That is real, per-movement album surgery on committed `.mid` source.

**My recommendation: Option A.** The instrument the albums actually use *is* a pitched gong ageng, the current voice already folds to a pitch, and A gives an accurate sample **without** rewriting two movements. Option B only makes sense if you specifically want the orchestral-tam-tam timbre and accept re-voicing the two movements. **This is the call I need from you before building.** (A hybrid — a pitched gong for GM14 default, and a separate near-pitchless tam-tam only if you later want it as its own CC0 sub-bank — is possible but I would not build it speculatively.)

## 3. The subsystem — `GongOneShot` (modeled on `SampledDrum`)

A new `Voice` implementor (likely in `sampler.rs`, beside `SampledDrum`), gated on `embedded-samples`.

- **Storage.** A new gong bank of CC0 WAV layers embedded via `include_bytes!` in an asset crate. The `SampledDrum`/`Bank` API is the template: velocity layers (`vel_hi` splits) and, if the source has enough takes, round-robins; lazy `OnceLock` PCM decode (`ferrosintesis-samples-drumkit/src/lib.rs:782-1004`). Crate choice: extend `ferrosintesis-samples-orchestral` (GM14 is a melodic-channel voice, not a drum) **or** a small new `ferrosintesis-samples-percussion` — decide when the source's size is known (a 36-layer gong may warrant its own crate; a 3–5-layer gong folds into orchestral).
- **Playback.** Linear-interp resample, reusing `SampledDrum`'s exact machinery (`step = 44100/sr * repitch`, fractional read — `sampler.rs:1151,1279`). `repitch = key_freq(fold_key(key, LO, HI)) / root` (Option A) so the gong stays a foldable pitched instrument over the album's modest range (C2…~Bb). Velocity → layer choice + a brightness/gain trim.
- **Lifecycle — the one real design point.** Unlike `SampledDrum` (note_off is a no-op, `released()` always true — percussion rings regardless), the gong is on a **melodic channel** and must free its slot: `note_off()` starts a **release fade** (the string/organ convention), and `render()` returns `false` once the sample is exhausted **or** the release fade completes. This matters because the engine has **no voice cap and no stealing** (`engine.rs:2142` — `max_polyphony` is a pure statistic); a 6–15 s ring authored densely would otherwise stack live slots indefinitely. Bounding the tail is the player's job. (`released()` returns true after the fade; `set_pitch(mult)` scales the repitch for channel bend.)
- **Dispatch.** In `altbank::make` at the GM14 arm (`altbank.rs:1078`):
  `14 => if samples { Box::new(GongOneShot::new(key, vel, sr, seed)) } else { Box::new(voices::tam_tam(key, vel, sr, seed)) }`
  — samples-gated, with the existing modeled `tam_tam` as the `--no-samples` fallback (so the modeled-only build and its oracles are untouched). The default bank (CC0=0 tubular bells) is not touched at all.

## 5. Oracles (fail-first, per the round-3 regime)

The new gong's contract, each RED on the current modeled voice:
- **Dense, non-tuned-bell spectrum in the sustain** — no single partial dominates the way the tubular-bell control does (distinguishes a gong wash from a pitched bell), while still carrying a foldable pitch centre (Option A).
- **Long shimmering ring** — sustain persists ≥ several seconds with a rising/roughening centroid (the chaotic bloom), measured on the real render.
- **Splash/strike transient** — early HF ≥ N× late HF (the existing `altbank_gm14_tamtam_splash_transient` idiom, recalibrated to the sample).
- **Pitch-fold correctness (Option A)** — the sample's fundamental folds/repitches to the authored key within tolerance (so the Bb chord tone and C2 anchor land in tune) — this is the album-use guarantee.
- **Recalibrate** the existing altbank G1–G4 (`altbank.rs:2376-2515`) against the real sample; **keep G5 green** (CC0=0 tubular bells byte-identical); the modeled-`tam_tam` fallback keeps its own oracles for the `--no-samples` build.

Reference target during tuning: `mdmidiemu`/a real module, and the source sample itself.

## 7. Risks & open questions

1. **The pitch decision (§2)** — the one blocking call; everything downstream forks on it.
2. **Source existence/fitness** — does a genuinely-CC0, pitched, multi-layer gong ageng exist at usable quality? Unknown until inspected. If only a near-pitchless tam-tam is available at CC0, Option A is off the table and Option B's re-voice is forced.
3. **Repitch range** — a single gong sample repitched across the album's span (C2…~Bb, a few semitones) is well within the linear-interp comfort zone the toms already use (~1 octave); low risk. A wide melodic range would not be.
4. **Tail bounding** — the release-fade-on-note_off must actually free slots (there is no engine backstop); an unbounded ring authored densely is the one way this could grow memory/CPU. The oracle/`render()` contract must enforce a bounded tail.
5. **Crate placement** — orchestral vs a new percussion crate; decided by the source's layer count. A 36-layer gong ≈ +several MB embedded — weigh against the binary-size budget (the repo already carries 16.68 MB of attack transients + the drum kit).
6. **Semver** — new voice + new asset crate are internal (`pub(crate)`)/additive; a minor bump at most; one bump per integrated task, owned by integration.
