## 1. The problem

Arthur: "the drums are quite far forward in most of our mixes… are they all balanced
correctly versus each other?" He asked a near-identical question on 17 July; that pass
(the SC-55 trim) deliberately **exempted every percussive/plucked/struck voice**, because
whole-note RMS unfairly penalises a fast-decaying envelope. So half the orchestra has never
been calibrated at all.

Three gain layers exist, and none coordinates with the others:

1. `normalize_loudness()` — iterative measure → scale → true-peak-limit until the **whole
   mix** is within 0.3 dB of −18 LUFS. A master fader. Cannot change internal balance.
2. `PROGRAM_TRIM_DB[128]` — static per-program trim, SC-55-referenced, 0.70× damped,
   ±6 dB clamp, **sustained families only**. Piano, ChromPerc, Guitar, Bass, pizz/harp/
   timpani, orchestra-hit, SynthFX, Ethnic, Percussive, SoundFX are all 0.0.
3. Drums — `kit_balance(key)` per-key trims **plus** a global `DRUM_FORWARD = 2.0`.

## 2. Verdict: the `DRUM_FORWARD` justification is inverted

Commit `3de59f9` (14 Jul) argued: *"a flat drum fader is absorbed by the −18 LUFS normalize
+ the −1 dBTP limiter (a +6.8 dB probe moved the master ~0.4 dB), so that was never the
lever either."* That inference is backwards, and the general form is stronger than the
empirical one:

- **A scalar loudness normalization is balance-transparent by construction.** It multiplies
  every channel by the same number, so it cannot absorb a *relative* change at any
  drum-energy fraction. Whatever the master did, drums/band moved by exactly the fader.
  The commit confused the **absolute** master level (pinned at −18 by design) with the
  **internal** balance (untouched by the normalizer by design).
- **The 0.4 dB is the signature of a lever that works.** Solving
  `10·log10(1 − p + 4.79p) = 0.4` gives `p ≈ 0.025` — drums were ~2.5 % of mix K-energy at
  the time (~16 dB under the mix, consistent with the buried-hats kit that same commit was
  fixing). Almost the entire +6.8 dB passed through as *relative* lift; only 0.4 dB left as
  common-mode. Had the master moved the full 6.8 dB that still would not be absorption — it
  would mean the drums stay put absolutely and the **band** drops 6.8 dB.
- **The only genuine absorber is the true-peak limiter** — nonlinear, bites only above the
  ceiling, clamps transients not integrated loudness. Its *differential* bite between
  `DRUM_FORWARD` 1.0 and 2.0 is plausibly a few tenths to ~1 LU, not the 3 dB the code
  comment claims. That "+6 nominal → ~+3 audible" figure was an ear estimate made **under
  the mistaken absorption theory**, never a measurement.

Chain of events: the value was picked by ear, on one track (Hey Jude), on top of a kit whose
internal rebalance (hats +13 dB, snare +5, toms +4) had **already** raised total kit energy,
under a theory that predicted half the boost would vanish.

**Two levers moved on 14 July, not one.** Any recalibration must separate the `kit_balance`
internal fix (genuinely correct — keep it) from the `DRUM_FORWARD` bus lift (the
over-correction).

The doc comment at `engine.rs:68-74` encodes the backwards model and must be rewritten.

## 3. Measurement method — and a bias to avoid

Our first measurement compared **isolated stems** (`--solo`, peak-normalized, absolute level
recovered as `measured + 20·log10(peak/0.891)`). The peak arithmetic is correct, but the
stem comparison has two biases, both **inflating** the drum figure:

1. **BS.1770 gating over-reads sparse stems.** The drums-alone integrated figure counts only
   blocks where drums play; the "everything else" stem fills the timeline.
2. **Solo stems see less `BusGlue` reduction** than the same material does in the mix (the
   2:1 compressor at `engine.rs:867` keys off combined level).

Face-value stem reading suggested drums carried ~90 % of mix energy. **Leave-one-out**, which
has neither bias, gives the honest figure:

| track | full mix | mix − drums | drums add | drum share of mix energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper Kites | −12.39 | −14.11 | **+1.71 LU** | 32.6 % |
| Three-Sixty-One | −12.52 | −14.96 | **+2.44 LU** | 43.0 % |

Real and high, but far short of the stem reading. **Leave-one-out is the calibration target;
stem ratios are diagnostic only.**

## 4. Two design principles

**P1 — separate the two questions the 17 July audit conflated.**
(A) *Instrument calibration*: is a ferrosintesis snare/piano event the right loudness
relative to a ferrosintesis string pad, per event? (B) *Mix contribution*: how much of this
mix's loudness is the drums? No single number answers both. One metric each; each
load-bearing for its own lever — (A) governs `PROGRAM_TRIM_DB`-class trims, (B) governs
bus-level constants like `DRUM_FORWARD`.

**P2 — never compare across envelope classes on an absolute equal-loudness assumption;
always difference against the SC-55 measured with the same metric, window and probe.**
The whole-note-RMS failure was not the window as such — it was that ferro and the SC-55 have
*different decay envelopes for the same program*, so tail-length differences leaked into the
level estimate. A short-window upper-percentile statistic is insensitive to tail length, and
whatever residual class bias the window carries (a 400 ms window credits a long-ringing hit
~3–5 dB over a short one at equal peak) **cancels identically in the ferro-minus-SC55
differential**, because both sides carry it.

**This is what makes percussive calibration valid where the previous audit had to punt.** It
generalises the move the audit already made once, for GM6 harpsichord.

## 5. The two load-bearing metrics

### M-CAL — per-program calibration (question A)

```
momentary_lufs(stereo, fs) -> Vec<f32>     // K-weighted 400 ms blocks, 100 ms hop.
                                           // Factor out of integrated_lufs; keep pub(crate).
S95(stem):
    m   = momentary_lufs(stem, fs)
    act = { b in m : b >= max(m) - 20.0 and b >= -70.0 }    // active-block gate
    return percentile(sorted(act), 0.95)

// probe per GM program p (gen_probe.py idiom):
//   C3 and C4, velocities 72 and 110, 1.25 s apart, dry
//   (CC91/93/94 = 0 authored AFTER the PC — fx_profile re-derives sends), samples on.
// per-note S95 over [onset, onset + 1.2 s]; level(p) = median of the 4 notes.
D_p    = (level_ferro(p) - median_ferro) - (level_sc55(p) - median_sc55)
trim_p = round_half(clamp(-0.70 * D_p, -6.0, 6.0));   dead-band 1 dB

// velocity guard: if |D_p(v110) - D_p(v72)| > 3 dB -> EXCLUDE p and flag it as a
// vel_amp / velocity-curve defect. A static trim is the wrong lever there.
```

Identical recipe to 17 July — reference module, damping, clamp, dead-band unchanged — with
**S95 replacing whole-note RMS**. The SC-55 side is measured once, offline, via
`mdmidiemu --synth sc55`; only the resulting constants land in the repo.

*Rejected candidates and why:* 3 s short-term LUFS (longer than a percussive event + decay —
the artifact returns); *max* rather than percentile (one hot bar sets it; fragile);
note-gated integrated LUFS (duty-cycle blind — a channel playing once a bar scores like one
playing continuously; and gating on sparse stems is the very artifact in §3); full
Zwicker/Moore psychoacoustic loudness (hundreds of lines from scratch, **no in-repo external
oracle to pin it against**, and the extra fidelity buys nothing once all comparisons are
differential); partial-loudness/masking models (large, unverifiable in-repo — the underlying
question is answered empirically by leave-one-out instead).

### M-MIX — per-channel contribution (question B)

```
loo_delta(midi, c):
    full  = integrated_lufs(render(midi, opts.with_solo(0xFFFF)))
    minus = integrated_lufs(render(midi, opts.with_solo(0xFFFF & !(1<<c))))
    return full - minus          // LU; RAW pre-normalization buffers both times
```

Measures contribution through the actual nonlinear chain — spectral/temporal overlap, glue
interaction, gating — with no additivity assumption. Deterministic (fixed seeds).
Implementable today through the sealed public API (`with_solo`, `offline::render`,
`integrated_lufs`); **no new `Options` knob, so a patch bump, not minor.**
Normalizing each variant to −18 first would destroy the delta — must be the raw buffer.

## 7. Architecture verdict

**Static calibration, extended — dynamic per-render auto-balance rejected.**

Four changes: (1) recalibrate `DRUM_FORWARD` against M-MIX by ladder
{1.0, 1.19, 1.41, 1.68, 2.0}, picking the rung that lands O1's groove **and** three real
album excerpts inside the reference band — expectation is 1.0–1.3, i.e. the 14 July
complaint was mostly the internal kit balance and the bus lift was the over-correction, but
*the ladder decides, not the prediction*; (2) rewrite the `engine.rs:68-74` comment with the
corrected model; (3) extend `PROGRAM_TRIM_DB` to the percussive families per §5;
(4) leave `normalize_loudness` and `kit_balance` alone.

**Strongest case for dynamic auto-balance, stated honestly:** the goal is perceptual balance
per piece; a static table cannot know that *this* arrangement buries the flute; a
measure-and-correct pass would fix all 135 tracks and every foreign MIDI without touching a
`.mid`; and it can be strictly deterministic, so "reproducible renders" survives.

**Why it loses anyway:**

1. GM defines a mix as instrument calibration (the module's voicing) × authored controllers.
   A faithful player owns the first and obeys the second. An auto-balancer is a third agent
   second-guessing both, and it **structurally cannot distinguish** "the synth mis-calibrates
   the flute" (synth's fault — fix once, statically) from "the composer buried the flute"
   (intent — keep). Every failure in this saga lived in the calibration layer. Fix the layer
   that failed.
2. This repo's method is **oracle-first authored dynamics** (`check_arc` contours,
   `analyze.py` RMS mirrors). A dynamic balancer sits between the composed arc and the render
   and actively fights it — a channel that swells gets pulled back *because* its contribution
   grew. Every existing audio oracle would become a measurement of the balancer's policy
   rather than of the composition.
3. A `.mid` would lose its single meaning: the render becomes a function of a mixing policy
   that will itself be tuned over time, so A/B-ing any composition change is confounded with
   the balancer's response to it.
4. As engineering it is a global feedback loop around a nonlinear chain (glue → limiter →
   gated meter) with convergence and stability obligations. The static table is O(1), already
   half-built, and already has an oracle culture.

Legitimate homes for that energy: **measurement without correction** (the O3 report) now;
and, only if a real foreign-file need materialises, an explicit opt-in `Options` knob (minor
bump under the sealed-builder pattern). **Do not build the knob speculatively** — that is the
M8 on-ramp.

## 9. Tripwires — stop and re-escalate

1. **The sweep is not a uniform shift.** If drum-forwardness is bimodal by mix-date cohort,
   size the `DRUM_FORWARD` cut to cohort 1 and handle cohort 2 by CC7 re-touch — **do not
   split the difference and make both cohorts wrong.** If the hot tail instead correlates
   with *hat density*, the culprit is `kit_balance`'s +13 dB hat trim and the fix layer
   changes (moderate `kit_balance`, keep the bus).
   *(Status: CONFIRMED non-uniform — see §10. This tripwire has already tripped.)*
2. **Ear vs SC-55 divergence.** The SC-55's kit balance is itself an opinion (a polite one).
   If Arthur's speaker-verified preference sits > ~2 LU from the SC-55 band — including if
   Hey Jude sounds *wrong* after recalibration — pin O1's band to his ear and record that in
   the oracle comment. Escalate rather than silently choosing.
3. **LOO instability.** If ΔI_drums varies > ~0.5 LU across excerpts of the same track, widen
   O1's band or pin a longer excerpt; if it stays unstable, fall back to un-normalized stem
   ratios with glue disabled — and re-escalate, because that breaks the premise that LOO is
   the robust contribution proxy.
4. **S95 and LOO disagree systematically** (per-hit calibration says balanced, contribution
   says drums hot). The residual is *event density* — the composer's choice, not a synth
   error. **Do not chase density with gain.**
5. **Velocity-dependent imbalance.** `D_p` spreading > 3 dB between vel 72 and 110 means the
   defect is in `vel_amp`/the voice's velocity curve; a static trim would fix one dynamic and
   break another. Excluded and flagged by the guard.
6. **Extreme envelope mismatch.** If ferro renders a program as a one-shot where the SC-55
   sustains it (or vice versa), even S95 differentials mislead — keep those exempt and
   documented, like the FX families.
7. **Render-diff contamination.** The `DRUM_FORWARD` change must alter *only* albums with
   ch-9 / XG-drum content (both paths carry the constant — `engine.rs:2968` and `:2996`).
   Melodic stems inside drum albums may shift slightly via glue relaxation — expected. Any
   change to a **drum-free** album is a bug, full stop.

## 10. Catalogue evidence (17-track sweep, stem ratio — diagnostic only)

Drums-minus-band, isolated stems (inflated per §3, but the **spread** is the finding):

| album | tracks | drums − band |
|---|---|---|
| fable5 — The Burning Meridian | 1 | **+7.51** |
| gpt5-6 — Atlas of Becoming | 4 | +1.73, +3.85, **+6.58**, **+6.36** |
| opus4-8 — amarok (Riverwake) | 1 | **+4.58** |
| fable5 — Big Weather / Hollow Hill / Signal Fire | 3 | +0.09, −1.79, −0.42 |
| gpt5-5 — Hours After Rain | 4 | −6.39, −6.03, −6.20, −5.63 |
| gpt5-3-spark | 4 | −8.88, −7.65, −9.76, −8.51 |

`n = 17` · **median −1.79 LU** · min −9.76 · max +7.51 · drums exceed the whole band on
**7 of 17** tracks.

**The variance is between *albums*, not random — a ~17 LU spread.** Each album's composer (a
different model) authored ch-10 velocity/CC7 conventions differently. gpt5-3-spark and gpt5-5
write drums 6–10 LU *back*; gpt5-6, Burning Meridian and amarok write them 4–7 LU *forward*.

Note the median track sits **below** the band. "Drums are forward in most of our mixes" is
true of the albums Arthur actually listens to (fable5 flagships, Atlas of Becoming), not of
the catalogue as a whole — a distinction that matters when sizing any global change.

**Consequence, and it is load-bearing for the plan:** no single synth-side constant can be
right for all of them. Removing `DRUM_FORWARD` outright shifts everything down ~6 dB, which
rescues the hot albums and **buries the already-quiet ones**. This is tripwire 1 confirmed,
and it is the strongest available argument for Arthur's two-step plan: fix the *synth* to a
defensible reference, then re-mix *albums* individually.

## 11. Decisions taken (Arthur)

- Align to the SC-55 so we are compatible with the pre-existing GM corpus. **Rationale is
  fidelity, not taste** — and it is what rules out dynamic auto-balance.
- Remove `DRUM_FORWARD` rather than retune it: a global kit lift is a *mixing* decision baked
  into the instrument, and albums wanting forward drums should author ch-10 CC7.
- Full reappraisal — **not anchored by the past**; the 0.70× damping and ±6 dB clamp are
  re-examined, not inherited.
- Exemplar tracks for ear validation: **Paper Kites** (`albums/fable5/Big Weather`) and
  **Three-Sixty-One** (`albums/fable5/Through Lines`).
