## 1. Why candidate 1 must not ship

Candidate 1 derived percussive trims from **max BS.1770 momentary block** (a 400 ms K-weighted
energy *mean*) differenced against the SC-55. Arthur's per-instrument ear pass on the neutral
audition contradicted the metric in **sign** on ~8 voices. Root cause (measured, not argued):

- A 400 ms mean **dilutes a note shorter than 400 ms by its duty-cycle**. The ferro−SC-55
  differential cancels that bias **only if both engines share the note's energy-time shape** —
  which they don't (different synthesis).
- Measured envelopes (Fable, from the probe WAVs): ferro GM28 palm-mute is a ~46 ms chunk
  (t90) diluted **8.7 dB**; the SC-55's rings 660 ms, diluted 2.6 dB → **~6 dB of the measured
  "deficit" is pure envelope artifact**, so the +6 boost made the *attack* ~2 dB hot. ferro
  GM33 bass decays (late/early −8.6 dB) while the SC-55 sustains flat (−1.5 dB) → the max block
  reads the *attack*, calling the voice "matched" while its audible *body* sits ~7 dB low.
- The error runs **both directions**: it over-reads attack-front voices (pianos, GM33, tuba →
  under-trim, leaves quiet) and under-reads short/spread/swell voices (palm-mute, dulcimer,
  pad, shamisen → over-boost, leaves loud). One bias, program-dependent → **no constant can
  fix it**.
- The "40/58 sustained validated" gate proves nothing here: a sustained tone is flat ≥400 ms
  in *both* engines, so dilution ≈ 0 — the gate validated the one class where the failure
  cannot occur. (And it was itself soft: 18/58 exceed 1.5 dB, worst 5 dB.)

**Verdict (all three): max-momentary is fit for sustained, UNFIT as the sole metric for the
percussive class.**

## 2. The fix — a multi-window, envelope-guarded metric

Not a from-scratch psychoacoustic (sones) model — the dominant error is *temporal*, so a
multi-window measurement on the existing K-weighted meter captures it. The spectral
(critical-band/equal-loudness) refinement is deferred (buys only the residual ~2 dB cases).

1. **Multiple time windows per note.** Measure paired ferro−SC-55 K-weighted level in separate
   windows: **attack** (~0–100 ms, the ear's integration scale), **body** (~0.4–1.0 s while
   held), **tail** (~0.4–0.8 s post). (Codex's finer split: 0–50, 50–200, 200–400, 400–800 ms.)
2. **Use the window that matches the SC-55's envelope.** Body where the *reference* note
   sustains (t90 > 400 ms — basses, most guitars); attack where it's a true one-shot
   (t90 < 200 ms — orch hit, timpani, short perc).
3. **Envelope guard (twin of the velocity guard).** If the windows disagree by **> 3 dB**
   (per key, per velocity), **no static trim is valid** — the voices differ in *shape*, not
   level → route the program to a **voice-fix list**, do not emit a strip trim. GM28 fails this
   by construction (a voice-model mismatch a strip trim can never reconcile).
4. **Apply the guard PER KEY before taking medians.** GM107 koto (~13 dB per-key tilt,
   MM-BUG-KILN-00039) and any pitch-tilted voice are then excluded automatically.

## 3. Fix the probe (Codex's catch)

The current probe measured the wrong voice for some programs: **GM33's sample bank ends at D2,
so 10 of its 12 probe pitches fell to the fallback bare model** (`sampler.rs:617/2687`), and
the per-key ferro−SC-55 spread was ~10 dB. Candidate 2 must:
- Use **natural-register pitches per program** (not one fixed pitch set).
- **Flag, per note, whether it exercised the sampled or the fallback path**, and either keep to
  the sampled range or measure both explicitly.

## 4. Fix the anchor — but it's a minor lever (Codex measured it)

Median-128 vs sustained-anchor vs piano-anchor move GM28/33 by only ~1 dB — the temporal
envelope is the whole story, the anchor a refinement. Still, do it right:
- Use **`median(F_q − S_q)` over a vetted cohort** of envelope-stable, non-guarded **sustained**
  programs — a **paired-difference** median, NOT two independent medians (the current
  `(ferro_p − ferro_med) − (sc55_p − sc55_med)` subtracts two independent medians).
- Median-128 pollutes the anchor with the ~44 biased percussive readings. Piano-anchor is worst
  (one envelope-mismatched program; ~+10.6 dB offset). Sanity: sustained-anchor vs 128-anchor
  shift should be < 1 dB — if larger, investigate before trusting either.

## 5. Clamp & voice-gain policy

**Keep the ±6 dB clamp.** A required trim > 6 dB is a **signal the voice's base gain is wrong**,
not a limitation to widen around. For the basses (GM32/34/35, ear+metric agree they are −8…−13
dB quiet — see MM-BUG-KILN-00016, bass family model-only, missing sampled attack/presence):
raise `amp` on BASS/UPRIGHT/FRETLESS **and re-balance the LA sample-layer gains (`LA_EBASS`/
`LA_PIZZBASS`) in lockstep** — the crossfade seam is calibrated against the current model amp —
then **re-run M-CAL** so the residual strip trim lands inside ±6. Widening the clamp would mask
a voice bug behind a balance knob.

## 6. Ordering & ear arbitration

1. **Timbre bugs first** (MM-BUG-KILN-00036–00041): a level pass on a broken voice is wasted.
2. Re-derive percussive trims with the guarded metric; **emit a trim only where the guard
   passes**.
3. **Guard-excluded voices**: fix the voice (envelope/timbre/gain) or ear-tune the strip by
   **repeated, randomized direct SC-55 A/B** — chasing *sign flips*, not ±1 dB (the ear pass is
   one imprecise 10-min pass at one SPL; its sign contradictions are the valid signal, its
   fine residuals are not).

## 7. Tripwires — stop and re-escalate

- **Attack window over-weights authored clicks** (palm chuff `click:1.4`, LA onsets). If
  attack-derived trims start contradicting the ear on clicky voices, exclude the first ~10 ms or
  lean on the body window.
- **Body window assumes held notes.** A voice played staccato in an album shifts perception back
  to attack; a body-tuned trim then reads wrong in context. A static trim can never close a
  decay-*slope* mismatch — only a voice fix can.
- **Voice-amp raises are not free**: they change drive into per-voice nonlinearities and move the
  LA crossfade seam → re-run the render-diff inventory and re-audition *timbre*, not just level.
- **BusGlue sits AFTER the strip** (`engine.rs:~3071`, unconditional 2:1 on bass-heavy programs
  with a low shelf) — a strip trim does not map 1:1 to output; verify the applied trim moves
  output by its nominal amount.
- **Everything is referenced to `mdmidiemu`, not hardware** — a per-program emulator level error
  is invisible to the whole method.
- **The sustained anchor presumes those trims are right**, but the ear flagged GM50/58 inside it.
  Sanity-check before trusting the frame.
