---

## Goal (one line)

Let a MIDI file author the driven-guitar amp and cabinet per channel, so GM29 and GM30 can be
built into genuinely different rigs from the score — with every file that does not author them
rendering byte-for-byte as it does today.

## The ask, in Arthur's words

> "An Epiphone going through X pedal to a Marshall cab can sound quite different from a Les Paul
> going through Y pedal to a different cab. Maybe what I'm really asking for is some more knobs
> which we can use to control the distortion & overdrive."

> "Has to be author — that's where the real magic will happen. We've got all the knobs available
> in the synth — exposing them is an obvious cool thing to do."

The knobs already exist. They are hard-coded constants inside `Drive`. This design is about
**reaching** them safely, not inventing them.

## Why this needs a design at all

The knobs are the easy part. Three things are not:

1. **`Drive` is destroyed and rebuilt** on Program Change, on CC0 bank select, and on CC121
   (`engine.rs:2626`, `:2409`, `:2643`). A rebuild throws away nine biquad states, the `MicroCab`
   delay line, and heap-allocates a fresh buffer. Any parameter that feeds construction is
   therefore either lost at the next rebuild or becomes a click generator.
2. **This exact area has bitten us twice.** MM-BUG-KILN-00033: authored CC93/CC94 discarded at
   Program Change, costing three Big Weather tracks minutes of lead-guitar echo. MM-BUG-KILN-00034:
   an NRPN select corrupting the RPN bend range to 24 semitones. This design edits the very guard
   added for 00034.
3. **The surface is permanent.** Once an album authors these into a committed `.mid`, that file is
   the reproducible source of truth. A wrong parameter number, or a knob whose top end aliases, is
   then baked into album source.

---

## Scope

**In:**
- Six per-channel amp parameters, addressed by NRPN, applied as **offsets** from the shipped
  program voicing.
- In-place (click-free, allocation-free) application — no `Drive` rebuild.
- A defined state table across Program Change / CC0 / CC121 / GM System On / XG System On.
- Correct RPN⇄NRPN latch mutual exclusion, and closing the `data_msb` side channel (§7).
- Oracles for each of the above, plus the inertness invariant.

**Out (non-goals):**
- **Changing any default.** The shipped 29/30 × main/alt matrix from `e2a78c1` is untouched. The
  render-diff for this change must be **0 of 124**. What the defaults *should* be is Part B, after
  Arthur's ear — the knobs are how we find out, so guessing now would be backwards.
- An enumerated cabinet/pedal palette (named amps).
- The batch variant-matrix renderer and the live-tweak app. Both are downstream of this surface.
- A Python authoring helper in the album engines. One line per album, when an album wants it.
- Exposing every constant. `tilt`, `MicroCab` depth, `dcb` and the filter Qs stay program-derived.
  Adding a seventh knob later is cheap; removing one from a shipped file is not.

## Approach

### 1. The parameter set — six knobs

Arthur's framing is guitar → pedal → cab. The knobs follow it:

| # | Knob | Stage | Drives | Why it earns a slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Drive** | pedal | `g1`, `g2` | The gain. The most identity-defining control on any dirty rig. |
| 1 | **Tone** | pedal | `voice` peak gain | Mid push ↔ scoop *before* the clipper — what separates a Tube Screamer from a Big Muff. Dynamics-dependent by nature; see §2.1. |
| 2 | **Tightness** | pre-amp | `pre` HPF corner | Measured in round 1 as the biggest "focus vs woolly" lever. Low end reaching a clipper eats headroom and smears IMD across every harmonic — damage no post-EQ can undo. |
| 3 | **Body** | cabinet | `cab[1]` peak gain | The reference panel put ferro 9–12 dB under both SC-55 and S-YXG50 in the low-mids. Now under authorial control rather than my taste. |
| 4 | **Presence** | cabinet | `cab[2]` peak gain | Cut vs smoothness. |
| 5 | **Cab Tone** | cabinet | `cab[3]`/`cab[4]` corner, **downward only** | Open-back vs closed-back, V30 vs Blue, cap vs off-axis mic are all first and foremost a high-frequency corner. This is the single largest perceptual "which cab" axis, and — unlike Tone — it sits after the clipper, so its effect is level-independent. |

**Bite (`bias`) was in the draft and is cut.** Two independent measurements killed it: `g1` and
`bias` enter the shaper as one expression (`(v*g1 + bias).tanh()`), so Drive swings the
even-harmonic ratio — Bite's entire reason for existing — further than Bite does, which makes the
separability criterion unsatisfiable by construction; and round 1 had already evaluated `bias` as a
voicing axis and *deliberately refused to vary it*, because below ~0.75 the referenced-tanh
asymmetry washes out and the `driven_sustain_stays_distorted` floors carry only 1.4–4.1 dB of
margin. Exposing a range 4.5× wider than the one already judged too destabilising would have been
the design contradicting its own predecessor.

**Cab Tone is downward-only on purpose.** `cab[3]`/`cab[4]` are the anti-alias decimation cliff
(`CAB_CLIFF`), so lowering the corner only ever *strengthens* alias rejection. Allowing it upward
would trade the alias budget for brightness — exactly the failure §2's Drive analysis is guarding
against.

### 2. Offsets, not absolutes — 64 is "as shipped"

Every parameter is `0..127` with **64 = no change**: a signed offset on whatever the program+bank
voicing already provides. This is the load-bearing decision, and it buys three things:

- **Inertness is structural.** An unauthored channel runs the existing code path unchanged.
- **The tuned matrix survives.** GM29-alt and GM30-alt were tuned against oracles in `e2a78c1`.
  Absolutes would discard that and force every author to dial a whole amp from nothing.
- **Two rigs from one gesture.** Pushing 29 one way and 30 the other starts from voicings that are
  already distinct, so a modest offset lands somewhere musical.

#### Value curves

Linear maps are the trap — `amp_sim`'s own comment says so: *"a linear map saturates the first
stage too early and Drive barely moves the tone"*. Gain and frequency are perceived geometrically,
so they map geometrically; EQ is already in dB.

Let `n = (v - 64) / 64`, so `n ∈ [-1, +0.984]` and **`n = 0` exactly at `v = 64`**.

| # | Knob | Law | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Drive | `g1 *= 2^(k·n)`, `g2 *= 2^(k·n/2)` | **`k` set by measurement — see below** | |
| 1 | Tone | `voice_db += 9n` | −9 … +9 dB | effect size is level-dependent (§2.1) |
| 2 | Tightness | `pre_hz *= 2^(0.6n)` | ×0.66 … ×1.5 (≈59–135 Hz from a 90 Hz base; 79–180 from 120) | ceiling 180 Hz |
| 3 | Body | `cab[1]_db += 9n` | −9 … +9 dB | |
| 4 | Presence | `cab[2]_db += 9n` | −9 … +9 dB | |
| 5 | Cab Tone | `corner *= 2^(n)` for `n ≤ 0`, else 1 | ×0.5 … ×1 (cliff 4000→2000 Hz) | never raises the corner |
| — | `post` | **derived, §2.2** | — | not authorable |

**Drive's exponent `k` is deliberately left open, to be fixed by measurement during
implementation.** The draft proposed `k = 2` (g1 ×0.25…×4). Review measured that at the top of
that range the worst fold-back bin rises to about **−36 dB, above the −40 dB budget
`drive_alias_floor` already asserts** — the code itself documents alias level as "exponentially
sensitive to drive". So the binding constraint on the top of this knob is the alias floor, not
taste. `k` is the largest value that holds −40 dBc across 4 (program × bank) × 3 fundamentals; if
`k = 2` fails, `k = 1.2` (g1 ×2.3) is still a musically large move. **Deciding this by measurement
before the surface is committed to a `.mid` is the entire reason this design exists** — once an
album authors Drive = 127, that number is frozen in album source.

The draft's per-knob clamps are removed. Five of six could not be reached by any shipped
(program, bank), so they were decoration; the curve bounds are the safety, and the alias, level and
range-safety oracles (AC 8) are what actually prove it. Only Tightness keeps an explicit ceiling
(180 Hz), because there the curve alone is not the constraint — round 1 measured a 120 Hz corner
costing ≈7.5 dB of fundamental at E2, and a 400 Hz corner (the draft's) removes 6.7–19 dB of a low
E while doing essentially nothing at A4. That is a mute, not a tone control.

#### 2.1 Tone is dynamics-dependent, and that is correct

Tone sits before two deeply-saturated tanh stages, so its effect is a function of signal level:
measured, ±9 dB of pre-clip EQ moves the mid band **0.50 dB on a loud note and 7.07 dB on a quiet
one**. That is not a defect — it is how a real overdrive pedal behaves, and round 1's two consults
both concluded that pre-clip EQ "decides which partials dominate the clipper" rather than acting as
a tone control. But it has two consequences the design must own:

- Its oracle (AC 3) must be measured at a **stated input level and pitch**, or the sweep will look
  broken at one velocity and fine at another.
- The README must say plainly that its depth tracks playing dynamics, so an author is not surprised
  when it thins out under a hard pick.

**Cab Tone exists partly to cover this gap**: it is the level-independent tone axis, because it sits
after the nonlinearity.

#### 2.2 `post` — an exact-identity ratio, and an honest tolerance

The draft claimed `post` was level-compensated "exactly as `amp_sim` does". That was wrong, and all
three reviewers caught it. `amp_sim`'s `post = 0.28 / (1 + 1.5d)` is a function of an absolute drive
parameter that the program path does not have; the four shipped values (0.12, 0.145, 0.0789, 0.0714)
are hand-measured constants that no such formula reproduces. A literal implementation would have
changed the level of every driven channel at the neutral setting — breaking bit-identity at 64.

The law is therefore a **monotone, strictly positive trim anchored at the knob's neutral point**:

```
post = base_post · 2^(−c·n)          c fixed by measurement, 0 ≤ c ≤ k
```

At `n = 0` this is `base_post × 2^0` = `base_post × 1.0` exactly, so the shipped constants survive
bit-exactly and AC 3's "64 is identical" holds by construction rather than by luck.

An earlier draft of this section reused `amp_sim`'s shape as `m(x) = 1 + 1.5·(2^(k·x) − 1)`. That is
**wrong outside `amp_sim`'s domain** and the cross-family review caught it: `amp_sim`'s `d` is
non-negative by construction (`d = drive/127`), whereas `n` here goes negative. At `n = −1` that form
gives a 6.5× makeup at `k = 1.2`, 33× at `k = 1.5`, **a division by zero at `k = log₂3 ≈ 1.585`, and
a negative `post` — a polarity inversion — at `k = 2`**. The exponential form above cannot go
singular or change sign at any `k`, which is the whole reason to prefer it.

**And it is a partial compensation, stated as such.** The level error a makeup trim must cancel is
not a function of the knob alone — it depends on pitch and input level too, so no `post(v)` fully
flattens it. The design does not claim otherwise. Instead AC 6 pins the residual by measurement
(`drive_level_probe` is already the harness), at two pitches and two input levels; if the residual
exceeds the stated tolerance, that is a finding to report, not a number to quietly widen.

### 3. Addressing — NRPN, `MSB = 0x30`

| Message | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CC99 = 0x30` | NRPN MSB — the ferrosintesis amp block |
| `CC98 = 0..5` | NRPN LSB — parameter index from §1 |
| `CC6 = v` | Data Entry MSB — the 0..127 value |

**Why NRPN and not the alternatives:**

- **Not undefined CCs** (20–31, 102–119). Cheap to author, but ferrosintesis is a *generic GM
  player*, and foreign files genuinely use those numbers. Silently reinterpreting them would be a
  fidelity bug in a player whose whole promise is faithfulness.
- **Not SysEx.** Verbose, needs a manufacturer ID we do not have, awkward from the stdlib-only
  Python engines — and the existing XG variation SysEx is the wrong shape anyway: one global insert
  on one part, structurally incapable of giving two channels two different rigs.
- **NRPN is the standard mechanism for exactly this.** "Non-registered" means manufacturer-defined
  by construction; per-channel; trivially authorable; and it already reaches both the offline and
  live paths through the shared `fn cc`.

**Collision risk — bounded, not eliminated.** NRPN has *no manufacturer namespace*: MIDI explicitly
permits different devices to reuse the same NRPN number, so no allocation is collision-free in the
abstract, and the cross-family review is right that the draft's "cannot collide" was overclaimed.
Casio's XW-P1/G1, for one, uses `0x30:00..05` for its own oscillator controls. The honest position
is that a foreign file targeting a Casio synth *could* carry NRPN `0x30` — and that this is
**harmless here**, for two structural reasons that do not depend on the number:

- The parameters are **ignored entirely on any channel whose program is not 29 or 30**, so a file
  that never plays a ferrosintesis driven guitar can never be affected, whatever it authors.
- On a channel that *does* play GM29/30, the worst case is an **amp tweak** — never a stuck note, a
  wrong pitch, or silence. Contrast KILN-00034, whose blast radius was every bend on the channel.

The genuinely collision-free alternative is a manufacturer-specific SysEx carrying a channel byte —
but that needs an assigned MIDI manufacturer ID, which this project does not hold and cannot
self-assign for a published crate. So SysEx is not actually available to us; NRPN is the best
mechanism we can use, and the containment above is what makes it safe rather than the number being
unique. The MSB stays `0x30` but the claim in this design is "contained", not "collision-free". If a
real Casio-file conflict ever surfaces, the block can move — the number lives in exactly one place
(the README contract and the CC99 match arm), never in album source, because albums author it
through a named `engine.py` helper.

### 4. Where the state lives — `Strip`, not `Drive`

```rust
// Strip
amp_params:   [u8;  AMP_PARAM_COUNT],  // authored 0..127, all 64 = neutral
amp_cur:      [f32; AMP_PARAM_COUNT],  // slewed knob position (block rate)
amp_primed:   [bool; AMP_PARAM_COUNT], // per-parameter: snap on first authoring, slew after
amp_authored: bool,                    // any parameter has been written (gates the whole path)
nrpn_msb: u8, nrpn_lsb: u8,            // NRPN latch, 127/127 = null
nrpn_data_msb: u8,                     // Data Entry MSB scoped to the NRPN latch (§7)
```

`amp_primed` is **per parameter, not a single flag** — the cross-family review caught that a single
bool would prime only the first of a same-tick six-parameter rig and ramp the other five up from 64.
It mirrors the `volume_primed`/`pan_primed` pair the repo already uses, generalised to the array:
each knob snaps on its own first authoring and slews thereafter.

`Drive` is rebuilt on three different events, so it cannot hold authored state — the KILN-00033
lesson restated: **program-derived values are defaults; authored values are channel state and
outrank them.** A rebuild re-derives the program voicing, then re-applies the author's intent.

`amp_authored` is the same opt-in idiom the repo already uses for `chorus_authored`,
`delay_authored`, `expr_authored`, `mod_authored` and `breath_authored`. It gates the entire new
code path, so an unauthored channel executes exactly the instructions it does today.

### 5. `Drive` stores its own base voicing — one copy, not two

`Biquad` stores only coefficients (`b0,b1,b2,a1,a2` + state); there is **no way to recover a
filter's centre frequency, Q or gain from a live instance**. The base values exist only as literals
inside `Drive::new`, `cab_biquads` and `cab_biquads_lead`. A naive `apply_params` would therefore
have to restate the whole voicing table — and Part B is *chartered to change that table*. The two
copies would silently diverge, and nothing would catch it: the 0-of-124 render-diff is unaffected
(no album authors NRPN), so the only symptom would be an authored channel rendering the old cabinet
while an unauthored one renders the new.

So `Drive` carries its base:

```rust
struct DriveBase {
    pre_hz: f32,
    voice: (f32, f32, f32),   // hz, q, db
    cab1:  (f32, f32, f32),
    cab2:  (f32, f32, f32),
    cliff: (f32, f32),        // cab[3], cab[4] corners
    g1: f32, g2: f32, post: f32,
}
```

`cab_biquads` / `cab_biquads_lead` return the parameter triples; `from_stages` builds the biquads
*and* stores the triples. One source of truth, written where the literals already live, read by
`apply_params`. Desync becomes structurally impossible rather than a thing tests must chase.

### 6. Application — in place, slewed, no rebuild

```rust
fn apply_params(&mut self, cur: &[f32; AMP_PARAM_COUNT])
```

recomputes the affected coefficients from `self.base` plus the offsets and writes them into the
existing biquads **without touching `x1/x2/y1/y2`, the `MicroCab` line, or `prev`**.

**No `dsp.rs` change is needed.** `Biquad::retune_peak` and `retune_highpass` already exist
(`dsp.rs:544`, `:516`) beside `retune_lowpass` — added for the reed voices, which move formant gain
with blowing pressure at control rate. The click-free in-place retune is a proven in-repo pattern,
not a new mechanism. This change touches `engine.rs` only.

**Smoothing runs at two layers, deliberately.**

- *Knob position* (`amp_cur`) slews toward the authored target at **block rate**, one-pole, using the
  existing `WAH_SLEW_S = 0.02` (20 ms) idiom — a precomputed coefficient, not a second invented time
  constant. This is what turns a *large authored jump* (Body 64 → 127 in a single message mid-note)
  into a ~20 ms ramp of small per-block coefficient steps. It matters because an in-place
  `retune_peak` swaps coefficients immediately and retaining `x1..y2` does **not**, on its own, smooth
  a *large* coefficient step — the cross-family review was right to flag this. Small per-block steps
  plus retained state *are* click-free (this is exactly how the reed voices already move formant gain
  under CC2); the block-rate knob slew is what keeps the steps small.
- *Drive's scalars* (`g1`, `g2`, `post`) additionally slew **per sample** inside `Drive::process`,
  toward the block target derived from `amp_cur[Drive]` — a handful of adds in a loop that already
  runs twice per sample and evaluates nine biquads. The scalars have no filter downstream to smooth
  them, so without this a swept Drive would staircase at `sr/BLOCK` = 689 Hz — the exact artefact this
  design names as its hazard, and the one the draft's block-only plan would have produced.

**Priming.** Each parameter snaps on its first authoring (`amp_primed[i]` false → true) rather than
ramping from 64, so a rig authored at t = 0 is in effect on the first sample. And a `Drive` rebuilt
by a Program Change or bank select **starts its scalars and `amp_cur` from the persisted values, not
from `base`** — otherwise the rebuild itself would briefly slew the whole rig up from neutral, an
audible seam the persistence in §4 exists precisely to avoid.

### 7. The RPN ⇄ NRPN latch, and the `data_msb` side channel

Storing a real NRPN number means the CC98/99 arm changes, so the KILN-00034 guard must be preserved
deliberately rather than by accident:

```rust
98  => { s.nrpn_lsb = val; s.rpn_msb  = 127; s.rpn_lsb  = 127; }  // NRPN select nulls RPN
99  => { s.nrpn_msb = val; s.rpn_msb  = 127; s.rpn_lsb  = 127; }
100 => { s.rpn_lsb  = val; s.nrpn_msb = 127; s.nrpn_lsb = 127; }  // ...and symmetrically
101 => { s.rpn_msb  = val; s.nrpn_msb = 127; s.nrpn_lsb = 127; }
```

Mutual exclusion is correct MIDI semantics — the most recent select wins.

**But the latch is not the only path, and the draft was wrong to claim it made KILN-00034
"impossible in both directions".** `s.data_msb = val` at `engine.rs:2424` runs on *every* CC6
regardless of which latch is live, and an LSB-only CC38 later reuses it. So:

```
CC99=0x30, CC98=0, CC6=100   → nothing written (RPN latch null) BUT data_msb = 100
CC101=0, CC100=0             → RPN 0,0 selected
CC38=0                       → (msb,lsb) = (100,0) → bend_range = clamp(100,1,24) = 24.0
```

That is the exact KILN-00034 symptom reached *without* the latch. The hazard is pre-existing and no
in-repo album emits CC38 — but this change would be **the first thing in the codebase that makes an
album author large CC6 values under an NRPN latch**, converting a dormant foreign-file hazard into
one our own source could trigger.

Fix: **record Data Entry only when a selector is actually live, and scope it to that selector.** CC6
writes `nrpn_data_msb` when the NRPN latch is non-null, `data_msb` when the RPN latch is non-null,
and **is ignored when both are null**. The cross-family review caught that "write `data_msb`
otherwise" still left the hole open from the *initial* null/null state: `CC6=100` with no selector,
then RPN 0,0 select, then `CC38=0`, still reaches `bend_range = 24`. A Data Entry with no live
selector is meaningless MIDI and must simply be dropped. Strictly additive — no RPN-only file
changes behaviour, because the only sequences affected are ones already broken today (this also
tightens a latent pre-existing hole, not just the one this feature would open).

`reset_all_controllers` must null the NRPN pair and both data MSBs. RP-015 says "the RPN/NRPN
selector"; the existing doc comment at `engine.rs:2683` already says NRPN, but only the RPN half was
implemented because the NRPN half did not exist.

### 8. Reset and state semantics

| Event | Amp parameters | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Program Change** | **Preserved**, re-applied to the rebuilt `Drive` | MM-BUG-KILN-00033 exactly: program values are defaults, authored values are channel state. |
| **Program Change off 29/30** | Preserved (dormant; `drive` is `None`) | The rig returns if the program does. |
| **CC0 bank select** | **Preserved**, re-applied | The bank picks a voicing, not the author's intent. |
| **CC0 = 127 (XG drum bank)** | Preserved (dormant) | Forces `alt_bank` false and rebuilds; ch-10 routing means no driven voice sounds. |
| **CC121 Reset All Controllers** | **Preserved**; both latches and data MSBs nulled | RP-015 nulls the *selector*, not the values a selector wrote — the precise distinction KILN-00033 was fixed to honour. |
| **GM System On** | **Cleared** | `hard_reset` reconstructs every `Strip`. |
| **XG System On** | **Preserved** | XG-effects-only by design. |
| **GS Reset** | **Preserved** | Clears `gs_drum` only. |

**Precedence against the XG amp-sim insert.** The XG variation insert *replaces* the program drive
(`engine.rs:3090`, an `if / else if`). That stays exactly as it is: when an XG insert is active it
wins wholesale and the amp offsets are dormant, re-engaging if the insert is removed. One amp and
one cabinet at a time — the rule the XG HLD already pinned. The existing branch is untouched.

## Acceptance criteria

The tolerances and floors named below are **pinned as reviewed test constants before the sweeps are
run**, not slid afterward to match whatever the implementation produces. Where a bound can only come
from measurement (Drive's `k`, the level residual), the criterion says so explicitly and the measured
value is *reported*, not silently adopted.

1. **Inertness, by render-diff against the pre-feature binary.** All 124 album MIDIs, rendered by the
   feature build, are **bit-identical** to the baseline binary built at `852a19d` — 0 changed. The
   comparison target is the frozen pre-feature executable, so this cannot pass tautologically.
2. **Inertness on driven-guitar renders, by exact comparison.** A set of probe MIDIs that *do* play
   GM29/30 (both banks) but author **no** amp NRPN render sample-exact — byte-for-byte — against the
   same `852a19d` baseline binary. This is the strong inertness test: it proves the refactor changed
   the driven path's output nowhere, not merely that an untouched channel is untouched.
3. **XG amp-sim path unchanged.** Because `from_stages` and `cab_biquads` are shared with
   `Drive::amp_sim` (`engine.rs:684`, `:725`) and **no album sends XG SysEx**, the album diff cannot
   see a regression there. A dedicated test renders the XG reference file's amp insert and asserts it
   is byte-identical to a signature captured from the baseline binary.
4. **Each knob moves its own axis, by the documented amount, and 64 is identity.** For each of the
   six, a sweep `0 / 32 / 64 / 96 / 127` moves the intended measure in the intended direction **and
   by at least a pinned minimum** (so a `/63`-scaled or epsilon-sized knob fails), measured at a
   stated pitch and input level; and `64` renders **bit-identical** to unauthored, verified for **all
   four** program × bank combinations (which makes a base-value desync impossible to miss).
5. **Cross-coupling is measured, not asserted.** Report the full 6×6 matrix of (knob → each knob's
   primary measure) in the journal. A knob whose primary measure is moved further by a *different*
   knob than by itself is not a knob and must be cut or compensated — the test that killed Bite.
6. **No zipper, and no click on a single large step.** Two oracles, because they catch different
   artefacts: (a) a parameter *ramped* through a held note produces no spectral line at `sr/BLOCK`
   (689 Hz) or its harmonics above a pinned floor, relative to a no-ramp control — a peak
   sample-difference detector cannot see this and must not be used alone; and (b) a *single large
   step* authored mid-note (e.g. Body 64 → 127, and first-authoring from neutral) produces no
   transient above the existing click threshold — this is what the block-rate knob slew (§6) has to
   earn.
7. **Level residual is bounded and reported.** Band-limited RMS at Drive `0` and `127` sits within a
   pinned tolerance of `64`, at two pitches and two input levels. `post` is a *partial* compensation
   by construction; the measured residual is reported honestly, and if it exceeds the pinned
   tolerance that is a finding, not a number to widen.
8. **Alias budget holds across the whole knob range.** `drive_alias_floor`'s fold-bin logic runs at
   4 (program × bank) × 3 fundamentals × Drive `{0, 64, 127}` and holds −40 dBc. **The measured
   result sets Drive's exponent `k`** (§2).
9. **Range safety.** Every parameter at `0` and `127`, on both programs and both banks: finite,
   DC-free, non-silent, no NaN, no sustained clipping.
10. **State table.** One test per row of §8's table.
11. **KILN-00034 stays fixed, in all directions.** The existing regression stays green; plus an RPN
    select after an NRPN select must not let Data Entry write an amp parameter; plus the stale-MSB
    path — amp NRPN `CC6=100`, then RPN 0,0 select, then `CC38=0`, asserting `bend_range` is
    unchanged (fails at 24.0 today); plus the **null-selector** path — `CC6=100` with *no* selector
    live, then RPN 0,0 select, then `CC38=0`, same assertion.
12. **The gate:** `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`,
    `cargo test --workspace` green (stdin closed); release build clean; and matched-gain `.opus`
    demonstrations of each knob at its extremes for Arthur's ear — the real final check.

## Decision log

- **Offsets from the shipped voicing, not absolutes.** Absolutes would discard the oracle-tuned
  29/30 × main/alt matrix. Offsets make `64 = unchanged`, so inertness is structural.
- **NRPN over undefined CCs**, because ferrosintesis is a generic GM player and must not
  reinterpret a foreign file's controllers. **Over SysEx**, because the XG variation insert is one
  global amp on one part — structurally incapable of the two-rig case that is the whole request.
- **Cut Bite (folded).** `g1` and `bias` are one expression in the shaper, so Drive moves Bite's own
  primary measure further than Bite does; and round 1 had already measured `bias` and refused to
  vary it. A knob that fails separability by construction is not a knob.
- **Added Cab Tone (folded).** The HF corner is the largest perceptual "which cab" axis and the only
  proposed control that is level-independent. Downward-only, so it can never weaken the anti-alias
  cliff it shares.
- **Drive's exponent is set by the alias floor, not by taste (folded).** Review measured the draft's
  `k = 2` pushing the worst fold bin to ≈−36 dB against a −40 dB budget. Fixing this by measurement
  *before* the surface is frozen in album source is the reason this design exists.
- **Tightness narrowed 2^(1.5n) → 2^(0.6n), ceiling 400 → 180 Hz (folded).** The draft's top end
  removed 6.7–19 dB of a low E — a mute, not a tone control, and the AC 3 sweep would have happily
  certified it as "monotonic in the intended direction".
- **`post` as an exact-identity ratio (folded, all three lenses).** The draft's borrowed `amp_sim`
  formula could not reproduce the hand-tuned constants and would have broken bit-identity at 64.
  The ratio form is exactly 1.0 at neutral; the residual is measured, not asserted.
- **`Drive` stores its base voicing (folded).** `Biquad` cannot be read back, so the alternative was
  duplicating a table Part B is chartered to change, with no mechanism to keep the copies in sync.
- **Per-sample scalar slew, block-rate biquad retune (folded).** Block-rate scalars would zipper at
  689 Hz — the exact hazard the design names — and the draft's click detector could not have seen
  it.
- **`data_msb` scoped per latch (folded).** The draft's claim that mutual exclusion made KILN-00034
  impossible was false; a third path exists, and this change would have been the first to make it
  authorable from our own source.
- **No `dsp.rs` change (folded).** `retune_peak`/`retune_highpass` already exist. Checking before
  designing a helper would have caught this; the pattern being proven in-repo strengthens the
  argument rather than weakening it.
- **Tone kept despite being dynamics-dependent.** That is what a pre-clip pedal control *is*. The
  design owns the consequence instead of hiding it: measured at a stated level, documented in the
  README, and paired with Cab Tone as the level-independent alternative.
- **Preserved by CC121.** RP-015 nulls the RPN/NRPN *selector*, not the values a selector wrote.
- **XG insert keeps absolute precedence.** One amp at a time; the existing branch is untouched.
- **Defaults unchanged; Part B sets them.** The knobs are how we find out what GM29 and GM30 should
  be. Guessing now, then re-guessing after Arthur listens, would be two guesses instead of one
  measurement — and would forfeit the 0-of-124 render-diff that makes this change provably safe.

## Adversarial review record

Three independent lenses (state semantics, DSP, scope/simplicity), then mutual refutation. 23
findings raised, 16 survived, 7 refuted.

**Folded above:** all 16 — the `post` derivation (found independently by all three lenses), the
alias budget at Drive 127, base-value duplication, Bite's non-separability, Tightness's low-register
mute, the zipper-blind click detector, the `data_msb` side channel, the already-existing `dsp.rs`
helpers, Tone's level dependence, and the per-program/bank instability of the Body and Presence
base values (addressed by publishing the full base matrix in the README).

**Refuted, with rationale:**
- *"Every `Drive` rebuild silently drops the authored rig"* and *"the settle optimisation loses the
  rig"* — both misread §6's slew gate as gating the re-apply. Re-application on rebuild is
  unconditional; only the *retune* is skipped once a value has settled.
- *"The apply site is specified in two different places"* — one site, the strip insert at
  `engine.rs:3086`; the second reference was to the rebuild path.
- *"CC0=127 is missing from the state table"* — now an explicit row, though the refuters were right
  that it is inert: ch-10 routing means no driven voice sounds.
- *"All six knobs are gains, so a different cab is unreachable"* — refuted 3-0 at the time; partly
  addressed anyway by Cab Tone, which is a corner frequency, not a gain.
- *"Offsets compound per block, racing to the clamp rail"* — misreads the law as an in-place
  mutation of stored state; offsets are recomputed from `self.base` each time, never accumulated.
- *"Inertness criteria cannot fail"* — AC 2 is an exact array comparison against a real render, and
  AC 3 now requires bit-identity at 64 across all four program × bank combinations.

## Cross-family review record

One pass, GPT-5.6-sol (Codex), decorrelated from the Claude adversarial panel. Seven findings; all
folded:

- **`post` law goes singular and negative (high).** My ratio form `m(0)/m(n)` reused `amp_sim`'s
  shape, which is only defined for non-negative drive; at `n < 0` it divides by zero at `k ≈ 1.585`
  and inverts sign at `k = 2`. Replaced with a strictly-positive exponential trim `2^(−c·n)` (§2.2)
  that cannot go singular at any `k`. **This was a real arithmetic error, not a preference.**
- **NRPN `0x30` is not collision-free (critical, as stated).** NRPN has no manufacturer namespace and
  Casio's XW series uses `0x30:00..05`. The claim is downgraded from "collision-free" to "contained"
  (§3), the containment argument is made explicit, and the true collision-free alternative (SysEx
  with a manufacturer ID) is recorded as *unavailable to us* rather than merely not chosen.
- **The `data_msb` fix still leaks from the null/null state (high).** "Write `data_msb` otherwise"
  included the initial no-selector state. Corrected: Data Entry with no live selector is dropped
  (§7), and AC 11 gains the null-selector case.
- **No priming state for a same-tick rig (high).** A single `amp_primed` bool would prime only the
  first of six same-tick parameters. Made per-parameter (§4), and a rebuilt `Drive` now seeds from
  persisted `amp_cur` rather than slewing up from base (§6).
- **Shared `amp_sim` path is invisible to the album diff (medium).** `from_stages`/`cab_biquads` also
  build the XG insert, which no album exercises. AC 3 now pins it by byte-signature.
- **Some ACs could certify a wrong build (medium).** AC 4 now requires a pinned *minimum* movement,
  not just direction; AC 1/2 compare against the frozen `852a19d` binary; floors and tolerances are
  pinned before the sweeps, not after.
- **Retune is not click-free for a *large* step (medium).** Retained biquad state smooths small
  per-block steps, not a single big coefficient jump. The block-rate knob slew (§6) turns any large
  authored jump into small steps, and AC 6 gains a single-step transient oracle.

## Residual risks

- **A foreign file using NRPN MSB `0x30`** gets an unexpected amp tweak. Contained to channels
  playing GM29/30 and bounded to a timbre change. Accepted.
- **Six may not be the right six.** Deliberately discoverable: Part B revisits the set after Arthur
  has heard them, and adding an index is additive and non-breaking.
- **Drive's usable range may end up smaller than hoped.** If the alias floor forces `k` well below
  2, the knob is less dramatic than Arthur may want. The honest answer then is to report the limit,
  not to widen the tolerance — and the fix would be higher oversampling for the shaper, which is a
  separate and much larger change.
- **Tone will disappoint at high velocity.** Documented, but documentation is not the same as
  meeting the expectation; Arthur may still find it useless in practice, in which case Part B should
  retarget it to a cabinet centre frequency.
- **Slew length is unvalidated by ear.** 20 ms follows the wah precedent, but a saturator is more
  revealing than a filter. If it zippers, the number moves; the mechanism does not.
