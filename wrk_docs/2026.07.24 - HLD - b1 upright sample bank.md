**Status:** design agreed; bake + wiring in progress.
**Author:** Claude (Opus 4.8, 1M), for Arthur.
**Scope:** turn Arthur's own Yamaha B1 acoustic upright recordings into a sampled
piano bank for ferrosintesis, wired as a **GM 0 CC0=5 alternate** so it can be

## 1. Source material

Arthur recorded his Yamaha **B1 upright** with a **Tascam DR-05** (24-bit / 48 kHz /
stereo). Each pass plays the keyboard **ascending in thirds**, each note held to full
ring-out. All raw takes live in `~/Downloads` and are Arthur's only copies (read-only).

| file | dur | pass(es) | state |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DR0000_0195.wav` | 528.8 s | **normal** (t≈1.6–167 s) then **soft** (t≈234–441 s) | clean; peak −11.0 dBFS |
| `DR0000_0197.wav` | 257.9 s | **hard**, 1st attempt | **clipped** (3558 rail samples / 10 notes) — superseded |
| `DR0000_0198.wav` | 164.6 s | hard, 2nd attempt | clipped + played too fast — unusable |
| `DR0000_0200.wav` | 302.7 s | **hard**, re-recorded at −9 dB gain | **clean**; peak −4.8 dBFS — the hard layer |

The ladder starts on **A0** (not C1): A0 C1 E1 G1 B1 D2 F2 A2 C3 E3 G3 B3 D4 F4 A4 C5
E5 G5 B5 D6 F6 A6 C7 E7 G7 B7 C8. The **soft** pass follows this only to B3, then
switches to the complementary chain from C4 (C4 E4 G4 B4 D5 F5 A5 C6 …). So the two
soft chains interleave to give near-whole-keyboard coverage at zig-zag pitches.

## 2. Measured facts (verified; full reports in `D:/tmp_b1/strands/`)

- **Gain of the re-recorded hard take.** 0200 sits **−9.4 dB ± 0.6 dB** below 0195/0197
  (three independent statistics agree to 0.6 dB; corroborated by a second workflow at
  −8.3 dB vs 0197, and by a noise-floor lower bound ≥5.6 dB). There is a real **~2 dB
  bass→treble tilt** (bass +8.3 / mid +9.2 / treble +10.3 dB to place on the shared
  scale), caused by the recorder being re-seated, not the gain knob. **Arthur played the
  new take at the same dynamic** (force difference −0.1 dB) — it is a true fortissimo
  layer, not a re-labelled mezzo.
- **Same instrument, same room, same mic distance, same voicing.** Tuning drifted
  +1.2 cents (thermal) over two hours. The recorder was **rotated/re-seated** between
  takes (stereo fingerprint + sub-bass coupling changed); benign for an L-channel bank.
  Three hard notes have anomalous L/R in 0200 (**G5 −7.8, A6 −4.8, G7 +6.0 dB**) — take
  the **R** channel for those three if L reads wrong by ear.
- **Tuning.** A4 = 437.45 Hz (~10 c flat of A440) plus a real **Railsback stretch**
  (A0 −53 c … 0 near G5 … +45 c at C8) and inharmonicity B from 4.9e-4 (A0) to 1e-2
  (top octave). A **bass/treble bridge break at F3/F#3** (B jumps 2.2× over 3 semitones).
- **Recording quality.** Noise floor −91…−95 dBFS/oct, flat, recorder-dominated. Mains
  hum negligible. Room dry (no dereverb). Sustain pedal **up** — notes are individually
  layerable. Strong **infrasonic rumble 2–5 Hz** (peak −46 dBFS) → needs a 20–22 Hz HPF.
  **Do not sum L+R** (≈3 dB HF shelf loss); take channel **L**.

## 3. Design decisions

### 3.1 Tuning — root = measured f1 → exact equal temperament
The engine plays a zone at `ratio = target_hz / zone.root` (sampler.rs:2303), where
`target_hz` is the requested key's ET frequency. Setting **`root` = each sample's
measured first partial f1** makes every note play at exact ET while its recorded
**inharmonicity rides along untouched**. This is the house convention ("we repitch from
the real f0 so the flatness never reaches the render") and the musically correct call:
the bank plays **in tune alongside other instruments**, but keeps the stiff-string
partial spread that makes an upright sound like an upright. The global 10-cent flatness
and the macro Railsback tuning curve are normalised out (intonation); the per-note
inharmonicity is kept (timbre). f1 comes straight from the slicer manifest (`f1_hz`).

> **SUPERSEDED 2026-07-24 — the bank ships TWO layers, not three.** §3.2 below is
> the original three-layer design; it was built, auditioned, and then cut down.
> See §3.2a for what shipped and why. Everything else in this document stands.

### 3.2 Velocity — three timbre layers, engine sets loudness
`LaVoice` scales the sample by `vel_amp(vel)`, the **same** velocity-level law as the
wrapped model (sampler.rs:2757-2775), and each layer's samples are peak-normalised. So
a multi-velocity bank's *loudness* comes from the engine's shared law (consistent with
every other instrument in a mix), while the layers supply **timbre per dynamic** — soft
= darker spectrum, hard = brighter, sampled from the real instrument. This is the
property no existing bank has: our soft/normal/hard are genuinely different recorded
spectra, not one sample re-EQ'd. The measured ~21 dB soft→hard *level* span is therefore
NOT baked as absolute level (that would fight the mix); it validated that we have three
distinct dynamics worth sampling and informs the velocity split points.
- **Layers & sources:** soft ← 0195 soft pass; normal ← 0195 normal pass; hard ← 0200.
- **Velocity split (tunable):** vel < 50 → soft, 50–89 → normal, ≥ 90 → hard.
- **Hard-layer gain:** apply **+9.4 dB** to 0200 before per-layer normalisation so the
  three layers share a scale before normalisation flattens them (keeps relative-timbre
  handoff sane; belt-and-braces since normalisation follows).
- **Soft treble:** above B3 the soft ladder has no material and no usable HF > 4 kHz.
  Derive the soft treble by low-passing + level-shifting the **normal** layer there
  (Arthur chose derivation over re-recording). Honestly: convincing, because a soft
  treble strike genuinely has little HF to lose.

### 3.2a What shipped — TWO layers, split at 60 (2026-07-24)

Arthur's ear found the bank "very dark when soft" and heard hiss on the soft
layer. Both complaints turned out to have the same root, and measuring it
collapsed §3.2's three layers to two.

**The soft layer was not a third timbre.** Comparing matched notes with the
noise floor subtracted first (`D:/tmp_b1/brightness2.py` — subtraction matters,
because broadband hiss lands in the numerator of any tilt statistic and makes a
noisy layer read as bright):

| pair | median tilt difference |
|---|---|
| normal − soft | **+0.8 dB** — the same timbre |
| hard − normal | **+1.9 dB**, and **+8…+25 dB above B5** — the real step |

So the soft capture bought ~1 dB of tone for 11 dB of SNR (28.1 dB vs 39.2 dB,
`D:/tmp_b1/snr_compare.py`). Rendered on a Tubular Bells passage written entirely
below velocity 50, replacing it with `normal` moved **−7.2 dB above 6 kHz** and
**+3.4 dB in the 2–6 kHz presence band** at unchanged broadband level: less hiss
*and* more tone. It was dropped. Bonus: 9.6 MB → 6.8 MB, under the crates.io cap.

**A re-record could not save it.** Arthur re-recorded the soft pass at maximum
input gain (0205/0206). Signal rose 20.1 dB; the noise floor rose 18.9 dB
(20.8 dB above 4 kHz). Net SNR change **+1.2 dB** — nothing. The noise is *ahead
of* the gain stage (mic self-noise plus room), so the gain knob cannot reach it.
Only a closer mic, a quieter room, or a better mic would. Moving the recorder to
the treble end (0206) also produced no measurable SNR gain.

**Split at 60, not 90.** With two layers the split decides how often the one
genuinely bright capture is heard. It is a real trade-off, measured over the
reference MIDI corpus vs the piece Arthur auditions with:

| split | corpus notes on `hard` | Tubular Bells Pt 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 60 | 80% | 48% |
| 70 | 63% | 2% |
| 90 (original) | 51% | 0% |

There is a cliff between 64 and 70 because that arrangement's piano tops out at
velocity 77. Note the corpus column: `hard` was never globally unreachable at 90
— **Tubular Bells is the outlier**, and tuning to it costs putting 80% of ordinary
piano music on the fortissimo capture. Accepted on Arthur's ear (2026-07-24), and
cheap to revisit: it is one comparison in `sampler::b1upright_bank`.

### 3.3 Zone layout
We have a real sample at essentially every ladder pitch (every 3–4 semitones), so the
**nearest-zone repitch is ≤ ~2 semitones** and never crosses an octave (clamp 0.5–2.0)
or the F3/F#3 bridge break by more than one step. Keep **all available zones per layer**
(no decimation) — denser = smaller repitch/stretch error and we already have the audio.
Each layer covers the whole keyboard (`nearest()` selects per key).

### 3.4 DSP chain (per slice, in order)
1. Take channel **L** (slicer already did; R for G5/A6/G7 if needed).
2. **20 Hz 4th-order Butterworth high-pass** (kills 2–5 Hz rumble + DC; −0.08 dB at C1).
3. **Resample 48 kHz → 44.1 kHz** with the repo's band-limited `resample` (NOT naive
   linear — a bright piano attack aliases audibly otherwise).
4. **Trim to onset**, keep ~1.6 s body (matches the grand bank's 1.508 s), short raised-
   cosine fade-out (~40 ms) so the tail never cuts or decays into the −91 dBFS floor.
5. **Per-layer peak-normalise** to a common ceiling (~0.9), one factor per layer so the
   natural across-keyboard level contour is preserved.
6. **16-bit** with TPDF dither.
`root` = manifest `f1_hz`. No per-file 0.9 normalisation (would flatten the contour).

### 3.5 Round robins — none in v1
One take per note per layer (0197's old hard is clipped, unusable as an RR). The
`rr2` bool in the bank fn returns the same zones. Fine — ydp-grand/honky-tonk are also
single-take. Revisit later using the G3/F4 restrikes if RR is wanted.

## 6. Risks accepted
- **2 dB bass→treble gain tilt** on the hard layer (mic re-seat). A single +9.4 dB
  leaves ~1 dB zone error at the extremes; per-region offsets absorb it if it bothers.
- **G5/A6/G7** anomalous L/R in the hard take — may need the R channel; check by ear.
- ~~**Crate size:** ~82 files ≈ 11 MB raw may exceed the 10 MiB crates.io cap.~~
  **RESOLVED 2026-07-24** — dropping the soft layer left 52 files / 6.8 MB.
- ~~**Derived soft treble** above B3 is synthesised from the normal layer.~~ **MOOT**
  — there is no soft layer.
- **The split is a step, not a crossfade.** Two captures with a hard boundary at
  velocity 60 means a 1-unit velocity change across that line flips timbre by up to
  12 dB above 6 kHz. Inaudible in most writing (level is continuous via `vel_amp`,
  only tone steps), but a slow crescendo through 59→60 could reveal it. A proper
  fix is a velocity crossfade, which `bank!` cannot express today — the accessor
  returns one `&'static [Zone]`. Deferred; raise it if anyone hears it.
- **Soft/hard beat** ~0.5 Hz around A4 from the 1.2-cent thermal drift if layers
  crossfade at one pitch; pitch-matching layers avoids it (deferred, and now only
  relevant to a future normal/hard crossfade).
