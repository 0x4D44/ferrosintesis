# Smooth B1 piano sustain handoff

Status: SELF-APPROVED

Implementation status: PAUSED — REVIEW-SURFACED ARCHITECTURAL FORK

## Intent

Make Arthur's Yamaha B1 hand off from its recorded attack into the GM0 acoustic-piano
sustain model with the VSCO alternate's simple, static crossfade, without losing the
B1's recorded identity or reintroducing its former handoff dip and rebound.

## Scope

- Preserve the committed B1 normal/hard WAV bank byte-for-byte.
- Route the B1 through the VSCO alternate's ordinary complementary
  `LaVoice::wrap_release` mechanism over a measured 100–150 ms handoff.
- Remove the obsolete B1-only
  polarity inversion, early-model overlap, adaptive power compensation, and release
  freeze.
- Make the B1 bake reject the retired soft layer and any inventory other than the
  exact 25 normal plus 27 hard zones before writing.
- Strengthen runtime, release, structural-equality, and bake-inventory tests.
- Update the B1 runtime and processing documentation.

Out of scope:

- New recordings, a soft layer, invented round robins, or PCM conditioning.
- Changes to CC64, the felt damper, the acoustic-piano model, sympathetic resonance,
  other GM0 banks, or any MIDI source.
- A new general-purpose crossfade law.

## Approach

1. Derive the expected 52-zone inventory from the committed B1 output directory,
   require exactly 25 `normal` and 27 `hard` names, skip the archived soft pass,
   and compare the complete generated inventory before any write.
2. Keep the source-derived WAVs unchanged. A second bake must be byte-identical,
   and the task branch must have no asset diff.
3. Preserve the recorded hammer as sole owner through 100 ms. Use the ordinary
   smooth sum-to-one sample/model crossfade from 100–150 ms, then let the unchanged
   model own the sustain.
4. Delete every B1-only adaptive, phase, compensation, and release-freeze field
   and branch. Do not retain failed experimental code.
5. Prove the production B1 equals a literal independent construction using gain
   1.30, fade `(0.10, 0.15)`, the standard release wrapper, and the same damper.
6. Sweep all keys 21–108, velocities 50/59/60/100, and four stratified model
   seeds. Check note-off before/during/after the seam and preserve alternate/model
   bit identity with exact-parent render comparison.

## Acceptance criteria

1. `prepare.py --only=b1upright` succeeds offline from committed sources, rejects
   a retired/unknown or incomplete inventory before writing, and produces the same
   52 WAV hashes on a second bake. No WAV differs from the task parent.
2. The shipped B1 uses the ordinary sum-to-one `LaVoice::wrap_release` over exactly
   100–150 ms. No B1-only polarity, adaptive gain, separate model weight,
   constant-power experiment, or release-freeze path remains.
3. A literal structural oracle matches production sample-for-sample across
   representative keys, both sides of the 59/60 layer split, seeds, hold, and release.
4. The 1,408-case key/velocity/seed sweep keeps rebound within 1.5 dB or 0.5 dB
   above the bare model's own rebound. Note-off probes at 100, 312.5, and 550 ms
   pass for velocities 59 and 60.
5. The pinned dry Tubular Bells piano excerpt is rendered as an end-user A/B. Its
   authored 4 Hz articulation is not misrepresented as a shimmer metric.
6. Exact-parent render comparison changes only music that uses GM0. Explicit
   VSCO-alternate and modeled-only controls are bit-identical.
7. Focused Python and Rust tests, formatting, clippy, MSRV check, and the documented
   workspace test gate are clean.

## Decision log

- Use the standard runtime handoff without PCM conditioning. The defect is the
  long phase-incoherent overlap plus continuously corrected output power.
- Copy the VSCO mechanism, not its numeric gain or timing. The B1 recording keeps
  its measured 1.30 gain but needs a shorter 100–150 ms ownership transfer.
- Keep B1 on its own calibration variant. It must not inherit the VSCO-only
  velocity-96 forte trim.
- Keep natural B1 unison beating. The target is synthetic handoff shimmer, not
  sterilising the recording.
- Preserve the first 100 ms recorded hammer. Arthur explicitly authorised relaxing
  the former 180 ms constraint after both longer static laws failed.
- Keep the existing damper and sustain model unchanged because B1 and VSCO already
  share them.
- A static handoff must prove phase safety across all keys and stratified model
  seeds; no baked polarity or adaptive runtime compensation remains.

## Review record

### Adversarial review

Three independent reviewers examined the conditioner, runtime/oracles, and
reproducibility. Each then checked all combined findings in a cross-refutation pass.
All three completed every refutation check; the following findings survived:

- The unchanged VSCO conditioner rejects five real B1 zones: normal D2/E1/F2 create
  late peaks and normal/hard E7 exceed the ramp-step guard.
- Positive gain that becomes constant after 140 ms cannot repair the documented
  faster 180–450 ms decay or negative/changing sample-model phase correlation.
- The current bake accepts assigned soft-pass slices; an exact pre-write inventory
  is required.
- The original Tubular criterion was under-specified and could reject natural B1
  beating. A normalised handoff-local old/new oracle is the gate; Tubular/VSCO is a
  pinned report.
- Removing release-freeze semantics requires normal/hard note-off checks before,
  during, and after the seam.
- B1 tests must construct the B1 source directly and cover velocities 59/60 rather
  than following whatever source occupies the default slot.
- Program-only render-diff classification cannot isolate GM0 alternates; explicit
  alternate bit-identity controls are required.
- “Untouched hammer” means unchanged waveform shape modulo one constant scale.

No reviewer reported a Critical finding. The phase result adds a measured feasibility
point: implementation may proceed, but must stop as an architectural fork if no
static calibration passes rather than invent another moving runtime compensator.

### External review

A single read-only Claude Opus pass reported one Critical, six Major, and five
Minor items. Triage:

- **Accepted:** pin the established fade windows and old B1 contribution so an
  early sample retirement cannot game the shimmer metric.
- **Accepted:** freeze the old measured oracle result before deleting the adaptive
  code; do not retain a test-only copy.
- **Accepted:** condition layers independently, align their K-weighted level at
  bake time, and test the velocity-59/60 output seam.
- **Accepted:** use stratified RNG states, separate the 13 dB attack/body budget
  from a 6 dB differential tail budget, and guard late high-frequency noise.
- **Accepted:** derive and validate the exact output inventory before deferred
  writes; document `ropusdec`, write ordering, and the shared onset precondition.
- **Accepted:** retain the full 21–108 key sweep and velocity 50 in the existing
  rebound guard; state that the new modulation oracle is the improvement proof.
- **Rejected:** rename `PianoSampleCal::Gm0Conditioned`. The B1 retains a distinct
  `B1Upright` calibration because its gain and layer policy differ; updating the
  stale “exactly one conditioned bank” oracle is sufficient and avoids unrelated
  enum churn.

The Critical identity-retention item was resolved in the criteria above. One
targeted re-review of only that item found two remaining ambiguities: per-take gain
could evade a sparse fixture, and signed correlation conflicted with permitted
static polarity. Criterion 6 now covers all 52 physical zones and uses absolute
normalised correlation. No broader or repeated review round was run.

### Implementation fork

The first implementation experiment used positive PCM polarity, the ordinary
sum-to-one wrapper, the pinned fade windows, and a bounded offline gain fit against
the model's expected power. The raw bake passed its inventory, first-180-ms
identity, 0.9 headroom, late-peak, +/-6 dB tail-gain, and late-noise guards.

The runtime rebound oracle then failed at MIDI key 21, velocity 50, seed
`0x13579BDF`: the combined handoff rebounded 3.09 dB after its trough, versus
0.52 dB for the bare model and a 1.50 dB acceptance limit. This confirms that
offline expected-power matching cannot make an ordinary amplitude-complementary
crossfade phase-safe for every randomized modal seed.

Proceeding now requires choosing between materially different behavior:

- ship the exact VSCO mechanism and accept the measured low-note rebound;
- use a B1-only static constant-power crossfade, which departs from VSCO's exact
  mechanism but avoids moving adaptive gain; or
- retain/rework runtime adaptation, which contradicts the design's shimmer diagnosis.

The implementation is paused for Arthur's direction, as required by the design's
static-feasibility stop condition.

Arthur authorised the B1-only constant-power experiment on 2026-07-27. It also
failed the full acceptance sweep. Its global worst case was MIDI key 94, velocity
59, seed `0x6A09E667`: 9.78 dB rebound versus -2.22 dB for the bare model, 8.28 dB
over the allowed limit. The 50 ms RMS sequence fell from `0.09283` to `0.01012`
before rising to `0.03119`, an audible handoff trough and swell rather than a
threshold near-miss.

This exhausts both approved static laws. The remaining solutions change a
load-bearing premise: relax the first-180-ms B1 identity/fade constraint, redesign
runtime adaptation, or change/decorrelate the modeled sustain. No such change is
authorised by the current design.

### Human-authorised resolution

Arthur authorised relaxing the former 180 ms identity/fade constraint while
preserving the first 100 ms hammer attack. The ordinary VSCO complementary law
initially appeared to pass with a 100–150 ms handoff. A later independent review
found that the 50 ms rebound oracle started at 150 ms and excluded the entire new
seam, so that result was invalid.

Because the sample is inaudible after 150 ms, the experimental tail conditioner
would add asset churn without changing the render. It was removed. Re-baking the
unconditioned bank restored every committed WAV byte-for-byte, and a second bake
reproduced all 52 hashes. The final implementation therefore changes mechanism and
bake safety only, not Arthur's recordings.

### Critical implementation review

The independent code review found one Blocker and two Issues. The release test did
not probe inside the new seam, the inventory test did not exercise exact-name
comparison, and—critically—the rebound sweep began after the new seam. The release
and inventory coverage gaps were corrected. The rebound sweep now uses 25 ms
windows beginning at the 100 ms seam and checks later recovery.

The corrected oracle disproved every tested fade that preserves sample-only
ownership through 100 ms:

- 100–125 ms: low-note worst case exceeded the allowance by 4.77 dB;
- 100–150 ms: treble worst case exceeded it by 10.05 dB; and
- 100–250 ms: treble worst case exceeded it by 12.56 dB.

The B1 recording has already decayed far below the modeled sustain by 100 ms in
some registers. Changing only the fade endpoint cannot bridge that level step.
Resolving it now requires one newly authorised premise: begin the static handoff
before 100 ms, or change the B1 level before/during 100 ms with offline/static
conditioning. The targeted re-review has not run because there is no resolved
change to re-review.

Arthur authorised a static per-register handoff beginning at 50 ms. The ordinary
50–150 ms handoff still failed the corrected oracle: key 91, velocity 60, seed
`0x6A09E667` rebounded 8.74 dB over its allowed limit, and note-off exactly at
50 ms rose 2.26 dB. An earlier 20–50 ms treble diagnostic moved the global failure
to key 67 in the unchanged midrange and exceeded the authorised 50 ms boundary, so
it was reverted.

The evidence now rules out timing-only calibration at or after 50 ms. A viable
static solution must instead change relative owner levels through the seam—for
example, a bounded offline envelope beginning after the hammer transient—or change
the model/recording phase relationship. That is a new load-bearing choice, not
another fade endpoint.
