# Smooth B1 piano sustain handoff

Status: SELF-APPROVED

## Intent

Make Arthur's Yamaha B1 hand off from its recorded attack into the GM0 acoustic-piano
sustain model with the VSCO alternate's simple, static crossfade, without losing the
B1's recorded identity or reintroducing its former handoff dip and rebound.

## Scope

- Envelope-condition the committed B1 normal/hard sample bank at bake time.
- Generalise the existing piano conditioner only as far as needed for banks whose
  layers have different note inventories and no round robins.
- Re-bake the 52 B1 WAV assets from the committed first-party Opus recordings.
- Recalibrate the B1 sample gain and handoff window against numeric audio oracles.
- Route the B1 through `LaVoice::wrap_release`, then remove the obsolete B1-only
  polarity inversion, early-model overlap, adaptive power compensation, and release
  freeze.
- Update focused generator/runtime tests and the B1 processing documentation.

Out of scope:

- New recordings, a soft layer, or invented round robins.
- Changes to CC64, the felt damper, the acoustic-piano model, sympathetic resonance,
  other GM0 banks, or any MIDI source.
- General-purpose sample-conditioning infrastructure beyond the two piano banks.

## Approach

1. Derive the expected 52-zone inventory from the clean committed B1 output
   directory, while requiring exactly 25 `normal` and 27 `hard` names and rejecting
   every other layer. Compare the full generated in-memory inventory against it
   before writing anything, so the retired soft pass cannot reappear as untracked
   output and no fourth hand-maintained filename list is introduced.
2. Refactor the existing deterministic conditioner around an explicit
   `sample name -> MIDI root` map. Keep the VSCO entry point, strict inventory, and
   output bit-identical for the same input. The shared core may compute trends and
   apply smooth log-domain gain, but bank-specific policy remains at its caller.
3. Fit each B1 timbre layer independently through the handoff, not merely the existing
   40–140 ms attack/body correction. Preserve the hammer-window waveform shape up
   to one constant per-take scale. Measure 50 ms RMS knots through 450 ms, match a
   smooth register trend, and reject any curve that creates a late peak, an abrupt
   20 ms gain step, more than the existing 13 dB attack/body correction, or more
   than 6 dB of additional differential tail gain from 140–450 ms. Align the two
   layers' K-weighted level with one bake-time layer scale so velocity, not the
   recording layer, continues to own loudness. Do not weaken a safety bound to admit
   the five zones that the unchanged VSCO conditioner rejects; alter the smooth
   target/ramp or leave a zone less corrected.
4. Re-bake from `samples/b1-upright/*.opus`; verify a second bake is byte-identical.
5. Establish static-handoff feasibility before deleting the runtime exception.
   First freeze the old adaptive implementation's amplitude-normalised modulation
   result and exact recipe as a regression baseline; do not retain the obsolete
   code under `#[cfg(test)]`. Then
   sweep representative keys, velocities 59/60 and 100, and multiple model seeds.
   Use stratified high-entropy RNG states rather than relying on small seeds whose
   first xorshift draw clusters near −1. Measure macro decay, sample/model
   correlation, and amplitude-normalised handoff modulation for the ordinary
   wrapper. Static bake-time polarity is permitted because it changes no envelope
   or runtime mechanism.
6. Keep the established 180–450 ms bass/mid and key-85+ 120–300 ms treble fade
   windows. Select a static polarity and gain only if the full sweep passes. A
   window change is an explicit identity trade-off and therefore an architectural
   fork, not a calibration knob.
7. Replace the B1-only wrapper call with the ordinary complementary crossfade.
   Delete runtime state and code that no longer has a caller. If no static
   calibration can satisfy the acceptance criteria, stop at that measured
   architectural fork rather than restoring adaptive gain.

## Acceptance criteria

1. With `ropusdec` and Python available, `prepare.py --only=b1upright` succeeds
   without network access from the committed Opus sources. Running it twice produces
   no tracked or untracked output difference on the second run.
2. The bake rejects anything other than the derived exact 25-normal/27-hard inventory before
   writing. The committed B1 bank has hammer-window shape unchanged modulo one
   constant scale, gradual bounded correction through 450 ms, smooth register
   body-level and decay trends, no conditioner-created late peak, and peak headroom
   at or below 0.9. The five zones rejected by the unchanged VSCO conditioner pass
   these unchanged safety properties through a measured B1 target/ramp.
3. The shipped B1 voice uses the ordinary sum-to-one sample-to-model crossfade.
   No B1-only polarity, adaptive gain, separate model weight, or release-freeze path
   remains.
4. Directly constructed B1 voices at velocities 50, 59, 60, and 100 across all
   keys 21–108 and stratified model seeds keep the attack as the peak. Their 50 ms handoff envelope
   rebounds no more than 1.5 dB or 0.5 dB above the bare model's own rebound.
5. An amplitude-normalised, handoff-local modulation oracle fails on the old
   adaptive implementation's frozen measured baseline and improves by at least 3 dB
   with the static handoff.
6. The B1 identity cannot be traded for an easy metric win: the established fade
   windows remain unchanged; absolute normalized waveform correlation of each
   conditioned/source physical zone through 180 ms remains at least 0.98 (allowing
   a deliberate static polarity); and every one of the 52 zones' K-weighted RMS over
   that interval remains within 1.5 dB of its old B1 source.
   A separate structural equality test proves the shipped B1 is exactly an ordinary
   wrapper, not that its gain is acceptable.
7. K-weighted output is continuous across the velocity 59/60 layer boundary, and
   note-off probes before, during, and after the crossfade pass for both sides of
   the velocity-59/60 layer boundary. Existing felt-damper tests remain green.
8. Conditioning does not expose recorder hiss: over 300–450 ms, the ratio of energy
   above 10 kHz to energy below 10 kHz rises by no more than 1 dB from the source
   bank at any physical B1 zone.
9. A pinned, dry piano-channel Tubular Bells excerpt is reported as an end-user
   A/B using a recorded path, source hash, sample rate, time window, filter, and
   normalisation formula. Its old/new 3–4.5 Hz result must improve; distance from
   VSCO is reported rather than gated because natural B1 unison beating remains.
10. A render-diff inventory built from the task branch's exact parent changes only
   MIDI files that sound GM0. Separate default/alternate probes prove every GM0
   alternate and the modeled-only GM0 path are bit-identical.
11. Focused Python tests, Rust tests, formatting, clippy, and the documented
   workspace test gate are clean.

## Decision log

- Use bake-time conditioning plus the standard runtime handoff. The defect is a
  sample/model envelope mismatch; continuously correcting output power at runtime
  creates the audible gain movement.
- Copy the VSCO mechanism, not its numeric gain. The B1 recordings and velocity
  layers have different absolute levels and require measured calibration.
- Keep B1 on its own calibration variant. It must not inherit the VSCO-only
  velocity-96 forte trim.
- Keep natural B1 unison beating. The target is synthetic handoff shimmer, not
  sterilising the recording.
- Preserve the established fade windows and the old first-180-ms B1 contribution.
  A shorter fade can make every handoff metric greener by deleting the recording;
  that would fail the intent.
- Keep the existing damper and sustain model unchanged because B1 and VSCO already
  share them.
- A static handoff must first prove it is phase-safe across model seeds. Positive
  envelope conditioning cannot change the documented negative/changing correlation;
  a static baked polarity is acceptable, adaptive runtime compensation is not.

## Review record

### Adversarial review

Three independent reviewers examined the conditioner, runtime/oracles, and
reproducibility. Each then checked all combined findings in a cross-refutation pass.
All three completed every refutation check; the following findings survived:

- The unchanged VSCO conditioner rejects five real B1 zones: normal D2/E1/F2 create
  late peaks and normal/hard E7 exceed the ramp-step guard.
- Positive gain that becomes constant after 140 ms cannot repair the documented
  faster 180–450 ms decay or negative/changing sample-model phase correlation.
- The current bake accepts assigned soft-pass slices; an exact pre-write inventory
  is required.
- The original Tubular criterion was under-specified and could reject natural B1
  beating. A normalised handoff-local old/new oracle is the gate; Tubular/VSCO is a
  pinned report.
- Removing release-freeze semantics requires normal/hard note-off checks before,
  during, and after the seam.
- B1 tests must construct the B1 source directly and cover velocities 59/60 rather
  than following whatever source occupies the default slot.
- Program-only render-diff classification cannot isolate GM0 alternates; explicit
  alternate bit-identity controls are required.
- “Untouched hammer” means unchanged waveform shape modulo one constant scale.

No reviewer reported a Critical finding. The phase result adds a measured feasibility
point: implementation may proceed, but must stop as an architectural fork if no
static calibration passes rather than invent another moving runtime compensator.

### External review

A single read-only Claude Opus pass reported one Critical, six Major, and five
Minor items. Triage:

- **Accepted:** pin the established fade windows and old B1 contribution so an
  early sample retirement cannot game the shimmer metric.
- **Accepted:** freeze the old measured oracle result before deleting the adaptive
  code; do not retain a test-only copy.
- **Accepted:** condition layers independently, align their K-weighted level at
  bake time, and test the velocity-59/60 output seam.
- **Accepted:** use stratified RNG states, separate the 13 dB attack/body budget
  from a 6 dB differential tail budget, and guard late high-frequency noise.
- **Accepted:** derive and validate the exact output inventory before deferred
  writes; document `ropusdec`, write ordering, and the shared onset precondition.
- **Accepted:** retain the full 21–108 key sweep and velocity 50 in the existing
  rebound guard; state that the new modulation oracle is the improvement proof.
- **Rejected:** rename `PianoSampleCal::Gm0Conditioned`. The B1 retains a distinct
  `B1Upright` calibration because its gain and layer policy differ; updating the
  stale “exactly one conditioned bank” oracle is sufficient and avoids unrelated
  enum churn.

The Critical identity-retention item was resolved in the criteria above. One
targeted re-review of only that item found two remaining ambiguities: per-take gain
could evade a sparse fixture, and signed correlation conflicted with permitted
static polarity. Criterion 6 now covers all 52 physical zones and uses absolute
normalised correlation. No broader or repeated review round was run.
