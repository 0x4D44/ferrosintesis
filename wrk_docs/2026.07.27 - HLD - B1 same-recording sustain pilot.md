# B1 same-recording sustain pilot

Status: SELF-APPROVED

## Intent

Prove, before changing the runtime or all 52 assets, whether a late loop made only
from the same Yamaha B1 note can extend the accepted recorded body without an
audible handoff, frozen beating, or periodic pulse.

Success means Arthur prefers the held pilot notes to the bounded sample-only
control and hears no new loop tell. This increment does not ship a sustain
mechanism.

## Scope

- Reconstruct the processed, onset-aligned archival note using the existing B1
  decode, slice, high-pass, resample, and normalization path.
- Exercise six deliberately different zone/layer pairs:
  `hard A0`, `hard B1`, `normal C3`, `normal F4`, `hard F4`, and `normal F6`.
  They cover a bottom monochord, a wound bichord, a mid trichord, both recorded
  hardnesses in the common register, and the likely no-loop treble boundary.
- Preserve the accepted recording through the old body. Replace only the old
  10 ms EOF taper with its contiguous archival samples, then enter a loop wholly
  inside that same note.
- Simulate the held voice offline through the last supported archival knot: first
  pass, wrapped continuation, recording-derived decay, and the existing
  felt-damper release at representative body/first-pass/loop positions. A separate
  ten-second audition may extend the final measured slope, but is explicitly
  extrapolated and cannot enter the natural-reference oracle.
- Write fixed-gain current-control, archival-reference, and loop-candidate WAVs
  to a new, empty output directory outside the repository for audition. The pilot
  must never rewrite the committed B1 bank.
- Project the all-zone payload from a deterministic register allocation against
  the 10 MiB package ceiling. Count every contiguous sample through the loop end,
  not just the loop span.

Out of scope:

- Rust/runtime changes, committed B1 WAV changes, a new codec, new recordings,
  modal or additive synthesis, two-playhead/granular continuation, and catalogue
  rendering.
- Treating numeric seam checks as proof that a loop sounds natural.
- Selecting final per-zone metadata for all 52 zones before the pilot passes.

## Approach

1. Add a small B1-specific pilot command beside the existing sample baker. It is
   a mutually exclusive early dispatch that returns before the ordinary baker.
   Resolve its destination, reject the repository and every sample-package root,
   reject any destination with a Git working tree in its ancestor chain, and
   require the destination to be new or empty. Decode the two committed Opus takes
   into a separate temporary directory, invoke the archival slicer, and reject any
   generated inventory other than the existing 25-normal/27-hard set before
   selecting the six pilot zones. A missing named pilot zone is a hard error.
   Validate all sources before the first output write and clean the decode
   directory on success or failure.
2. For each selected slice, run the exact B1 high-pass/resample/onset alignment.
   Apply the same peak scalar as its current baked body. Assert sample parity
   against the committed asset up to the start of its final 10 ms taper.
3. Estimate the slow recorded decay from 50 ms RMS frames at 25 ms hops over
   1.5 seconds to the archival end. Measure each take's background from the
   note-free interval before its first detected strike, ending at least 250 ms
   before the onset. Reject a proposed background whose RMS trends downward or
   retains coherent pitched energy; if no valid interval remains, jointly fit a
   constant floor and decaying power rather than treating the note tail as noise.
   Power-subtract the validated floor and censor frames within 6 dB of it. Take
   robust local medians at knots near 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6.5, and 8 seconds;
   widen the bass median window to ride over beats; enforce a non-increasing dB
   table. Retain a knot only when it has enough uncensored frames, and require
   supported observations at loop entry, through one whole loop, and at the last
   natural-reference comparison.
4. Search only the contiguous post-body recording, bounded by the all-zone
   appended-PCM allocation: roots through B1 receive at most 1.25 seconds, C2–B3
   0.75 seconds, C4–B5 0.50 seconds, and C6 upward no appended loop. This projects
   the exact current 52-zone inventory to about 9.46 MB of raw included WAV data
   before headers/metadata, leaving about 9.8% of the 10 MiB ceiling; the actual
   conservative check includes RIFF headers, generated metadata, crate files, and
   both raw and packaged `.crate` size. Candidate lengths are at least 0.5 seconds.
   Use rounded multiples of the measured first-partial period only as search seeds.
   Search endpoint offsets through ±one-quarter period (bounded to 256 samples),
   report cycle error, and let broadband seam, half-window level, brightness, and
   wrap-reconstruction terms decide after dividing out only the slow fitted decay.
   Estimate dominant beat periods from the decay-divided archival envelope and
   add in-budget candidates near their multiples. Print loop/beat-period alignment,
   including modulation below 0.5 Hz when the archive is long enough; these are
   diagnostics that explain a listening failure, not gates that claim to prove
   naturalness. Reject candidates under 20 dB estimated SNR. F6 explicitly tests
   the measured `no continuation required` proxy; it passes when the archival note
   is within 6 dB of the validated floor by the old body end, and then skips loop
   gates.
5. Keep the first pass contiguous. At the chosen loop, use a short offline
   constant-gain crossfade seam of 20–80 ms, seeded by pitch periods but selected
   by the same broadband cost. Constant-gain is deliberate because the two sides
   are coherent material from one note; equal-power would raise their overlap.
   Flatten only the slow decay inside the baked loop copy; retain faster beat
   motion. Simulate cyclic four-neighbour cubic reads at every boundary and
   multiply by the fitted play-time decay relative to loop entry. Run the complete
   seam/level battery and periodicity diagnostics at −2, 0, and +2 semitones.
   Pitch bend changes read rate without changing wall-clock decay.
6. Render each candidate through its last supported archival knot, plus note-offs
   during the body, first continuation pass, and loop. Also render the untouched
   bounded sample-only control and the processed natural archival note. A separate
   ten-second listening file may extend the terminal supported slope, clamped to
   −1..−12 dB/second until silence; label it `EXTRAPOLATED`, and never compare its
   post-archive section to the natural reference. Print every loop point, wrap
   score, fitted envelope, payload, and oracle result beside the WAV paths.
7. If one candidate fails, permit one alternate window/length search for that
   zone when its register allocation admits one. The 0.50-second C4–B5 allocation
   is explicitly single-shot because it equals the minimum loop length; one seam
   failure is terminal there. A second failure elsewhere ends the pilot for that
   zone; do not tune repeated hidden iterations. The later design may choose a
   shorter natural tail, two-playhead continuation, or new source material with
   Arthur.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The pilot runs entirely from committed sources and reaches its mutually
   exclusive mode before ordinary baking. It rejects resolved destinations inside
   the repository, any Git working tree, or a sample-package root, and requires a
   new or empty output directory. Its source inventory must be the exact current
   25 normal and 27 hard zones, and every named pilot zone must exist. Successful
   and deliberately failed pilot tests leave the SHA-256 set of all tracked B1
   assets unchanged and clean their temporary decode trees.
2. For every pilot zone, processed archival PCM and the committed body agree
   through the frame before the body's final 10 ms taper, within one 16-bit
   quantization step. The active writer deterministically truncates scaled floats
   to i16 without dither, so the bound covers only the reconstructed float path
   and read-back scaling. The candidate first differs only where that EOF-only
   taper previously began.
3. The decay fitter is deterministic, monotone, and uses a validated note-free
   take background or a jointly fitted constant floor. It rejects data without
   uncensored support at loop entry and loop end, at least eight frames spanning
   one complete loop, and enough populated knot neighborhoods to reach the final
   natural comparison. A proposed floor with pitched coherence or a material
   downward trend is rejected.
   A synthetic two-stage decay with beat ripple recovers each authored knot
   within 1 dB while preserving the ripple in its flattened residual.
4. Every loop is at least 0.5 seconds, lies inside the same archival note and its
   register's contiguous-byte allocation, clears the 20 dB SNR floor, and reports
   first-partial cycle error. The quarter-cycle seed neighborhood is a search
   bound and diagnostic, not a quality gate. The broadband seam score, not cycle
   count, selects the endpoint. A deliberately displaced endpoint must fail the
   seam oracle. A measured no-continuation F6 is exempt from loop gates and retains
   its naturally bounded body.
5. On each simulated hold through the last supported archival knot, at −2, 0, and
   +2 semitones:
   - the seam sample step is no larger than the in-loop 95th-percentile step;
   - the seam-adjacent 50 ms RMS step is no larger than the 95th percentile of
     adjacent steps in the corresponding natural/in-loop material;
   - the beat-smoothed slow envelope stays within 2 dB of the archival reference
     while both have uncensored observations; smoothing spans at least one
     measured beat period, or uses the fitted slow-decay trajectories when the
     archive cannot resolve one;
   - click detection is clean;
   - 0.5–8 Hz modulation depth remains within a calibrated ratio of the
     archival note rather than collapsing to a static hold.
   Freeze modulation margins from only the natural-reference and deliberately
   broken controls before scoring a candidate. Compute modulation on the
   slow-decay-divided residual over identical SNR-valid windows and record the
   exact formula, window, constants, and control provenance. Report, but do not
   gate on, loop-lag autocorrelation and wrap-rate spectral lines with seams
   included: exact repetition makes autocorrelation intrinsically high even for
   a clean loop, so audition owns the periodicity verdict.
6. Note-off during body, first pass, and loop applies the exact current felt curve:
   T60 = `clamp(0.45 * 2^((60-key)/24), 0.18, 0.95)` through key 88, and
   12 seconds above key 88. Pin Python values against a captured constant table in
   `test_prepare.py`, with a provenance comment naming the Rust formula, for
   A0/key 21, C3/key 48, F4/key 65, key 88, and F6/key 89. The multiplier is
   `10^(-3/(T60*sample_rate))`, with the voice reaped below `1e-4`. The damper is
   multiplicative on the held decay and introduces no click.
7. Bent renders track their requested pitch, keep the play-time decay table
   unchanged, and pass every criterion in item 5 through cyclic cubic wraps.
8. A deterministic allocation names every current zone and counts all contiguous
   i16 PCM through its maximum loop end, RIFF headers, metadata, and crate files.
   Print raw included bytes and the generated `.crate` size; the more conservative
   figure must leave at least 5% headroom below 10 MiB. Failing that projection
   rejects this representation before runtime work.
9. Focused Python tests, the full sample-tool Python suite, formatting, and
   `git diff --check` pass. No Rust or catalogue gate is needed because this
   increment changes no runtime or asset.
10. Arthur auditions the six fixed-gain candidate/control pairs. Any organ-like
    freeze, wrap-rate chug, timbre flicker, 1.5-second gear change, metallic
    phasing, or electric-piano-like over-sustain fails the pilot regardless of
    numeric results.

## Decision log

- Use an offline six-zone pilot before all-zone assets or Rust. Fable's partner
  review identified the wound bichord as the make-or-break case and recommended
  direct, long, beat-aware loops over two short additive grains. This is the
  cheapest experiment that can refute that representation.
- Keep loop selection offline and bake fixed metadata later. The source bank is
  known, and runtime search would add audio-thread work and make results harder
  to reproduce.
- Enter through contiguous source samples. The accepted body needs no synthetic
  handoff; its 10 ms end taper is an EOF artifact and may be replaced by the
  recording it had truncated.
- Fit a small monotone dB table instead of a nonlinear two-exponential model.
  It follows the recording's prompt-to-aftersound knee and is straightforward
  to test. Natural-reference gates do not extrapolate past the evidence; the
  separately labeled ten-second listening file may extend a bounded terminal
  slope.
- Keep the loop long. Wind-style loops above ten wraps per second freeze a piano
  beat state. The pilot spends its limited bytes on at most two wraps per second
  and measures the remaining periodicity explicitly.
- Do not start with the natural-tail companding fallback. A temporary 11.025 kHz
  logarithmic-tail prototype fit the byte budget and preserved the real decay,
  but measured only 30–36 dB reconstruction SNR in low/mid tails and about
  10 dB once treble notes reached their floor. It also requires a new asset
  representation. Full-band PCM plus existing loop concepts is the smaller
  first hypothesis; the temporary prototype remains evidence if loops fail.
- Human audition is load-bearing. Seam and envelope oracles catch known defects;
  they cannot decide whether repeated beating still reads as a piano.

## Adversarial review record

Two independent reviewers critiqued the draft, then each attempted to refute the
other's findings against the repository. The following findings survived and are
folded into this revision:

- A ten-second hold exceeded the archival evidence. Natural-reference gates now
  stop at the last supported knot; the optional ten-second file is explicitly
  terminal-slope extrapolation.
- A contiguous first pass can consume more bytes than the loop itself. Loop entry
  is now bounded by a deterministic all-zone register allocation, and the budget
  includes every retained prefix plus headers, metadata, and package output.
- The final 250 ms of a bass note is not a trustworthy noise floor. The pilot now
  uses validated pre-first-strike ambience or a joint constant-floor fit.
- A first-partial period is only a seed on an inharmonic bichord/trichord. The
  endpoint search is wider and broadband scoring owns the decision.
- Fractional cubic reads can expose a seam hidden at native rate. Every audio
  oracle now runs bent and unbent with cyclic four-point indexing.
- Raw decaying-envelope autocorrelation was ambiguous and candidate-aware
  calibration could fit the answer. Thresholds are frozen from matched,
  decay-divided natural and broken controls before candidates are scored.
- The F6 boundary could correctly require no continuation. That is now an
  explicit measured outcome rather than a loop failure.
- The pilot CLI needed a fail-closed boundary. It now has exclusive early
  dispatch, resolved-path rejection, new/empty output, validate-before-write,
  and tracked-asset hash invariance.
- Decay support had been too weak, the package projection underspecified, and
  the copied felt damper unpinned. Each now has explicit coverage and constants.

One proposed extra high-band repeating-noise metric was refuted as a required
gate: total SNR is not claimed to prove naturalness, and the load-bearing audition
already rejects repeating hiss or any other loop tell. It may remain diagnostic
output. The one-LSB body parity was also upheld as an explicit tolerance, not an
exactness claim. No critical or unresolved architectural finding survived.

## External Fable review record

The single fresh-eyes Fable pass found one unimplementable acceptance gate and
five smaller issues. All significant/useful items are folded into this revision:

- Loop-lag envelope autocorrelation approaches one for every exact repeated loop,
  good or bad, especially when seams are excluded. It is now diagnostic only;
  modulation depth and human audition own the static/periodic-tail decisions.
- Absolute 1 dB adjacent-frame and raw 2 dB reference bounds could reject the
  deliberately retained multi-string beats. Seam steps are now relative to the
  source's own 95th percentile, and reference tracking uses beat-smoothed slow
  envelopes.
- The primary risk lacked a beat-period measurement. The pilot now estimates and
  reports beat periods, tries in-budget beat-aligned candidates, and reports slow
  modulation below 0.5 Hz where the archive can resolve it.
- F4's 0.50-second allocation admits only one minimum-length loop. It is explicitly
  single-shot rather than promising a nonexistent alternate.
- The parity tolerance now states the observed deterministic, non-dithered
  quantizer, and the destination guard rejects every Git working tree rather than
  only the invoking repository.

Useful preferences also fixed the coherent crossfade law and bounds, made the
quarter-cycle value diagnostic rather than a tautological gate, captured the
Rust damper table in Python instead of implying a Rust change, hard-failed missing
pilot zones, and required temporary decode cleanup. No item was rejected. The
review found no architectural fork; its one must-fix oracle defect is resolved
above. The one permitted targeted native re-review checked only those corrections
and returned `PASS`; no critical or high-severity defect remains.
