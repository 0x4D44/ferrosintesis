# 2026.07.13 — JRN — Arthur's reference-audition feedback: the weak voices

Arthur auditioned the GM set via `demos/ferrosintesis_reference` and reported ~20 weak
voices. This is the log of diagnosing them and fixing the unambiguous ones.

## The shape of the answer

His list is not random. Splitting it by *what the album catalogue actually plays* (I
censused every committed `.mid` for program-changes and channel-10 keys) sorted it
immediately:

- **Used in albums** (fixing them changes music people hear): drum keys 49,50,51,52,53,54,
  55,56,57,81; GM 43, 105, 113, 114, 115, 117.
- **Audition-only** (no album touches them): GM 96, 104, 109, 120-127; drum keys 71, 72, 80.

And by *failure class*:
- **A level bug**: GM 120-127 render **24-39 dB below** a piano struck at the same velocity
  (measured). "Mostly silence" was literally right. They are also one `SfxNoise` struct —
  a single band-passed white-noise burst — serving all eight programs, with **no sustain
  segment at all**, so seashore/helicopter/applause die in ~1 s regardless of note length.
- **A tuning bug**: GM 43 (below).
- **Timbre**: everything else.

## What I got wrong (worth recording)

1. **My first probe rendered silence and I nearly believed it.** A MIDI program change is a
   TWO-byte message; I emitted three, which desynced every subsequent delta-time. The
   "result" was a perfect 1.000 ratio in every band — which should have been the tell. Fixed
   the writer, and the finding evaporated.
2. **My headline cymbal hypothesis was refuted by my own experiment.** I predicted the model
   was linear and therefore velocity-invariant in timbre ("a real cymbal's nonlinear cascade
   makes ff *qualitatively* brighter"). Measured: the ff/pp brightness tilt is **2.04**, not
   1.0. The model does get brighter with velocity. Good hypothesis, wrong.
3. **I diagnosed GM 43 as an envelope problem** (its RMS peak tracks note length — it
   crescendos and never settles, where the alt bites in at 55 ms). That observation is real,
   but it is **not** the dominant defect, and I had read the wrong voice: default GM 43 routes
   to `BowedString` (a waveguide), not the `Bowed` saw whose preset table I was reading.

## The actual GM 43 bug — and why "alt sounds better" was a real signal

`BowedString::set_freq` programs its loop as `sr/f - loop_comp`. The cello (42) subtracts
**3.85** samples of loop latency; the contrabass fell into the `_` arm and subtracted **1.0**.
An under-compensated *fixed sample* latency is a pitch error that **grows with pitch**.
Measured (fine Goertzel sweep, settled sustain, samples off):

```
key         28     36     43     47     52     60     67    spread
GM43 main -6.1   -8.0  -10.7  -14.3  -19.8  -31.7  -45.6   39.5 c
GM43 alt  +2.9   +2.9   +2.9   +2.9   +3.8   +3.8   +5.6    2.7 c
GM42 cello-1.6   -0.7   -2.5   -0.7   -1.6   -2.5   -0.7    1.8 c   <- in-code control
```

A **constant** offset is inaudible ("the band tuned to A=442"). A **pitch-dependent** one
corrupts INTERVALS: a fifth renders ~14 c narrow, so a sustained triad beats against itself
— on the bass, the harmonic foundation. That is what Arthur heard, and it is why he preferred
the alt. One literal.

The code comment beside it justified the 1.0 as *"keeps 1.0 to stay byte-identical to Stage 1"*
and called the flatness *"small and characterful in the bass"*. Both premises are dead: the
`.opus`/`.wav` renders are now git-ignored build output, so there is no committed audio to
stay identical to. **A stale justification outlived its premise and hid a real bug for months.**

## The audition harness was also lying

`demos/ferrosintesis_reference/programs.py` has no register OVERRIDE for GM 43, so it inherits
the strings family row **(48, 79)** — while `ALT_BANK[43]` gives the alt **(28, 60)**. So the
A/B auditioned the **main an octave-and-a-fourth above a double bass's compass**, exactly where
its pitch bug is worst (−31 to −45 c), against the alt played *in* compass. The main voice was
real-but-flattering-nobody; the test was also rigged against it. Two of the reported items
(GM 43, GM 113) are partly measurement artefacts. **Until the harness is controlled, every
future A/B is suspect.** (GM 113: every one of the 101 agogo notes in the catalogue sends CC0=1
and plays the *alt*; the default agogo voice sounds in zero albums.)

## Landed this session

- **GM 43 tuning**: `loop_comp` 1.0 → 3.85. Oracle `bowed_string_tuning_is_pitch_independent`
  asserts (a) |cents| ≤ 8 and (b) — the load-bearing clause — **spread ≤ 5 c** over each
  instrument's OWN compass. Verified fail-first: red at spread 20.6 c on the old literal.
- **The tom ladder**: `47 | 48 | 50 => tom(240.0, ...)` was ONE arm — the top three GM toms were
  literally the same drum, bit-identical but for pan. Split to 240/293/352 Hz, continuing the
  ladder's own geometry. Oracle `tom_ladder_is_six_distinct_drums` asserts a strictly ascending
  ladder **and** that no two tom buffers are bit-identical. Note: the existing golden
  `v3_toms_settle_near_table_pitch` **pinned the bug** (it asserted key 48 sounds at 240 Hz);
  its expected values are corrected and it now covers all six toms — the tolerance is unchanged.
- **GM 114 steel pan**: its mode ladder stopped at the 4.18 partial — at C4 that is 1.1 kHz, so
  the voice had *no energy above 2 kHz*. Extended to 8.6× plus a brighter strike. The oracle
  `steel_pan_is_bright_metal` is calibrated against the crate's own instruments, which separate
  by ~400×: METAL bars (vibes 0.047, tubular 0.047) vs WOOD bars (marimba 0.0001, xylo 0.0001).
  **Pre-fix the steel pan measured 0.0002 — numerically a wooden bar.** Post-fix: 0.0405.
- **A new `Modal` construction invariant**: `attack_s < strike-noise t60`. The alt steel pan
  shipped with a 10 ms attack ramp against an 8 ms mallet burst, so it **gated its own strike**
  to ~12% of peak. Cheap to state; catches the whole bug class.

## The cymbals — escalated to Fable, captured as an HLD

Everything Arthur flagged in the cymbals is one architectural fact: the wash is a
`Biquad::highpass` **built once** and decayed by **one scalar**, with the 44-mode plate **17 dB
underneath it**. A time-invariant filter with a single exponential cannot make a time-varying
spectrum. Measured, the model's t60 is **flat at ~3.0 s across every band from 200 Hz to 14 kHz**;
a real cymbal *darkens* (t60 1.0 s at 10-14 kHz). Our crash's centroid is within 8% of our own
closed hi-hat's. The ride *brightens* as it decays. See
`wrk_docs/2026.07.13 - HLD - cymbal plate synthesis and its oracle.md`.

**The hardest part was not the fix — it was finding an oracle that can detect a bad cymbal on a
box with no ears.** Sixteen candidate clauses were proposed and then *measured*: **eleven were
worthless** (already passing on the broken code, or unsatisfiable, or rejecting the real
recording as a reference). The principle that explains it:

> Every cheap scalar — flatness, centroid, kurtosis, prominence, band ratios — is a spectral
> **SNAPSHOT**, and the wash's filter corner is free to sit anywhere, so it dominates all of
> them. **Measure decay RATES and STRUCTURE, never snapshots.**

The proof is in the ground truth we own: the two crash round-robins (same cymbal, different
strike) agree *to the decimal* on decay features (tilt −11.0 dB/s both) while disagreeing ~30%
on snapshot features. Decay rates are a property of the **plate**; snapshots of the **strike**.

Also found: **`render_drum` (drums.rs:2313) hardcodes `Kit::V1`**, but the engine ships **V3**.
So every existing "V3 cymbal" oracle is measuring the dead legacy voice the engine never
selects. That is *how* the cymbals got this bad with a green suite.
