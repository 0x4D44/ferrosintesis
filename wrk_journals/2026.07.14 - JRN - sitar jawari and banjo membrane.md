# 2026.07.14 — JRN — GM 104 sitar jawari + GM 105 banjo membrane

Task: both "sound terrible" from the audition. Banjo (GM 105) is used in committed
albums (amarok, Hollow Hill); sitar (GM 104) is audition-only. Implemented as one
coherent design on the shared `PluckPreset`/`KsLoop`/`Pluck` structs.

## Root causes

- **Sitar**: the old preset faked the jawari with `grit` — a memoryless waveshaper.
  Relative 3rd-harmonic distortion of a memoryless shaper is ∝ A², so the buzz loses
  2 dB per 1 dB of decay — gone by −40 dB. The 12 kHz `bright` was a second dead end
  (−3.2 dB at Nyquist). Plain KS with a lowpass loop is the *opposite* of a jawari.
- **Banjo**: the preset was a generic bright pluck with a single wooden-box peak EQ
  (720 Hz). A banjo's body is a tensioned mylar DRUM HEAD; there was no membrane
  anywhere in the voice, so no thunk and no head honk.

## What was built

1. `JawariSpec` + `Jawari` (voices.rs): an envelope-tracking, one-sided, REFLECTING
   contact inside each KS loop (both polarizations), plus a contact-gated one-sided
   delay-shortening ("wrap") on the loop tap. `PluckPreset.jawari: Option<JawariSpec>`
   (None = inert, bit-identical plain-KS path).
2. `PluckPreset.membrane: &'static [(f32, f32, f32)]`: parallel bandpass head modes
   at the classic (0,1)/(1,1)/(2,1) inharmonic ratios 1.00/1.59/2.14, driven by the
   string+pick signal pre-body-EQ. Empty slice = inert.
3. Engine tarab bus: `Sympathetic::sitar` — 13 chromatic combs C4..C5, fb 0.955,
   damp 5.2 kHz, fed only by program-104 strips, processed only once a 104 strip has
   been seen (`sitar_seen`), switchable for the A/B oracle (`render_buses` 5th arg).

## The two measurement-driven surprises (worth remembering)

- **Amplitude-domain contact cannot make a jawari.** Both a compressive clip and a
  reflecting fold, envelope-tracked or not, dump the distortion energy into h1–h4
  (h1 +14 dB late!) and leave everything above h6 decaying exactly like plain KS.
  Measured via late-window (0.6–1.0 s) Goertzel harmonic tables. The buzz engine is
  the DELAY-MOD leg: a delay shift is a phase shift ∝ frequency, so the n-th partial
  sees n× the modulation index — that is what flattens the spectral envelope.
- **Grazing beats deep contact.** Parameter sweep: shallow thresholds
  (ratio 0.80→0.45 of the tracked envelope) give 7–11× the plain-KS late high-band
  share; deep contact (≤0.28) rectifies into low harmonics and loses the buzz. Also:
  a PROPORTIONAL (unsaturated) contact-depth signal for the wrap outperforms a
  saturated square gate for the same reason.

Also: at comb feedback 0.955 the tarab resonances are a few Hz wide, so the comb
damper's phase delay detunes them off the played pitch — the sitar instance
phase-compensates its delays exactly as `KsLoop::delay_for` does (piano/guitar
instances untouched, they are wide enough not to care).

## Boundedness argument (jawari in a feedback loop)

The contact map J is pointwise nonexpansive: |J(s)| ≤ |s| for every input and every
internal state (proof in the `Jawari::process` doc comment; restitution and wrap are
clamped at construction so this holds for any future preset). J therefore only ever
removes energy from the loop; the linear contraction analysis
(`coupled_loop_margin_holds`: (loop_gain·|H_damp|)² + k² < 1) remains a valid upper
bound, so the string decays to silence — no runaway, no limit cycle. The wrap leg
re-reads the existing (bounded) delay-line contents through the same interpolated
tap every bend already uses; the empirical stability oracle
(`sitar_jawari_is_stable_across_register`) covers it across keys 48–83 × vel
{30,120}: envelope peaks in the attack, decays strictly at 1 s stride, terminal
silence, voice dead < 12 s.

## Dead ends kept for the record

- Compressive one-sided clip (soften 0.12): buzz band within 10% of plain KS.
- Reflecting fold alone: h1 +14 dB, buzz band *below* plain KS (rectification).
- Saturated (square-gate) wrap depth: dumps into h1–h4, weaker than proportional.
