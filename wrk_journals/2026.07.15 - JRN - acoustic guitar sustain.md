# 2026.07.15 — JRN — Acoustic guitar sustain (nylon 24 / steel 25)

**Task.** Arthur: "our acoustic guitar … the non-muted version doesn't seem to
have much sustain … make it more realistic." Investigate + fix.

## Diagnosis (measured, not guessed)

Built an isolated `#[ignore]` sweep harness plus real-engine probes (single-note
envelope trace, held-decay, arpeggio valley depth), baseline binary at HEAD vs the
change. Three findings:

1. **The note-off release was the primary culprit.** Album guitar parts are written
   overwhelmingly as SHORT notes — picked arpeggios / strummed chords of ~0.2–0.55
   beats, gate < 1 (verified across every album that uses prog 24–27). So MIDI
   note-off arrives in ~0.15–0.25 s and the synth's RELEASE governs the sound, not
   the string's natural decay. The acoustic presets inherited `rel_t60 = 0.15 s`
   (the `DEFAULTS` value) — a fast amplitude chop that kills the ring ~0.3 s after
   note-off. A freely-vibrating un-muted string is NOT damped at note-off; it rings
   on. The prior-art record shows this note-off behaviour was never a deliberate
   choice for acoustics (an absence, not a decision).

2. **Nylon's natural decay was genuinely short.** Held E3: RMS t60 ≈ 1.2 s,
   fundamental t60 ≈ 1.9 s; by +1 s it was −43 dB. High notes collapsed fast (E4
   held: −64 dB by 1 s). Steel was better (~2.4 s) but on the short side.

3. **G6 release-darkening and the echo bus are NOT the levers.** Temporarily
   disabling G6 changed the released tail ~1 dB (the tail collapse is the natural
   harmonic decay, not the darkening). The acoustic-guitar echo send (0.08) + CC91
   reverb partly fill the tail in the mix, which is why the isolated release effect
   (dramatic) shows up as a more modest but real +4–9 dB in the full mix.

## Fix (minimal, three fields per acoustic preset)

`crates/ferrosintesis/src/voices.rs`:
- **NYLON (24):** t60 2.8→**3.8**, bright 3200→**3800**, rel_t60 0.15→**1.1**.
- **STEEL (25):** t60 3.5→**4.5**, bright 5200→**5600**, rel_t60 0.15→**1.1**.

`rel_t60 1.1` = "let-ring": short notes now sing and overlap like a real
fingerpicked/strummed guitar, bounded so voices still reap (no hard polyphony cap
for acoustics — a voice lives until it decays < 2e-5). MUTED (28) keeps its 0.08 s
chop (that IS the palm-mute articulation). Skank/staccato chops stay tight because a
0.11 s note is already −46 dB by +0.3 s even with the long release (measured).

Considered but dropped for simplicity: a `course_t60`/`course_mix` nudge to
foreground the slow "aftersound" polarization (added only ~2–4 dB) and a bigger
`bright` bump (brightens the nylon timbre). Both remain easy follow-ups if Arthur
wants more.

## Performance (voice pile-up check)

Longer voice lifetime (let-ring) raises simultaneous voice count, so I timed a
guitar-dense track. Hollow Hill P1 (841 s audio, ~15k notes): baseline 89.7 s vs new
89.7 s render — **1.00×, no regression**. The few extra decaying guitar voices are
dwarfed by fixed per-sample costs (reverb/mix/other channels). Voices still reap
(env < 2e-5 ~1.5–2 s after note-off), so lifetime is bounded. The two very large
tracks (The Long Turning 43k notes, Riverwake 64k) render slowly at BASELINE too —
track size, not the change.
