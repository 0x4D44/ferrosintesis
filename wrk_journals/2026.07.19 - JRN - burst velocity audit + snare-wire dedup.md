Follow-ups surfaced by the MM-BUG-KILN-00001 fix (brush-slap re-strike burst

## A — audit the other `with_bursts` sites for the 00001 velocity-mismatch class

The 00001 skeptic panel flagged two other `with_bursts` call sites as *possible*
same-class bugs: hand clap (key 39, `drums.rs:~1711`) and guiros (keys 73/74,
`drums.rs:~2127`). Investigated both.

**Result: neither is the 00001 bug.** The distinguishing factor is
`membrane_velocity`. The brush slap uniquely built its noise bands through
`membrane_velocity`, which *velocity-scales* the noise amp by
`noise_vel_gain(vn) = 0.5 + 0.5·vn²`; its burst was a fixed absolute `0.50`. That
mismatch (first contact scaled, burst not) is what made the burst/first ratio grow
as velocity fell.

The hand clap and guiros instead pass **fixed** noise bands straight to
`Drum::new` (no `membrane_velocity`), with **absolute** burst amplitudes, and the
whole voice is scaled only by `gain = k·v`. The construction RNG draws depend only
on `seed` (not velocity — `Drum::new` draws `edge`/`jf`/`jd` + one per band, a
count independent of velocity for these empty-tone single-band voices), so
`render(v) = gain(v)·[velocity-independent shape]` — a pure scalar multiple. Hence
their burst/first-contact ratio is **exactly** velocity-invariant and cannot
dominate a soft hit.

Proved it empirically with a new guard test
`absolute_burst_drums_scale_purely_by_gain`: `render(30)·c` reconstructs
`render(100)` (c = the gain ratio) to **rel err 5.5e-8 (key 39), 1.1e-7 (73),
8.6e-8 (74)** — float epsilon. The test pins the invariant so a future change that
velocity-scales one side but not the other reintroduces the 00001 class and fails.

(Note: a *constant* burst-dominates-first ratio, even if >1, is a calibration
choice — NOT the 00001 bug, which was specifically the ratio *growing* at low
velocity. The test correctly targets the velocity-dependence, not loud bursts.)

No code fix needed for A — the audit is clean; the value is the durable guard.

## B — dedup the snare-wire D1 noise-gain into `noise_vel_gain`

`drums.rs:~1673` had a hand-inlined 3rd copy of the noise-velocity law
(`let d1_noise = 0.5 + 0.5 * velnorm * velnorm;`) that MM-BUG-KILN-00001 had
factored into `fn noise_vel_gain`. Folded it to `noise_vel_gain(velnorm)` —
byte-identical (same f32 ops, same precedence). Confirmed by the repo's own frozen
oracles: `v1_drum_render_signatures_are_stable`, `brush_render_signatures_are_stable`,
and every snare oracle pass **unchanged**, so the V3/Brush snare render is bit-exact.
No render-diff needed — the frozen signatures are the stronger byte-identity oracle,
and a byte-identical change cannot contaminate other keys.
