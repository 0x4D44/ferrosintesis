## The road here (worth recording — most of it was a dead end)
1. Arthur: the sampled kit sounds wrong for this track. Shipped bank 1 (`Kit::Synth`,
   PC 24) = today's modeled voices, sample-replacement off. Integrated.
2. Arthur: "closer but not the same" as 12 July. Correct — bank 1 uses TODAY's modeled
   voices (drifted) and lacks the 12-July sampled-attack overlay.
3. So I built a **byte-exact frozen 12-July snapshot** (`drums_0712.rs`, 2472 lines +
   6 embedded WAVs, `Kit::Legacy0712`). It WAS byte-exact — all 85 key×velocity probes
   matched a rebuilt 12-July binary. **But it was the wrong target.**
4. Arthur then flagged the **hi-hat**. Git archaeology: the hi-hat lives on a different
   code path (`cymbal()`/`CymSpec`) from the crash/ride (`MetalPlate`), and was changed
   on **9 Jul** (`a19c662` kit-v2 — added the DR3 open-hat sizzle) and **10 Jul**
   (`0c37435` v0.12). The 11 Jul "P-C" overhaul touched only crash/ride, NOT the hat.
   So the 12-July freeze carried the *post*-overhaul hi-hat.
5. A/B rendered three generations from ONE binary through ONE engine (throwaway
   `FERRO_DRUM_KIT` env override) so the kit was the only variable:
   A=`V1` (pre-kit-v2), B=`V2` (kit-v2, pre-P-C), C=`Legacy0712` (12 July).
   **Arthur picked A** — the original hi-hat, before the DR3 sizzle.
6. A is just `Kit::V1`, already in the tree → the entire frozen 12-July module became
   dead weight and was **removed** (2472 lines + 538 KiB of WAVs deleted).

## Lesson
"Restore what it sounded like on date X" is the wrong framing when the thing you
actually want is an identifiable *voice generation*. Bisecting by ear across the kit
generations the codebase already keeps (V1/V2/V3) found the answer in three renders
and cost one line — versus a byte-exact historical freeze that was technically
perfect and still wrong. **A/B the candidates before building the time machine.**
