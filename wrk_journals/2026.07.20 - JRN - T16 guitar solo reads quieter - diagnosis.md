# 2026.07.20 — JRN — Three-Sixty-One: why the guitar solo reads quieter

## Ask

Arthur: "In Three-Sixty-One, I feel the guitar solo towards the end has become quieter.
Is that right? Is that a levels thing about our instruments? Or was it wrong originally?
And now the track would benefit from a tweak in levels?"

## Method

Built ferrosintesis at eight points in its history in throwaway detached worktrees
(`/d/worktrees/midi-music/AB-<sha>`), re-rendered T16 with each, and measured the ch14
stem against the rest of the mix. All working files under
`/d/worktrees/midi-music/20260720-TSK-HUM-t16-guitar-solo-level-investigation/_ab16`.

Two methodological points that mattered:

- **Stems are normalized.** `--solo N` renders are peak-normalized (or −18 LUFS), so a
  solo-stem RMS says nothing about level in the mix. Un-normalize with the CLI's reported
  raw `peak`: `raw = out * peak / 0.891`. The `peak {:.2}` print rounds, so every
  stem-derived absolute figure carries ±0.2–0.45 dB — sub-1 dB claims built on stem
  scalars are not resolvable. Narrowband measurements on the *delivered mixes* are.
- **Identify what the listener actually heard.** Decoded Arthur's committed listening
  `.opus` (mtime 19 Jul 18:52) with `ropusdec` and matched its per-2 s band-RMS envelope
  against candidate renders. Best match 0.016 dB → it is the **V1 original drum kit**
  render, *without* the later pick-click cut. His previous copy (18 Jul 22:35) was the
  **realistic sampled V3 kit**. That, not "HEAD vs some memory", is the A/B his ears did.

## Findings

### 1. The guitar's level did not change (refuted my own first hypothesis)

Guitar stem md5, by build:

| build | md5 | note |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ `6184b50` (18 Jul 22:43) | `b42017d…` | what Arthur heard on 18 Jul |
| `65eb354` (18 Jul 22:43) | `7ce7081…` | **MicroCab** — the only change that reached the lead |
| `d605c29` (19 Jul) | `ee383fa…` | pick-click cut, **0.000 dB** level effect |

Delivered guitar level across the A/B: **−0.94 dB absolute, +0.13 dB relative to the rest
of the mix.** The drum-kit swap alone, controlled with one binary, is −0.57 dB (drop two) /
−0.74 dB (finale). None of that is audible.

Refuted with measurements, do not resurrect:
- **HF masking by the brighter kit** — the rest of the mix got *quieter* in every band the
  lead occupies (745 Hz −0.6, 877 −1.7, 1021 −1.0, 1365 −0.1, 2454 −0.4 dB). Cymbals sit
  ~9 Bark above the lead; the masking contribution is arithmetically nil.
- **Bus-glue compression ducking the lead under the peakier kit** — −0.06 dB.
- **The pick-click cut** — 0.000 dB, and it post-dates Arthur's render by two hours anyway.

### 2. The real change: `65eb354` MicroCab reshaped the lead (bisected, byte-exact)

`65eb354` "MicroCab cabinet fine structure for GM29/30" landed **10 minutes after** Arthur's
18 Jul render. Bisect is exact: `6184b50`'s stem is byte-identical to the 18-Jul render,
`65eb354`'s is byte-identical to today's.

It is a sparse-FIR cabinet ripple: *"6 magnitude alternations ≥1.5 dB across 900–3600 Hz,
swing 6.2 dB … band-average normalized so the ripple recolors without moving level."*

Band-average normalized is true — **for broadband material**. A sustained lead holding one
pitch for 7 beats gets whatever the ripple does *at that pitch*. T16's finale sits on E6 and
C6, and both land in troughs:

| held tone | f0 | Δf0 | Δh2 | Δh3 | Δ broadband |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E6 (key 88, 10 of 15 finale tones) | 1318.5 Hz | **−1.03 dB** | +2.90 | +2.78 | −0.76 |
| C6 (key 84) | 1046.5 Hz | **−2.08 dB** | −1.35 | −1.35 | **−2.00** |
| G6 (key 91) | 1568.0 Hz | +0.71 | −1.29 | −0.01 | +0.72 |
| A6 (key 93) | 1760.0 Hz | +0.53 | −0.33 | +2.65 | +0.39 |

Octave-band, guitar stem, dense 4 s of the finale soar: **707–1414 Hz −2.10 dB** — exactly
where the finale's fundamentals live — while every band above it rose. Finale broadband RMS
−0.25 dB but **peak +2.4 dB**.

So the lead lost body and gained edge. The ear judges a pitched tone's loudness largely from
its fundamental and low harmonics, so "thinner and peakier at the same RMS" reads as
*quieter*. This is the mechanism.

### 3. The kit swap steals loudness budget from everything

Kit::V1 vs the sampled V3 kit: **+9.9 dB at 6–14 kHz, +19.4 dB at 14–20 kHz**. The guitar has
essentially nothing above 5 kHz (32.7 dB down on its own 1–3 kHz energy), so this is not
masking — it is a **loudness-budget transfer**. Removing everything above 5 kHz swings the
V1-vs-V3 loudness difference from +0.331 dB to −0.307 dB, i.e. the kit's top end is worth
**+0.638 dB of K-weighted loudness** that the −18 LUFS normalizer takes straight back off
every instrument. It also drives the true-peak limiter: 1.9–2.4 % of windows above 1 dB gain
reduction vs 0.0 % for the sampled kit, worth another −0.18 dB.

**The "near-Nyquist aliasing" reading of this was investigated and refuted.** The V1 kit does
measure −39.7 dBFS at 21–22.05 kHz vs the sampled kit's −60.1, but 21–22.05 kHz is 1050 Hz
wide against a 115 Hz third-octave — 9.6 dB of bandwidth advantage before any signal. Per-Hz,
V1 reads −69.9 dB/Hz at 21–22 kHz vs −67.2 dB/Hz at 500 Hz: the HF is 2.7 dB *quieter*, not
7.4 dB hotter. The per-Hz spectrum is flat from 10 kHz to Nyquist with no rise and no mirrored
image — the opposite of an aliasing signature — and `Kit::Synth` measures 1.4 dB *hotter* still,
so it is not a V1 property at all. Mechanism: modeled cymbals are white noise
(`dsp.rs:23 Rng::white`) that is only ever **high**passed (`drums.rs:302`, bands built at
`:897/:907/:926/:946`), and the RBJ highpass has gain exactly 1.0 at Nyquist (`dsp.rs:461`).
Aliasing from partials is ruled out by inspection (pure sines, highest modeled partial 14.4 kHz,
`MetalPlate` capped at 14 kHz); `Kit::V1` never touches the sample interpolator
(`drums.rs:1547`). So this is a legitimate timbre observation — the modeled kit is hissier than
recorded cymbals — affecting V1, V3-modeled and Synth alike. Not a defect; a default-on timbre
change if anyone wants to act on it, so Arthur's call.

Ch-10 PC 25 (Kit::V1) census, running-status-aware parse of all 124 committed album MIDIs:
17 files — all 10 of `fable5/Slipstream`, `Through Lines` tracks 10 and 16, and all 5 of
`gpt5-6/Bright Matter`.

### 4. The ending genuinely tapers — and always did

Present in **both** renders Arthur A/B'd, so authored, not a regression:
- Guitar-minus-band in 700–2500 Hz: **+2.14 dB at beat 600 → −3.76 dB at beat 608** — a
  5.3 dB fall at 300.0 s while the lead is still playing.
- The lead's last note releases at **beat 632 (311.25 s)**; the orbit riff runs to 313.8 s,
  the saw to 315.0 s, the drums to 315.9 s. For the final **~3.75 s the ratio is −22.3 dB**
  because the lead is simply gone.

Note the finale is otherwise a *good* place for the lead: against the rest of the band in
700–2500 Hz it is better off than drop two (−1.58 vs −2.06 dB) and gains +1.2 dB absolute.

### 5. Three voices permanently contest the lead's band

Guitar, saw walker (ch5 GM81) and orbit steel (ch0 GM114) sit inside a **0.63 dB window** in
700–2500 Hz. Short-term the guitar is below the saw in 46.3 % of 30 ms windows and below the
orbit in 39.2 %. In the C6 third-octave — where the last 24 beats live — the guitar is 1.8 dB
below the saw and **6.9 dB below the orbit**.

### 6. Candidate fix measured, not modelled: ch14 CC7 118 → 127

Patched only the CC7 value in a scratch copy of the MIDI (`patch_cc7.py`, running-status
aware; album source untouched) and re-rendered with the HEAD binary. Delivered guitar level in
the lead's band, against the 18-Jul render Arthur A/B'd:

| state | DROP2 | FINALE | last 24 beats | vs rest (finale) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 Jul (pre-MicroCab) | −33.45 | −32.48 | −33.63 | −1.75 |
| today (CC7 118) | −34.53 (**−1.08**) | −33.30 (**−0.82**) | −35.14 (**−1.51**) | −1.58 |
| fix (CC7 127) | −33.44 (**+0.01**) | −32.22 (**+0.26**) | −34.06 (**−0.42**) | **−0.46** |

CC7 127 restores the pre-MicroCab level essentially exactly, and improves the lead's ratio to
the rest of the band by **+1.1 dB** everywhere. The last 24 beats stay 0.42 dB short because
that passage is C6-heavy and C6 is where MicroCab's ripple trough is deepest (−2.08 dB).
Composition layer, T16 only, no version bump, zero blast radius.

## The fix (Arthur chose composition + "fix the MicroCab too")

### Synth layer — the ripple depth is a property of the BANK

Not a re-tune of `MICRO_CAB_TAPS`. The band-average normalization is *correct* for
broadband material — a chugging rhythm guitar spreads its energy across 900–3600 Hz and
averages the comb out. It is only invalid for a voice that HOLDS ONE PITCH. That is
exactly what separates the two driven banks, so the depth belongs to the bank:

- `MICRO_CAB_LEAD_DEPTH = 0.25` scales the tap gains for the CC0 alt bank (`DRIVE_LEAD`).
- The default bank keeps depth 1.0 — **bit-identical**, so every rhythm-guitar track is
  untouched and the two existing MicroCab oracles pass unchanged.
- `MicroCab::with_depth` replaces `new`; normalization is recomputed from the scaled taps,
  so the band average stays unity at every depth (depth moves the ripple's *deviation*,
  never the band's level).
- `Drive` gained an `alt` field. The strip rebuilds the insert when the **bank** changes,
  not just the program — and the CC0 handler rebuilds too, so a foreign GM file that sends
  CC0 *after* the program change gets the same result as our albums, which order
  bank-select first.

Worst deviation any held pitch can suffer, 900–3600 Hz: **3.39 dB → 0.85 dB**, with the
ripple still measurably present (1.54 dB swing — a cab, not a DI).

Blast radius, from a running-status-aware parse of all 124 committed album MIDIs: **11
tracks** send CC0≠0 on a GM 29/30 channel — all 10 of `fable5/Slipstream` plus T16.
Everything else is byte-identical.

New oracles: `sustaining_lead_cab_ripple_is_bounded_at_every_held_pitch` (RED if the depth
is restored to full, and RED the other way if someone flattens the ripple to nothing) and
`cc0_bank_select_reaches_the_driven_cabinet_in_either_order`.

## Surprises

- **My leading hypothesis was wrong and the numbers killed it.** "Brighter cymbals mask the
  lead" is intuitive and false: the cymbals are ~9 Bark above the lead and the mix got
  *quieter* in every band the lead occupies. The kit hurts via the loudness normalizer and
  the limiter, not via masking.
- **A level-neutral filter is not level-neutral for a sustained lead.** MicroCab normalizes
  its ripple on band average, which is correct for broadband material and wrong for a voice
  that holds one pitch. Worth a guard: check preset ripple depth at the pitches a
  *sustaining* preset (DRIVE_LEAD) is actually asked to hold.
- **`build.py --verify` does NOT write the `.mid`.** It rebuilds the Score in memory, runs
  the oracles against that, and re-parses the *existing* file. So after editing a movement,
  `--verify` alone reports green on your new music while the committed MIDI still holds the
  old — I rendered a "fixed" track that was byte-identical to the unfixed one and only caught
  it because a byte comparison of the two MIDIs said IDENTICAL. Always run a bare
  `python build.py` first, then `--verify`.
- **Re-check a stem's raw peak after every re-render.** The un-normalization scalar is the
  CLI's reported peak; reusing a stale one (0.18 for a stem that now peaks 0.21) silently
  shifts every absolute dB by 1.3 dB. It made the first "fixed" measurement look like the
  fix had barely worked.
- **The album is in three different synth states on disk.** Tracks 01–09 and 11–15 are the
  old sampled kit (18 Jul 22:33–22:38), track 16 is the V1 kit (19 Jul 18:52), track 10 was
  re-rendered 19 Jul 21:30. Any cross-album A/B is confounded — run
  `cargo run --release -p render-catalog` before further listening judgements.
