# 2026.07.20 — JRN — velocity-curve alignment to the GM references

Task branch: `task/20260720-DEV-HUM-instrument-balance-oracle-and-drum-forwa`
(reused — this is a later pass of the instrument-balance work).

## How we got here

Arthur asked why Three-Sixty-One's guitar solo reads quieter, then widened it: are the
instruments balanced against each other, and is there auto-level logic? That produced
`wrk_docs/2026.07.20 - HLD - instrument balance oracle + drum-forward recalibration.md`
and a decision to align to the SC-55 for GM-corpus compatibility, undamped, using **two**
references (SC-55 + S-YXG50; the MU80/MU50 are known-broken).

While pre-flighting the level-calibration probe, **PF11 (velocity-scaling sanity check)
surfaced a systematic defect that matters more than the level imbalance**, and blocks the
level derivation. This journal covers fixing that first.

## The finding

Measured on the 12-program pre-flight probe, 6 pitches, level = max momentary BS.1770
block, calibration build (trims inert, bus glue bypassed):

| engine | v72 -> v110 | spread across programs |
|---|---|---|
| SC-55 | **+7.25 dB** | +6.51 .. +7.42 (tight) |
| S-YXG50 | **+6.78 dB** | +6.41 .. +7.18 (tight) |
| ferrosintesis | **+3.25 dB** | +1.96 .. +8.89 (wild) |

Both references agree and are consistent. ferrosintesis has **less than half the dynamic
range in dB** and is inconsistent where they are consistent.

## Root cause (traced, not guessed)

`dsp.rs:726` — `vel_amp(v) = (v/127)^1.6`. On its own that is 5.89 dB over 72→110.

But the call sites do not use it uniformly. Census across `crates/ferrosintesis/src/`:

- **39 sites** use bare `vel_amp(vel)` → 5.89 dB
- **18 sites** wrap it in a floor `X + Y*vel_amp(vel)`, with **six different constant
  pairs**: `0.4+0.6` (x11), `0.55+0.45` (x2), `0.35+0.65` (x2), `0.25+0.75`, `0.22+0.82`,
  `0.03+0.22`

`0.4 + 0.6*vel_amp` compresses 72→110 to **2.71 dB**; `0.55 + 0.45*vel_amp` to **1.88 dB**.
The measured ferro median of 3.25 dB with a 1.96–8.89 spread is exactly what a mixture of
these idioms predicts.

Reference points: amplitude linear in velocity would be 3.68 dB over this interval;
velocity-squared would be 7.36 dB. Both modules sit near velocity-squared; ferrosintesis
sits near linear, and the floor idiom pushes it below even that.

## Why it matters (two consequences)

1. Every composed dynamic arc renders at roughly 40 % of its intended dB range on melodic
   voices. The album oracles check the arc is *present in the MIDI*; nothing checks the
   synth reproduces its magnitude.
2. **Drums use bare `vel_amp` with no floor** (`drums.rs:588`) — ~5.9 dB of velocity range
   against melodic voices' ~2.7 dB. So drums pull ahead of the band as a passage gets
   louder. That is a balance *instability*, not an offset, and no static level constant can
   fix it. Strong candidate for why Arthur hears drums forward specifically in loud sections.

## Constraint discovered while reading

Not every voice should track velocity. `voices.rs:14084
cathedral_organ_steady_level_is_velocity_independent` asserts <= 1.5 dB — correct, real pipe
organs are velocity-independent. Any global change must not "fix" that.

Sampled voices also select velocity *layers* (`sampler.rs` `piano_bank`, `grand_bank`,
`steinwayb_bank`, `kawai_bank`, `contrabass_bank`, `cello_bank` switch on velocity
thresholds), so velocity carries timbre as well as level there. A level-only change must
not disturb layer selection.

## Log

- Extended the probe with a `velcurve` set (12 programs x 2 keys x 9 velocities
  8..127) to characterise the reference curve *shape*, not just its value at two points —
  two points give a slope, and the design needs to know whether the modules are a power
  law, a piecewise curve, or something else.
- Pre-flight results carried over from the level work: PF4 (trap closure) exact at 0.000 dB
  on all 12 programs, PF8 (silence) and PF2 (clipping) pass, PF7 meter calibration reads
  -22.993 against the EBU 3341 normative -23.0.
- Bus-glue bypass justified empirically: with glue active, levels move -0.34..+0.83 dB
  across just 12 programs (1.17 dB spread). A pure scalar would be 0.00 and would cancel in
  median-referencing; it does not cancel.

## The reference curve, measured (9 velocities, 3 engines)

Fitting `amp = (v/127)^k`, least squares on level vs `20*log10(v/127)`, v >= 32:

| engine | median k | range |
|---|---|---|
| SC-55 | **1.997** | 1.67 .. 2.06 |
| S-YXG50 | **1.981** | 1.96 .. 2.05 |
| ferrosintesis | **0.660** | 0.36 .. 2.03 |

Level relative to each program's own v=127 (median over programs, dB):

| v = | 16 | 32 | 48 | 64 | 80 | 96 | 110 | 127 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC-55 | -28.4 | -23.9 | -17.0 | -11.9 | -8.3 | -4.9 | -2.6 | 0 |
| S-YXG50 | -34.8 | -23.5 | -16.7 | -11.4 | -7.5 | -4.6 | -2.3 | 0 |
| ferro | -8.9 | -7.5 | -6.6 | -5.4 | -3.7 | -2.3 | -1.4 | 0 |

`(v/127)^2` reproduces the SC-55 to **0.22 dB worst-case** over v=32..110 (recomputed
independently). Two independent implementations agreeing that closely is a specification —
the DLS/SoundFont convention — not a preference. At v=32 ferrosintesis renders a note
**14.8 dB louder** relative to its own fortissimo than the SC-55 does.

## Design (Fable 5 consult, verified before adoption)

`wrk_docs/2026.07.20 - HLD - velocity law alignment to k=2.md`. Exponent 1.6 -> 2.0 at the
single definition site; delete the 14 level floors; keep 3 timbre floors.

Three corrections Fable made to MY census, all re-verified at source:

- `voices.rs:6914` is a filter **cutoff** (`spec.cutoff * (0.55 + 0.45*vel_amp)`), not a level.
- `voices.rs:8080` is **bow speed** — its own comment says "bow SPEED drives brightness".
- `voices.rs:5367` is a 0.012-scale **chiff** transient; level contribution negligible.

Blind floor deletion would have turned two of those into level laws and wrecked timbre.

My census also MISSED a true level floor at `voices.rs:8098`
(`amp: amp_base + amp_span * vel_amp(vel)`) because it uses computed rather than literal
constants, so a literal-pattern grep never saw it. Lesson: a syntactic census of a numeric
idiom finds the instances that were written the way you imagined them.

Nice property of the change: every literal floor pair except GM5's sums to exactly 1.0, so
deleting them leaves **v=127 output bit-unchanged** — the curve rotates about full velocity
rather than moving the top. The hand-tuned fortissimo voicing is preserved.

## Review outcomes

**Adversarial (3 lenses, cross-refutation): 11 raw findings, 2 survivors — both the same
defect.** The `voices.rs:8098` BowedString floor does not sum to 1.0 (1.18 cello / 1.80
contrabass), so the design's "delete the floors" would have dropped the contrabass 5.11 dB
at FULL velocity — violating its own v=127 invariant, and erasing the deliberate 3.7 dB
cello/contrabass register balance. Folded: the transform is now `(X+Y)*vel_amp` universally,
which makes the bug class impossible rather than patching one site.

Nine findings were refuted, several *empirically* — refuters copied the workspace, applied
the design's edits and measured. Refuted: the GM24/25 v^2.8 objection (the guitar lands in
band once the edits are applied), the GM29/30 waveshaper objection ("load-bearing number
wrong by ~5 dB"), both sax objections (the claimed ~19 dB embedded layer step does not
exist), the AC7 circularity, and T6's non-computability.

Worth noting for my own calibration: I relayed several of those to Arthur as significant
BEFORE refutation ran. They did not survive. Cross-refutation is not ceremony — a
plausible-sounding objection from a competent reviewer is still often wrong, and reporting
critique before it has been attacked misleads.

**External (Codex gpt-5.6-sol, degraded):** this machine's `codex-windows-sandbox-setup.exe`
is missing, so Codex could not read any repo file. It correctly refused rather than
fabricate; re-run with the design inlined. It reviewed judgement and acceptance criteria but
could NOT verify any file:line claim. **Residual risk carried: no external reviewer has
checked this design against actual source.**

Codex's two blockers were both against MY test harness, and both were right:
- the oracle used unweighted RMS while the evidence used BS.1770 momentary — and the
  *retained* timbre floors make spectrum velocity-dependent by design, so the two estimators
  can diverge. Now uses `momentary_lufs`. Measuring the fix with a different instrument from
  the one that found the defect is how you ship a green suite and a wrong synth.
- AC8 compared absolute levels where it must compare normalised curves.

Also adopted from Codex: assert per key rather than averaging (averaging hides one key low
and one high); AC3 must sample ACROSS the layer seams (the shared fit set contains neither
51/52 nor 95/96, so as written it could not see a seam at all); and 16 programs cannot prove
"every velocity-sensitive voice" — `voices.rs:3861` has its own `powf` literal, so a voice
can bypass the shared helper entirely.

## The 128-program census (fail-first, current code)

Adding the full sweep took the suite to ~15 s and immediately paid: it shows the bimodal
structure directly, and found two cases the 16-program probe would have missed —
**GM24/25 at k=2.26** (above 2.0; LA sample layer) and **GM109 bagpipe at k=0.00**

GM64-67 saxes 0.22-0.40 (most compressed of all).

## Second consultation (Fable 5 + Codex gpt-5.6-sol-xhigh) — six real defects found

After implementing, I consulted both models on how to finish. Between them they found six
real defects in my work, including one I introduced and one where I wrote a false statement
into the code with test authority. Both said the core change is right and to LAND it.

Fixed (all verified before adoption):
1. **Sub-v32 blow-up (I introduced this).** `ScaledVoice::gain = (v/127)^(exp-2)`
   extrapolated a fit made over v>=32; measured GM57 +13.1 dB at v8, +32.9 at v1. Clamped
   the correction at the fit floor (COMP_FIT_FLOOR = 32/127) so it freezes below the
   measured domain. Fitted range bit-identical.
2. **"Timbre untouched" was FALSE (Codex).** The 3 kept timbre floors call `vel_amp`, which
   I changed, so the lead cutoff / bow speed / chiff all moved up to 5.2% at mid velocity.
   Split into `vel_ctrl` (keeps the historic 1.6 curve for expressive control mappings) vs
   `vel_amp` (the square LOUDNESS law). The references measured loudness, not a cutoff law.
3. **False organ exclusion (Fable).** I excluded GM16-20 saying "pipe organs are
   velocity-independent". Measured: k=2.000 exactly, both keys, all five — they follow the
   law perfectly. Un-excluded. An exclusion with a false justification is worse than a wide
   tolerance: it records a wrong fact with test authority.
4. **Differential-pairing break (Fable, generalises).** `fretless_bass_35_mwah_blooms`
   pairs `make(35)` (now ScaledVoice-wrapped) against a bare `Pluck::new`, so the residual
   carried -4.4% of the whole voice. Symmetrized the pair; documented the hazard at
   `ScaledVoice` for the ~30 programs it could affect (only GM35 tripped).
5. **Timpani bound (Fable).** The 8x peak cap was only satisfiable under the retired 1.6
   exponent; (120/40)^2 = 9x by construction now. Re-derived the bound FROM THE LAW.
6. **EP bark turnover (Fable confirmed by measurement).** Real, same class as the bowed
   strings — peaks at v~105. Narrowed the test to its valid domain with a self-retiring
   guard; filed with GM42/43 as MM-BUG-KILN-00027.

Fable corrected the mechanism both Codex and I had backwards: the old 1.6 law drove
sub-127 velocities HARDER (x^1.6 > x^2 for x<1), so it masked the turnovers by
over-saturating the middle, not by never reaching the top. My "saturation because the new
law drives them harder" hypothesis was arithmetically impossible.

Also filed:
- MM-BUG-KILN-00027 — GM42/43 + GM4 voice-model velocity turnover near full velocity.
- MM-BUG-KILN-00028 — harpsichord LA onset does not track its vel_sense model (crossfade

Golden re-pins: a scripted pass re-pinned 17 rms values with a SAFETY GATE that refuses any
fixture whose centroid or envelope moved (timbre must not change) — 0 refusals, every shift
matched the analytic law delta (worst 0.011 dB). NYLON canary and the FNV trumpet hash
re-pinned analytically. The golden-mix centroids that moved were verified as level-driven
aggregate re-weighting (each channel plays v40/90/120; quiet notes drop, so the aggregate
tilts toward the loud note), not per-note timbre change.
