# 2026.07.22 — JRN — KILN-00042: generalizing the KS treble hold

Fixing the Karplus-Strong register-dependent decay collapse across the 20 non-piano
`Pluck` presets. GM6 harpsichord / GM7 clavinet are out of scope — another agent is
mid-flight on the piano family.

## UNDERSTAND — the measured baseline

Built a wider probe than the standard calibration one: 20 in-scope GM programs x 9 keys
(40–80, ~3.3 octaves) x v110 = 180 notes, 471 s. A 9-key axis so a power law can be
*fitted* rather than interpolated between two points. Bass gets a −12 offset instead of
mkprobe's −24, which would put the bottom keys under the BS.1770 RLB knee (the
knee-pollution trap already recorded in the bug's scope note).

Rendered all three engines and metered them. **Both references agree closely with each
other and both disagree with ferro the same way:**

| | median register tilt | median decay rate |
|---|---|---|
| **ferro** | **5.00x** | **42.1 dB/s** |
| SC-55mkII | 1.82x | 11.8 dB/s |
| S-YXG50 | 1.72x | 12.3 dB/s |

So ferro is **~3.5x too fast in absolute terms** *and* tilts **~2.8x too steeply** on top
of that. Worst: GM107 koto 14.1x, GM26 jazz guitar 13.5x, GM30 distortion 7.5x, GM24
nylon 7.3x.

**GM24 nylon still shows 7.3x despite already carrying `treble_hold_hz: 500`** — the same
finding as yesterday's DRIVE sweep, now confirmed on the preset the lever was originally
written for. The 500 Hz anchor is too high to bite in the playing register.

## The analytic model — and why it was worth building

Mirrored the decay law in Python (`_cal/law.py`) so anchors can be fitted in milliseconds
instead of by minutes-long render sweeps. Validated against the measured ferro rates:
**median measured/model 1.58, p10–p90 0.90–2.39** over 164 notes.

The ~1.58 offset is expected and *not* an error: the model is fundamental-only while the
BS.1770 metric is broadband, and partial n sees ~n² the damper loss. The p10–p90 spread
means the model is good enough to **choose** a candidate but not to certify one — so the
workflow is fit analytically, then confirm by render.

## The finding that reshapes the fix

Decomposing each preset's loss into its two terms at the bottom and top of the register
splits the 20 presets into two groups that need **different levers**:

**Damper-dominated — the anchor fixes these (11):** CLEAN, DRIVE, FRETLESS, HARP, JAZZ,
KOTO, NYLON, OUD, PICK, SLAP, UPRIGHT. Some are extreme at the top of their range —
UPRIGHT 1657 dB/s, FRETLESS 1293, KOTO 451.

**loop_gain-dominated — an anchor does nothing (8):** BANJO, DULCIMER, HARMONIC,
SHAMISEN, SITAR, SLAP_POP, STEEL, UKULELE. Their `t60_base` is simply short (BANJO 0.75,
SHAMISEN 0.55), so they lose 47–64 dB/s at the *bottom* key where the damper contributes
~0.

**Both:** MUTED (0.45 / 1600).

Note **STEEL is loop-dominated yet is one of the two presets that has an anchor** — the
lever was authored where it was least needed.

This matters for scope. The 8 loop-dominated presets can only be fixed by lengthening
`t60_base`, and that is a **voicing change, not a mechanical one**: a banjo genuinely is
a short instrument. Against the references ferro's banjo is still 3.6x too short at the
bottom (63.1 vs SC-55's 17.4 dB/s), so there is real headroom — but how much of it to
take is Arthur's ear, not my arithmetic.

## DESIGN — three candidate architectures, tested

### Physics, verified numerically before trusting any of it

- **Above the anchor, damper loss is flat in f.** Confirmed: DRIVE at anchor 300 holds
  0.84–1.42 dB/s from k60–k75.
- **`fc ∝ f` (a plain tracking filter) leaves loss ∝ f.** Confirmed: d/f is constant at
  0.066. **So the bug ledger's "make the cutoff track the fundamental" recommendation does
  not flatten anything** — it trades f³ for f¹. I have corrected the ledger.
- **Flat-region loss ∝ anchor³/bright², so equal loss ⟺ anchor ∝ bright^⅔.** Confirmed
  exactly: anchors derived that way give an identical 3.37 dB/s at k80 for presets whose
  `bright` spans 900–9000.

A pleasing corollary: with `anchor ∝ bright^⅔` the `sr·0.45` saturation ceiling falls at
**key 72 for every preset**, independent of `bright` — the design has one well-defined
working ceiling instead of a per-preset one.

### Candidate A — derived anchor (one global constant). Built, rendered, measured.

`anchor = 0.6934 · bright^⅔`, i.e. one constant replacing 20 hand-fitted numbers.

| | median tilt | median rate |
|---|---|---|
| baseline | 5.00x | 42.1 dB/s |
| **candidate A** | **3.19x** | 37.8 dB/s |
| references | 1.7–1.8x | ~12 dB/s |

Spectacular where the damper dominates — **KOTO 14.1x → 1.0x**, DRIVE 6.9x/7.5x →
0.7x/0.7x (matching SC-55 exactly), HARP 6.9x → 2.1x, JAZZ 13.5x → 3.7x. Untouched where
the loop term dominates, exactly as the diagnosis predicted (BANJO 3.4x → 3.4x, SITAR
4.1x → 4.2x).

**But it has a real flaw, and the render found it.** PICK 3.4x → 4.2x and FRETLESS
4.5x → 5.0x got *worse*. Cause: `bright` is the in-loop damper cutoff, so it controls
**timbre as well as decay**, and holding the loss target constant inflates it brutally for
dark presets:

| preset | bright | inflation at k72 |
|---|---|---|
| UPRIGHT | 900 | **22.1x** |
| FRETLESS | 1050 | 18.9x |
| MUTED | 1600 | 12.4x |
| KOTO | 1900 | 10.4x |
| DRIVE / SITAR | 9000 / 8500 | 2.2x / 2.3x |

An upright bass whose damper cutoff moves from 900 Hz to ~20 kHz is not an upright bass.
And the newly-alive high partials decay fast (n² loss), which is why the *broadband*
metric regressed on exactly those presets. **Candidate A buys tilt with timbre, at a price
that scales with how dark the instrument is.**

### Candidate B — normalize the damper at the fundamental. Rejected on stability.

`loop_gain / damp_mag(f)` would make the fundamental decay at exactly `60/t60`. But
normalized gain at DC is `1/damp_mag(f) > 1`, so the loop self-oscillates: **17 of 20
presets go unstable** somewhere in the register (UPRIGHT reaches loop gain 1.35, FRETLESS
1.27). Rejected as stated — though it points at the right idea.

### Candidate C — make the in-loop damper unity-gain at the fundamental (a SHELF)

The tension both A and B hit is that one lowpass is being asked to do two jobs. The fix is
to stop asking: replace the in-loop **lowpass** with a **high shelf** that is unity below
its corner and attenuating above it. Then

- the damper contributes **no** fundamental loss → the f³ term disappears by construction;
- decay becomes exactly `60/t60` — designed, not emergent;
- `bright` becomes purely a timbre control (orthogonal parameters);
- gain ≤ 1 everywhere → unconditionally stable, unlike B;
- residual tilt is only the `f^0.55` t60 scale = **2.2x**, already close to the references'
  1.7–1.8x;
- `treble_hold_hz` and its anchor can retire entirely.

It needs no new filter type and no biquad in the inner loop — a one-pole shelf is one
multiply-add over the existing `OnePole`:

    shelf(x) = hf + (1 - hf) * lowpass(x)      // unity at DC, `hf` at Nyquist

This is my leading candidate. It is a bigger change than an anchor tweak, but it *removes*
a parameter interaction rather than adding one, which is the direction the repo's
simplicity philosophy points.

## The scope line I want to hold

Inverting the references for the *absolute* rate says `t60_base` needs a median **2.2x**
lengthening — but per-preset and not even in a consistent direction (MUTED 9.3x longer,
SHAMISEN 6.5x, yet KOTO 0.3x and DULCIMER 0.7x, i.e. *shorter*).

That is **voicing, not a defect**. A palm mute is supposed to be short; that the
references' muted-guitar patch rings longer makes ferro different, not wrong. So I propose
scoping this bug to the **register law (the tilt)**, which is unambiguously a defect —
nobody designs an instrument whose top octave dies 5x faster than its bottom, and both
references independently agree at 1.7–1.8x — and leaving the absolute rate as a separate,
ear-led voicing pass.

## Fable's verdict, and where it corrected me

Fable confirmed both contested physics claims (fc ∝ f leaves loss ∝ f, so the ledger's
"track the fundamental" recommendation is wrong; anchor ∝ bright^⅔ is the constant-loss
scaling) and recommended candidate A framed explicitly: one global ceiling `L_MAX` on the
damper's decay share, with the anchor *derived* from it rather than hand-set.

It also killed my candidate C. I had thought a unity-at-the-fundamental shelf would avoid
the corner inflation. It does not: to be unity **at f**, the corner must sit above f, so
corner inflation is unavoidable for high notes on dark presets. C reduces to A. Good
consult — that's a wrong idea caught before I built it.

**And it found a real flaw in my evidence.** Yesterday's DRIVE anchor sweep is
contaminated: GM29/30 render through the engine's `Drive` tanh, deliberately driven past
the knee, and a saturator compresses level trajectories. I tested Fable's tripwire
directly — comparing the model's predicted residual tilt against measurement, per preset:

- **linear-path presets track the model**: CLEAN 0.94, JAZZ 0.96, UPRIGHT 1.03,
  FRETLESS 0.94, SHAMISEN 1.06, SLAP 1.11 — median ≈ 1.06.
- **DRIVE reads −11.1x and −6.3x** — measured residual *negative* where the model says
  +23 dB/s. Completely decoupled.

So "300 Hz flattens DRIVE" was measuring the compressor, not the string. Yesterday's
*decision* (discard the +6 dB trim) stands — it rested on the register collapse being
real, which every linear preset confirms — but the anchor value never should have been
calibrated there. L_MAX = 1.5 dB/s now rests on Fable's independent subordination
argument instead (the damper is ~5 % of the median preset's top-of-register decay).

## IMPLEMENT — what landed

`treble_hold_hz: f32` → `damper_hold: DamperHold {Off, Derived}`, with
`KS_DAMP_DBPS_MAX = 1.5` and the anchor derived from it. Default is **Derived**: the f³
collapse is a defect, so presets opt *out*, not in. NYLON/STEEL's hand-picked 500 Hz
anchors are gone — measured, they were holding the damper at 37.6 and 17.3 dB/s, so they
had only lifted the E6 cliff onto a still-fast plateau.

Result, analytically, across the register: **17 of 18 `Derived` presets land at
0.89–1.01x of the tilt the `t60` key-scale intends.** UPRIGHT is the lone outlier.

**Oracles.** A closed-form primary oracle (`ks_decay_law_holds_across_register`) asserting
both the ceiling and the tilt for every preset × key × velocity — ~4,800 points, no audio,
sharing `DamperHold::anchor` and `OnePole::lowpass_mag` with the shipped code so the two
cannot drift. Proven to fail before the fix (NYLON 5.18x) and pass after. Plus a vacuity
guard on coverage, and an authoring pin that lists every opt-out — closing the loophole
that a preset quietly set to `Off` would silently leave the primary oracle's coverage.

## Two exclusions, one measured, one still open

**BASS and FRETLESS are `Off`,** blocked on a pre-existing coupling this fix *exposes*
rather than causes. The in-loop damper corner scales with velocity, so the decay RATE does
too — but on a real string, damping is a property of the string, not of how hard you pluck
it. Today that error is invisible on these two because they are so dark that every note
dies almost instantly at every velocity (GM35 key 60: 661 dB/s at vel 32, 144 at vel 127),
leaving only the onset measurable, so level tracks vel² cleanly. Hold the damper open and
the notes finally sustain — at which point their velocity-dependent decay contributes
slope and GM33/GM35 bend to k = 2.38/2.62 against the k = 2 ± 0.25 square law.

I swept for a global escape and there isn't one: the velocity law only comes back at
L_MAX ≈ 24 dB/s, which is more decay than the references show in *total*. A hold cap
doesn't rescue it either (cap 4 still leaves GM35 at 2.50), and once these two are
excluded the cap earns nothing at all — so I removed it rather than keep a knob that
buys nothing. The correct fix is to move velocity→brightness out of the loop damper and
into the excitation, where `pick_lp` already does it. That is a re-voicing, not this
defect.

## The fork — the fix compresses authored brightness contrast

Three independent oracles now fail for **one** reason, and it is not a re-baseline:

- `ukulele_variation_is_brighter_and_shorter_than_nylon`: uke centroid 1083 vs nylon 1245.
  The relationship has **inverted**. Above the crossover the corner is `f^1.5/K^1.5` —
  *independent of `bright`* — so two presets converge on the same damper curve. The darker
  preset gets the bigger lift, and an authored contrast disappears.
- `sitar_jawari_buzz_survives_decay`: the jawari's late 2–6 kHz share vs plain KS narrows
  to 0.0579 vs 0.0563. Same cause — plain KS is now bright too.
- The corner inflation I measured earlier: 22x for UPRIGHT at key 72, 19x FRETLESS,
  12x MUTED, against 2.2x for DRIVE. It scales inversely with how dark the preset is.

This is intrinsic, not a bug in the implementation: flat decay *requires* the corner/f
ratio to grow as √f, so high notes are necessarily brighter relative to their fundamental
than low ones. Fable flagged exactly this as the place Arthur's ear must arbitrate.

**And the sustainer breaks** (Fable's tripwire 4, confirmed): `sustain_holds_high_notes`
has DRIVE_LEAD at key 88 **rising +8 to +12 dB** over its window instead of holding at
−8.3. `sus_headroom` sets the e-bow drive from the loop deficit *including* the damper;
holding the damper open shrinks that deficit, so the sustainer over-drives. A lead that
swells 12 dB is not shippable — the sustainer needs recalibrating against the new law, or
DRIVE_LEAD needs excluding.

## LANDING — Arthur approved after the listening test

Built a 12-file A/B audition (Reference Audition tracks 1/2/4, a purpose-built contrast
probe for the uke/oud XG variations the FRA cannot reach, and two album tracks in a mix).

**One flaw in the first cut, worth remembering:** the CLI loudness-normalises every render
to −18 LUFS *independently*, which for an A/B cancels the very thing under test — the fix
makes notes ring longer, so NEW gets scaled down and every *unchanged* instrument then
reads quieter on NEW. The controls appeared to differ. Re-rendered un-normalised via
`raw_dump` with one common scalar per pair; controls then read **+0.00 dB** and the koto's
sustain window **+10 dB** (vs the +4.4 dB the normalised files had suggested). On the
Reference Audition tracks the bias was only 0.03 dB, since most of their 32 voices are
unchanged — it was the contrast probe (+3.27 dB) that was badly skewed.

Also caught: I had reported the driven guitars' 6.9x/7.5x → 0.7x as a clean win. It is an
**overshoot** — the references sit at 1.4–2.3x, so 0.7x means the top now rings *longer*
than the bottom.

## Getting to green — 9 failures triaged

**The sustainer (2 failures) — resolved by exclusion, and the exclusion is principled.**
`DRIVE_LEAD` is the ONLY preset with the e-bow sustainer, and that sustainer's whole job is
holding a note at constant level — so the f³ collapse this bug fixes is *already masked
there by another mechanism*. Excluding it costs almost nothing musically and removes a whole
subsystem interaction. Ledgered the interaction as KILN-00049: `sus_headroom` pins the
equilibrium in the LOOP domain (and the measurements confirm deficit/k/knee stay flat across
the register, exactly as designed) while the oracles measure the OUTPUT, whose mapping is
pitch-dependent — held level climbed −6.1 / −3.3 / −1.6 / +4.4 / **+12.9** dB over keys
64–88. A re-captured `SUS_HOLD_REF_OFFSET_DB` can re-centre a spread but not remove one.

**The pitch tests (2 failures) — confounded measurement, now a STRONGER oracle.**
`measure_pitch` was a zero-crossing counter behind a 700 Hz lowpass; a KS sustain can carry
a second harmonic stronger than its fundamental, so it reported a pitch part-way to 2·f0
regardless of pre-filter (the hammer-on buffer read 890/978/1100 Hz behind 250/700/1000 Hz
lowpasses; true f0 587.9). Replaced with normalised autocorrelation + octave guard +
parabolic refinement. **Verified by mutation, not by it passing**: bending one semitone
instead of two reads 466.31 Hz (true 466.16) and still fails the assertion; hammering to
C#5 instead of D5 reads 554.81 (true 554.37) and still fails. Tolerances unchanged.

**GM33/GM35 — ledgered as KILN-00048.** The damper corner scales with velocity, so decay
*rate* does too (~4.6x across the velocity range). Physically wrong — damping belongs to
the string, not the player — and invisible today only because the f³ collapse kills every
note at every velocity. Hold the damper open and it leaks into the level law.

**Process note.** I told the triage subagents they could build and run tests in my
worktree. One of them edited `voices.rs` and added a module *while I was running the
suite*, which briefly made four failures "vanish" and nearly had me draw a false
conclusion. Its work was good (it implemented the autocorrelation fix, and I verified it
independently), but next time these agents get `isolation: worktree` or a read-only brief.

## 2026.07.23 — the law changed after a two-expert consult

Arthur approved the audition and said land it, prepare samples, iterate on trunk. Before
landing I consulted BOTH Fable (its own earlier design) and gpt-5.6-sol (independent
family) on the identity-convergence problem the absolute ceiling had. They **disagreed**,
which was the most useful outcome — I tested both empirically:

- **gpt-5.6-sol: relative budget.** Bound the damper to ρ=0.30 of each preset's OWN
  authored `t60` loss, not a global dB/s. It rejected the absolute ceiling outright
  ("structurally erases `bright`") and caught a real error in my algebra (held loss is
  `L/(q²j²)`, I'd dropped the ±8% bright jitter j).
- **Fable: global anchor.** One anchor frequency (~170 Hz), `fc = bright·(f/F_A)^1.5`, so
  the corner RATIO equals the bright RATIO and ordering is preserved by construction. It
  predicted the relative budget would t60-order the corners and invert koto/uke.

**I built and measured both.** Fable's prediction was right about the mechanism: the
relative budget IS t60-ordered. But when I measured with the **vetted** broadband
`centroid` (the metric the ear-correlated oracles use, not my first `spectral_centroid`
which over-weighted an inaudible high band), the relative budget's identity is preserved in
the playing register — koto within ±4% of nylon, uke brighter through key 60 — and it
PASSES both vetted rendered oracles (ukulele, sitar). The global anchor, despite its
elegant algebra, FAILS the vetted ukulele oracle (uke ≈ nylon when held, 1222 vs 1253),
because — per Fable's own tripwire 2 — the ukulele's rendered brightness is not
corner-driven, so forcing corner-ratio preservation does not restore it.

**Decision: ship the relative budget** (`KS_DAMP_BUDGET = 0.30`). It passes every vetted
oracle and fixes the collapse (median tilt 5.00→3.42x vs references 1.72–1.82x). Its
residual — held corners re-order by t60 in the TOP register (uke drifts under nylon above
~key 64; koto's held corner exceeds nylon's) — is ledgered as **KILN-00050** for the
on-trunk iteration Arthur asked for. Neither shared law wins both axes; the real lever for
the residual is per-instrument voicing, not the shared law.

### Oracles, made honest

- **The self-fulfilling closed-form ceiling is gone.** Under the relative budget the
  damper's own share is pinned at `ρ·A/vel_scale²` by construction, so ANY closed-form
  assertion about that level just restates the law. The closed-form oracle now asserts only
  the one emergent, non-self-fulfilling property — the **register tilt** — plus a coverage
  guard. (I tried a subordination bound `d_damp ≤ 0.60·A`; it fails at low velocity because
  the velocity→timbre law deliberately darkens soft notes, which is exactly the
  self-fulfilling `1/vel_scale²` term — so it can't be made honest and was dropped.)
- **New RENDERED identity oracle** `damper_hold_preserves_instrument_identity`: the
  requirement-side twin both experts asked for. It renders uke>nylon and steel>nylon and
  their forced-`Off` twins at the identity key (60), on the vetted `centroid`, and requires
  the hold to keep the SIGN and ≥55% of the authored contrast. A convergent law (the
  rejected absolute ceiling) fails it; so would a future sample/filter flattening.
- **`shaped_g7` fixture** transported by measured base-vs-branch delta (6 cells: NYLON 64
  and JAZZ 64), documented, with the honest note that NYLON 64 loses canary status while
  the sub-crossover cells still pin the harness.

Suite: **613 pass / 0 fail.** Clippy clean.
