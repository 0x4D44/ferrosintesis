# 2026.07.22 — JRN — driven-guitar +6 dB lift: keep or discard?

**Question (Arthur).** An unintegrated 3-day-old branch lifts `PROGRAM_TRIM_DB[29/30]`
by +6 dB "for mix presence". I had flagged it as a possible workaround for
MM-BUG-KILN-00042 (the Karplus-Strong register-dependent decay collapse). Decide.

**Verdict: discard.** Proven by measurement, not argument.

## What I did

1. **Read the branch.** `006fda6`, clean worktree, nobody mid-flight — so no collision
   and the decision is purely technical. Its stated cause: "the Drive stage was
   calibrated for SOLO loudness", evidenced by a distortion lead reading 8.2 dB under a
   fingered bass despite a higher CC7.
2. **Checked the routing.** `29 | 30 => Pluck::new(&DRIVE, …)` (`voices.rs:12112`), and
   `DRIVE` never sets `treble_hold_hz` → inherits 0.0 → unmitigated f³ damper law. So
   GM29/30 are squarely inside 00042's scope.
3. **Measured the decay** on the standard 6-key probe. Register spread (decay at key 73
   minus key 48): **GM29 +15.9 dB, GM30 +15.2 dB**. The cliff sits between keys 63 and
   68. A lead lives above that cliff; a bass lives below it. That *is* the reported 8 dB
   inversion — the "solo vs mix calibration" story is wrong.
4. **Ran the counterfactual.** Authored `treble_hold_hz` on `DRIVE` across an anchor
   sweep (experiment only, reverted — `voices.rs` restored from a pre-edit copy and
   `git status` confirms clean).

## The useful result — the anchor sweep

| anchor | GM29 spread | GM30 spread |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (today) | +15.9 | +15.2 |
| 500 Hz (NYLON/STEEL) | +12.7 | +11.9 |
| **300 Hz** | **−0.1** | **−1.9** |
| 200 Hz | −0.1 | −1.9 |

**500 Hz barely helps** — I predicted this from the frequency arithmetic before running
it (key 68 = 415 Hz sits below a 500 Hz anchor), and the measurement confirmed it. That
matters beyond this branch: it falsifies the "just author `treble_hold_hz` at 500 like
NYLON/STEEL" reading of 00042's fix direction. **The anchor is per-preset and must sit
below each instrument's cliff.**

300 Hz flattens the tilt essentially to zero; 200 Hz buys nothing further and starts
over-holding the mid register. So 300 is a well-conditioned optimum, not a fitted guess.
Keys 48/53/58 are unchanged — the fix is surgical.

**Integrated loudness recovered by the fix alone:** GM29 key 73 **+6.4 dB**, GM30 key 73
**+7.1 dB**, lead-register mean +4.0 dB, low-register mean **+0.0 dB**.

That is the whole +6 dB the trim was reaching for, delivered where it belongs and nowhere
else. Landing both would leave the lead register ~12 dB up and the low register 6 dB up
for no reason.

## Surprises / notes

- **The trim hit the ±6 clamp.** In hindsight that was the tell: an ear correction that
  saturates its own limiter is usually fighting the wrong variable.
- **The panel does still say GM29 is a little quiet** (SC-55 +4.3, S-YXG50 +1.7, spread
  2.65 → agreement). I nearly used that to argue "keep a reduced trim". I don't, because
  that residual was measured on the broken decay and is partly an artifact of it. Re-derive
  after the fix, then ask.
- **GM30 has no reference datum at all** — guards failed on all 6 keys on both references.
  There was never any evidence for its +6; it was inherited from GM29 by association.
- **A real design divergence surfaced that is NOT a bug.** `DRIVE` sets `sustain: 0.0` on
  purpose (round-3 U2): the default driven bank decays, and the *sustaining* voice is the
  CC0 alt bank `DRIVE_LEAD`. Both references sustain. Flattening the tilt won't reconcile
  that — it's a voicing decision for Arthur, recorded in 00042 as an open question rather
  than folded into the fix.

## Where it landed

Evidence and decision appended to `bugs/MM-BUG-KILN-00042.md`. The branch is left in
place as the record of the original observation — the complaint was real, the diagnosis
wasn't.
