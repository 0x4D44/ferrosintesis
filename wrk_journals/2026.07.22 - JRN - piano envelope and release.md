# 2026.07.22 — JRN — piano envelope and release (MM-BUG-KILN-00042)

## Intent

Arthur reported that the default Ferro piano in the Ferrosinthesis Reference
Audition sounds thumpy on the second and third notes, while SC-55 and S-YXG50
carry those notes into one another. The agreed sequence is to normalize the
source-bank macro-envelopes first, then calibrate a piano-only release from that
stable base.

Task success is a coherent default GM0 upright across round robins and adjacent
zones, followed by enough key-up damping to bridge the FRA phrase's short gaps
without pedal-like smear. MIDI authorship, other programs, spectral character,
and unrelated sample banks are out of scope.

## Reproduction and diagnosis

The opening GM0 figure in demos/ferrosintesis_reference/tracks/audition.py is
D4, F#4, A4 at velocity 104 and 96 BPM. Each note lasts 0.5 beats (312.5 ms)
and starts 0.6 beats (375 ms) after the previous one. The key-up gap is therefore
62.5 ms. No CC64 or CC68 participates.

The voice seed alternates the default upright round robin. Those three keys read
the C4 and G4 forte zones, so the phrase deliberately crosses both a zone and a
round-robin boundary.

Measured from the tracked 44.1 kHz source assets:

- peak normalization is already uniform, but the 8–38 ms attack versus
  100–180 ms body ratio varies by more than 20 dB across the forte bank;
- forte same-zone round robins differ by roughly 4.9 dB at G2, 5.5 dB at C3,
  4.3 dB at G3, 4.3 dB at C4, and 8.7 dB at C6;
- C4 forte takes are body-heavy (-5.2/-9.5 dB in that ratio), while G4 is
  attack-heavy (+2.4/+0.3 dB), matching Arthur's note-order observation.

The delivered FRA render loses 16–23 dB during the three short gaps. A retained,
matched SC-55 dry piano probe loses about 6 dB over the same 62.5 ms. A low-pass
comparison did not show a material extra sub-200 Hz transient in the selected
source takes, so the reported thump is primarily time shape, not a separate
bass-resonance defect.

Code confirms the two layers:

- prepare.py trim_to_onset conditions each file independently and only
  peak-normalizes it;
- LaVoice applies a generic 60 ms sample-release T60;
- acoustic_piano applies a 100 ms model-release T60;
- at FRA note-off, the LA crossfade is still about 90 percent sample-owned.

## Design direction

The draft HLD uses a deliberately small bank-level transform:

- one robust linear attack/body trend per velocity layer;
- scalar body gain, smoothed in after the first 40 ms;
- no filtering, compression, resynthesis, dependency, or runtime flag;
- a hard 12 dB correction bound and tracked-asset oracle;
- a release decision only after the conditioned FRA render is measured.

The design is in:
wrk_docs/2026.07.22 - HLD - GM0 upright envelope and release.md.

## Review and implementation log

### Design review

Two independent reviewers checked the draft from the sample-conditioning and
release/routing sides, then each tried to refute the other's findings against
the code. Seven findings survived and were folded into the HLD: full bank
seam/attack coverage, a no-secondary-swell guard, explicit dry release windows,
two-bake determinism, runtime zone-edge ownership, alternate-bank isolation, and
numeric reap/render-diff rules.

The one external Claude Opus pass completed read-only after 762 seconds. It
confirmed the diagnosis and found a critical ambiguity: using the non-bright
GM0 path as a release selector would also select every GM0 alternate bank.
The revised design now threads an explicit optional override and authors it only
from the default program-0 arm. Claude also exposed a gap in ratio-only
conditioning: equal attack/body ratios can retain absolute RMS steps due to
different crest factors. The conditioner therefore fits both a per-layer shape
trend and a shared absolute body-level trend.

One targeted re-review checked only the critical routing correction. It returned
RESOLVED against the default GM0, GM1, alternate, and undefined-CC0 fallback call
sites. The design is now SELF-APPROVED. No architectural fork remains.
