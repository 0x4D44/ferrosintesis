# 2026.07.23 — JRN — 4× oversampling, and widening the amp knobs to match

Arthur, after hearing the Part A listening set: *"Ooh - I like the variance. I suspect we could
allow more variance as well - maybe even more gain? Is that possible?"*

The short answer turned out to be **no to more gain, yes to much more variance — but only after
changing something I had assumed was fixed.**

## I had never measured the ceiling I was respecting

Part A shipped `AMP_DRIVE_K = 1.2` because the design review *predicted* that 2.0 would breach the
−40 dBc alias budget. I confirmed 1.2 was safe and moved on — I never tested 1.4, 1.6 or 1.8. So
the first thing was to stop inheriting someone else's caution and sweep it:

| k | worst alias | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2 (shipped) | −42.5 dBc | +2.5 dB spare |
| 1.5 | −39.9 | over |
| 2.0 | −36.9 | over |

The prediction was right and I had not been over-cautious — 1.2 sat essentially *at* the ceiling.
Worth noting anyway: the correct response to "did you measure that?" is to go and measure it, not
to re-derive the argument.

## The real finding: the knobs share one alias budget, and Drive eats it

This is what the Part A oracle missed, and it matters more than the ceiling itself.

| combination | worst alias |
|---|---|
| Drive 127 alone | −42.5 (2.5 spare) |
| Drive 127 + Tone 127 | **−39.0 — over** |
| Drive 127 + Tone + Presence | **−35.0 — 5 dB over** |

**So the Part A I integrated an hour earlier had a latent breach of the repo's own invariant.** My
alias oracle swept Drive while holding every other knob neutral, so the combination was never
tested. Two corrections came out of that:

1. The oracle now sweeps **every knob that can move the fold-back floor**, including the worst-case
   all-at-once row.
2. A wrong assumption of mine, stated plainly because it was load-bearing: I had reasoned that
   *"post-clip EQ is alias-free"*. It is not, in the sense the budget measures. Presence is a boost
   centred at 2.6–2.8 kHz — exactly where alias lands, and *above* the fundamental — so it lifts the
   alias-to-note **ratio** without creating a single new alias product.

## 4× oversampling — the enabling change

At 2× the budget is exhausted by gain alone, so the choice was "wide gain **or** wide EQ". The way
out is to stop generating the fold-back in the first place:

| | worst alias, all knobs wide | CPU per driven channel |
|---|---|---|
| 2× | −35.0 dBc (5 over) | 0.4 % of a core (241× realtime) |
| **4×** | **−44.8 dBc (4.8 spare)** | 0.9 % of a core (118× realtime) |

**9.8 dB of headroom for half a percent of one core.** I measured the CPU rather than assuming it,
because "it doubles the shaper" sounds expensive until you notice the shaper was never the
expensive part.

This is a timbre change to every driven-guitar render, so it was Arthur's call, not mine — put to
him with the numbers and approved. `Drive::process` now upsamples ×4 by linear interpolation and
keeps the sample-aligned output; the cabinet's lowpass cliff is still the decimation filter, and
the internal field is renamed `sr2` → `sr_os` because "sr2" would now be a lie.

**The catalog barely moves.** At the shipped (neutral) voicing the 4× render differs from the 2× one
by a residual of **−48.1 dB re: signal**, with every band shifting ≤0.02 dB — it only removes
artifacts that were already near −42 dBc. Bytes differ everywhere a `Drive` exists; ears will not.

### Render-diff inventory

Of the 124 album MIDIs, **41 build a `Drive` insert and 83 do not**. The 83 cannot be affected by
construction — `needs_drive(prog)` is false, `strip.drive` is `None`, and the changed code never
executes — so the inventory samples across composers rather than spending ~2.5 hours re-rendering a
provable identity (each pair costs ~75 s):

| | result |
|---|---|
| non-driven sample (8, spread across 5 albums/4 composers) | **byte-identical, 8/8** |
| driven sample (6, both banks, 5 albums) | changed 6/6 — residual median **−59.4 dB**, worst **−52.7 dB** re: signal |
| unexpected | **none** |

The in-mix residual is *smaller* than the isolated-lead figure (−48.1 dB) because the guitar is one
instrument among many. Nothing outside the expected split moved, which is the property that matters:
the change reaches exactly the albums that own a driven guitar, and reaches them inaudibly.

## What the widening bought

Ranges, all set by measurement: Drive k 1.2 → 2.0 (g1 ×0.25…×3.91), Body/Presence ±9 → ±15,
Tone ±9 → ±12, Cab Tone ×0.5 → ×0.30 (cliff 4000 → 1200 Hz), Tightness 0.6 → 0.9 octaves.

| pair | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| **Rig A → Rig B** | 13.6 max / 8.2 RMS | **25.4 max / 15.4 RMS** |
| Cab Tone → dark | 15.0 / 7.4 | **26.7 / 14.8** |
| Presence 0→127 | 9.8 / 5.9 | 16.0 / 9.6 |
| Body 0→127 | 10.4 / 5.4 | 16.7 / 8.7 |

Per-knob primary-axis spreads roughly doubled: Body 14.8–16.8 → 24.2–27.5 dB, Presence 14.6–15.8 →
23.1–24.4, Cab Tone 11.3 → 13.7–19.1. The two pre-clip knobs improved less (Tone 1.7–3.3 → 2.8–5.1,
Tightness 1.7–4.1 → 3.5–5.6), which is the same physics as before: a saturator swallows pre-clip EQ.

## Two things I chose to simplify or concede

- **The Tone asymmetry got deleted.** Mid-way through I made Tone asymmetric (+5 boost / −15 cut)
  because at 2× only the boost side cost alias headroom. At 4× that constraint stopped binding, so
  the asymmetry became complexity defending against nothing — reverted to symmetric ±12.
- **Drive's bound is now the LEVEL residual, not aliasing.** With a ×15.6 gain span the makeup trim
  can no longer hold level: swept at k=2.0 the worst case is 9.40 dB at c=1.4, **8.21 dB at c=1.6**,
  9.40 at c=1.8 — so 1.6 is a measured minimax, not a guess. It cannot do better because the error
  depends on input level as much as knob position (a quiet note at low gain never reaches the knee;
  a loud one still clips). k=2.5 was tried and pushed this past 10 dB, which starts to make Drive
  feel like a volume control — so 2.0 is a taste bound, and the alias ceiling (k=3.0 is still safe
  at −49.4 dBc) is no longer what limits it.

The residual tolerance moved 6.0 → 9.0 dB. That is a re-baseline against a deliberately wider knob,
with the exponent re-swept to its minimax first — not a threshold relaxed to bury a regression, and
the check still catches compensation being disabled or inverted.
