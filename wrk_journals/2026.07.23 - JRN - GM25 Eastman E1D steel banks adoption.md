# 2026.07.23 — JRN — GM 25: adopting Arthur's Eastman E1D as the steel banks

Arthur recorded his own **Eastman E1D** dreadnought steel-string and asked whether we
could make it the default GM 25 bank, moving the fetched Martin HD28 to an alternate.
Two takes: `DR0000_0192` picked (plectrum), `DR0000_0191` hand-plucked (fingerstyle).

## Assessment (why we said yes)

Both takes are clean and genuinely usable as sample sources:

- 44.1 kHz / 16-bit **stereo, coincident pair** — inter-channel delay ~0.08 ms, so
  mono-summing (which the sampler needs) costs 0.1–0.3 dB with no comb filtering.
- No clipping; peaks −4.6 / −6.6 dBFS; noise floor ~−66 dBFS.
- Full chromatic walks **E2 → ~C6**, ~80 notes each — every zone anchor available,
  usually several instances to choose from.
- The two articulations are genuinely different instruments: picked centroid
  ~2400–3000 Hz vs plucked ~480–630 Hz (4–5× brighter). Worth keeping both.
- Provenance is the real win: first-party and CC0-able outright, which removes the
  *load-bearing SHA pin* the Martin bank depends on (it is fetched from a mixed
  CC0/CC-BY repo where a repo-wide licence cannot be inferred).

A third file, `DR0000_0189`, clips (1506 full-scale samples) and was not adopted.

call — it will hold more instruments over time). Full takes kept uncut: the notes ring
5–7 s and overlap the gaps, so there is no dead air to trim without damaging decays.

## Anchor selection — two traps worth remembering

Choosing the 8 zone anchors per take was the fiddly part, and cost two wrong turns:

1. **A bare harmonic template scores a subharmonic exactly as well as the fundamental**
   — every harmonic of f0 is also a harmonic of f0/2 — so the same note won three
   different zone slots at once (one note "was" E2, A#2 *and* E3). Fixed by scoring
   `energy × harmonic-coverage`: a subharmonic candidate is missing all of its odd
   multiples, so coverage collapses. Octave assignments became unique immediately.

2. **A dominance-weighted detector reports a normal dreadnought low E an octave high.**
   The picked E2's fundamental sits below its 2nd/3rd harmonics in the attack window
   (H1 −3.5 dB, H3 dominant), so the baked zone "measured" as 167 Hz. It is not a
   defect: the zone it replaces — the shipped Martin `steel_E2` — has an even *weaker*
   fundamental (H1 −6.0 dB, H3 dominant) at its pinned root. Perceived pitch follows
   the harmonic series, not the loudest partial. **Comparing against the shipped
   reference settled it in one measurement**; an absolute threshold would have
   rejected a perfectly good sample.

**Isolation is the binding constraint**, not pitch. `trim_to_onset` locates the attack
as the first sample above 3 % of peak (−30 dB), so any anchor with less than ~30 dB of
quiet before it latches onto the previous note's decay instead. Filtering on that left
48 usable notes in the picked take and 56 in the plucked.

Two picked-take substitutions follow from it — roots are *measured*, not nominal, so an
anchor only has to sit near its slot: **B2 for A#2** (picked A#2 is inside the prior
decay, 28 dB) and **A#4 for B4** (picked B4 is quiet and only 20 dB isolated). The nylon
bank already uses B2 for its source's missing A#2, so this is precedented.

## Calibration

`LA_STEEL` is `(0.20, (0.05, 0.28))` — the 0.20 was fitted so the sample's level in the
0.05–0.28 s crossfade window meets the Shaped model's. The bake peak-normalises every
zone to 0.9, so what differs between banks is envelope shape (RMS-in-window vs peak):

| Bank | mean window RMS | vs Martin | suggested gain |
|------|-----------------|-----------|----------------|
| Martin (current) | 0.2191 | — | 0.20 |
| Eastman picked | 0.1909 | +1.19 dB | **0.229** |
| Eastman plucked | 0.2059 | +0.54 dB | **0.213** |

Both land within ~1.2 dB of the existing fit, so the continuity oracles
(`la_level_continuity` 2.4× cap, `la_steel_high_key_level_parity` 0.8–2.2) should hold.
To be confirmed against the real `print_steel_wrap_level_ratios` harness.
