# 2026.07.23 — JRN — giving the driven guitars' CC0 alt bank its own lead amp+cabinet

Arthur: *"GM029 main sounds the same as GM029 alt — need more difference"*, same for GM030, and
the leads sound **thin**. Fix at the amp/cabinet layer, where a driven guitar's identity lives —
explicitly not by changing the string decay (that would undo KILN-00042, landed the day before).

## UNDERSTAND — the defect, in one number

The `Drive` insert's `alt` flag changed **only the `MicroCab` ripple depth**. Feeding identical
input through the two inserts on the unmodified baseline:

| lead − main, baseline | prog 29 | prog 30 |
|---|---|---|
| body-vs-presence tilt (800−2800 Hz) | +1.17 dB | +1.08 dB |
| low-end focus (100−800 Hz) | **+0.05 dB** | **+0.20 dB** |

**0.05 dB.** The two banks ran the same amp. Everything telling them apart was on the string.

**The string does not rescue it, and measuring that reframed the whole task.** I extended the
string-side oracle to lead pitches expecting to find the contrast had *narrowed* (the brief's
premise, since KILN-00042 makes main `DRIVE` ring longer up-register). It hasn't — the
sustain-index gap is 14.3 dB at key 45 and **grows to 60.3 dB at key 69**. The main still decays
to nothing while the lead holds.

So why do they sound alike? Because a lead line's notes last a few hundred milliseconds, and
**nobody ever hears that tail**. Over the span that is actually audible the two banks went
through one identical amp. That is the complaint, exactly, and it is why the amp is the right
layer. Worth remembering: a large gap in a long-tail metric can coexist with "sounds the same".

## The two-expert consult, and where each was right

Fable (imaginative) and gpt-5.6-sol at `max` (convergent, cross-family), briefed identically.
They **agreed on every structural point** — architecture fine, `g2` cannot create sustain, "air"
is cone fizz, presence ~2800 not 3000 at high Q, the draft's centroid/presence-ratio oracle is
too gameable, add a THD *ceiling*, extend alias/DC/level probes to alt.

Three of my draft's ideas were simply wrong, and both consults caught them:

1. **"Kill thin with a pre-clip mid formant."** No: at these operating points both tanh stages
   are deeply saturated, so pre-clip EQ mostly decides *which partials dominate the clipper*
   rather than acting as tone control. The cab is the only linear tone stage — curing thin is a
   **cab** job.
2. **"Push presence into stage 2 so the lead cuts."** That is the recipe for **fizz** — boosting
   2.3 kHz into a clipper multiplies its IMD skirt. Lead channels go *darker* into the last
   stage and add presence *after*, linearly.
3. **"More g2 buys sustain."** No — memoryless shaper, and `DRIVE_LEAD` already holds at
   constant level via the e-bow sustainer. `g2` changes the tone of the hold, not its length.

Fable also supplied the lever I had **missed entirely**: raise the **pre-clip high-pass**
(90→120 Hz). Low end entering a clipper eats headroom and smears intermodulation across every
harmonic — mush generated *inside* the nonlinearity, which no post-cab EQ can remove. It is the
single biggest "focus" lever and it was hard-coded.

Where they **disagreed**: bias. Fable wanted 0.8 vs 0.9 as a 29≠30 axis; Sol quantified that 0.8
costs H2 against floors with only 1.4–4.1 dB margin and recommended 0.85 for both. I took Sol's
caution and **measured it**: at 0.85, 29's H2 moves −18.5 → −18.2 dBc and 30's *improves*
−23.3 → −19.0. The caution cost nothing, and 29≠30 is carried by three stronger axes anyway.

## IMPLEMENT

`Drive::new` gains an `alt` branch returning a whole lead voicing; `from_stages` now takes the
cabinet array and the pre-HPF corner. Main bank untouched.

**The lead cabinet had to be keyed on program, and finding that out was the useful part.** The
first cut shared one lead cab and *failed* the end-to-end oracle: 30's lead sat **0.68 dB** from
its own main at keys 63/65. 30's lead deliberately keeps a deep pre-clip scoop — that is what
makes it 30 — which cancels a shared cab's body fill. It is a genuine constraint clash: 30's
lead must differ from 30's *main* (wants more mid body) and from 29's *lead* (on that axis,
less). Per-program lead cabs serve both. Sol had named this exact fallback in advance.

## Three measurement traps, all found by measuring

1. **A signed two-band ratio through the full engine is useless here.** It swung −1.7 to
   +8.8 dB across *adjacent keys* for a fixed pair of amps: at these fundamentals it mostly
   measures which harmonics land in which band, and the two banks also play different strings.
   Replaced by a direction-agnostic multi-band **distance**, scored as a register mean plus a
   per-key non-collapse floor.
2. **A band set starting at 200 Hz is blind to this change.** My first calibration script used
   200–4200 Hz and concluded the fix did almost nothing (main↔alt separation +0.08 dB, some
   probes *worse*). But the pre-HPF (120 Hz) and cab resonance (100→120 Hz) live **below** the
   lowest band. Re-run from 80 Hz: **+0.80 dB, rising on every probe.** I nearly drew exactly
   the wrong conclusion from my own instrument.
3. **Deep saturation is legitimately odd-harmonic dominated** — for the *shipped* rhythm amps
   too (H2−H3 at amp 0.5: 29-main −14.4 dB, 30-main −15.6). So `m2 > 0.1*m3` only tests the
   asymmetry mechanism at *moderate* drive. When my extension of that oracle failed on the alt
   bank I swept 32 operating points before touching anything, and the honest fix was to assert
   each claim where it actually lives — DC-free everywhere, H2 *present* everywhere, H2
   *dominant* at the tail — while keeping the original single-point pin verbatim so nothing the
   oracle used to catch was lost.

## Verification

- **RED at HEAD, observed not assumed.** Inserted the new measurement into a pristine baseline
  worktree: all six clauses of `alt_drive_is_a_distinct_lead_voicing` fail there (tilt +1.17 /
  +1.08 against floors 1.5 / 2.5; low +0.05 / +0.20 against ≤ −2.5 / −4.0). Then restored the
  baseline to byte-identical with HEAD.
- **Main bank bit-identical**, verified by exact array comparison of six probe renders — not by
  assertion.
- **Alias** (Fable: retreat under 6 dB margin): worst −54.4 dBc vs a −40 bar over 3 fundamentals
  × both programs; alt no worse than main.
- **Chords → IMD** (Sol's tripwire): Slipstream's alt channels are monophonic — max polyphony 2,
  80–100% strictly single-note. Cleared empirically.
- **Level**: lead matched to its own rhythm amp within 0.01 dB on a 300–3400 Hz band, so the A/B
  isolates timbre. Deliberately not a loudness lift — Arthur discarded a +6 dB GM29/30 trim under
  KILN-00042, to be re-derived separately.

## Render-diff — the prediction was exact

124 album MIDIs, both binaries: **11 changed, 87 same, CONTAMINATION 0**, 26 NOT REACHED. An
independent scan finds exactly 11 MIDIs that sound a driven guitar on a nonzero-CC0 bank — the
ten Slipstream tracks plus `Through Lines/16 - Three-Sixty-One` — and exactly those 11 moved.
The 26 NOT-REACHED are the known harness artifact (render_diff classifies by sounded *program*,
not by *bank*), and here they are the bit-identity property being demonstrated rather than a
gap: every main-bank-only driven track rendered byte-for-byte unchanged.

## Census — who this actually reaches

Scanned all 124 album MIDIs: exactly one album authors the alt bank — **`fable5/Slipstream`**
(CC0=1 on ch14/ch15 across all ten tracks; 9,598 alt-bank notes repo-wide, 60% of them in the
lead register). So Slipstream's character genuinely changes and needs a listen; every other
driven-guitar album (Big Weather, Hollow Hill, Seven Kinds of Sunlight) is main-bank-only and
renders byte-identical.

## Open for Arthur's ear

The **120 Hz pre-shaper high-pass**. It is the main focus lever, but 12.4% of alt-bank notes sit
at E2–B2 and lose ≈7.5 dB of fundamental to it. The A/B set includes dedicated low-register
probes for exactly this call. If low leads sound too light, that corner is the first retreat
(120 → 105 Hz) — not the cabinet.
