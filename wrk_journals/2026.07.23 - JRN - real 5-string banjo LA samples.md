# 2026.07.23 — JRN — GM 105 banjo: real 5-string replaces the dull ganjo

**By:** Claude Opus 4.8 (1M) · **Task:** banjo LA samples from Arthur's real 5-string recording

## Trigger

Arthur: "Our banjo sounds like it's being played through a hanky — muffled, no twang."

## Diagnosis (measured — this box has no ears)

1. The GM 105 banjo was LA synthesis: an sfzinstruments/**ganjo** onset (a 6-string
   *guitar*-banjo, chosen for its CC0 licence, not its tone) crossfaded into `Pluck(&BANJO)`.
2. The ganjo source WAVs are spectrally **dull** — 99.9 % of energy below ~1.5 kHz, ~0 %
   above 4 kHz (a bright pluck reference reaches ~10 kHz). No twang in the source.
3. Worse, the LA layer **discards the model's own bright onset** (the `LaVoice` contract
   zeroes the model over `[0, fade_start)`) and substitutes the dull sample — so the sample
   layer was *removing* twang, not adding it. (This generalises a 2026.07.14 CR that measured
   the LA layer deleting 26–91 % of every program's model onset; the banjo is the worst case
   because its sample is duller than its model.)
4. Web survey: **no** usable bright CC0/CC-BY 5-string bluegrass banjo multisample exists
   (the one real 5-string set online is GPL-3.0; every CC0 library has no banjo).

## Fix

Arthur recorded his own open-back 5-string banjo (open-G gDGBD, Tascam close-mic, note by
note). From that one take:

- **Extraction** (`tools/ferrosintesis-samples/banjo_extract.py`): onset-segment → robust
  octave-correct f0 (harmonic-sum refined by autocorrelation) → **QC gate** (reject clipped,
  pitch-ambiguous, buzzy/low-clarity, next-pluck-bleed, too-quiet, too-dull) → trim to a
  ~0.5 s onset → peak-normalise → TPDF-dither to 16-bit. A first pass (12 zones, loosest
  "brightest+loudest" pick) shipped some **bad notes** — Arthur's ear caught them; QC found
  why (a "B5" that was really 241 Hz junk, a contaminated D3, a buzzy G#5). The gated pass
  keeps **24 near-chromatic zones D#3–G#5**, every one clean.
- Roots are the MEASURED sounding pitch, so the player's slightly-sharp high-fret fretting is
  captured per-root and repitched back in tune by `LaVoice`.
- **Muffle → brightness:** the close mic carries a lot of low-mid body, so even the real
  onset read dark. A one-pole presence **high-shelf on the sampled attack** (`LaFx.shelf`,
  +14 dB > 2.5 kHz, banjo route only) restores the steel-string ring. Whole-file centroid
  773 → 1344 Hz, rolloff 11 → 19 kHz (matched to Arthur's pick off a brightness ladder). A
  model `bright` bump was tried and reverted — it made ~no difference to the render (the
  shelf-lifted attack dominates the spectrum), and reverting keeps the ear-tuned model voice
  and its oracle untouched.

## Lesson candidate

An auto-picker that optimises "bright + loud + long" is NOT the same as "sounds right":
it shipped a wrong-octave and a contaminated take. Gate sample takes on pitch-CLARITY,
octave-AGREEMENT (autocorr vs harmonic-sum) and next-pluck CONTAMINATION, not just level.
