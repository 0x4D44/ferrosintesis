# 2026.07.24 — JRN — GM 120 fret noise: a sampled Eastman E1D bank

Arthur asked whether the Eastman E1D samples we'd just adopted for GM 25 could source the GM
120 fret-noise track. Short answer: not from the *guitar* takes, but from a purpose-made
recording — which he then made, and which is now the default GM 120 voice.

## Why the guitar takes couldn't do it, and the audition that proved it

A first investigation (three independent audio detectors over the two GM 25 masters) found
**no extractable fret squeak**: a chromatic walk of plucked notes has the fretting hand
placing and lifting, not sliding, and the single detectable slide sat 12 dB under a ringing
note. Sensitivity was proven (synthetic rasps injected into the takes were found down to
−50 dBFS while masked), so the null is real, not a blind detector.

Then a three-engine audition quantified *what was wrong* with the existing modeled GM 120, so
we'd know what "better" had to hit. Rendered the same probe on ferrosintesis, a cycle-accurate
Roland SC-55mkII, and a Yamaha S-YXG50 (both via `mdmidiemu`, `D:\language\mdsc55`), measured
per-engine fret-vs-guitar so the comparison was engine-independent. An adversarial re-check
corrected two of my first numbers (windowing artefact on the centroid; dry-vs-wet on the
decay). Verified result: the modeled burst was −23 dB under a steel-guitar chord (references
−11.6 / −10.9), centred ~3.8 kHz with ~37 % energy above 4 kHz (references ~1.7–2.0 kHz,
~2 %), and ~60 ms long (references ~230–340 ms). It read as a distant click. So a gain bump
alone — the original KILN-00040 fix direction — would have made a *louder wrong sound*.

## The recording

Arthur recorded two takes on the E1D with all strings damped at the nut (`DR0000_0203`,
`0204`; 48 kHz/24-bit). Acceptance battery: 42 clean slide candidates, character dead on the
references (median peak band 2000 Hz, 5 % > 4 kHz, low-mid −24 dB). `0204` clips, but only in
the spoken "change" slate — one actual slide lost. 8 body-knock taps also captured (parked
for a future note-off `stop_thump` job — see scratchpad).

**Selection gotcha worth the lesson entry:** a "no ringing note under the rasp" harmonicity
gate rejected *all* 42 candidates. A slide's winding-pass rate makes it quasi-periodic
(autocorr 0.28–0.44) — that IS the zip, not a note. Real note contaminants show as a 50–160 Hz
peak with low-mid at the peak; a rasp peaks at 1–3 kHz with low-mid 20–30 dB down. Gate on
band + low-mid, not periodicity. And self-cutting doesn't need `trim_to_onset`'s 30 dB
isolation — cut at the local envelope minimum and fade.

## What shipped

- New CC0 crate `ferrosintesis-samples-fretnoise`: 12 finger-slide one-shots, 16-bit mono
  44.1 kHz, ~1.0 MiB. Bake `tools/ferrosintesis-samples/fretnoise_bake.py` (resample, body-RMS
  equalise the round-robin, TPDF dither) from committed lossless `cuts/` — reproducible.
- `sampler::sampled_fret_noise` / `FretNoiseOneShot` (gong-one-shot pattern; native rate, no
  repitch; seed picks the round-robin take), dispatched from GM 120 behind `if samples`.
  `--no-samples` keeps the modeled `SfxNoise` burst. Cache wired into `prewarm()` + the
  exercise sweep (passes the lazy-cache coverage oracles).
- `FRETNOISE_LEVEL = 0.23`, calibrated so the slide sits −11.2 dB under the guitar. Rendered
  character now lands between the two hardware references on every axis (centroid 1784 Hz,
  1.3 % > 4 kHz, 220 ms).
- Five sampled-path oracles (render `samples = true`, so the layer isn't vacuously green):
  beats-modeled-level (differential control), narrowband-rasp-not-hiss, one-shot-while-held,
  pitch-independence, round-robin-variety. The modeled oracle (renders `samples = false`) is
  untouched and green.
- Fixed MM-BUG-KILN-00040 → Fixed (awaiting two-eyes). Updated CLAUDE.md / README / DESIGN
  (GM 120 is no longer "the toneless placeholder").
