# 2026.07.24 — B1 upright: the soft layer was hiss with no tone, so it's gone

Arthur heard two things in the freshly-built three-layer B1 bank: it was "very
dark when soft", and the soft samples carried an audible background hiss. He
re-recorded the soft pass to fix the hiss. Measuring the re-record showed it
could not work, and measuring *why* collapsed the whole design from three layers
to two.

## The re-record could not have worked

Arthur re-recorded the soft pass at **maximum input gain** (`DR0000_0205`), plus
a treble-focused pass with the recorder moved to the high end (`DR0000_0206`).

| take | median note | resting floor | SNR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0195 soft (old gain) | −37.9 dBFS | −65.9 | **28.1 dB** |
| 0205 soft (max gain) | −17.8 dBFS | −47.0 | **29.3 dB** |

Signal +20.1 dB, floor +18.9 dB, **net SNR +1.2 dB**. Above 4 kHz — where hiss
actually lives — the floor rose 20.8 dB against the signal's 20.1, so SNR went
*down* 0.7 dB. **The noise is ahead of the gain stage** (mic self-noise plus
room), so the knob amplifies it in lockstep with the piano and can never help.
0206 was no better: identical resting floor, and per-note level differences that
track how hard Arthur struck the key rather than mic position — he moved the
recorder *along* the keyboard, not closer to it.

Max gain also cost headroom: 966 railed samples, four strikes at 0 dBFS, best
usable strike at −0.8 dBFS.

**Arthur was right and I was wrong twice** earlier in this task — I called it
"tape hiss" (it is all digital) and then blamed "limited dynamics of the
recording" (24-bit quantisation sits ~50 dB below the measured floor). He said it
was background noise, not electrical. The measurement above is his hypothesis,
confirmed.

## The finding that actually mattered

While measuring the re-record I checked how much timbre separated the three
layers at all, **with the noise floor subtracted first** — this is load-bearing,
because broadband hiss lands in the numerator of any spectral-tilt statistic and
makes a noisy layer read as bright. Raw, soft looked ~1 dB from normal; that
could have been entirely noise. Subtracted, it still was:

| pair | median tilt difference |
|---|---|
| normal − soft | **+0.8 dB** — the same timbre |
| hard − normal | **+1.9 dB**, and **+8…+25 dB above B5** — the real step |

So the soft capture cost 11 dB of SNR (28.1 vs 39.2 dB) to deliver ~1 dB of tone.
It was hiss with nothing to pay for it.

And a velocity census explained the darkness complaint: Tubular Bells' piano
channel is 3573 notes, min 36, median 64, **max 77** — with the split at `>= 90`
the hard capture, the only genuinely bright one *and* the cleanest at 51 dB SNR,
**never played at all**. The 20-second excerpt Arthur first auditioned was
velocity 64 throughout, i.e. 100% normal layer. Dark because the bright layer was
unreachable; hissy because a third of the notes came from the worst-SNR capture.

## What shipped

Two layers, `< 60` normal, `>= 60` hard. Rendered A/B on passages chosen so one
variable moves at a time (`tools/gm0-audition/renders/A*.opus`, `B*.opus`):

- **t=570–600 s**, piano 100% below velocity 50 — soft layer vs normal layer:
  broadband **−0.1 dB** (unchanged, as designed — `vel_amp` owns level),
  2–6 kHz **+3.4 dB**, >6 kHz **−7.2 dB**. Less hiss *and* more tone.
- **t=45–75 s**, piano all velocity 60–69 — normal vs hard: broadband **+0.1 dB**,
  2–6 kHz **+1.7 dB**, >6 kHz **+12.0 dB**.

Arthur: "in both those samples the piano is noticeably brighter and the hiss is
gone." Dropping soft also took the crate 9.6 MB → 6.8 MB, under the crates.io cap
that §6 of the HLD had flagged as a publish blocker.

**The split at 60 is a real trade-off and is recorded as such** (HLD §3.2a). Over
the reference corpus it puts 80% of piano notes on `hard` (51% at a split of 90).
Tubular Bells is an outlier — its piano tops out at 77, while half of all piano
notes across the other 23 corpus files are ≥90 — so `hard` was never globally
unreachable, only unreachable *in the piece Arthur judges by*. Taken on his ear,
and it is one comparison to change.

## Traps hit

- **The first A/B was a silent no-op.** The base file `_tb_b1.mid` already carried
  `CC0=5`, and my mid-piece excerpt tool faithfully replayed that latched
  controller over the bank it was injecting — so all five "variants" rendered
  byte-identical. Caught only because the audit script compares samples before
  reporting. Any A/B harness needs that identity check; a plausible-sounding
  render is not evidence the variable moved.
- **Extracting a window from the middle of a MIDI needs state replay** — tempo,
  time signature, program change, every controller, pitch bend — or the excerpt
  renders with default programs and no expression. `prep_audition.py` only trims
  from the start; `D:/tmp_b1/excerpt.py` does the carry-forward.
