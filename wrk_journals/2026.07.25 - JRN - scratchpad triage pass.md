## Triage: 46 open observations

Verification was fanned out to four read-only subagents (tooling, DSP/voices,
docs/tests/dead-code, product/licensing), each briefed to re-open the cited code and
return still-real / already-fixed / moved / not-applicable with evidence, plus a
ledger dedupe check. Everything actioned below was then re-confirmed first-hand
before editing — the subagent reports are data, not authority.

### The headline: a third of the backlog was already dead

**17 of 46 items no longer held.** Nine had been fixed by tracked work that nobody
came back to strike from the scratchpad (KILN-00012, -00027, -00044, -00069, -00090,
-00096, -00100, REQ-00025, -00026, -00027); eight had premises that no longer
matched the code.

That ratio is the real finding of this pass. The scratchpad had become a place where
observations went in and nothing came out, so roughly 37 % of what an agent read
there was misleading — and a stale note is worse than no note, because it looks
like knowledge.

Three were not merely stale but actively **wrong**, and acting on them would have
caused harm:

- **`midi.rs` running status.** The parked "cheap hardening" (`status = 0;` after
  meta/SysEx) is now the opposite of correct. MM-BUG-KILN-00096 deliberately made
  the parser carry the running-status latch ACROSS meta/SysEx, because real
  sequencers emit such files. Applying the note would reintroduce a failure on
  exactly the files ferrosintesis now plays.
- **MSRV lowering to ~1.70.** Unreachable. `Cargo.lock` is `version = 4`, so every
  `--locked` gate command needs cargo 1.78 regardless of source edits, and
  `div_ceil` already needs 1.73. The note also undercounted the call sites 4 → 19.
- **The `--solo 8` render hang.** Fixed two days after it was parked, and never a
  stuck voice: a sparse solo mix leaves the always-running reverb/chorus/echo buses
  churning denormal arithmetic in their IIR tails. Re-ran the exact repro rather
  than trust the closure — 65 s, exit 0, full 148.5 MB WAV.

Two of those deserve a note.

**The generator bug was bigger than reported.** The parked note blamed ~100-char
lines. The real threshold is rustfmt's 60-char `fn_call_width` against an entry of
width `2*len(name)+35` — so **any WAV name over 12 characters wraps**, and 23 of the
25 generated crates already carry the wrapped form. The generator has therefore
never been fmt-clean for a realistic bank; every regen to date was silently followed
by a manual `cargo fmt`. This is the CLAUDE.md "hand-maintained lists" pattern in a
new costume: the reported symptom was the newest instance, not the defect.

**The README sweep followed that same rule** — enumerate the whole list, don't fix
the reported entry. Of the claims checked across all 25 asset crates, only three
were wrong, but one was badly so: the drumkit README still described itself as the
cymbal crate, hours after today's split moved the accent cymbals to `-drumkit2`.
The `ferrosintesis/README.md` figure the note flagged had already been fixed and is
now derived by `payload.rs`.

### The new oracle was adversarially tested

`no_two_asset_crates_ship_the_same_wav_basename` guards `embedded_wav`'s
first-match-wins `.or_else` chain across 24 crates. There is no live collision — 1156
WAVs, zero duplicate basenames — so it protects the next bank rather than fixing a
present bug.

Per CLAUDE.md's rule that a derived oracle is only as good as its predicate, it was
tested by trying to defeat it: planting a duplicate `rain_loop.wav` in `-gong` turns
it red naming both crates, and removing it turns it green again. It also scans every
`ferrosintesis-samples-*/samples/` on disk rather than the default feature list — a
superset of the lookup chain, so no feature combination can slip past — and carries
a vacuity floor so a broken scan fails loudly instead of passing empty.

### Two measurements that changed a verdict

Neither of these would have been settled by reading code.

**The GOLDEN fixture item flipped.** It asked for a re-pin of three drifted rows.
Re-running `print_golden_fixture` on HEAD shows ch 0 at −39.19/1079.7 (committed
−38.45/1048.8) and ch 4 at −33.04/811.3 (committed −32.54/843.8) — but ch 8, the
strings canary the note actually named, now matches **exactly**. So the item is
half stale. More importantly, re-pinning is no longer obviously right: ferrosintesis
renders are not bit-reproducible across fleet machines, and these tolerances are
what absorb that. Pinning one box's numbers could red the fixture elsewhere. Left
open as a decision, not a cleanup.

**The `Sine` phasor drift is real and linear.** The parked note called it slow float
error. Simulating the exact f32 recurrence gives +0.022 dB at 200k ticks, +0.089 at
800k, +0.397 at 3.2M — growth ×4.09 and ×4.44 per 4× ticks. That is **linear**, i.e.
a systematic exponential in magnitude, not the random walk the note implied (which
would be ×2.00). At full sample rate that extrapolates to roughly +3.3 dB over a
ten-minute track; at control rate (`sr/CTRL`, where the `control_lfo` LFOs live) it
is only ~0.1–0.2 dB.

### The adversarial panel earned its keep

Three items looked like tracked bugs worth filing. Rather than file them, nine
independent skeptics (three per candidate, briefed to REFUTE, defaulting to refuted
when evidence is thin) went at them. **None was filed.** Two were killed outright
and the third was reframed — and in each case the killing evidence was something
none of the earlier verification passes had looked at.

- **LA fade dry-out — refuted 3/3.** Every structural fact was correct: `end_taper`
  is armed in one constructor, the budget test iterates guitars only, ~25 wrap sites
  are unguarded. But the harm does not exist, for an *asset-side* reason nobody had
  checked: every LA onset bank is baked through
  `tools/ferrosintesis-samples/prepare.py::trim_to_onset`, which applies a squared
  fade-out to each cut. The recording is already at digital silence before the read
  pointer reaches dry-out, so the gate switches off a sample that is already zero.
  Retired.

- **Pedal hi-hat — reframed.** The physical argument is sound (a foot-closed hat has
  no stick) and no oracle asserts 42 ≠ 44. But the shared 42|44 profile *including*
  the stick tick is written into `wrk_docs/2026.07.09 - HLD - ferrosintesis drum kit
  v3 default.md`, which is `Status: SIGNED-OFF`, at line 109. I confirmed the status
  line and the entry first-hand. Filing a bug against a signed-off design decision
  would have been wrong; it is a revision question for Arthur.

- **`Sine` drift — refuted 2/3.** The mechanism and my measurement both survived,
  but the *unit* was wrong. There is no `Sine` in `engine.rs` at all — every one of
  the 24 sites is a per-note voice field, a control-rate LFO, or a bounded table
  loop — so the accumulator lives for a note, not a track. The longest held note on
  a sustaining program across all 124 album MIDIs is 114.72 s, where the GM 16
  drawbar partials spread ~1.6 dB; and any voice that retunes under vibrato destroys
  the bias, because each new `(cr, ci)` draws a fresh random-sign delta. Kept open at
  low priority only because CLAUDE.md is explicit that ferrosintesis is a generic GM
  player: a foreign ten-minute drone still reaches ~8 dB of inter-partial spread.

The pattern across all three: the parked note stated a true *mechanism* and inferred
a *consequence* that nobody had tested. Three of three inferred consequences were
wrong or unreachable.

## What I would tell the next pass

- **Verify before triaging, always.** 18 of 46 items did not survive contact with
  current code, and three would have caused harm if implemented as written. The
  cited `file:line` is a hypothesis about stale code, not a fact.
- **The scratchpad needs sweeping far more often than this.** At 46 open items it
  had become net-negative: more than a third of what a reader found there was wrong.
- **Run the adversarial panel before filing, not after.** It cost one workflow and
  prevented three bad ledger entries — two of which I was confident about.
- **Re-read the whole list when one entry is reported wrong.** That is already
  CLAUDE.md doctrine, and it paid twice here: the rustfmt bug was 23 crates wide, not
  one, and the README sweep found the drumkit crate describing itself as something it
  had stopped being hours earlier.
