# 2026.07.26 — JRN — B1 upright becomes the GM0 default

Arthur asked for one thing: make his own Yamaha B1 upright the default GM 0
piano, and shuffle the existing alternates along by one. The renumber itself was
half an hour. What it *exposed* took the rest of the session, and that is the
part worth recording.

## What shipped

New GM 0 bank-select line-up:

| CC0 | recording | was |
|-----|-----------|-----|
| 0 | B1 upright | 5 |
| 1 | VSCO upright | default |
| 2 | Salamander | 1 |
| 3 | Steinway B | 2 |
| 4 | Headroom | 3 |
| 5 | dark-Salamander | 4 |

The mechanism changed as well as the numbers. There were two hand-written
line-ups — one in `voices::make` for the default, one in `altbank::make` for the
alternates — plus a third in the README and a fourth scattered across ten asset
crates. They had already drifted (that was MM-BUG-KILN-00122). They are now one
ordered table, `voices::GM0_SOURCES`, that both dispatch sites index. Re-voice by
re-ordering the table; nothing else needs to move.

## Three things I got wrong, in order

**1. I assumed calibration belonged to the slot.** It belongs to the *bank*. The
outgoing default is the one GM 0 bank that is envelope-conditioned at bake time
and needs a 4x make-up gain; the others are peak-normalized and take 0.90.
Demoting it to CC0=1 had to carry that 4.00 with it. Had `cal` stayed a
slot-named constant (`GM0_DEFAULT_VOICING`), the renumber would have quietly
applied a 4x gain to a bank that did not want it. Putting `cal` inside each
`Gm0Source` makes that structurally impossible, and the oracle asserts the
pairing by NAME rather than index so it survives the next re-order too.

**2. I assumed the B1 was ready to be a default.** It was not, and the reason it
looked ready is instructive: the attack-is-the-peak, gap-release and
damper-continuity oracles only ever probed the **default slot**. The B1 sat at
CC0=5 for three days rendering a held note that peaked at the hammer, sank ~5 dB
by 400 ms, and then swelled back **above its own attack** by 800 ms. Nothing
tested it. Promotion is what put it under the lamp.

*Lesson: an oracle that probes "the default" is not testing a voice, it is
testing a slot. Every selectable alternative is unproven until it is promoted.*

**3. I assumed more gain would hold the note up.** Exactly backwards, and only a
sweep caught it. Raising the sampled layer's make-up gain does fix the swell —
the recorded hammer stops being outrun by the model — but it makes fast repeated
notes worse, because the louder onset decays faster than the model beneath it, so
the 62.5 ms key-up gap drops harder: 9.99 dB at gain 1.30, 10.45 at 2.00, 10.78
at 2.40. The two properties pull opposite ways and the window where both hold is
only about 1.2–1.3 wide. Lengthening the crossfade (0.55/0.65/0.75 s swept) hurt
both. Shipped 1.30 — the top of the window, most attack margin available without
losing gap behaviour — as `PianoSampleCal::B1Upright`.

## The oracle I retired, deliberately

`assert_attack_is_peak` carried a `piano-low` exception granting GM 0 a 4th
attack window, because the conditioned VSCO bank peaked late. The B1 does not
need it, so it is gone rather than inherited. This mattered: had the exception
been left in place it would have silently absorbed the B1's late-swell defect
instead of exposing it. Retiring a special case is part of changing the thing the
special case was for.

## The oracle I converted

`gm0_fra_phrase_keeps_energy_through_short_key_up_gaps` asserted a flat
`drop <= 10.0 dB`. Its sampler-side twin was converted to damper-derived ratios
by MM-BUG-KILN-00104, on the grounds that a flat bar "assumed every key shared
one release, which is the thing the felt damper models away". The engine-side
twin never got that treatment, and the leftover flat 10 dB sat within 0.1 dB of
what the damper itself prescribes at key 66 (9.91 dB) — so a voice that followed
its own damper faithfully passed by luck. The calibrated B1 passes the old flat
bar too (9.99), so converting it is robustness, not goalpost-moving; I checked
that deliberately before touching it.

## What I did not fix

The bass still notches. Key 36 dips ~5 dB near 400 ms and recovers to roughly its
attack level; key 72 is worse (a ~16 dB notch recovering to −9). Gain cannot
reach it — it is the sampled layer's decay *shape* through the crossfade, not its
level — so it wants an equal-power or decay-matched handoff, or a conditioned
bake. Raised as **MM-BUG-KILN-00130** rather than bodged.

## Render-diff

Declared `--program 0`. No album or demo authors CC0 on a piano channel (checked
across every engine under `albums/` and `demos/`), so the alternates' renumber is
inert on committed scores; the only expected movement is albums that play GM 0 at
all, via the new default recording and its calibration.

## Housekeeping

MM-BUG-KILN-00122's prescribed fix ("say CC0=4") was overtaken — the renumber
moved dark-Salamander to CC0=5, which is what its docs claimed all along. Marked
Fixed with a Resolution section explaining why the original instruction must not
be applied, and left for second-eyes closure. While in there I swept all ten
piano asset crates rather than the one reported: eight were stale, and four were
not GM 0 at all (the Kawai, YDP, MuseScore grand and honky-tonk still called
themselves "GM0 alternate bank N"). The template that regenerates those headers
(`gen_crate_lib.py`) was emitting a stale number too, so it was fixed at source.
