# 2026.07.26 — JRN — the violin family onto the bowed waveguide

Arthur asked why the fiddle (GM 110) sounded synthy and whether better samples
would help. They would not have. This is what the investigation found and what
it turned into.

## The diagnosis, and why samples were the wrong lever

GM 110 rendered as `Bowed`: a bandlimited sawtooth through three static peak
biquads, a one-pole lowpass that opens with the envelope, and a fixed-rate sine
vibrato. That is the textbook synth-strings recipe, so "synthy" was an accurate
description of the topology rather than a matter of taste.

The onset was already a real recording — CC0 solo violin arco from VSCO 2 CE —
but `LA_FIDDLE` crossfaded it out between 80 and 280 ms, the earliest handover
of any bowed program. Everything after ~0.28 s, which is essentially the whole
of a sustained melody note, was the saw. A better onset bank would have changed
the first quarter-second and nothing else.

The better engine was already in the repo and had been since v0.9.
`BowedString` — a stick-slip bow-friction waveguide — shipped for the cello and
contrabass, and its own header made exactly the argument this task needed:
*"a saw through a fixed EQ is a static harmonic stack … an instrument's identity
is in the time domain."* The violin family had simply never followed.

## What the spike actually settled

The risk I expected to kill the migration was `loop_comp`. It is a fixed sample
count compensating the reflection filter's phase delay, and that delay is
frequency-dependent, so I expected it to fall apart two octaves above where it
was fitted and force a redesign.

It did not. The implied loop latency is flat at 3.86–4.10 samples across keys
55–89 and across the whole bow-force range. One refitted constant serves. That
single measurement is what turned "a week, maybe" into "a day".

What the spike did find was breakage above key 75 at high bow force, in two
distinct forms: keys 77–79 lock cleanly an octave up, while keys 85–89 stop
speaking altogether. Mapping the ceiling per pitch showed the failures are
NON-monotonic — 81/83 take full force while 77/79 and 85/89 do not — which said
these are discrete mode-crossing resonances, the same family as the known 46–50
wolf band, and therefore likely movable by `beta` rather than needing a
bow-force cap. That turned out to be right.

## Two near-misses in the measurement itself

Both would have shipped a wrong conclusion, and both were caught only because
the measurement was checked before it was believed.

**The repo's `mean_period_samples` cannot see mode collapse.** It bandpasses at
the *expected* f0 with Q=4 before counting crossings, so it presupposes its own
answer: when the waveguide locks onto another partial, the filter attenuates the
true tone and its own ringing pulls the crossings back toward f0. Fine as a
tuning gate on a voice already known to hold its mode; useless for a survey
hunting mode collapse. I wrote `autocorr_pitch` instead — and gave it a
self-test, which immediately caught plain autocorrelation making the classic
octave-DOWN error (a pure 1396.9 Hz saw read as 699.1 Hz). Without that
self-test the spike would have reported phantom collapse across the top octave.

**The seeds were not random.** Seeds 7/17/23 — used by the shipping wolf-band
gate — are all small, and `Rng` seeds xorshift32 raw, whose first draw from a
small seed is ≈ −1.0. All three collapse bow force onto 2.2003–2.2010 out of an
intended 2.2–2.9. The gate claimed to cover "every per-note seed" while testing
one bow force three times, on the one axis this waveguide fails on. I initially
mis-read this as a production bug; the engine is fine (it uses a Knuth hash), so
the defect is that the GATES are weaker than production. Re-running with
engine-realistic seeds is what surfaced the cello octave lock below.

## Two pre-existing defects found and fixed

- **GM 42 cello keys 74 and 76 lock an octave up** (+1203 cents) whenever the
  per-note bow force draws near the top of its range — roughly one note in eight
  up there. Fixed with a bow-force ceiling over its top few semitones. Its
  `beta` cannot move: 0.140 is what holds its own 46–50 wolf band.
- **The wolf-band gate's seed blindness**, above. Replaced by per-program
  register gates using the engine's seed formula and the natural `slope` draw.

## Design decisions worth recording

**Every constant is measured.** `loop_comp` (3.16 / 3.36 / 3.07) was fitted and
iterated to a fixed point; the values fall monotonically with brighter
`refl_sustain`, exactly as the reflection filter's phase delay predicts, which
is an independent consistency check that they are not arbitrary. `beta` 0.170
sits in the middle of a measured clean window (0.168–0.176, one pinhole at
0.172). `amp` was fitted so the median sustain level matches the voice being
replaced, because albums are mixed against the old level.

**The bow-force ceiling nearly shipped as dead weight.** I built it for the
violin family, and a calibration test — asserting that each lever must FAIL when
removed — proved it never fired once `beta` was 0.170. It was deleted from the
violin family and kept only on the cello, which genuinely needs it. Worth
keeping that calibration habit: a gate that only ever passes is
indistinguishable from a gate that measures nothing.

**Compensate vs exempt was decided by measurement, not by analogy.** GM 42/43
are exempt from the square-velocity law because their raw curve turns over at
the top and a scalar exponent cannot fix a non-monotonic curve. The obvious move
was to exempt 40/41/110 the same way, since they are now the same voice. Their
raw curves turned out to be strictly MONOTONIC, so they are compensated instead
(t = 1.211 / 1.189 / 1.230). Exempting them would have filed a correctable
deviation under an exemption that exists for an uncorrectable one.

**The fiddle lost its identity to the migration and had to get it back.** The
saw carried a per-program `bite` (0.230 against the violin's 0.100); the
waveguide had no equivalent, so GM 110 inherited the violin's onset exactly and
its identity oracle failed. Two false starts before the right fix: scaling
`bite` moved the metric 1.02x and moving the grit band did nothing measurable,
because the grit is bandpassed INTO the loop where the reflection filter and
body damp it. A lever sweep showed the loop-brightness levers saturate near
1.08x even at absurd values. The fix was `contact_noise`: broadband bow-hair
noise radiated straight from the contact point rather than coupled through the
string. Physically real — not all bow noise travels through the body — and zero
for every other voicing, so the four pre-existing programs are untouched.

Its sample handover also had to move (0.08→0.28 to 0.11→0.34): a waveguide takes
time to build its limit cycle where the saw was instant-on, and the old fade
finished while the model was still speaking, leaving a 1.72x RMS step mid-note.
Every other waveguide program already hands over later for exactly that reason.

**Two tests changed their MEASURE, not their threshold.** `cc1_mod_wheel_adds_vibrato`
and `bowed_natural_cc1_aftertouch_composition_is_bounded` measured 40/110 with
the zero-crossing `cycle_freq_spread`, which cannot count a waveguide's strong
low harmonics. They now cover GM 44, the one bowed program still on the saw;
their claims about 40/41/110 moved to `bowedstring_family_pitch_bounded`, which
measures by autocorrelation — the remedy this repo had already adopted for GM 43.
Changing the measure is legitimate; changing a threshold to fit would not have been.

The violin's natural vibrato dropped 0.0060 → 0.0048 in the same pass. At the
deeper value its autonomous excursion was 34 cents and CC1 could only lift it
1.87x — an always-on wobble the player cannot get out from under.

## Left undone, deliberately

GM 44 tremolo strings is now the only bowed program still on the saw voice, and
the only one with no sampled onset at all. It was not migrated: tremolo is rapid
bow-direction change, so a waveguide needs per-stroke re-articulation rather
than the gain LFO `Bowed` fakes it with, and GM 44 is a SECTION sound, so the
solo-string waveguide may be the wrong target entirely. That design question
should be settled before implementation. Raised in the bug ledger. No album in
the repo authors GM 44, so nothing currently renders differently for it.
